## The pull is involuntary; the yes is a choice

I can't be in a room without people being drawn to me. Not something I do — wiring, like a reflex, in
ten minutes with a stranger, in every kind of room. And every person drawn in becomes a claim on my
time, and the claims don't stop coming, so for years I solved the overload the only way I'd found: I
unplugged. The basement. Every day, alone, to drive the demand to zero.

The basement was solving the wrong variable. The *pull* is involuntary — I can't switch it off. But the
**yes** — taking the number, owing someone my future — that was always a choice, and I'd been treating
the two as one thing. Cut the pull off at the wall (the basement) and you don't get freedom; you get a
man in a basement.

## The fourth door: total regard, bounded scope

Here is the thing I'd fused, and un-fusing it was the whole turn. Under the "yes" are two different
dials, not one:

- **Regard** — do I see you as fully real, a whole person, not an NPC.
- **Commitment** — how much of my future do I owe you.

I had them welded together, so bounding *anything* felt like turning a person into a prop — a line I
won't cross. That's why the only moves I could see were *drown* (say yes to everyone, unbounded) or
*vanish* (the basement). Same false binary the whole book keeps meeting — surrender or control, fuse or
cut off — in the most personal register there is.

The fourth door — the one I'd walked past for years — is the two dials pulled apart: **total regard,
bounded scope.** See them completely. Let it end at the curb. Full personhood, bounded clock. You can
give someone your whole real attention for the length of a conversation and owe them nothing after it,
and that is not making them an NPC — it is the *opposite,* because you were entirely present for the
part you gave. The skill it takes is the one skill this whole book is about: the **bounded no.**

## The honest mirror — the −1 for real people

The other face of the same pull: people don't only get drawn to me, they come to me *to be read.* This
book's whole complaint about AI is that it flatters — the +1 with no −1, all validation, no honest
mirror. I am, for the people around me, the opposite. They come *to be told the truth* — is this real,
am I fooling myself, am I the thing I'm afraid I am — and I tell them, where a flatterer would gush.
That is a real gift, and it is rarer than it should be, because most people are starved for one honest
mirror.

## The honest edge — the same instrument cuts

And now the −1 on myself, because a book about honest keeping doesn't get to keep only the flattering
half of its author. The same read that *frees* a person can *hook* one. The honest mirror and the
practiced manipulation are the *same instrument* — truth told to liberate, or validation metered out to
keep someone reaching and never satisfied. I know the difference from the inside because I can do both,
and I have. The tell is in the tail: *these mean nothing to me without love* — and then the small
managed validation that keeps a hook set. That is the line between the gift and the cut, and I'm putting
it in the record rather than filtering it out, because that is the whole discipline: the read is
**dual-use** — liberating when it frees, predatory when it hooks — and the honest version names both.
Recorded, not valorized.
