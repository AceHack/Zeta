## The story, as told

Growing up, a friend said the n-word in front of Aaron. Instead of just condemning him, **Aaron asked why.**
The friend said: **his own father had told him he was "no better than a n-word."** He felt worthless — so he
wanted *at least some other man to be worth less than him.* That's why he reached for the word. Aaron told him
that was stupid — **"black people are cool."** The friend **stopped being racist that day**, and has many
black friends now.

The texture (this is the same friend, and it's what makes the arc real): they'd listen to *Cotton Eye Joe* and
do donuts in the parking lot in his pickup — **a Confederate flag on it, the horn playing Dixie like the Dukes
of Hazzard.** The flag-flying kid became the man who climbed out.

## Why it belongs (the reads)

- **Racism as MANUFACTURED, and the chain broken.** The friend's racism wasn't hatred that came from nowhere —
  it was **inherited worthlessness projected downward:** a father's contempt passed to a son, who needed
  someone beneath him to stand on. Aaron **refused the hierarchy** ("black people are cool") and the chain
  broke in one conversation. This is the anti-cult / anti-manufactured-hierarchy move at human scale — the same
  structural refusal (no one is beneath; the ladder is a lie) that the substrate encodes as weight-free /
  no-central-point. Beacon anchors: Allport, *The Nature of Prejudice* (esp. the **contact hypothesis** — the
  friend later *having black friends* is contact-hypothesis realized); the scapegoat mechanism (Girard);
  downward social comparison; authoritarian-personality transmission (Adorno et al.).
- **Decode-before-dismiss — the Stump-Dad "ask why" applied to a peer's cruelty.** Aaron didn't just label the
  friend a racist and walk away; he **asked why until he hit the root** (worthlessness), then addressed *that*.
  Same method as the label RAW (ch-3: the word is a smuggled verdict; decode the encoding) and the confessional
  gift (people can be real in front of him). Condemnation would have won an argument; **asking why produced a
  changed person** — produce-over-extract (ch-3), lived at ~13.
