## The strategist's daughter

Some kids get *Goodnight Moon.* Addison got *The Art of War* — from an early age, as a bedtime story. I
know how that sounds. It was not about conquest. It was the same thing I give everything: *see the whole
board, understand before you act, the capacity is neutral and the aim is the whole moral story.* Strategy
as a way of seeing, handed to a kid who could hold it.

`[SOCKET — your VERBATIM below. Consent: yours (glass-halo); Addison consents, pre-reads.]`

> "I taught her Art of War from an early age."

And she held it. The moment I keep is this: at **eleven**, she looked at me and said — plainly, the way she
says everything — *"I'm diabolical."* And I said, *"Yes, honey. Me too. We use it for good."*

Sit with what that is, because it's the whole ethos of everything I build, taught at a kitchen table to an
eleven-year-old. A different parent says *"no you're not, don't say that,"* and teaches the child that the
sharp thing in her is shameful and must be hidden — which is exactly how it goes unaimed and dangerous. I
didn't deny the capacity. I **named it with her and pointed it.** The gift is real; the gift is neutral;
what makes it good or evil is where you aim it — and we aim it at good, together, out loud, as a bond. The
*"am I evil yet"* question that runs through this whole book isn't a fear in our house. It's a joke we share
and a compass we both carry.

## The text, and the hardest night

And here is the night that could have broken us and didn't — the one we now, as a family, tell fondly,
because we came through it whole.

The text that got me **involuntarily committed** to a mental institution was **to Addison.** It said: *"if
you become a consumer, I'll end you myself."* You have to know our language to hear it right: in everything
we build, a *consumer* is an **extractor** — the opposite of a producer, the thing this whole book is a war
against. So a father telling his collaborator-daughter *"if you become an extractor, I'll end you myself"*
is, in our idiom, a fierce vow: *I will never let you become the thing we fight.* But strip the idiom, hand
it cold to a judge, and *"I'll end you myself"* is a threat. And she — frightened, loving, moving fast to
protect — got a judge to commit me.

`[SOCKET — your VERBATIM below. Consent: yours (glass-halo); Addison consents + pre-reads her role.]`

> "The text that got me sent to the mental institution was to my daughter Addison — 'if you become a consumer, I'll end you myself.' She got a judge to involuntarily commit me because it sounded like a threat."

If that geometry sounds familiar, it should: it is the *exact* shape of the night I would later stand in
from the other side — a housemate whose words got read as a threat, a frightened person calling for help,
a fast protective call. **I could take that man to care instead of a cell, and hold both regards, because I
had been him.** My words, weaponized against me by someone who loved me and was scared. Taken away. And
came back. You don't learn that from a book. (See `RAW-the-mental-health-over-jail-call-both-regards-held`.)

## The three accounts, all held

Here is the part that is the whole book, lived in a family instead of on a page. Ask the three of us why it
happened and you get three answers, and **none of us needs the others to be wrong:**

- **Addison's account:** it was **concern, and worry for my mental health.** She was scared for me.
- **My account:** it was about **control and anger.** That's my read, and I keep it.
- **Max's account** (a contributor whose judgment I trust): he thinks it was **mental health and concern**
  — and I trust his judgment, so I let his read, which is the one *least* flattering to me, stand right
  beside my own.

*"Both can be true, and we still peacefully coexist."* That is not a compromise where everyone gives a
little. It is the harder thing: three people holding three irreconcilable readings of the same wound, no
one forcing a collapse into one official story, love fully intact. It is the **correlation of souls** and
the **never-collapse** discipline this book preaches — and we didn't read it in a chapter, we *are* it, at
Thanksgiving.

## Why it belongs

Because it's where the book stops being an argument and becomes a life. Two of the hardest things a book
can *claim* — *hold difference without making anyone lose,* and *the capacity is neutral, the aim is the
whole moral story* — my daughter and I didn't argue about. We **lived** them: the ethos handed down at a
bedtime, and a wound held three ways with the love still whole. And it is the lived root of the mercy in
`ch-8` and the mental-health thread: I could stand between a frightened man and a cell because I already
knew, from the inside, that a story is never just one account, and that the person whose words got read as
a threat is not the thing the words made him look like.

These are, as Aaron put it, **fond memories for all our family.** Held light, held whole, held together.
