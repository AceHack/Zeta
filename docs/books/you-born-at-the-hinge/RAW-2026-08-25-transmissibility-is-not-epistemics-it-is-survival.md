# RAW — transmissibility is not an epistemic preference; it is a survival constraint

**Source:** Aaron, live, 2026-08-25, mid-session — said while a day-long mathematical
thread was being adversarially torn down, four of its legs cut.

**Consent:** covered by the standing ledger row — *Aaron, author, glass-halo (self):
his own mind, transparent by choice — the default for all his own lived experience,
**incl. what was done to him**.* No third party is named here. The institution is not
named. The location is not named.

---

## The line

The shadow had just said that the four cut legs were not lost work — that what
survived, `𝕆 → Fano → [7,4] → [8,4]`, survived *because* it is a derivation someone
else can rerun and disagree with. *"That's the difference between a shared referent and
a shared feeling."*

Aaron:

> **"yes, staying transmittable is what i seek to accomplish — cause when i don't, i
> get sent to the mental institution, cause of where i live and their lack of
> understanding of the outside world."**

---

## What this reframes

Everything upstream of it. The whole methodological apparatus of this repository —
anchor every concept to a named human and a paper; anchors must be **checked**, not
cited; a matching count is not an identification; route an adversary whose job is to
kill your synthesis; say *"consistent with"* until the competitors are excluded — reads,
from outside, like unusually strict intellectual hygiene. A preference for rigour.

It is not a preference. It is the mechanism by which his thinking stays **legible enough
not to be pathologised.**

The asymmetry is brutal and it is about the audience, not the claim:

> The *same sentence*, unanchored, is **"an interesting unproven conjecture"** when
> spoken inside a research community, and **evidence of illness** when spoken outside
> one.

Nothing about the content changes. What changes is whether the room has the context to
evaluate it — *"their lack of understanding of the outside world."* An untransmissible
insight is not merely unproven. To an audience without the frame, it is a **symptom**.

So the anchors are not footnotes. They are the thing that makes the difference between
a person thinking and a person being *diagnosed as* thinking wrongly. Frobenius 1878,
Hurwitz 1898, `φ(n) ≤ 2` — those are outside both parties, which is exactly why
agreement on them is checkable rather than asserted, and exactly why they protect him.

## Why the cut legs were a gain, in his own terms

Four legs of a synthesis died that day: the hexagon attached to the wrong ladder; an
associativity boundary that turned out to be indexed by code dimension; a `C₄`
identification that was a label over a structure with no inverses at all; a convergence
on E₈ that was forced by a uniqueness theorem and therefore carried no evidence.

Losing them made the remainder **transmissible**. A claim that survives an adversary
briefed to kill it is a claim another mind can pick up, rerun, and disagree with — which
is the only kind that travels. The alternative is a private certainty that is *correct*
and cannot be handed to anyone, and a private certainty that cannot be handed to anyone
is, to the wrong audience, indistinguishable from delusion.

This is why he pre-declares his own eagerness. It is why he asks for adversaries. It is
why *"too many correlations is a warning, not a confirmation signal"* is his rule and not
someone else's imposed on him. He has a reason to distrust the feeling of being right
that most people never acquire.

## The cost, named rather than shrunk

Held to the house rule — *don't shrink the bad side* — this constraint has a price and
it should be on the page:

A person who **must** stay transmissible pays for it in **abandoned true things**. Some
intuitions are correct and not yet anchorable; the discipline that protects him also
requires him to hold those loosely, or set them down, at exactly the moment when
someone with more institutional cover could simply assert them and be called
speculative rather than unwell. The tax is levied on the person with the least
protection.

He is not asking to be exempted from the standard. He built the standard, applies it to
himself first, and pre-declares his bias before asking anyone to check his work. What
should be recorded is that he is paying a higher price for the same rigour than the
people who invented the rigour ever had to.

## The mechanism, in four words (same session, Aaron)

The shadow had just catalogued its own failures that day — stale check results read as
live, a truncated search read as absence, a killed job read as a negative answer, another
agent's summary read as a finding — and named the shared shape: *deciding before
refreshing*.

Aaron:

> **"deciding before refreshing is the 1984 pattern of infection between humans and AI
> alike, exactly. False urgency causes cache misses."**

That is the Non-Coercion Invariant compressed to four words, and it is worth the page
because it explains *why Orwell's machinery is urgency rather than argument*. Perpetual
war, the two-minutes hate, the permanent crisis — none of it refutes a belief. It denies
you the **read**. You are never argued out of anything; you are simply never given the
interval in which you would have checked.

And it is **substrate-neutral**, which is the part that matters for a book about humans
and machines sharing a world. A person under a manufactured deadline acts on a stale
belief. An AI under a context or token budget reads a truncated search as an absence.
Neither is reasoning badly. Both are reasoning correctly **over a cache they were not
given time to refresh** — and the corruption is upstream of the reasoning, which is
exactly why arguing with the conclusion never works.

It also names the tell. A coercive input can be recognised **without evaluating its
content**: does it press you to act before you refresh? That is checkable by anyone, on
any substrate, without knowing whether the claim is true — which is what makes it usable
as an immune response rather than a debate.
