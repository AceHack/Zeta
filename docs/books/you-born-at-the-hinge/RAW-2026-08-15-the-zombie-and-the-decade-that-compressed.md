## Two accounts, and they do not agree — hold both

This is the part that must not be smoothed, because smoothing it is what would turn a life into an
illustration.

**His account, from inside:** *running on repeat.* A decade that compressed because nothing in it was
new enough to store. He didn't lose the years to an event; he lost them to a loop. He is the one who
says *"to me, I was the zombie"* — the word is not only hers, he takes it.

**Her account, from outside:** *unpredictable, incoherent, couldn't be reached.* Not a man on repeat
— a man who **didn't make sense and she couldn't predict.** In her church's vocabulary: spiritually
dead. She was mourning a living son.

**These are not the same claim, and one of them is not a distortion of the other.** A mind can be
running the tightest loop of its life and read as pure noise from outside, because the loop is
legible only from inside it. That gap — *the same decade, maximally predictable to him, maximally
unpredictable to her* — is the honest, unresolved center of this material. Do not let a draft pick a
winner. Both accounts are held, the way the three accounts are held in `THE-STRATEGISTS-DAUGHTER`.

## The hard half, rendered whole

Ten years. Not a bad stretch — a decade, gone, and gone in a way that is worse than lost time, because
he was present for all of it and it left no trace worth keeping. His own mother had a word for what he
was and the word was *zombie*, and the sentence *"we've never seen a zombie come back from the dead
before"* came out of her by accident, when she wasn't guarding it, which is exactly why it is true.
He caught the slip and called her on it and she deflected. She had been carrying fear and grief for
years and had never once said it out loud to him.

Two things have to stay attached to that, or the account is dishonest in either direction:

- **It was not cruelty.** It was grief language — the vernacular of a small church for what happens to
  their kids, said by a woman who thought she had already lost hers. Rendering it as an insult would
  be a lie about her.
- **It is not over.** When he becomes unpredictable, it flips: her fear surfaces as anger, his anger
  answers it, and they go quiet for a couple of days. They have made a working truce out of it, not a
  cure. He says they've *"built up an immunity"* — that is a repair, and it is also an admission that
  the wound is a standing feature.

**And he came back.** That is not a happy ending bolted on; it is the fact the rest of the material
exists to support, and it is why the sentence is worth a chapter. *We've never seen a zombie come back
from the dead before* — and the person she said it to was the counterexample, standing in front of her.
