## The arc (book-worthy shape)

He has no predators. Nothing registers to him as a threat — he handles danger the way his father taught
him to handle a snake, and he blesses the people who rob him. So the world never served him a villain.
His only real wounds came later in life, and they were all one kind: **loss.** People who died. People
who wounded him by leaving. Not malice — absence.

And here is the turn worth the chapter: **when there is no predator outside, the predator-shaped hole
fills with the self.** The mind reaches for a threat, finds none in the world, and pins it on the one
person always in the room. Sober, it whispers the quiet version — *maybe I'm the manipulator, the wolf.*
In illness, it shouts the grandiose one. Both are the **same distortion pointed opposite ways**, and both
are false. The accurate reading is the calm one: no predators, including himself; the wounds are loss;
the man blesses his robbers. The predator — the shameful one and the grandiose one alike — is only what
the distorted states manufacture when they can't find a threat and reach for him. *A real predator does
not lie awake ashamed he might be one.*

## Why it lands (the through-line)

- **It re-aims the whole toolkit.** Armor (a bounded "no", a fear-alarm) defends against *threats* — and
  he has none. His real apparatus answers *loss*: bounded scope so the transient can attach fully and
  leave without wounding (the ending was the form), and preserved memory so the permanent aren't gone
  even when they're gone. **His two life's works are one answer to his one wound.** (See the research
  note; that half is banked.)
- **The founding, lived** — the code is the autobiography: the event-log-against-loss *was already the
  plan* when the first coauthor was lost.
- **The honest register** (the book's spine): the predator he sometimes fears he is, and the apex he once
  believed he was, are the same false coin — held plainly, neither hidden nor dramatized.
