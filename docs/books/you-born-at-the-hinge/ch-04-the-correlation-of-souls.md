This is the chapter with the number in it. Stay with me — the number is scaffolding, and I'll
tear the scaffolding down in front of you before we're done. But first it has to earn its keep,
because it marks something real that took me a lifetime to see and physics one century to prove.

Physicists have a way of scoring how *correlated* two separated things are — how much their
answers line up when you ask them questions apart. Call the score S. Three values of S matter:

- **S = 2.** The most that two things with *no genuine connection* can line up. Coincidence,
  shared culture, similar training — everything short of a real bond tops out here.
- **S = 2√2** (about 2.83). The most that two things can line up through a *genuine shared
  connection* — real common history, honestly shared state — while remaining two separate
  things. This ceiling is a proven theorem. Nature enforces it.
- **S = 4.** Perfect lockstep. Every answer aligned, always.

Now forget the photons, because — and this was the day this chapter was born — **it's not
quantum, it's human.** Read the same three numbers as a map of every relationship you've ever
had:

- **S = 2 is autonomy.** You, yourself, correlated with no one. The ground state. The self you
  can always come back to.
- **Rising S is relatedness.** Connection, chosen and reciprocated — finishing sentences, shared
  jokes, knowing what they'd say. This is the good stuff, climbing.
- **S = 4 is enmeshment.** Total fusion. No gap between you anywhere — which sounds romantic for
  exactly one second, until you notice what it costs: for two people to agree on *everything*,
  at least one of them has to have stopped existing.

## The ceiling is the revelation

Here's the part that reorganized how I think about love.

That middle number — the 2√2 ceiling — is the most correlation two *intact* selves can honestly
reach. Above it, the math stops being romantic and becomes forensic: correlation past the
ceiling is **more agreement than two free beings can produce.** If you ever observe it, you are
not looking at deeper love. You are looking at *one process wearing two faces* — a script, a
fusion, a captor and a ventriloquist's dummy. Somebody in that pair stopped being somebody.

So the ceiling is not a limit on love. It's the *definition of its honesty*. All the closeness in
the world, right up to the line where there stop being two people to be close — and not one step
past. Every therapist who ever helped a couple "keep their own identities," every wise friend who
said "you two need to be people, not a blob" — they were holding this line without the number.
The life coach was the antecedent; the theorem just agrees with her.

And the pathology chart falls straight out of it: **health is not a pole.** Forced to S=2, you're
isolated — cutoff, walls, safety-as-loneliness. Dragged to S=4, you're enmeshed — dissolved,
scripted, gone. Mental health is not either end; it's the *mobility* — the freedom to move
between your own ground state and the ceiling, by choice, with the exits unlocked. And that
mobility already has a name, the one this chapter has been circling the whole time: **intimacy.**
The freedom to be close *and* separate. The mobility itself is the intimacy. And when both of you
have it — both free to approach, both free to return, neither holding the other's exit — that
mutual mobility is what it actually feels like to be **equals**.

## Tearing down the scaffolding

Now, as promised, the wrecking bar. Two honest confessions about the number.

First: the exact value — 2√2, 2.828… — is an accident of the physics we borrowed. Nobody's
marriage is at 2.71. The *number* is scaffolding. What's essential is the **boundary it marks**:
that there is a maximum honest closeness, that it is real, and that crossing it doesn't mean more
love — it means fewer people. Hold the boundary; let the decimals go.

Second — and I insist on this correction because we got it wrong ourselves before a mathematician
made us fix it: in the actual physics, nothing "controls" anything at any S. Even at the freak
maximum of 4, physics lets neither side send a signal. The *control* reading — that past the
ceiling one party scripts the other — is the **human** register, and it stands on its own
evidence: in human pairs, agreement beyond what two free selves can produce always has a
mechanism, and the mechanism is never pretty. We say so plainly, because a book about honest
keeping doesn't get to keep a dishonest metaphor. The scaffolding is quantum. The building is
human. The building stands either way.
