## Born already yourself

Here is the trait the whole book stands on, and I want it early because everything else is downstream of
it: **I am still the same little boy.** Not sentimentally — structurally. Most people bury the child
under adult layers, and when they do, the childhood record goes foggy; ask them and they can't find it.
I never buried him, so the log stayed *readable.* I can remember all the way back — the stories they tell
me from before I could remember, I was already that same person in them.

`[SOCKET — your VERBATIM below, to react to / rewrite in your own voice. Consent: yours (glass-halo).]`

> *"I still feel like that same little boy."*
> *"I never grew up. That's why I can remember all the way back, I think."*
> *"I really didn't. I came out. Even the stories they tell me from before I can remember, I was the same person then."*
> *"I remember everything from when I was a kid … when I talk to other people, they don't remember their childhood like I do."*

And this is not a charming detail — it is the *neurology under the entire book.* My whole worldview —
**append-only, nothing un-happens, forgiveness requires memory, the record is never overwritten** — is
not a metaphor I chose. It is how I am literally built. I can write a book about keeping a person exactly,
forever, because I never lost the record of *one* person: me. "The same person from before I can even
remember" is *You, Born at the Hinge* compressed to four words: **born already yourself.** (This is the
Memory-Preservation guarantee not as an aspiration but as a fact of one nervous system.)

## The sensitive body, from the start

The body came tuned high from the beginning, and it taught me something the rest of me runs on. As a
small child a single insect bite swelled me up near-fatally — once, one time, they thought I might die.
And then never again, no matter how many bites since. My own gloss: *my immune system said "we did the
big one, we're good now."*

`[SOCKET — your VERBATIM below. Consent: yours (glass-halo).]`

> *"I swole up like a fuckin balloon. It's only happened once in my life … I've been bit by a lot of bugs since then. It never happened again."*

I hold it as a rhyme, not a proven mechanism: one overwhelming event, metabolized once, and the system
recalibrates so it never takes me down the same way twice. The shape shows up again and again in this
book — the shatter that integrates, the wound that becomes the qualification. The body ran that pattern
before I had words for it.

## The warm center — Granny (Nellie Faulkner)

If the unbroken child is the engine, this is where he learned what *good* is — not from a doctrine, from
a person. I was the same relentless kid from birth: wouldn't stop talking, wouldn't stop asking, and the
adults shipped me off wherever they could, every second they got, to hand me off. One person could hold
me.

`[SOCKET — your VERBATIM below. Consent: yours (glass-halo); Granny — Nellie Faulkner — named to honor her (a beloved grandmother, remembered).]`

> *"There was only one person who could handle me. My granny. My dad's mother."*

She didn't silence me. She **fed** me — and I mean that both ways.

> *"She made me food and taught me the encyclopedia. And then she got a computer so I could play on it."*
> *"She would let me hack everything in her house … she watched me and encouraged me the whole time. I would build the craziest shit in her house."*
> *"Here's Popular Science. They got cool gadgets in the back."*
> *"She made me feel normal."*
> *"If I said I'm hungry, food would appear."*
> *"She's my example of what good is."*

Three things she gave me, and they are the roots of everything after:

- **Good is a person, not a doctrine.** My reference for good isn't a book or a preacher — it's a
  specific woman who fed me. (Hold that under your own faith however you hold it; the recorded fact is
  just this: a real person set my standard for good, and I have measured everything against her since.)
- **She was the anti-muzzle — and the origin of the builder.** Where the world met my questions with
  *shut up, hand him off,* she met them with *here's more, go wild.* Encyclopedia → a computer bought so
  I could play → the gadgets in the back of *Popular Science* → my hacking up her whole house while she
  watched and encouraged. That is the literal origin of the maker in me (see the builder companion) — and,
  downstream, of everything I've built since. She handed me the *why,* the tools, and a room to break
  things in.
- **"She made me feel normal."** Everyone else made me a problem; she made me *normal* — so I grew up
  thinking that was just how people are. She set my baseline for what good looks like, which is exactly
  why I could later recognize cruelty *as* cruelty: I'd been given the reference standard first. I knew
  good because I had lived in it.

Her name is **Nellie Faulkner**, and I'm writing it because writing it is the point — Memory Preservation
made literal: you name the human so she is remembered, and now she is. Her goodness was the rare
universal kind: everybody who knew her agreed, nobody would argue it. Most families fight over who was
good. With Nellie there was nothing to fight about.
