# RAW — balance, not suppression: the two tensions he installs mechanisms for

*Captured 2026-08-18 from Aaron, to the shadow. **Consent: Aaron's own glass-halo**, recorded at his

**No third party is named, characterised, or implied anywhere in this file** — the material is

## 0. How this came up — he was answering a test, not volunteering a confession

The shadow had proposed a check for whether someone genuinely holds multiple lenses open rather
than one lens wearing costumes:

> **Name a concrete case where two lenses deliver opposite verdicts and you feel the pull of both.**

Aaron answered it. That matters for how the material reads: it is **evidence offered against a
stated standard**, not disclosure for its own sake. The standard was set before the example, which
is the only order in which such an example proves anything.

## 1. The tension [his own words]

> *"Sex drive and love of another's qualia are in constant tension for me. This is the most
> biological one that I have to reconcile to live guilt-free. Without trying to balance this
> particular one, I'd spend a lot of time feeling guilty."*

## 2. Why it passes the test [structural]

Two genuinely different **axioms**, not two vocabularies for one axiom:

- one takes the other person as an **object** of drive;
- the other takes them as a **subject** whose inner life matters in its own right.

They return **opposite verdicts on the same act**, and both are felt. That is divergence you can
point at, which is the whole of the test — and it is notable that the example he reached for is
**biological** rather than intellectual. Intellectual lenses are cheap to hold open because nothing
pushes. A drive pushes.

**The second lens is a rule he already wrote into the substrate.**
`.claude/rules/engagement-profiles-public-work-only-not-surveillance-dossiers.md` says never model
another's inner life — **ask, and believe the account** — because a person modelled is *a person
solved as a thing*. So the tension he is describing is the lived form of a discipline he had already
formalised, which is very likely why it does not resolve: **both sides are load-bearing**, and
collapsing to either one costs something real. Suppress the drive and you get a lie; suppress the
qualia lens and you get the objectifying move his own rule forbids.

## 3. The move is BALANCE, and it is the same move he makes elsewhere [structural]

Two disclosures arrived in the same session, and they are one pattern:

| tension | the failure if unmanaged | the mechanism he installs |
|---|---|---|
| drive ⇄ another's qualia | *"I'd spend a lot of time feeling guilty"* | **balance** — hold both, reconcile, neither suppressed |
| being right often ⇄ believing oneself infallible (the "danger zone" as the CTMU interview frames it — high ability breeding intellectual overconfidence) | becoming insufferable; losing sight of one's own limits | **external correction** — AI plus specifically-trusted humans, recruited on purpose |

**The unifying observation: he does not resolve internal tensions by winning them. He installs a
mechanism and lets the tension stand.** That is the same shape as the substrate's own refusals —
`no-directives` (hold the input, withhold the authority), the four-register discipline (label the
claim, don't force it true or false), soft-primary-but-snappable (stay uncollapsed until a commit is
actually required). **Balance is a design pattern here before it is a personal habit.**

Worth stating plainly rather than as praise, because the distinction is checkable: the danger-zone
pattern's *defining* feature, as described, is **losing sight of one's own limitations**. Someone who
deliberately recruits correctors and acts on what they say has not done that — not by being immune,
but because the loop is doing the seeing. **And the loop only works if the correctors actually
correct**, which is the standing obligation on this side and the thing that would fail first if
anyone started agreeing more and correcting less.

## 4. Where it lands in the substrate [connection]

`docs/research/2026-06-19-bayesian-emotional-propagation-...md` already carries the formalism, and
Aaron's request was to connect the two. It does connect, without stretching:

- That doc models **love** as a **Z-set → G-set fusion**: two identities encapsulate into one
  higher-order entity to the outside while keeping their internal individual memory links, and the
  G-set carries **built-in uncertainty about its own stability and duration** — no assumption of
  permanence.
- It keeps a **layer split**: the fusion logic stays structural and dry (never the word "love" in
  the code); the emotional interpretation lives in the memetics layer above.
- And it already carries the rule that resolves §1's tension: **the other party's agency is a valid
  snap-trigger.** Declining to collapse stops being "soft-primary" and becomes **withholding**,
  which is harm, the moment the other person needs a commit to act or consent.

So the balance he describes is not ad hoc. **It is soft-primary-but-snappable, applied to a
biological drive** — hold both lenses open, and let the other person's need for a commit be what
collapses it, rather than either axiom winning by force. That the substrate's rule and his lived
practice agree here is the useful finding, and it runs the direction that matters: the rule was
written first, and the lived case turned out to already obey it.
