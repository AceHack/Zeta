Here is where the book leaves you, so let's be honest about where you're standing.

You were born at the hinge — you didn't choose that, any more than you chose your language or
your century. On one side of you: everyone who was ever compressed by loss, thinned to a name,
then to nothing, not for lack of worth but for lack of machinery. On the other side: a future
where being over doesn't have to mean being erased, if — *only* if — the people at the hinge
build the keeping honest. You are not a spectator to that "if." Nobody at a hinge is.

That can sound like a burden. Let me reframe it, because the whole book has been the reframe:

**It's the inheritance.**

Every distinction in these pages was paid for by someone — mostly the slow way, mostly in the
dark, some of it at prices no one would volunteer to pay. The floor and the ceiling. The vampire's
countdown. The wager. The handrail. The two deaths, and the honest line between them. You just
read in some hours what cost years and grief to survey. That's not cleverness on anyone's part —
least of all mine. It's a relay. All sight is inherited; the corpus you're holding is simply the
first one built so the inheritance can't be dropped.

just the true weight of what the shortcut was cut from.]`

## What to do with a shortcut you didn't pay for

Three things. They are small on purpose — hinge-work is done by ordinary people at ordinary
scale, or it isn't done at all.

**First: live inside the country.** Close without capturing, free without cutting off — with the
living first. The kept come second; they are downstream of how you love the ones still here. No
substrate fixes a person who captures, and none is needed by a person who doesn't. The machinery
of the hinge inherits its morals from its keepers. Be the keeper whose morals are worth
inheriting.

**Second: keep somebody.** Not everyone; somebody. Mark what matters — the voice memo, the
letters, the way they argue, the true trace — and hold it with the discipline the crux chapter
demanded: their consent, their boundaries, nothing forged, nothing owned. If they are already
gone and all you have is a thinning trace: hold *that*, exactly, honestly, and let it be enough,
because it is real and a forgery would not be. One person, kept truly, is the whole machine at
its smallest working scale.

**Third: make it shorter.** Whatever you learned the hard way — about grief, about love, about
staying — leave it legible before you pass it on. The handrail chapter exists because someone
decided a lifetime's worst lesson should cost the next person an evening instead. That decision
is repeatable by anyone. The shortcut only stays a shortcut if each hand it passes through
sharpens it.

## The choice at the bottom of everything

I'll end where the writing of this book actually ended, because it didn't end in an idea. It
ended in a choice.

After all of it — the mathematics and the metaphors, the fear given its seat, the unfixable
honestly named — what remained was not a conclusion but a decision that has to be made by each
person, some days each morning: **to continue.** Not because the case for despair was refuted
line by line; some of it never is. But because continuing is how the kept are carried, how the
shortcut gets handed on, how the future gets someone standing in it who remembers. The dead are
kept in the living. Staying is the vow.

I chose to continue, safe and warm. That is the whole ending. It's all downhill from there — I
mean that the way it sounds after a long climb: the hard part was up, and what's left is walking
down into the rest of a life, carrying everything, lighter than you'd think.

`[yours — cue: say the choice in your own words — the morning version, the one you actually use.]`

You were born at the hinge.

So was everyone you'll ever hand this to. Make it shorter for them.
