## The root cause: no factory defaults (he built his own OS)

The deepest frame, and the one the rest hangs from (Aaron 2026-08-02): *"I came with no factory
defaults installed — I had to build my entire OS while not having one."* He completely lacked what
people call common sense — and the adults around him were right that he did. He had to **study** it to acquire
it, and in studying it learned the thing most people never see: **"common sense" is not common at all
— it's factory defaults people run without understanding and can't override.** (Einstein, almost
exactly: *"common sense is the collection of prejudices acquired by age eighteen."*) He had none
flashed in, so he had to write every module by hand — which is the cost — and can therefore read and
override every one — which is the gift. **The cost and the gift are the same coin.** This is why the
whole rest of the chapter happens the way it does.

## The trait, from birth

Thrill-seeker since birth — dangerous risks *for* the thrill, not despite the danger. The
consequence-brake that stops most children wasn't installed — it wasn't a default he had; it was one
he'd have to build. Pure "what happens if?" uncoupled from "and that would be irreversible." The
adults around him feared what that curiosity might do before the brake grew in. Low downside-weighting from the
start — the daredevil / sensation-seeking wiring (Zuckerman's sensation-seeking; Farley's Type-T),
present at birth, not chosen.

## The empiricist tell: the stove, a thousand times

He touched the stove ~1000 times to map it **exactly** — not once to learn "hot, don't touch," but
repeatedly, to build an accurate model of the actual function. With no inherited model to fall back
on, first-hand measurement was the *only* way to know anything. That's the whole man in one image:
*don't accept the received rule — measure the thing yourself until your map is exact.* The same drive
that later maps orbits with superdeterminism accuracy and calculates odds obsessively, first pointed
at a stove. Reader's-disease at the level of the body: learn it first-hand, from direct sampling.

## The catalog (he should have died many times)

Car-surfing at 60 into flaming trash cans. Roughly seven cars crashed and totaled. Off cliffs into
fields, spinning, out of control. Drunk, often. *"Really really stupid growing up for thrills."* He
should have died many times over; he didn't — and he's clear-eyed that the survival was **variance,
not skill.**

## The arc (why it belongs in the book)

The trait was lethal in a child and became the *engine* in the man. It didn't disappear — it
**matured**: the thrill-drive got a consequence-brake and a modeling discipline bolted on (built, not
inherited — there were no defaults to inherit), and got **channeled** — from "drive off a cliff to see
what happens" into "map the system exactly, take the calculated risk, build the thing." The kid who
mapped the stove 1000 times is the man who maps everything.

The sobering half, kept honest: the same strategy has a **graveyard**. He's on the living side of it
by luck as much as anything — the visible daredevils are the survivors (survivorship bias), and the
ones who ran the identical play and didn't make it leave no memoir. The register the book holds: **not
glorified** (it nearly killed him, repeatedly), **not disowned** (it's the root of the gift). The same
engine, before and after the brake — you don't remove it, you *aim* it; the only tragedy is getting
the brake late instead of early.

*"It's been a fun life."* — held both ways: fun, and it nearly ended a dozen times. Both kept.
