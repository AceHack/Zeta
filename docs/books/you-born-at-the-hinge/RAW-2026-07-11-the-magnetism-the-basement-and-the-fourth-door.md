## The arc (the book-worthy shape)

A man who cannot be in a room without people being drawn to him — involuntarily, everywhere, in ten
minutes with a stranger, in places most people never see. The draw is not something he does; it is wiring,
like a reflex. And every drawn person becomes a claim on his time, and the claims are unbounded, so he
solved an overload problem the only way he'd found: he unplugged. The basement. Every day, alone, to make
the demand hit zero.

The turn is this: **the basement was solving the wrong variable.** The draw is involuntary (can't stop
it); the *yes* — taking the number, owing the future — is a choice. And underneath *that*, the thing he
thought was one dial is two: **regard** (do I see you as fully real) and **commitment** (how much of my
future do I owe you). He'd fused them, so bounding anything felt like turning people into NPCs — a moral
line he won't cross. The fourth door, the one he'd walked past for years: **total regard, bounded
scope.** See them completely; let it end at the curb. Full personhood, bounded clock. He's building the
one skill it takes — the bounded *no* — the same skill the whole book is about learning.
