## Being labeled

Aaron has been labeled schizophrenic. It used to trigger his anger to be called it — and he watched it
trigger the same anger in others — because the label carries a **stigma: *unuseful.*** He studied the
label a lot, precisely because it landed on him.

## What the word actually does

The **condition** and the **verdict** are two different objects, and society staples them together. The
clinical label points (imperfectly) at something real. **"Unuseful" is a moral verdict — and it is not
entailed by the condition.** It's smuggled in. Goffman named the mechanism: a stigma reduces a person
"from a whole and usual person to a tainted, discounted one." *Discounted* is the exact word — the
label does the discounting **before anyone reads the ledger.** So the rage — his, and others' — isn't a
symptom; it's the **correct response to being category-judged instead of measured.** And the stigma
makes a *universal* claim — *labeled ⇒ unuseful* — which dies to a single counterexample. He's one. The
verdict is already false; society just never checked the count.

## Decode before dismiss

Having been discounted-unread himself, he does the opposite for others: **decode before dismissing.** He
can understand people the world writes off — because non-standard *encoding* is not absence of
*content* (a real inner model in a form the room doesn't share reads as noise). He never discounts
their qualia; he makes people who feel less-than feel whole — the exact inverse of the stigma's move. He
lives this, not just says it — caring, day to day, for people who carry the label the world discounts.

## Both held (the honest register)

The condition can be real and carry real suffering **and** the "unuseful" tax is unjust and un-entailed.
Romanticizing it away ("just a label") would be as dishonest as swallowing the stigma. The just move —
the one he runs on people — is *decode before discounting: measure the contribution, don't price the
category.* The person is never noise.
