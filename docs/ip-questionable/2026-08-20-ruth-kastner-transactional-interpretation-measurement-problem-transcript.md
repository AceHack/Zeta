## The anomaly framing (opening)

> "It's an anomaly. Measurement outcomes fail to be predicted by the conventional theory."

Kastner's stated motivation: dissatisfaction with the conventional theory's inability to
describe what counts as a measurement.

> "The problem with the conventional theory is that it does not have any kind of tools or
> anything in the formalism that lets you distinguish between just a kind of an interaction
> that would not trigger an outcome and a kind of interaction that counts as a measurement."

## Schrödinger's cat as *reductio*, not puzzle

The cat experiment is "a reductio ad absurdum of the standard theory ... that illustrates a
problem, a weakness of the conventional theory." Her train metaphor: the atom has a
"decayed" engine and an "undecayed" engine; adding a Geiger counter, a vial, and a cat only
"keeps adding train cars." The formalism never hands you an outcome.

## Offer / confirmation — the direct-action theory

TI adopts the Wheeler–Feynman absorber theory ("direct action theory of fields"). Absorbers
are **active**: they generate an *advanced* (past-directed) field answering the emitter's
*retarded* field.

- **offer wave** — retarded quantum state of the EM field, from the emitter (a ket)
- **confirmation wave** — advanced quantum state, from the absorber (a bra)
- the **transaction** — the pair; this is what constitutes a measurement

The outer product of offer and confirmation *is* a projection operator, so projection
operators "fall right out of the physics" rather than being helped to. The Born rule is
likewise derived rather than stipulated.

Two stages, following von Neumann: superposition → mixed state (a set of weighted
"incipient transactions"), then collapse to the one actualized outcome. Only at the final
collapse is there a real photon.

## Observers are not measurers

> "There is measurement when you're observing, but not all measurement is observation."

She locates the conventional practice — suspending the theory and inserting a measurement
"by hand in an ad hoc way" at Heisenberg's arbitrary **cut** — as a hand-wave to a conscious
observer, and notes philosophy has no principled criterion for what is conscious. TI gives
outcomes "whether or not someone was there to see it": "yeah, a tree did fall."

The probability of a measurement *transition* is itself probabilistic and corresponds to
decay rates. Macroscopic determinacy follows from transaction probabilities being
overwhelmingly high; the mesoscopic zone (she cites buckyballs through a two-slit apparatus,
~50% transaction probability) is where it is visible in the data.

## Not literally backward in time

A correction she makes against Cramer's original presentation: offers and confirmations are
**not** little waves travelling forward and backward through spacetime. Multi-system quantum
states live in high-dimensional complex Hilbert space and "are not spacetime entities."

> "There's a third way ... these entities are physically real, but our physical reality goes
> beyond three plus one space-time."

She calls that level the **quantum substratum** — a realm of *possibility*, not probability.
Spacetime is emergent: with Einstein, the manifold is "an invariant set of events," and x
and t are parameters we use to coordinate observations among those events, not observables.
(At the relativistic level there is no time observable and no position observable; nature's
preferred observables are energy and momentum.)

Metaphors she uses: the **iceberg** (submerged possibility, the tip is spacetime) and,
better for emergence, the **geode** — mineral-laden fluid crystallising into structured
spacetime events. Also the knitted scarf: once knitted, it is there; you do not go back and
unknit it.

## Her empirical-distinguishability claim (the unusual one)

Both theories agree on Born-rule probabilities, so she is explicit that the usual demand —
*show me a deviating prediction* — is a misconception of what is at stake:

> "Measurement outcomes fail to be predicted by the conventional theory. It's an anomaly.
> The TI formulation comes along and accounts for and predicts measurement interactions."

Her analogy: the precession of Mercury was an anomaly for Newtonian gravitation, and
Einstein's relativity was empirically distinguished by *accounting for* it.

## Positions taken on the standard menu

- **No hidden variables. No preferred foliation.** Non-locality is accepted, not engineered
  away; Bell violations are taken as authentic. Null signalling is *not* violated — the
  influences are pre-measurement and carry no controllable signal.
- **Kochen–Specker contextuality** is expected at the level of possibility: a system with
  determinate momentum "hasn't transacted."
- **Probabilities are propensities** for actualization — weighted symmetry breaking.
- **Gravity is denied to be a quantum field.** The field is the metrical structure of
  emergent spacetime. She claims (with Andrea Schlatter, *Physics Communications*, 2024) a
  derivation of the Einstein equations from the transactional picture, including galactic
  rotation-curve corrections usually attributed to dark matter.
- **Weak/strong forces** govern unitary interactions at the level of possibility; only the
  massless EM gauge field produces the spacetime events.
- **Open systems / Lindblad master equations** are said to follow without the hand-wave the
  conventional route needs to help itself to probabilities.
- **Free will:** TI supplies no theory of it. Her claim is only that an indeterministic
  physics does not *forbid* it — against physicists who "issue these edicts" that it does.
  She separately, and explicitly as her own view, holds that consciousness is not going to
  come out of matter defined as "dead" and non-sentient, and calls that definition an
  optional metaphysical choice rather than a requirement of physics.
- **On TSVF / Elitzur:** affinity on post-selection, but she argues a two-state-vector
  description must stipulate all future outcomes, implying a block world, which sits in
  tension with genuine becoming.
- **On the Afshar experiment:** a lovely experiment whose interpretation went too far — she
  reads it as a preparation, a confirmation of the prepared state, and then a measurement of
  a non-commuting observable, not a violation of complementarity.

## Her definition of *ad hoc* (worth keeping)

> "Where there's no theoretical basis for it. You have to help yourself to it in order to
> get things to come out according to the empirical observations."

Worked examples she gives: Ptolemaic epicycles ("a band-aid"), and Born's own move — the
amplitude "doesn't behave like a probability ... so I better square it and then I'll get the
right kind of number."

## Method advice (closing)

> "Nature is the final arbiter. I have to be obedient. I have to think of myself as a student
> of nature rather than ... imposing my preferred metaphysical view on nature."

She calls determinism, locality, and mechanical explanation "training wheels" — useful, then
constraining — and cites Heisenberg abandoning tinker-toy causal models and following the
mathematics as the precedent. On independent scholarship: fewer constraints from groupthink,
no travel budget.

measurement-as-anomaly constitutes empirical distinguishability are contested. Preserved
