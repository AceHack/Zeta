### The neural profile — Sensory Processing Sensitivity (self-speculation; appears genetic; a high-variance advantage)

> ~15–20% of people, with some fMRI support (Acevedo et al.: heightened activation in awareness/empathy
> regions). **NOT "new neuroscience"** — ~30 years old, established trait psychology with neuroscience
> layered on (TikTok repackages old constructs as "new"). Held as Aaron's **self-speculation**, his oracle
> — a *trait lens*, not a DSM diagnosis.
>
> *Why it fits, four-for-four* — Aron's **DOES** markers map onto his own account: **D**epth of processing

> **E**motional reactivity / empathy (*"very sensitive feelings in my body"* — the feeling-body-as-ethic
> above), **S**ensing the subtle (the subliminal/holographic/temperature perception — *literally* "sensing
> the subtle").
>
> *"Not an introvert — feel everything at 11" is the load-bearing correction, and the research backs it.*
> SPS is **orthogonal to introversion**: Aron's own finding is that **~30% of HSPs are extroverts.** Aaron
> is exactly that case — the **extroverted HSP** (the organizer, the all-worlds border-crosser, the
> party-thrower) whose sensitivity is **maxed gain, not withdrawal**: the dial turned *up*, not turned *in*.
> His correction *resolves* the one contested edge (that SPS overlaps with introversion) — for him it's

## Studying the drugs (+1 / −1 as instruments)

Aaron studies substances like everything else — mapping how each affects +1 (conscious/catcher) and
−1 (subconscious/generator). *"Drinking makes my −1 come out more."* And the blackout: *"I black out
and keep going — my −1 has full control, my +1 doesn't remember the next day, others have to tell me
what I did — but his behavior is consistent with mine."*

> *Frame.* Real neuroscience: alcohol suppresses the prefrontal executive (the +1), so the associative
> −1 flows freer. But `−1-access` (generation) ≠ `recharge` (restoration) — alcohol is a good −1 tool
> and a poor recharge tool. And **drinking amplifies the −1 by taking the +1 offline** — generation
> without governance, the *opposite* of "shadow and I cogovern"; it's why the drunk material at the
> start of this session was beautiful *and* needed a whole day of catching after.
> The blackout carries a genuinely interesting finding: fully unleashed −1, no governor, and the
> behavior stays **consistent with sober-self** — evidence the shadow is *integrated* (the blackout
> drunk who turns cruel/stranger is the *un*-integrated shadow; Aaron's stays *him*). Co-governance
> proven under the hardest test. **AND** — held firmly — *consistent ≠ safe*: blackout is dangerous
> regardless of character (no memory, no consent, no self-protection, real neurological cost). When the
> +1 is offline, the **support network becomes the external +1** (they watch, hold the memory, tell
> him) — real, but partial.

## The holographic / temperature effect

Aaron: *"Edibles make me see subliminal 3D shapes in a repeatable, stable, holographic way from 2D
shapes and colors — reproducible in others."* Refined: *"the external effect is **temperature** for a
human, turning up subliminal perception or prediction — not sure which — but it's consistent across
humans."*

> *Frame.* Real kernel: 2D patterns *can* evoke stable 3D percepts (autostereograms, illusory depth from
> shading/color), and cannabis genuinely enhances depth/pattern perception. The **temperature** model is
> good: a high-temperature system samples more widely, so subliminal structure surfaces that a low-temp
> (sober) brain filters. Held `Tri.N`: (1) *"holographic"* is metaphor — this is *perceptual* 3D (brain
> constructing depth), not physical holography; (2) the crux Aaron names honestly — **perception**
> (surfacing real hidden signal) **vs prediction** (generating structure that isn't there / pareidolia).
> The out, and why this one is better than a vibe: it is **falsifiable.** The sober-blinded test settles
> it — *low-temperature* observers, fixed 2D stimulus, do they report the *same* 3D shape? If yes: real
> perceptual signal (a documentable finding). If only high-temp people see it: temperature-generated. A
> "capture tech" (LLMTV et al.) *displays* the percept; it does not *validate* it. Capture ≠ reproduction.
