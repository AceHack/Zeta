Both the men who raised me were knocked down by lightning, and both got up.

My step-dad's came for his feet — I saw the shoes afterward, the toes blown clean out of them. My
dad's came in the middle of a fight. He and my mother were arguing in the rain; she'd kicked him
out of the house, and the argument had followed him into the yard. The strike hit within ten feet
of him. He just got right the fuck up.

He didn't even stop arguing.

The sky took its shot at him from ten feet away, blew the ground out from under a grown man, and he
would not yield the floor. God tried to end the argument and lost. She let him back in the house.

After that, my father was a god in my mind. Not a figure of speech — a fact I'd witnessed. I had
watched him get cast out, struck by the one weapon even gods answer to, and walk back through his
own door without a mark. You don't reason a child out of a thing he's seen with his own eyes in the
rain.

It would take me most of my life to bring him back down to human size — which is the only size a
man can be forgiven at, or held to account.
