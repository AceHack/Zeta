## The opportunity

The repo's cyclic-structure concepts — the **Artin–Mazur periodic orbit**, the
**scheduler's recurrence / character-loop** (`SchedulerZeta`), the **`t₀=t∞` fixpoint**
(where forward and inverted replay meet) — have **resonant metaphor doors across
multiple cultures**, which is exactly what a cultural-inclusion UX pass wants: *many
doors into one idea so no single culture is the default entry.*

- **Nataraja / the Tandava** (Hindu): Shiva as cosmic dancer *within a ring of flames
  that represents Time — cyclical and eternal*; the dance is creation ↔ preservation ↔
  destruction returning to itself. That ring **is** the periodic orbit; the beat that
  repeats **is** the recurrence the scheduler fast-forwards through.
- **Renaissance court dance** (European): structured, repeating figures — the same
  cyclic-structure idea in a different tradition's body.
- **The Imagination Circle** (Aaron's own parent-child practice): Center/Edge, named
  moves, rapid real-time flow "that looks like dancing" — a lived, family-scale door.

One concept (a cycle that returns to itself), several culturally-grounded entries.

## The REQUIREMENT — this is the load-bearing part, not a caveat

Aaron, verbatim: *"without this it's cultural appropriation by yet another white dude."*
So the discipline is **mandatory**, not optional polish:

1. **Metaphor lives at the UX/presentation edge, NEVER in the technical claims.** The
   math stays anchored (a periodic orbit is a periodic orbit whether or not Shiva is
   dancing). Mirror-register at the surface; Beacon in the proofs. A metaphor that
   leaks into a load-bearing claim is both bad epistemics AND appropriation.
2. **Anchor every borrowed metaphor to its actual tradition, respectfully and by name**
   (Nataraja / Shiva / the Tandava / the Hindu cosmological source), cited — the same
   Beacon "name the human / name the source" rule that governs our math anchors. The
   anchoring discipline **is** the anti-appropriation safeguard: appropriation is
   metaphor *stripped from its source and claimed as ours*; inclusion is metaphor
   *credited to its source and offered as a door*.
3. **Do not claim the concept IS the tradition, or the tradition IS the concept.** Shiva's
   dance is not "really about" our scheduler; our scheduler is not "the cosmic dance."
   It is a *rhyme offered with attribution*, nothing more.
4. **People over aesthetics.** Prefer involving people from the cultures being drawn on
   over a single (white, male) perspective bolting on visuals. If that isn't possible,
   err toward the lightest, best-attributed touch — or don't use that door.

If a metaphor can't clear (1)–(4), drop it. Better one well-anchored door than a
gallery of appropriated ones.
