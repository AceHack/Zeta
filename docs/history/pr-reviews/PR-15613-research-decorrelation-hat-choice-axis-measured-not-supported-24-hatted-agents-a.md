## Description

Measures whether persona hat-CHOICE actually decorrelates, or whether the audition is
ceremony. A new **axis** in the existing `decorrelation-harness.ts` framework — not a
new framework. Status `toy`; every claim carries its register.

**The mechanism is NOT SUPPORTED — and not refuted either. The sharper finding is that
the question it argues about is close to moot at this scale: hats decorrelate very
little, so how you distribute them is a second-order effect on a first-order effect
that is nearly absent.**

7 260 logged generations across 4 local models, two experiments. Raw JSONL committed so
every number is recomputable offline without a model.

## The strongest result is methodological, and the data produced it

On `llama3.2:1b`, self-selected agents **nearly doubled abstention recall** (0.393 vs
0.201) at *better* precision, while losing 5 points of accuracy. Under any single merged
quality score condition B is simply **worse**. Under two numbers it is not worse — it is
at a different operating point, trading raw accuracy for a much better-calibrated
refusal, which for a verifier role is plausibly the trade you want.

> A merged score would not have shown a smaller version of this effect. It would have
> shown the **opposite sign**, and the whole behavioural difference would have been
> invisible.

## The controls, because a comparison without them decides itself

- **Calibration gate.** Condition N (no hat, identical agents) reads ρ̄ = **1.000 ±
  0.000** over 91 defined pairs. Precommitted: if it had not, no other E2 number would
  have been reportable.
- **Noise floor.** Cross-phrasing divergence is judged against the within-phrasing
  split-half divergence of the same sampler, *and* against a permutation null that holds
  group sizes fixed so finite-sample JSD bias cancels. The permutation test is the
  load-bearing one and the ratio is explicitly the weaker, conservative statistic.
- **Pre-registration**, written before any E2 generation and reproduced unedited —
  including the prior that turned out wrong. It is what makes *p* = 0.052 a miss.
- **Bias ledger.** Two irremovable biases, one favouring each condition, named with the
  direction each cuts. A third (roster cycling that would have manufactured duplicate
  hats in condition A) was found in a dry run and removed.
- **Mutation testing.** 10 defects injected into the metric functions, **0 survived**.
  Two of the mutants turn this harness *into* the defects below; the suite refuses both.

## The two reported harness defects, verified rather than repeated

- `pipelineAccuracy` — **real, and sharper than reported.** It is the oracle-best union
  of correct sets, so it assumes a free oracle picks the winner on disagreement. Not
  inherited.
- GAIN's denominator — **real, but the reported mechanism was wrong.** It is not
  milliseconds; it is a hardcoded constant `2`, and the latency fields that *are*
  collected are never read by `measureAxis`. Corrected: it says energy and measures
  **nothing**. Not inherited — no GAIN is computed, wall-clock is labelled latency, and
  the cost denominator is an explicitly-labelled FLOP proxy. There is no unprivileged
  joule meter on this machine and saying so beats a number that looks like energy.

## What this does not touch

Nothing here says the audition is worthless. E1/E2 measure *decorrelation*. Asking
rather than assigning also buys **consent**, and a mechanism can fail its instrumental
justification while keeping its ethical one intact. Nor does a null on 1–3B local models
with seed-only instance diversity refute the hypothesis at fleet scale — that limit is
stated in §3 and never waved. The highest-value follow-up is a powered replication at
60+ agents per condition; at ~200 ms/generation it is cheap.
