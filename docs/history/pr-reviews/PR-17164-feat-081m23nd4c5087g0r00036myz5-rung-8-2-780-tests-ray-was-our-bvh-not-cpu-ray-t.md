## Description

## The finding

Rung 7 (#17155) concluded **"CPU ray tracing: no, not at interactive rates"** from **2,780 triangle tests per ray** and a **7.6× BVH speedup**, and defended those numbers:

> *"With 738× overdraw a ray genuinely meets on the order of a thousand triangles; 2,780 tests to find them is a small constant over the true answer."*

**The premise is right and the inference does not follow.** Measured on the same scene, same framing:

| quantity | measured |
|---|---|
| triangles a ray genuinely crosses (hitting rays) | 1,586 |
| **triangles at-or-before the NEAREST hit** | **0.49** |

A nearest-hit query does not have to find the thousand crossings — it has to find the **first** one. So 2,780 is not a small constant over the answer, it is ~5,700× it. The 7.6× was a measurement of our BVH, not of CPU ray tracing.

## Diagnosis — two separable defects, both measured

**1. The traversal was never front-to-back.** Replaying rung 7's *identical* tree, traversal replaced:

| traversal, same tree | tri/ray | µs/ray |
|---|---|---|
| rung 7's | 2,777 | 131.4 |
| + distance-stack cutoff | 1,600 | 74.0 |
| + near/far ordering | **1,319** | **57.0** |

2.1× free — and the decomposition matters: cutoff 1.74×, ordering 1.21×. Holding SIMD and threads out was to isolate the algorithmic lever, and that applies inside the rung too.

**2. The tree cannot separate the primitives.** Σ leaf box surface area ÷ root's = **2,577** (a perfect partition is 1.0), because the mean triangle bbox is 29.3% of the scene diagonal. Even a *perfectly ordered* traversal over rung 7's tree costs 1,205 tests/ray.

## Task 1 — spatial splits

**Stich, Friedrich & Dietrich, *Spatial Splits in Bounding Volume Hierarchies*, HPG 2009**, over binned SAH (Wald 2007), with Sutherland–Hodgman polygon chopping (CACM 1974) so a split is tight and not merely legal.

**M2 Ultra · Bun 1.3.14 · ONE thread · NO SIMD · 128²:**

| # | configuration | render | µs/ray | **tri/ray** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | median + rung 7 traversal (**baseline**) | 2,153 ms | 131.4 | **2,781** |
| 4 | SAH + ordered | 373 ms | 22.7 | 574 |
| 5 | **SBVH + ordered** | **268 ms** | **16.3** | **440** |
| 6.3 | subdiv 4³ + SAH + ordered | 36 ms | 2.2 | 34 |

| | rung 7 | rung 8 | factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| tests/ray | 2,780 | **440** | **6.3×** |
| vs exhaustive scan | 7.6× | **62.8×** | **8.3×** |

**Every accelerated structure is pixel-identical to rung 7's image** and bit-identical to brute force on every seeded ray.

### The subdivision rival wins on speed and loses on exactness

Midpoint subdivision is **7.4× faster than the SBVH** (2.2 vs 16.3 µs/ray). It is paid for twice:

- **58× the memory** — 234 MB against 4.0 MB.
- **It is not exact.** SAH and SBVH move **0** pixels; subdivision moves **2.9%**. Midpoint subdivision is exact in exact arithmetic (`embed3d` is linear), but a midpoint rounds into a `Float32Array` — children leave the parent plane by 3.4e-8, depths shift by 4.5e-9 relative, and every differing pixel is a depth tie decided differently.

**SBVH wins.** Rung 7 declared its tie-break so the answer would not depend on traversal order; a rival that reintroduces the ambiguity through rounding gives that back. Subdivision stays, measured, with its price stated.

### A property rung 7 left unstated

Perturb **every coordinate by one float32 ulp**: **393 / 16,384 pixels change (2.40%)**. Control — re-rendering a byte-identical copy: **0**.

> **The tracer is deterministic. The scene is ill-conditioned.** Different properties. Rung 7's tie-break covers the ~1% of *exact* ties; it cannot help the larger population sitting *within one ulp* of a tie.

## Task 2 — is it the projection? **No.** A clean negative result

All **C(8,3) = 56** eigenlayer 3-frames (the 8 axes verified orthonormal to 1e-9) plus 60 seeded random orthonormal frames:

| family | overdraw min | max | spread |
|---|---|---|---|
| 56 eigenlayer triples | 598.2 | 834.3 | **1.39×** |
| 60 random frames | 598.0 | 732.0 | 1.22× |
| **shipped `embed3d`** | **737.6 — rank 33 of 56** | | |

Mean triangle bbox ÷ scene diagonal: **26.0%–29.3% across every frame measured**.

> **No 3D embedding escapes it.** The best frame buys 1.23× on overdraw and nothing on primitive size, against the two orders of magnitude a BVH needs. **The pathology is intrinsic to 4_21.**

Two honesty notes: the three frames tied for the minimum each collapse **6,912 faces to zero area** — they win by losing a ninth of the surface. And a deliberately squashed frame scores **334**, better than everything, while showing strictly less of the object; overdraw is not a quality score on its own.

**Why the spread is so small** (`unmetered`, offered as explanation): concentration of measure — Lévy/Milman, and Cauchy's 1841 surface-area formula. An average over 60,480 faces of a smooth function on V₃(R⁸) concentrates about its mean.

### One premise in the brief is refuted

The 27-faces-per-edge figure is **not** a flattening artifact. `edgeFaceIncidence` runs on the combinatorial face list — no coordinates, no embedding — and reports **min 27, max 27 over all 6,720 edges**. Every edge of 4_21 lies in 27 of its 2-faces *in R⁸*. No embedding can change a number no embedding produces. (Also measured: all 60,480 face centroids stay **distinct** in 3D — the crowding is overlap of extended triangles, not coincidence of position.)

### Can visibility be answered in 8D?

Not usefully. A linear R⁸→R³ map has a **5-dimensional kernel**; a 3D ray pulls back to a 6-flat, and 6+2−8 = 0 — one point per 3D crossing. Nothing is saved; **the overlap *is* the kernel**. What 8D *could* buy is a **derived** tie-break replacing the authored one (tied faces are distinct in R⁸) — filed, not built.

## The revised answer

| resolution | SBVH (exact) | subdiv 4³ (2.9% perturbed) |
|---|---|---|
| 128² | 319 ms — **3.1 fps** | 38 ms — **26.7 fps** |
| 256² | 936 ms — 1.07 fps | 127 ms — **7.9 fps** |
| 512² | 3,341 ms — 0.30 fps | 437 ms — 2.3 fps |

**128² is interactive today**, one core, plain TypeScript, no SIMD — against rung 7's 0.5 fps. **256² is reachable.** **512² exact is not**, and that remains the honest boundary. Rung 7's threading and SIMD estimates now sit on top of *these* numbers.

The general form worth carrying: **an accelerator's cost must be compared against the answer size of the query you are actually asking.** Same measurement, same scene, opposite conclusion.

## Not claimed

Not a state-of-the-art SBVH — reference *unsplitting* (Stich §4.3) is named, not built, and is the likely reason duplication sits at 1.435× rather than ~1.2×. No claim about other hardware or the browser. No 8D visibility algorithm. The concentration-of-measure explanation is `unmetered`.
