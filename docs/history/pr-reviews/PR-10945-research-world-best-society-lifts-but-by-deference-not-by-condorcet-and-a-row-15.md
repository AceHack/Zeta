## Description

Aaron 2026-08-14: *"lets see if we can do the same trick proving that the society is better than the best individual and see if we can also come up with some system in wich we can prove the world is better than the best society in the similar fashion."*

**Verdict: it lifts — but by DEFERENCE, not by Condorcet.** The lift has nothing to do with `n`, `c`, or `ρ`. It turns on one structural property of the aggregation rule: *can the rule imitate its own best part?*

## Row 15 is two theorems sharing one row

There are two files named `CondorcetBoundary.Tests.fs`, proving different theorems about different aggregation rules. Row 15's **evidence anchor** (11 properties, `tests/Tests.FSharp/`) proves the **union** rule, whose own header states *"ρ\* = 1 is the hard boundary"* — no `ρ*`, no `c*`. Row 15's **stated condition** (`ρ < ρ*`, `c > c*`) comes from the **majority-vote** module, which those 11 properties never touch; and its `c*` is an *argmax* there, promoted to a *threshold*.

That is why both an unconditional and a conditional reading of row 15 look supported. Under the union rule the unconditional reading is correct; under majority it is false.

## Is row 15 level-generic?

**The union half, yes** — `expectedSocietyIdentical` is a pure function of `(n,c,ρ,Σv)` with nothing individual-specific in it, so the lift is instantiation. But the one-parameter family is **not closed** under composition (the `ρ_flat` a flat society of `n·m` would need varies with `c`: 0.366 → 0.090 across `c ∈ [0.05,0.80]`). Hierarchy is not a relabelling of a flat population — a positive structural result.

**The majority half, in form only.** The single assumption baked in at *both* levels is **identical agents**, and that is where it breaks.

## Unweighted majority: REFUTED, with zero correlation needed

Exact enumeration, independent units, all `cᵢ > 0.5`:

```
0.99,0.55,0.55,0.55,0.55  ->  P(maj)=0.7548  vs best 0.99   FALSE
0.85,0.52,0.52            ->  P(maj)=0.6947  vs best 0.85   FALSE
0.70,0.70,0.70,0.70,0.70  ->  P(maj)=0.8369  vs best 0.70   true   <- identical: the row-15 regime
```

Random sweep: **unweighted majority loses to its best member in 58.8% of 20 000 draws.** Row 15 never meets this because it fixes one `c` for every agent, making "best" and "typical" the same word. Societies differ from each other far more than members differ within one — so the assumption fails hardest at exactly the level we were asked to lift to.

## ρ is not a sufficient statistic (new defect, beyond the relayed two)

By de Finetti, the exact criterion is `E_θ[maj_m(θ) − θ] > 0` — it depends on where the mixing law sits relative to `θ = ½`, which a scalar correlation cannot express. Counterexample at `m=9`, `ρ=0.2495`, **inside** the shipped "safe" `ρ*(9)=0.25`:

```
P(world majority correct) = 0.37369     (analytic 0.373734)
P(single society correct) = 0.50001
world - society           = -0.12632    the lift FAILS
```

— confirmed by 40M-trial direct exchangeable simulation, no analytic formula in the loop. Meanwhile in the *exact Gaussian* model correlation never reverses the result (45/45 up to `ρ=0.99`). **Two worlds, same `m`, same `ρ`, opposite verdicts.** The shipped bound is unsafe for `c ≲ 0.68` and over-conservative above it — it crosses the true reversal locus rather than bounding it.

In words the counterexample is not a pathology: *with prob 0.2 the shared framing is right and every society is near-certain; with prob 0.8 it is subtly wrong and each society is 37.5% likely to be right.* That is the **shared-wrong-paradigm** scenario — the most plausible failure mode for societies built on one substrate. Majority vote turns a 50/50 into a 37%.

## The discharge — Dominance Lift Theorem

> An aggregation rule `A` is **deferential** if the class `R` it is optimal over contains every projection `πᵢ`. Then `P(A) ≥ maxᵢ P(unit i)`.
> *Proof:* `π_{i*} ∈ R`, `A` optimal in `R`, so `P(A) ≥ P(π_{i*}) = maxᵢ P(unit i)`. ∎
> By induction over levels: `P(world) ≥ P(best society) ≥ P(best individual)` — no `n`, `c`, `ρ`, no identical-units assumption, arbitrary depth.

| rule | deferential? | lift |
|---|---|---|
| union | yes (`⋃Aᵢ ⊇ A_{i*}`) | ✅ tautologically |
| unweighted majority | **no** — no way to defer | ❌ 58.8% failure |
| log-odds-weighted majority | yes (`{πᵢ} ⊆ R`) | ✅ 0 failures / 20 000 |

**Anchor checked:** Nitzan & Paroush 1982 — optimal weights are `log(cᵢ/(1−cᵢ))` and depend *only on your own competence*, not on others' or on `m`. That is what makes the rule scale-free: no central tally is needed to compute your own weight (§1).

Honest scope: correlation re-enters as a *modelling* requirement (condition on the shared component) rather than a threshold — which is better news, since `ρ*(4)=0.111` may be unreachable in principle while modelling a correlation is an engineering task.
