## Description

docs(research): the 80/20 line — where prediction stops being reducible

Ferries Aaron's observation that the zeta scheduler, ferry throttler and chip8/9 predictor
are "all trying to prove this wrong" against Jenann Ismael's argument that reality viewed
from within is incomplete — maybe only in the 80% case, the other 20% needing "chaos
cartography and per step navigation because it's not reducible."

Source excerpts quarantined in docs/ip-questionable/ per that folder's own policy
("minimal, attributed, removable"). Excerpted rather than the full ~2h14m transcript;
the research note restates every claim it uses, so deleting the excerpt costs nothing.

What the write-up adds beyond the ferry:

1. "This" is three claims, not one. The relativistic-access claim does NOT bite on our
   schedulers -- wrong regime, and a past light cone is not a Cauchy surface. Only the
   self-referential claim is a real obstruction for us.

2. The 80/20 line already has a name here: the Applicative/Kleisli membrane from
   2026-08-16-avoiding-app-*. `ArrowApply === Monad` makes that an identity, not an
   analogy. Wolfram's computational irreducibility is the third route to one boundary.

3. "Prove it wrong" is the wrong frame. Ismael's result is EXISTENTIAL (there is a
   question you cannot answer), not universal. It forbids 100%, says nothing about 80%.
   So the systems chart the boundary rather than refute it -- which is why "cartography"
   is the right word and "conquest" would not be.

4. Aaron's Feynman reading, added mid-write: the indefinitely-many futures are an
   INTEGRAND, not a gap. Most of the sum cancels; the 80% is where it concentrates and
   the 20% is where it does not. Registered honestly -- the exact classical cousin is
   Laplace's method / large deviations, and that is what to cite in a classical setting.
   Also corrected his aside: Ismael brackets the quantum substructure explicitly, so she
   likely did not miss this so much as have no need of it.

5. A prediction of mine, checked and RETRACTED. I expected ferry-throttler to be the
   counter-predictive case and chip8/9 the cleanest. four-corner-feedback.ts is coupled
   maximally -- the feedback corner is prescriptive, carrying a -1 retraction, a new
   generator function and a suggested lane -- and it is STILL not Ismael's device,
   because coupling is not the discriminator. The SIGN is. Her construction is f(p) = !p,
   which has no fixed point; that absence is the entire paradox. A throttle loop
   converges, so a fixed point exists and the equilibrium is predictable. Same polarity
   discipline the module already records for the retracted Int construction.

Also notes that drain-scheduler and heat-aware-scheduler both live INSIDE ferry-throttler,
so two of the three systems Aaron named may be one subsystem -- flagged for him to confirm
rather than assumed.
