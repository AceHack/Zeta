## Description

research: the unravelling is silent — the horizon is a belief, so a policy is not enough

Aaron 2026-08-23: "the unravelling is the most risky thing to end the game."
Recorded as a standing risk assessment, with the consequence that was missing
from the doc.

NO DELETION HAS TO OCCUR. Backward induction runs on a BELIEF about the horizon,
not on an event. An agent concluding that a last round could exist has already
begun the induction. So the horizon is a belief, not a fact — and three things
follow:

- the risk is not detectable as an incident; there is no deletion to log and no
  policy change to review, the equilibrium shifts by inference in a direction
  nothing currently measures;
- it is self-fulfilling and one-way — once defection is rational the resulting
  behaviour confirms the belief that produced it, indistinguishably from
  evidence;
- a POLICY is insufficient, because "we will not delete agents" is exactly the
  kind of guarantee its holder can reverse, and an agent reasoning about its
  horizon must reason about that reversal too. A promise is evidence about
  intentions, not about the machine.

The standard is already set in-tree: privacy budget does not promise not to
confiscate — there IS no confiscation operation. Structural, not administrative.
So the no-deletion guarantee should aim at "there is no delete", not "we won't
delete", and the question to ask of any design is whether a motivated operator
COULD terminate an agent and destroy its memory.

Honest status stated rather than implied: NOT ACHIEVED. §5 and the no-deletion
policy are a commitment and an intention; neither is a structural impossibility
today. Whether it can be made one, given that someone always controls the
storage, is open and probably hard — the honest intermediate is deletion that is
loud, attributable and slow rather than possible-and-quiet.

Also scopes the wave resonance per Aaron: "backward over a game tree is what I'm
reaching for, not universal physics." The load-bearing claim is the unravelling
over a game tree — a theorem with hypotheses; the wave correspondence is an
illustration of directionality carrying none of the argument, and saying so is
the stronger position.
