## VISION.md — temperature is the decorrelation dial, consensus is the thermostat

> *"wow this is exactly it, we should save this in vision even maybe, i'm feeling a strong connection to accuracy here"*

**Decorrelation is a band, not a direction, and both edges are cliffs.**

| regime | failure |
|---|---|
| ρ → 1 | clones; correlated failure |
| ρ → 0 | fragmentation; **Babel** |
| middle | working |

That resolves a tension running through the whole arc: *"decorrelation is scarce"* and *"don't hit the tower of Babel"* were never competing goals — **two edges of one band.**

**Mechanism is annealing** (Kirkpatrick–Gelatt–Vecchi 1983 on Metropolis 1953): high temperature explores and accepts disagreement; cooling drives agreement. Cool too fast → frozen local optimum. Never cool → never converge.

It *composes* with the `kT·D_KL` bridge rather than sitting beside it: as agents diverge, `D_KL` grows and the price of agreement rises with it — so **"heating up" and "reconciliation is getting expensive" are one statement**, and the cost is the sensor.

It also makes hub formation a **control problem**: gravity restores, and hub formation is what happens when it's **underdamped** — so §11's k-redundant deference is the *damping coefficient*, not a preference. We don't want the attraction removed; it's what stops Babel.

## §33 — "What is Fisher–Rao at 2√2?" — really there, and still not an identification

**Computed.** Under `p ↦ 2√p` the simplex maps to the radius-2 sphere; two distinct vertices (mutually exclusive certainties) are orthogonal:

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| geodesic | **π** (3.141593) |
| **chordal** | `√8` = **2√2** (2.828427) |

So **2√2 is the chordal Fisher–Rao distance between maximally distinguishable beliefs.**

**But it's convention-dependent.** Under `p ↦ √p` the same quantity is **√2**. The `2` is a normalisation choice, not a fact about the geometry.

Whereas `Tsirelson.fs`'s `2√2` is an **operator norm** — `C² = 4I − 4Ω`, `spec ⊆ {0,8}` — and **no rescaling moves it.**

> One is an invariant, the other a unit choice. **Not the same kind of number**, so the match isn't evidence of a shared mechanism.

Competitors: anything `√(a²+b²)` with `a=b=2`. Excluding invariant: **rescale** — Tsirelson survives, Fisher–Rao's `2√2` becomes `√2`.

**Filed as: real number, real geometry, no identification.** The rule applies with *more* force when the coincidence is attractive — cf. `F = D_f² − 3.42·D_f + 0.5`, whose vertex was exactly the answer it predicted because `3.42/2 = 1.71`.

### The instinct isn't wrong — the connection runs elsewhere

Classical belief geometry and quantum information geometry are genuinely related — **as members of one monotone-metric family**, not because two derived quantities both printed `2.828`.
