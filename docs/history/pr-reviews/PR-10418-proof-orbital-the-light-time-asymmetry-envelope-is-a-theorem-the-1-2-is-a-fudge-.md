## Routing decision, stated before the work

The brief framed this as a real-analysis bound over a transcendental fixed point. That is
correct about Kepler and **wrong about the theorem** - and the difference is the whole routing
call. The claim is about **states**, not orbits; Kepler only decides *which* states occur.
Quantify over states, apply the exact identity `||R·u + t·v||² = R² + 2Rt(u·v) + t²||v||²`,
and what remains is a purely polynomial `∀`-sentence over the reals: the **decidable** theory
of real closed fields (Tarski 1951; Collins 1975), i.e. z3's `nlsat` fragment. No trig, no
`sqrt`, no fixed point, no ephemeris.

| Tool | Call | Cost of getting it wrong |
|---|---|---|
| **TLA+** | **Rejected categorically** | No reals. TLC would go green on a *discretisation*, not on the bound - human-weeks spent producing false-green CI on a property the tool cannot express. |
| **z3 (QF_NRA)** | **Routed** - the algebraic core. **0.03 s.** | Caveat stated in-file: `nlsat` emits no replayable certificate. That is exactly why BP-16 applies. |
| **Lean 4 + Mathlib** | **Routed** - the cross-check, and it earns its place | Not ceremonial: it discharges the 3D→scalar reduction the SMT encoding *assumes*. |
| **Interval arithmetic** | Deferred - a Taylor-remainder bound covers the residual more cheaply | CPU-days of branch-and-bound on the part that turned out algebraic. |
| **FsCheck** | **Rejected as evidence for a bound** | Sampling cannot certify a supremum. Adopting it reproduces the exact defect being fixed. |

## Verdict 1 - the envelope is a theorem, and it is sharp

Each one-way light time is pinned by the **receiver's** speed bound (not the transmitter's -
the asymmetry lives entirely in which endpoint moved). Both branches proved in both tools.

It is also **sharp**: equality is attained, with an exact rational witness produced
independently by z3 and re-checked in Lean - `τ_AB = 1/7`, `τ_BA = 1/12`, both sides `= 5`.
No floating point in either certificate.

Two z3 blocks **drop a hypothesis** and confirm the envelope then breaks, so the hypotheses
are demonstrably load-bearing: Cauchy-Schwarz, and `V` bounding the speed across the **whole**
light-time interval rather than at the transmit epoch only.

## Verdict 2 - the `1.2` is a fudge factor

Sharpness settles it: the bound has **zero slack in the worst case**, so no multiplicative
margin above 1 is justified by anything the model can produce, and none below 1 is safe.

It is also the wrong **shape**. The genuine residuals are *additive* and do not scale with
`δ_speed`; the dominant one (ephemeris error) is independent of `V_A + V_B` entirely. And a
multiplicative margin **vanishes exactly where the additive residuals do not** - the same
cancellation-hole argument #10387 correctly made against projections, applied one level up to
the margin itself. Replacement is `δ_max = δ_speed + δ_model` with four named, provenanced
terms (§13 noninterference applied to the budget).

## Four refutation attempts, all failed

Third independent implementation (Python, analytic Kepler state including **analytic
velocity** - no finite difference), 21k epochs at 1-hour step.

1. **Kepler curvature vs the un-multiplied envelope** - minimum slack is **2644
   curvature-allowances**. Never close.
2. **The V-sup-over-interval hole** - real, but **0.0011 ms**.
3. **`sup(exact/envelope)` = 0.857119779** against a theorem ceiling of 1. Divided by 1.2 this
   reproduces #10387's `0.71428` **independently** - a genuine confirmation of their number,
   and simultaneously proof that the `1.2` is not what makes the bound hold.
4. **Is the correct "sum" term itself a bound?** No - it under-states by 0.0059 ms somewhere.
   A projection is an estimator, never a bound. #10387's structural argument is confirmed *and*
   applies to their own leading term, which their document does not quite say.

The last row floors out on double precision, not physics. The honest statement is

## The finding neither prior document contains: the ephemeris is unnecessary

The envelope is monotone in `R`, `V_A`, `V_B`, so bounding each by a closed form gives a
**constant**:

```
δ_speed(constant) = 253.5731 ms    perihelion speeds + max range, nothing else
δ_curv            =   0.0276 ms
provable δ_max    = 253.6008 ms    Earth-Mars, all time, no ephemeris
```

That is **0.0095% of RTT** in extra cone width, against a maximum true asymmetry of 121.76 ms
anywhere in the scanned window - so a zero-input bound lands within ~2.1x of the truth. It
discharges the V-sup hypothesis *exactly*, and it **removes the finite-difference velocity**
and with it a byte-lock blocker: `helioVel` differences two ~2.3e8 km positions 1 s apart,
amplifying libm divergence by ~8 orders of magnitude, and `sin`/`cos`/`atan2` are **not**
bit-identical across the four oracles. `sqrt` is correctly rounded and is.

Every defect in `081KZY5W6AJ087G0R003EE7PY6` lives in machinery this constant does not use.

**This does not close the defect record.** D3 (solar occlusion) is untouched, and a consumer
needing a *tight* estimate rather than a bound still needs the ephemeris fixed and the JPL
Horizons vectors the proposal correctly names as blocking. The narrow claim: for
`BusRegime`'s cone-widening the constant strictly dominates, and shipping it removes a
false-conviction risk from `main` without waiting on Horizons.
