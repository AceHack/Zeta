## Description

Aaron 2026-08-24 named the shape library **"the ultimate tiebreaker"** for disambiguation, and observed that humans are fooled by well-studied perceptual effects while **"AI is good at avoiding these overlaps in perception, from my experience so far."**

That framing raises the stakes on a UX property. `anti-babel-preserve-reconcilability` lists shape as the carrier that **does not route through words at all** — what is consulted *after* vocabulary has stopped being reliable. So:

> **A tiebreaker that is itself ambiguous is worse than no tiebreaker, because it is consulted exactly when every other channel has already failed.** The error is not merely uncaught — it is structurally uncatchable.

## 2. The headline finding — `crossing` ~ `lightcone`

Both are **an X centred on the identical point (325,165)** in the identical frame.


(catalog: 171 pairs, **median sup 0.227**, p90 0.509)

Everything separating them: **6.3°** of arm slope, a hue assignment (which the 2026-08-19 criterion already says is *not* a distinction), and a **51px occlusion gap**.

**And the gap is the entire semantic payload.** `crossing` means *"who crossed OVER whom is remembered"* — encoded as a break drawn **as an occlusion cue**, which is an explicit invitation to **amodal completion**, the mechanism whose whole function is to continue a contour behind an occluder. *The mark asks the visual system to do the thing that destroys its content.* Complete the gap and `crossing` **is** `lightcone` — substituting a **causal** claim for a **braid-order** one.

Aaron's *"what our brains are trained to ignore or generate"* — his two verbs are exactly the two mechanisms (§4).

## 3. The asymmetry — split, not accepted or denied

He framed it honestly as an observation. As a hypothesis it needs **three readers, not two**:

| reader | confusable set | is it what consults the tiebreaker? |
|---|---|---|
| **exact** (hash) | **measure zero** — his claim is **TRUE** of this one | only in a build gate |
| **human glance** | **large** — §6 | yes |
| **learned** (VLM/CNN) | **different again** | **yes, and increasingly** |

Szegedy 2014 + Ilyas 2019 establish the learned reader's metric is **a different metric, not a degraded copy** of ours. So **human-confusable and machine-confusable are different sets, and a tiebreaker must clear the UNION** — the exact reader contributes almost nothing to it, which is why relying on it (as TIER 0 does) *looks* like coverage and is not.

**Unexpected corollary:** Geirhos 2019 (texture-vs-shape bias) would **invert** the rule shipped on 2026-08-19 — we spend capacity on base form and discard texture, which is right for a human and possibly backwards for the reader that would actually read a diagram. Marked `unmetered`; §10.7 says how to measure it in an afternoon.

## 4. The rule (§7)

Guard the **supremum over the declared quotient family**, never one coarseness — a check at `r=0` reports `crossing`/`lightcone` **clean**. The **slope** names which reader is at risk. Left-right reflection is a family member (human mirror-invariance). And: **meaning must never rest on a feature vision COMPLETES** — the mirror of the 2026-08-19 result that added ink survives blur, since *removed* ink is filled in.
