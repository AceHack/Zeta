## Description

`multiplexed-duplex-transport.ts` credited *"the maintainer's own MultiplexedWebSockets"* with no citation. The citation is **Stainback & Higgins, "Hub and Agent Communication Through a Firewall", US 2018/0109563 A1 → US 10,834,144 B2** (granted 2020-11-10, priority 2016-10-13, Itron). Anchored in `PRIOR-ART-LIST.md` and in the module header.

## What the patent establishes, with paragraph cites

- **Initiation direction ≠ capability direction.** The agent dials *out* (firewall-friendly, 443 with the handshake inside TLS so stateful inspection can't see it) and then **serves** commands. This is why four-corner has no client/server axis to swap — **both ends are both.** It also corrects my own earlier CPT mapping: parity here is a *symmetry of the design*, not an operation applied to it.
- **Command-ID multiplexing over one pipe (¶0058)** — verbatim the mechanism in `multiplexed-duplex-transport.ts`. Zeta's upgrade is **ZetaId over GUID**: a GUID is opaque; a ZetaId is self-describing, its `Category` nibble telling the far side "this is a channel."
- **The feedback loop, nine years early (¶0072, the "real-time pattern")** — *"blurs a distinction between a request and a response … allows for … a feedback loop."* That's the four-corner feedback wire, named in the patent before `four-corner.ts` typed it.
- **Capability at the edge, never the centre (¶0017, ¶0041)** — commands are *referenced* by name+parameters but *defined and executed* only at the agent, so a compromised hub cannot escalate. Same discipline `four-corner.ts` preserves by being pure interface.

## The boundary is load-bearing, not boilerplate

The patent is **hub-and-agent**: a *central* hub, multi-tenant, Paxos-synchronised routing tables, hub as addressing authority. **Zeta is the decentralized version and must stay that way** — no hub, no central registry, ZetaId-routed peer-to-peer, `localDuplexPair` as the DST-replayable case.

The entry states two consequences so neither drifts:

1. **Design constraint.** Any proposal reintroducing a hub, central registry, or leader-elected routing table is outside the architecture. Manifesto §1 already forbids it — named explicitly here because **the centralized design is the one the maintainer knows best and is therefore easiest to reach for.**
2. **Credibility.** The decentralized claim is credible *because* the same person built and patented the centralized one. That's earned standing, not a disclaimer.

Also credits **WebSocket (RFC 6455)** as the transport the patent extends, **SignalR** as the .NET multiplexing precedent named in the patent, and **Paxos (Lamport)** which the patent invokes for hub consensus — precisely the component the decentralized version does not need.
