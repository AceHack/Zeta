## Description

## The question, and the correction to it

`src/Bayesian/CliffordAntiSybil.fs` embeds a Gaussian belief into flat **Cl(3,0)** and calls two
agents Sybils when one belief stream is a rotor away from the other. The assignment: Fisher–Rao is
the canonical metric (Čencov 1982) and is not Euclidean — on a categorical simplex `p ↦ 2√p` gives
the round sphere — so should this be spherical? Which signature? Does the test survive? Is flat a
legitimate local approximation, and if so is the fix a one-line comment?

**The premise is right and points at the wrong module and the wrong term.**

## Wrong module — it is a hyperboloid, not a sphere

The simplex-becomes-a-sphere result is about the **categorical** family, which is what
`BeliefConvergence` folds and what the 2026-08-18 falsifier analysed. `CliffordAntiSybil` embeds
`Gaussian` — the **location–scale** family — whose Fisher–Rao geometry is **hyperbolic**,
`K = −1/2`. Same theorem, opposite sign of curvature. Computed three independent ways: the
orthogonal-metric curvature formula on the KL-Hessian-verified Fisher metric, the closed-form Rao
distance (Atkinson & Mitchell 1981) checked against direct arc-length integration, and the
hyperboloid inner product with `⟨X,X⟩ = −1` to 1e-13.

## Wrong term — curvature is not what is broken

The error splits in two, and only one of them is the curvature:

| term | order | behaviour as steps shrink | size |
|---|---|---|---|
| **curvature** (holonomy, Gauss–Bonnet) | second | **vanishes**, linearly in `D_KL` | 1% of the score at `D_KL ≈ 0.038` nats/step (a 0.28σ mean shift) |
| **chart** (`δ_ij` used where the Fisher matrix belongs) | zeroth | **converges to 116.57°** | 20°–160° across ordinary beliefs; metric condition number 508 at `ν=20, τ=10` |

The defence "Fisher–Rao is Euclidean to first order" slides from *"the metric is a quadratic form"*
to *"the metric is the identity quadratic form"*. The flat chart's angles equal the Fisher–Rao
angles at **exactly one belief in the whole manifold, N(0,2)** — a measure-zero set, verified as a
numerical zero with every neighbour non-conformal.

## The measured price — the metering test, failed

Dimensional analysis first: `ν = μτ` carries `[x]⁻¹`, `τ = 1/σ²` carries `[x]⁻²`, so `Cl3.norm`
adds `[x]⁻²` to `[x]⁻⁴`. That predicts unit-dependence. Measured, on one unchanged pair of streams:

| k (`x ↦ kx`, a pure unit change) | shipped flat score | Fisher–Rao score |
|---|---|---|
| 0.01 | **0.999752** | 0.157017 |
| 1 | 0.412369 | 0.157017 |
| 100 | **0.000006** | 0.157017 |

Same agents, same beliefs, opposite verdict. Fair caveat: an **exact** clone still scores 1.0 under
any unit, so the extremes are sound — what is unit-dependent is the graded middle, i.e. the
decision boundary. And:

| relation | flat | Fisher–Rao |
|---|---|---|
| **real** mask `x ↦ 3x+5` | 0.699809 — missed at a 0.9 threshold | **1.000000** (a theorem: it is an isometry) |
| **impossible** mask (90° chart rotation, drives `τ < 0`) | **1.000000** — certain clone | off-manifold |

Clone-vs-independent AUC 0.959 → **0.746** when agents work in different units, FPR 18.7% at a 0.9
threshold.

## The answers

1. **Signature: Cl(2,1)**, not Cl(4,1) and not Cl(3,0). Cl(4,1) is CGA of ℝ³ — wrong dimension for
   a 2-manifold. Identified **by invariants, not a count** (both Cl(3,0) and Cl(2,1) are
   8-dimensional with 3-dimensional rotor groups): **non-compactness** (Rao distance is unbounded),
   **zero divisors** in the even subalgebra (`(1+e₁₃)(1−e₁₃) = 0`, so `M₂(ℝ)` not ℍ), and the
   existence of **boosts** (`e₁₃² = +1`). A Sybil's rescaling mask is a boost with rapidity
   `−log a`, verified exact to 1e-16.
2. **The test survives verbatim and gets better.** At a point the tangent algebra is Cl(2,0)
   regardless of curvature, so the rotor machinery is untouched — curvature enters only through
   transport. The masking group falls **4 → 3 → 2** parameters (flat similarity → `Isom⁺(H²)` →
   the `ax+b` relabelings an adversary can actually perform), and each rung removes coincidental
   matches.
3. **Flat is not a legitimate local approximation** — see the table above.
4. **So a comment is not the fix.** A comment saying "flat is the first-order approximation" would
   be false. Landed instead: an honest label on the module (its falsifier now exists **and fails**,
   per `toy-is-free-metered-must-be-earned`), the research doc, and work-item
   `081M0FT2JZV087G0R003HXFCEW` for the real change.
