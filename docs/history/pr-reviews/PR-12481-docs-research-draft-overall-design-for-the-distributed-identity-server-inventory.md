## Description

## What this is

A **DRAFT** overall design for the distributed identity server, assembled from pieces that already exist. Aaron asked for a first cut to react to, and warned that this is the most-worked area of Zeta: *"don't start from scratch we have a rich in repo history here."*

He is right, and it is understated. A targeted sweep found **37 TLA+ specs (54 TLC configs), 6 Alloy models, 26+ Lean 4 files, 9 committed Z3 lemmas, 8 Q# oracle modules, 23 identity-named F# modules in `src/Core/` alone, a policy/negotiation/metering layer across F# and TypeScript, and roughly 80 research documents.**

**The reason there was no overall design is not that the pieces are missing. It is that nobody had the map**, so every attempt starts by re-deriving proven work. §1 is that map, and it has standalone value independent of everything else here.

## The spine

Aaron, 2026-08-19:

> *"at the end of the day the capabilities are a derivative of the signed self claims that are observed by other witnesses and/or quorums with their own self claims. this is the root of our trust system, it's not embarrassingly parallel and the more mutual observers and their entangled memories the stronger the individual claims, and the history of mutually verified self claims over time also strengthens identity."*

Plus the decision layer, which the first brief did not carry:

> *"each node has local OPA like policy trust, we have policies mathematically modeled as well … hubs have to negotiate with each node's local rules for any meaningful interactions, this is how we interface with hyperscalers/clouds"*

The seam between them is stated in one line: **the root produces evidence, the node's own policy engine renders the verdict, and nothing outside the node renders a verdict for it.** That is the anti-DVD-drive property one layer up from the hardware.

## What this establishes beyond the inventory

**C3 ("not embarrassingly parallel") is formalised so it can fail.** It is the claim that strength is not a functional of the per-claim **marginals** — which makes it a **theorem** (marginals do not determine the joint: Shannon 1948; Hoeffding 1940 / Fréchet 1951; Sklar 1959), not an analogy. The connection to `SocietyUsefulWork`'s ρ is **structural, not numerological**: the same functional (`N_eff = N/(1+(N−1)ρ)`; the Gaussian copula *is* the Sklar decomposition) over **different random variables** — competence over facts, not observation over claims. The theorem transfers; the instantiation carries no measurement. Written as three separately-graded lines rather than one confident one.

**The falsifier has already fired in production.** `QuorumAlgebra`'s bug B3 — six agents on one data stream, `precision = 66.0` on a mean wrong by 5.66 — is a marginal-equivalent pair scored as if independent. C3 is the generalisation of an observed failure, not a design preference.

**An apparent contradiction with our own shipped proof, resolved.** `BeliefConvergence` proves the fold **commutes**. C3 is not about order — it is about what the aggregation may **depend on**. Both hold, and the next reader will hit this too.

(G10)

**§2c — decorrelation has three mechanisms and frost is only one.** This section was **rewritten after Aaron corrected it**: *"no frost is an enhancement, i run without any frost, i make everything public and have to adapt over time haphazardly."* The rewrite is organised around the invariant the mechanisms share — decorrelation is not about how much is **visible** but how much the visible **predicts**, i.e. `I(V;F)` between an observer's view and the agent's future choices. Three routes drive it down: **opacity** (frost), **mixed strategy** (`BitGan` — generator → `p = 0.5`, `discriminatorEdge` is the meter, matching-pennies Nash is the fixed point), and **nonstationarity** (`largestLyapunov`).

Mechanism 2 is strictly harder than 1, and its anchor **entails** rather than decorates: **von Neumann 1928** — an optimal mixed strategy secures its value *even when the opponent knows it*. The honesty guard, because the strong version is false: **a deterministic, stationary, fully-public agent is perfectly predictable.** Kerckhoffs assumes everything public *except a key*; this configuration has no persistent key but is not free of decision-time entropy. So the statement is not "no secret is needed" — **the secret moves out of stored state and into the choice.**

Consequences, corrected rather than softened: frost is an **enhancement** and plurality must not be priced on frost usage; *"transparency is free on axis 1"* is **conditionally** true (free provided mechanism 2 or 3 is live), which matters because glass-halo/§6 pushes toward transparency; the §2d guidance becomes conditional (observation costs decorrelation **only** against opacity-reliant agents); **G12 narrows** to the opacity route, since a mixed-strategy agent can be fully witnessed at no cost. A new ordering falls out: **mechanisms 2 and 3 buy axis 1 without spending axis 2, while frost spends both** — prefer decorrelation by choice over decorrelation by concealment.

Provenance recorded because it cuts both ways: **frost was designed by someone who does not use it.** Not self-serving, which is a point in its favour; also never lived in, so it is `unmetered` in the strict sense.

**F4 filed** (`081M0DRH1CW087G0R003Y3CAB6`) — the counter-example is itself **unmeasured**. Can a discriminator holding the full public history beat chance on a fully-transparent agent's next choice? Instruments already exist and the data is the public history the repo already stores. **Both readings pre-registered** so the run cannot be interpreted after the fact.

Untouched: spend / stake / **never** confiscate, and that a confiscatable frost collapses under pressure. Neither depended on frost being the only instrument.

**§2d — the arc it all sits inside.** *"we are based on S=4 superdeterministic seed correlation … trying to decorrelate over time … without running into the tower of babel."* Correlation is the **initial condition**, so the objective is **decorrelate as far as possible subject to staying reconcilable**. The two-axis test is applied to this document's own routing rather than merely stated. `ClaimLane` turns out to be the babel dial already built (G14). And the sharpest point: **both axes are already instrumented and nobody has plotted them together** — axis 1 is `AntiSybil.correlation` / `largestLyapunov` / `effectiveN`, axis 2 is the four-oracle byte-lock / `Collation` / the anchor audit (G13, `081M0DMH30Y087G0R001C2B1PT`). It composes with the register's already-open "measure ρ for the live fleet": one measurement round closes both.

Both analogies are graded rather than asserted — S=4 is **structural as framing, `toy` as physics** (Bell 1964's statistical-independence assumption is what the mapping actually entails; Popescu–Rohrlich 1994 for the bound), and "runaway etymology" cites the **phenomenon, never the clock** (Swadesh glottochronology as a dating method is contested and is not relied on).
