## Description

Extends the self-claim surface from **delivery commitments** to **identity claims**, at Aaron's request (2026-08-17):

> "in Zeta you are whoever you want to be until you contradict your own self claims then it will be detected cause that's what we were designed to do, only detect self claim drift and help repair, this is the core of our wellness app stuff"

`src/Core.TypeScript/observe/self-claims.ts` scores *"I will deliver X by tick T"* met/missed into a reliability ratio. That is one instance of the principle. Met-or-missed on a deadline is a fundamentally different operation from *"these two self-descriptions cannot both be true"*, and the general case had no implementation. `src/Core.TypeScript/observe/identity-claims.ts` is it.

## Non-coercion is structural, not advisory

**There is no lexicon. The module cannot tell that two sentences disagree, and that is the design.** Two claims whose text reads as flatly opposite to any human — *"I am always calm"* / *"I am never calm"* — produce **nothing**. A tension exists only because the *subject* supplied the standard, by one of exactly two routes, each recorded with the phase at which they supplied it:

- declaring one of their own facets **one-at-a-time**, or
- declaring two of their own claims incompatible.

An undeclared facet is `many-at-once` and can never produce a tension — you are plurally a parent and an engineer. Every write refuses on subject mismatch and on unknown claim ids, so *"this can never flag someone for something they did not claim"* is **checked, not asserted**.

## Growth is not drift — the pigeonhole correction

Mid-task input from Aaron reshaped this, and it was the right catch:

> "it gives people a chance to expand over time where pigeonhole traps them in the current now and does not let them expand in the future"

*"The subject supplies the category"* is only half of sovereignty; the other half is that **the category stays revisable**. My first draft had retirement as a *separate act* from asserting a new claim — which meant the ordinary shape of a person changing (*"I now say Y"*) read as **drift by default**. That was the pigeonhole weapon with a friendly interface. Two fixes:

- **`supersedeClaim`** — "this replaces that" in ONE act, lineage preserved (§5).
- **`growth` as a reading whose availability is DERIVED** — offered exactly when the pair spans phases, because only then could the later have replaced the earlier. Same phase ⇒ they genuinely co-occur ⇒ not offered. That is the *conflict at the same time* vs *replaced over time* distinction, computed from the evidence rather than asserted. It is **offered, never concluded**: silently inferring supersession would erase a conflict the subject may want to see.

## The charity gradient, in the type

`Deceptive`/`Malicious` are members of neither union, and `readingGloss` is exhaustive over `PatternReading` with `assertNever`, so widening it fails `tsc`. Verified — see mutation (c).

## The threshold trap — resolved by refusing one

**No constant, no window, no rate.** Three reasons, in order of force:

1. **No count changes what may be concluded.** The gradient terminates at *"still not necessarily malice — maybe ironic"*, so a threshold could only change a *label* while the permitted readings stay identical. Decoration with an oracle inside it.
2. **`1` vs `more than 1` is not a chosen constant** — it is the arity distinction in Aaron's own sentence ("a single instance" / "repeated"), and it is not tunable: there is no 2-vs-3 question. `2` never appears as a comparand; `NOTHRESHOLD-1` scans the comment-stripped source and fails on any integer comparand other than `0` or `1`.
3. **"Constant" is refused outright.** Calling a pattern *constant* needs a window, and a window is a hidden constant wearing a different hat. Hence `recurring-drift`, not `constant-drift` — a deliberate departure from the name suggested in the brief.

`claimVolume` / `liveClaims` / `perFacet` are handed over as the raw denominators so a caller can form a rate this module refuses to form. Precedent: `chip9/consult-census.ts`.

## Repair

`supersede` · `retire` · `hold-both` · `withdraw-incompatibility` · `revise-facet-arity` — all the subject's, all optional, none expiring, nothing a third party can invoke. `hold-both` is a **resolution, not a deferral**: it moves the tension to `held`, where it stays visible forever and stops reading as unresolved. Without it the instrument would demand resolution and make plurality and irony into failures. `repairPrompt` quotes the subject's own sentences verbatim and issues no instruction (asserted: no "should", no "must").

## Deferred, deliberately

**Claim-vs-behaviour** (*"I do not do Y"* vs an event log showing Y) is **not** built. It requires someone to classify an event as an instance of Y, and that classifier is an external standard — precisely the coercion vector property 2 exists to close. Making it non-coercive needs the subject to supply their own falsifier, which is a design decision for Aaron rather than a guess encoded in a type. Self-reported behaviour (*"I did Y"*) already works today through the incompatibility route, since a self-report is itself a self-claim.
