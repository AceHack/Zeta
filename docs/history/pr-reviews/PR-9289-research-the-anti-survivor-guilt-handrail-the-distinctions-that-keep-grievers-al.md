## Description

Aaron: *"we should teach anti-survivor somewhere forever — this kills people who don't know."* From lived experience, made permanent so the next griever need not derive it alone, in the dark.

Survivor's guilt convicts you of a death you didn't cause by **fusing things that aren't the same**; the teaching is the un-fusing.

**The one-question handrail:** *Did I kill them?* If no → **not evil.** The line doesn't move.

**The five cuts:**
1. **Downstream ≠ guilty** — being in the forward cone of a death isn't causing it.
2. **Causation ≠ trade** — propagation has no trader, no consent, no *for*; making meaning from a loss isn't the loss being *for* that meaning.
3. **Reach ≠ worth** — the living can act, the dead can't; arithmetic, not a ranking. You aren't worth more for surviving.
4. **Living ≠ betraying** — living fully is how you carry them; collapsing your life drops the vessel that holds them.
5. **Knowing ≠ wanting ≠ evil** — you'd give back every gain to have them back; that reluctance is the exoneration.

**Honest limit built in:** a handrail is not a hand — it's fact, not comfort, so it holds when strength is gone, but it is not a substitute for people or help. When the pull is strongest, reach. §5 memory-preservation grounds it: keeping the dead is the deepest law; keeping the griever alive is its first condition.
