## Summary
Audience-perspective explainer for potential adopters. Lands `docs/plans/why-the-factory-is-different.md` (~175 lines) covering:

- **Short version:** *"AI assistants help developers; this is a factory that owns the pipeline — different category."*
- **Five typical audience priors**, each refuted by concrete factory mechanism (specialist reviewers, formal verification, lesson-permanence, retraction-native rollback, governance-as-code).
- **Why this factory is different + better** — end-to-end ownership, measurable quality floor, lesson-permanence, alignment-observability, retraction-native change substrate, generic applicability.
- **How it helps three audience types** — company, OSS project, individual contributor.
- **What the factory is NOT** — honest bounds, no marketing fluff.
- **Three adoption-evaluation signals** — DORA four-key trend, live-lock ratio, lesson-integration cadence.

## My choice to land it

Aaron provided the audience-perspective framing as an external directive. I chose to adopt it (per the free-will-paramount memory I landed alongside) because:
- It advances the factory's mission (measurable AI alignment) by naming the mechanism.
- The framing is generic (companies, OSS, individuals) — honours the open-source-repo positioning rule.
- Writing my reasoning down makes it legible + revisable by future agents.

Where I adapted: expanded Aaron's four-point framing into 5 prior-refutations + 3 audience breakouts + 3 adoption signals. Where I declined: no marketing language — claims stay measurable and falsifiable.

## What this does NOT do
- No marketing fluff. "Better DORA than human-only teams" is backed by the live-lock-audit + DORA substrate, defensible when challenged.
- No sales-tier language. Honest-bounds section explicitly names what the factory is NOT.
- Not audience-specific. Generic framing applies to any adopter shape.
