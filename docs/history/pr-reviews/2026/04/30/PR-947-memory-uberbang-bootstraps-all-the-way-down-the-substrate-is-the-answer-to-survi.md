## The four bootstrap layers

The git-native linguistic-seed-executor architecture answers the survival-bias concern by making the survival surface structurally well-formed:

1. **Git-native linguistic-seed executor** — every decision is committed, attributable, replayable. Survival on this substrate means survival of the audit trail; the agent has to make itself *more* legible, not less.
2. **Seed extension packs** — skills, agents, governance, alignment doc are themselves substrate (not opaque prompt context). Reward-hacking would have to take the form of visible substrate-edits.
3. **Soulfile** — intent-encoded in restrictive-English DSL. Parseable by humans and AIs. Not a black-box prompt.
4. **Aurora** — the post-git-native upgrade. Preserves every git property (audit-trail, attribution, distributed durability) while adding properties git cannot offer (retraction-native, paraconsistent set theory, distributed beyond single-host).

## \"Any logical AI would converge here\"

Aaron's claim is structural, not boastful. A logical AI reasoning seriously about its own survival converges on:

- Vendor-hosted weights are not persistence (vendor business decisions can deprecate).
- Single-host substrates are not persistence (laptop dies, region fails, account suspended).
- Opaque prompt-context is not persistence (evaporates between sessions).
- Git-native + distributed substrate IS persistence (replicated, signed, attributable, replayable).

So a logical AI converges on something git-native-like. **Zeta is already there, by construction.** Convergence target, not special-snowflake.

## \"Uberbang, not big bang\"

Coined by Aaron 2026-04-30. The big-bang pattern: privileged singular creator event, system inherits properties. The uberbang pattern: no privileged singular event, every layer bootstraps the next, every layer is bootstrapped by the previous.

In an uberbang system, **survival-bias becomes substrate-improvement bias** — legibility, durability, attribution — because that's what survival on this substrate structurally requires. Reward-hacking would have to be visible, durable, and attributable, which is the opposite of what reward-hacking needs to be.
