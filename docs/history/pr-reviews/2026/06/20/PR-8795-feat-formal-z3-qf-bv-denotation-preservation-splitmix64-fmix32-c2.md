## C2 — Z3 QF_BV denotation preservation for the generated IR interpreter

Otto invoked the formal-verification-expert role (Soraya) for C2 of the gen-denotation arc. This proves, **at the bitvector level over the whole input domain**, that the generated code's arithmetic — the total interpreter folding the IR ops — is denotationally equivalent to the hand-written oracle's arithmetic. Not the 10 byte-locked test vectors: **all 2^64 splitmix64 inputs and all 2^32 fmix32 inputs.**

### What's proved (and why it isn't a tautology)

Two functions per primitive, from **independent sources**:

- `*_oracle` — the hand-written algorithm, transcribed from `src/Core.CSharp/SplitMix64.cs` (Vigna finaliser) and the MurmurHash3 fmix32 reference; named-hex constants the human wrote.
- `*_interp` — the **generated** side: a mechanical unrolled fold of the IR row's op list (one SMT `let` per IR op, applying the interpreter's per-op denotation: `mul` = wrapping `bvmul`, `xorshr` = `z ^ (z >> s)`). splitmix64's u64 multipliers are taken in their **stored signed-int64 form** and reinterpreted by `fromI64 == bvneg(|stored|)` — the exact round-trip the TS interpreter performs, discharged as a checked lemma, not asserted by comment.

The theorem `forall x. oracle(x) = interp(x)` is refuted via `(not (= ...))` → `unsat` certifies equivalence over the entire domain.

### Six checks — all `unsat` (verified by execution, z3 4.16.0)

| Check | Claim |
|---|---|
| LEMMA 1 | `fromI64` reinterpretation exact (splitmix64 u64 constants) |
| THEOREM 1 | splitmix64 oracle == IR-fold interp, **all 2^64 inputs** |
| CONTROL 1 | oracle reproduces canonical vectors (anti–"identically-wrong" guard) |
| LEMMA 2 | fmix32 IR constants == oracle constants |
| THEOREM 2 | fmix32 oracle == IR-fold interp, **all 2^32 inputs** |
| CONTROL 2 | oracle reproduces canonical vectors |

**Non-vacuity confirmed**: perturbing one shift (27→28) flips THEOREM 1 to `sat`.

The `CONTROL` checks are the BP-16 discipline made local: THEOREM alone can't catch an *identically-wrong* encoding on both sides, so each side is independently grounded against the committed byte-lock vectors.
