## CORRECTION 2 — Mac Lane coherence does NOT let you strictify this away

The brief flagged this as the trap and asked for a verdict. Verdict: **coherence does not
trivialise it**, for two independent reasons, and neither is the one usually given.

1. **Coherence presupposes α.** Mac Lane 1963 / CWM VII.1–2 is a theorem *about* monoidal
   categories; it applies only once (α, λ, ρ) satisfying pentagon + triangle have been
   exhibited. It cannot be used to skip exhibiting them. What it buys afterwards is that no
   *further* coherence obligation arises.
2. **Braided coherence is strictly weaker than monoidal coherence.** Joyal–Street 1993 prove
   that in the free braided monoidal category two formal maps are equal **iff their
   underlying braids agree** — not "every diagram commutes". In a braided, non-symmetric
   setting "every diagram commutes" is *false*, and `braidR_not_symmetric_perm3` is precisely
   a diagram that does not commute. The slogan people quote is the **symmetric/monoidal**
   coherence statement and it does not transfer.

What *is* available is strictification, and `⟨V⟩` already lives in the strict model:
`Hom.toArrow : Meno.Arrow<V list, V list>` models `V^⊗n` as a list, and list append is
strictly associative with a strict unit. So the hexagons are provable there at **all** block
sizes, with α = `id`. Both models are carried in the file.

## What is proved

**Non-strict (tuple) model** — α, α⁻¹, λ, ρ; `pentagon`; `triangle`; `hexagon1_tuple`;
`hexagon2_tuple`. Hexagon 1 is definitional; **hexagon 2 reduces exactly to
`(x·y) ▷ z = x ▷ (y ▷ z)`**, i.e. to conjugation being a genuine action.

**Strict (list) model** — `strict_assoc` / `strict_leftUnit` / `strict_rightUnit`;
`hexagon1_strict` and `hexagon2_strict` at **all** block sizes `(m, n)`, via the closed form
`c(xs, ys) = (prod xs ▷ ys) ++ xs`.

**The closed form is pinned to the shipped braid word** — `cBlock_singleton_eq_interp`,
`cBlock_one_two_eq_interp`, `cBlock_two_one_eq_interp` check `c_{1,1} = σ₁`,
`c_{1,2} = σ₁σ₂`, `c_{2,1} = σ₂σ₁` against `interp`, which folds crossings letter by letter
and knows nothing about `List.prod`. Without these the hexagons would be theorems about a
definition I invented.

## Copy / discard — the STRONG form, and exactly how strong

Requested: a guard making copy/discard *unrepresentable*, not merely absent. What is proved:

- `interp_length` — every `⟨V⟩` hom **preserves tensor degree**;
- `interp_invWord` / `interp_injective` — every `⟨V⟩` hom is **invertible** (`⟨V⟩` is a groupoid);
- `no_hom_between_distinct_degrees` — hence **`Hom(V^⊗m, V^⊗n) = ∅` for `m ≠ n`**. Copy
  `V → V⊗V` and discard `V → I` are not "absent by convention": **the hom-sets they would
  inhabit contain nothing**;
- `no_copy`, `no_discard`, `no_copy_lengthPreserving` — the three exclusions, by **three
  different arguments** (degree up, degree down, injectivity). The length-preserving copy
  `Δ∘ε` is the control showing degree alone is not the invariant.

**Strength, stated plainly.** This is `∀ w : List ℤ` — every braid word, not a sample. The
merged F# rung-1 guard (PR #10617) is syntactic (private union, no function can be lifted in)
with a falsifier over the 85 words of length ≤ 3; this is the semantic half, universally
quantified. **The residual weakness, named:** the correspondence between Lean `interp` and F#
`crossingOnList` is a hand transcription checked by reading, not by a machine. That is
`unmetered` and is labelled as such in the file header. I am not claiming the cross-language
link; I am claiming each side separately.

## Negative controls — the hexagons DISCRIMINATE, and they are INDEPENDENT

- `oppR_yangBaxter` + `hexagon1_opp` + `hexagon2_opp_fails`: the **opposite conjugation rack**
  `x ▷' y = x⁻¹·y·x` satisfies Yang–Baxter (it is a rack — Joyce 1982), satisfies hexagon 1,
  and **fails hexagon 2** (concrete S₃ witness, `decide`). So **hexagon 2 carries information
  Yang–Baxter does not**, and hexagon 1 alone is not the check.
- `hexagon1_swap` + `hexagon2_swap`: the plain swap satisfies **both** hexagons — as the
  symmetry must. So the hexagons are **false-green for a symmetric category**; that is
  `braidR_not_symmetric_perm3`'s job, same shape as the P4/P5c trap in
  `081KYWEM90908QG0R002NHEMZE`.

Read together: **Yang–Baxter, hexagon 1, hexagon 2, and σ² ≠ id are four independent
obligations**, and none implies another.

**Out-of-band mutation check** (scratch copy, not shipped): replacing `xs.prod` with
`xs.reverse.prod` in `cBlock` turns `hexagon1_strict`, `hexagon2_strict` and
`cBlock_two_one_eq_interp` **RED**. The `(1,1)` and `(1,2)` cross-checks survive — correct,
since reversing a one-element list is a no-op, which is why `(2,1)` is the discriminating one.

## Scope NOT taken, and why

No Mathlib `MonoidalCategory` instance. `⟨V⟩`'s objects are `ℕ` (tensor degree) and its homs
are braid words; a `Bundled`/`CategoryTheory` instance would be a large amount of plumbing
around exactly the same equations, and it would diverge from `MenoBraidedRMatrix.lean`'s
deliberate set-theoretic "abstract certificate" style. Matching the house style was the
choice. The limit is named in both file headers.

Also not taken: the **F# FsCheck hexagon suite**. The Lean file is a certificate, not a
property test. Left open and said so in `MenoBraided.fs`.
