## Description

> *"i'm trying to unlock the speed with making sure AI are decorrelated and can move forward without humans cause they can **prove** their decorrelation"*

That sentence makes three programmes into one, which is why it belongs in VISION rather than a research note.

## Why a human is in the loop at all

**Not because humans are safer by nature** — that's the hierarchy claim this project doesn't make, and it was corrected out of this file in #12832.

The gate is there because **nobody can currently verify that N agents are genuinely N.** A fleet phased to one seed might be N independent minds or **one mind wearing N hats**, and the difference is invisible from outside. The human is a *proxy for a check nobody can run.*

## Which makes the cost legible

> *"biometric ceremony is the slowing to human time"*

It isn't primarily an **authority** mechanism, it's a **rate** mechanism — a deliberate mass in the path dragging phase time down to human tempo. Which is exactly how gravity is modelled here: *phase-time slowing under heavy consensus.* **The gate is a gravity well.** Real work, real price.

So **speed is bought by making the check machine-runnable, not by removing it.** An agent that can *prove* decorrelation from its peers has mechanically supplied the evidence the human was standing in for — the discharge condition for the second admitted hierarchy, in operational rather than legal terms.

## And this is why falsifiers are the currency

| | the question it answers |
|---|---|
| **falsifier** | could this claim have come out **false**? |
| **decorrelation proof** | could these agents have **disagreed**? |

Both demand **the counterfactual**; both are worthless without it. A test that cannot go red proves nothing about the code; agents that could not have disagreed prove nothing by agreeing.

> *"A claim that cannot fail is worse than an absent feature"* and *"ρ → 1 is clones"* are **one principle in two costumes**: agreement is only evidence when disagreement was available.

## The measurement is checked, as of today

`n_eff = n / (1 + (n−1)ρ)` is not a heuristic. For the equicorrelated Gaussian, `I(μ) = 1ᵀΣ⁻¹1 = n_eff/σ²` — so **Kish's design effect is a Fisher-information ratio**, verified against our own shipped `effectiveTrialCount()` by linear solve to a worst relative error of **`1.26e-15`** (#12809, check C1).

So *"how many independent agents is this fleet worth"* has an answer with units.

## Honest limits — this is the claim most worth over-reading

**ρ is a statistic, not a proof.** It's estimated from binary indicators by one-way ANOVA, so the identity says *our formula is the Gaussian Fisher information*, **never** *our agents are Gaussian*. A statistic can be gamed by an adversary who knows it, and nothing yet stops an agent manufacturing **surface disagreement while sharing the reasoning underneath**.

> **Provable decorrelation is the goal, not the status.** What exists is the meter; what's missing is the proof.

Saying so *is* the discipline — a metric presented as a proof would be precisely the vacuous claim this section names as the obstacle.
