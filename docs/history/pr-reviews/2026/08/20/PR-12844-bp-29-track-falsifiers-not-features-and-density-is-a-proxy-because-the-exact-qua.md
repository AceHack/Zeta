## The rule (BP-29)

> **Track falsifiers, not features.** A claim that cannot fail is worse than an absent feature — it *looks* like a guarantee and carries none, so it costs an audit to tell apart. That is exactly the cost trust exists to remove.

**Baseline:** 899 / 1107 TypeScript test files are negative-bearing — **81.2%**, so roughly one in five carries no refusal-shaped check. Work-list emitted largest-gap-first.

## New in this revision — the proxy is a *theorem*, not a concession

> *"expect(true).not.toBe(false) — oh wow we captured godel here"*

The instinct is right, and the precise anchor is **stronger than Gödel**:

| layer | what it actually is |
|---|---|
| the plain fact | **Shannon** — a tautology carries `−log(1) = 0` bits, because it excludes nothing |
| the Gödel-shaped part | nearer **Tarski's undefinability of truth**: a suite cannot express *"this test is meaningful"* **within itself** — which is why density defers to mutation, and why the mutation runner can't validate its own mutants either |
| **the rigorous form** | **the equivalent-mutant problem is undecidable** (Budd & Angluin 1982). *"Is this test a falsifier?"* reduces to *"does a mutant survive that shouldn't?"* — so **vacuity detection is undecidable in general** |

> So the honest limit isn't *"density is a proxy because we settled."* **The exact quantity is undecidable.** Every practical instrument is an approximation, and the only question is which one at what cost — mutation testing included, since it's the best *decidable* approximation and its incompleteness has a name.

**And this is why the ADR refuses a gate** — now following from the theorem rather than from caution: **a threshold on an undecidable quantity is a threshold on a proxy, and proxies under pressure get optimised directly.**

*Register: the theorem is **cited, not checked** — no formalisation was run. The tautology claim is arithmetic.*
