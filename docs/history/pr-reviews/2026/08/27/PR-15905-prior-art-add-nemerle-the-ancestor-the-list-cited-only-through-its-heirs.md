## Description

`docs/PRIOR-ART-LIST.md` carried F# type providers and noted *"Roslyn source generators are the C#
simulation of the same"* — with **no entry for the thing both descend from**. Nemerle's Lisp-style
macro system did compile-time syntax-to-syntax metaprogramming on .NET roughly a decade earlier.
Citing the successor without the ancestor is the anchoring rule's own failure mode.

## Why it earns a row rather than a footnote

Because a macro takes and returns syntax, **a macro can take a macro**. That is abstraction over
rule-*constructors* — `(* → *) → (* → *)` — not over sites. Which is exactly the distinction between:

| | mechanism | decidable? |
|---|---|---|
| collapse N near-duplicate rules into one template | first-order, Datalog-shaped | **yes** |
| a combinator turning one checker into another | higher-kinded, macro-shaped | **no** |

The entry states that cost, because giving up the termination guarantee *is* the tradeoff.

## Lineage corrected, and the correction is the point

Nemerle is **Polish** in origin — University of Wrocław: Michał Moskal, Kamil Skalski, Paweł Olszta,
under Leszek Pacholski — **not Russian**. An AI search summary reproduced the common error on
2026-08-27 and Aaron routed it here. The Russian association is real but downstream: Vlad Chistyakov
became a principal contributor, and the team JetBrains hired in June 2012 was Russia-based, going on
to build Nitra *using* Nemerle.

Under `anchor-to-human-prior-art` the named humans are the deliverable, so the origin is not a
detail.

## Two things the entry explicitly does NOT claim

Both are commonly asserted and neither is checkable from here:

1. That Nitra ever shipped as the parsing foundation of ReSharper or Rider — Rider runs on the
   ReSharper backend, which predates it, and Nitra appears to have been archived.
2. The exact publication venue of the Nemerle macro papers (Skalski/Moskal/Olszta, ~2004).

Recorded as **unchecked rather than omitted**, so the next reader knows which half is verified.
