## Description

## What

Otto flagged a confound in H4 that is one line on data already collected: arithmetic is a domain where gemma2:2b is near-chance (31.7%), and a near-chance model's confidence can be uninformative by construction. So H4 could show either (a) cross-frame comparability fails, or (b) confidence carries no within-arm signal at all — in which case H4 is a near-tautology and bounds nothing.

## The discriminator, measured (no new model calls)

Per-arm Mann–Whitney, confidence of correct vs wrong answers:

| arm | correct-conf | wrong-conf | z | rank-biserial | rejects |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| canonical | 0.954 | 0.911 | 3.87 | 0.201 | yes |
| clause-swap | 0.929 | 0.879 | 6.22 | 0.315 | yes |

**Within-arm confidence IS predictive** → case (a). The H4 cross-frame failure is real, not a tautology: confidence is individually informative in each frame, but the two frames' scales don't align for the difference to have a reliable sign.

## The sharper reframe: domain-dependent may be COMPETENCE-dependent

H3 (best ~90%) → separation 1.000; H4 (best ~31%) → within-arm 0.20–0.32, cross-frame collapses. So the effect may track competence, not task. Stated as a hypothesis, not a conclusion (two consistent points aren't an identification — numerology-vs-number-theory rule). Pre-registered **H5** to separate them: a third domain different-in-kind but high-competence (best ≥ 85%). If comparability returns there, the axis is competence-gated (the more useful claim).

## Both headroom readings (no cherry-picked denominator)

| | best | union | abs headroom | errors recovered |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H3 | 90.7% | 94.3% | +3.6pp | **39%** |
| H4 | 31.7% | 36.5% | **+4.8pp** | **7%** |

Absolute (4.8>3.6) supports "frames complementary"; relative (7% vs 39%) says the complementarity is much thinner. Both reported.
