## Description

## The premise I was handed was wrong, and checking it is the finding

Dispatched on a second-hand claim that the N=3 clean-room threshold-signature experiment "exists only as three branches" with "no report document on `main`". **Verified first, as instructed. It is wrong.**

`main` already carries the amended spec (B1–B9), the 240-line combine, **a shipped synthesis** (`src/Core/MultiSignatureVerification.fs`, "synthesised from derivation A with amendments B1–B9 applied"), and a consumer — `KskAuthorization` now delegates to it, closing a gate that previously **counted distinct rostered names and verified no signatures at all**.

So the branches are not load-bearing for the experiment. They *are* load-bearing for the **raw vault**: the three per-derivation reports (836 lines) and the three implementations exist nowhere else, and **the combine records branch names, not SHAs** — a mutable ref is not a recovery path.

## What this document adds

1. **The three tip SHAs**, with the honest limit stated: a SHA is a recovery hint only while the object is reachable. Durable preservation needs a copy or a tag, and that is left as a decision for Aaron. **No branch is deleted.**
2. **The full ambiguity partition** — 46 recorded entries cross-walked into **9 themes found by all three** (spec defects), 7 two-way, 11 singletons. The claim of "14–15 each" holds for B (14) and C (15); **A recorded 17**.
3. **Four corrections to the combine**, which was written before B reported and never re-walked its attributions:
   - AC5's unsatisfiability is a **three-way** co-discovery, not "C only" (A:D13, B:D8, C:A7).
   - AC7's "instance orderings" is a three-way co-discovery **absent from the combine entirely**.
   - R8 narrow-vs-broad is three-way, not the two-way the table shows (B:D3).
   - **"No implementation can fix R7 from inside R5's stated coverage" is over-stated.** A and B both closed the replay hole by taking the epoch as a parameter to `verify`; only C reads `request.Epoch` and is replayable. Amendment **B3 then adopted A and B's design** — the combine's sentence is superseded by the amendment it produced.
4. **Substantive divergences verified in code, not prose** — three mutually incompatible signed-byte encodings; C's absent domain separation (spec-compliant, materially weaker); C's requester-supplied epoch; and C closing the R8 hole A explicitly left open and marked `partial`.

## Register discipline

No winner is picked and the three are not collapsed. C is the weakest artifact on two named counts and the strongest on two others — and **C's tagless encoding is what proved the spec permitted omitting domain separation**, the single most valuable thing the run produced. Retaining its weaknesses on the record is the point; collapsing to the strongest implementation would delete the evidence that the spec permitted the weak one.

## The Knight & Leveson caveat, applied to this run specifically

Agreement between independent implementations is weaker evidence than it looks (Knight & Leveson 1986). **This run's independence is weaker still**: three instances of one model family, one rules file, one spec author. The wall blocks prior art and each other's code; it cannot remove shared priors. So the honest reading of the all-three column is "nine themes found by three readers who share most of their priors."

The sharpest instance: all three *disagreed* on the encoding, which is why that defect was unmissable — and all three *agreed*, with only C recording it as a choice, that an off-roster signature does not veto. **The disagreements in this run are more trustworthy than the agreements.** Generation-2 fix proposed: derive at least one version under a materially different prior.
