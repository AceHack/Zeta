## Description

Aaron, generalizing the density test from the dynamical-zeta thread: *"if we can mechanize these checks to split these two classes for all agent errors human and AI this can really help categorize things."* And on the cause: *"you are trained on the internet and most humans are very over confident and speak like they know the answer when they only suspect the answer. i'm fallible to this too, this is why our decorrelation is so important."*

## The gap

`DerivationProtocol.fs` types what supports a **positive** claim — `Evidence`, `supportsClaim`, which deliberately refuses `AssertedOnly` / `MutantSurvived` / `NotConfirmed`. There is **no counterpart for the negative claim**, so nothing stops *"I did not find X"* being reported as *"X does not exist."*

Worked instance from the same day: I searched for an ordering theorem under "individual/society/world," found nothing, and wrote that it did not exist. It exists, is PROVEN, and is indexed under **Condorcet**. Artifact-class reported as intrinsic-class.

## The criterion is probe DIVERSITY, not probe count

Borrowed from where the distinction is sharp. A natural boundary is established by showing singularities are **dense** on the boundary (Ostrowski–Hadamard), never by failing to continue. In GR the Kretschmann scalar is *finite* at a horizon (obstruction is your coordinates) and *divergent* at r=0 (obstruction is real) — coordinate-independence is the whole point.

So record **where** each probe failed: same locus across independent methods ⇒ intrinsic; scattered loci ⇒ artifact.

## Independence is measured, not assumed

| probe set | ρ̂ |
|---|---|
| same weights, **different persona** | **0.651** |
| **different weights** | **0.096** |

Five persona-diverse probes are ≈ **one probe counted five times**. That is the operational cash-out of the decorrelation point, and it makes `Established` require probes decorrelated *by construction*.

## Two tiers — Aaron's correction to my first draft

I claimed absence can only ever be graded, because a knowledge base has no completeness. He corrected it: the linguistic seed is meant to be **fully enumerable** with **provably closed packs**, so *"every new pack does not bring in infinite english."* Over a declared closure, exhaustive cover **is** available and absence recovers the status of a theorem. Conditional, not fundamental.

The seed/pack architecture also turns out to **be** the continuation architecture, term for term: seed = domain of convergence, pack = extension, provably-closed = uniqueness, "infinite English" = the natural boundary.
