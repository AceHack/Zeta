## Description

## The premise that was false, generalized

PR #10759 was filed on the premise *"the tie-break behaviour is byte-locked into the four-oracle
treaty."* It was not. Its own summary is the sharpest statement of the class:

> **"the treaty pinned a POINT; the prose claimed a RULE."**

Aaron 2026-08-15: *"we may have this in other byte locked or golden vector treaties, sometimes the
golden vector is the pinned point and kind of vacuous other than everyone byte agrees to this file,
not that the file is actually meaningful."*

This audits the class and closes it mechanically. **Zero golden-vector files are modified.**

## The distinction, held throughout

A golden-vector treaty does **two separate jobs**:

1. **Cross-oracle agreement** — N implementations produce identical bytes. Real, essentially never
   vacuous, and **deliberately not audited here**.
2. **Rule pinning** — the vectors exclude named alternatives. Vacuous unless some vector actually
   discriminates.

Every finding below is of the form *"the prose claims rule R; no vector distinguishes R from R′."*
Not one is "this file is vacuous."

## Method — mutate the RULE, not the code

Spec-side mutation testing, one level up from `mutation-runner.ts`. That tool mutates the *code* and
asks whether a test notices; this asks whether a *vector* notices. **27 alternatives across 12
treaties, every count computed by evaluating the alternative against the pinned inputs.**

## Findings — 1 gap, 3 explained zeroes, 23 genuine exclusions

### The gap: `z-set-merkle` `nonAsciiOrdering` — 0 of 6

The seed states the rule twice — `"ordering"`: *"...not culture-sensitive string comparison"* and
`laws.nonAsciiOrdering` — and **names a vector `non-ascii-ordinal-bytes` after it.**

| alternative leaf ordering | vectors that change |
|---|---|
| culture-sensitive collation (ICU) | **0 of 6** |
| UTF-16 code-unit order | **0 of 6** |
| descending byte order | 4 of 6 |

That vector's keys are `e é Ω 中` — three scripts, no case or accent conflict — so **all nine
locales tested (en de sv da tr fr cs lt et) order them exactly as UTF-8 bytes do.** The name asserts
what the data cannot show.

**The remedy is known and in-repo.** Its sibling ladder treaties `bag/` and `z-set/` *do* pin
culture-invariance (**2 of 6** and **2 of 8** change under ICU), via one accented-Latin key beside an
ASCII letter (`áéíóú` vs `b-005-ASCII`). Priced, not landed: this seed is cross-verified by **six**
oracles (F#/C#/Rust/TS/Go/Python), so a new vector needs a fresh XxHash128 root reproduced by all
six. Filed `081M02RX1PN087G0R003WK66GV` (residual `081M02PEST7087G0R00253HRV0` verified present on
`main`).

The UTF-16 row is **blocked**, not a gap: any discriminating input is astral, and there F#/C#/TS
(code unit) and Rust (byte) genuinely disagree — such a vector would *break* the treaty.

### Zeroes that are NOT defects — the triage that keeps this honest

- `expect: "excluded"` → **0 changed ⇒ FAIL.** The treaty pins a point, not the rule.

## Honest limits

- **The harness is generic; the evaluators are hand-written per treaty.** That is inherent, not a
  shortcut: "plausible alternative rule" is a judgement about what a competent implementer might
  have chosen, and cannot be enumerated mechanically. What *is* mechanical — and is the whole
  point — is refusing a zero nobody explained.
- **A zero is ambiguous by nature.** It can mean "no discriminator" or "not actually a different
  function." The `kind` field exists precisely because the count alone cannot tell you, and three of
  the four zeroes found turned out to be the benign kind.
