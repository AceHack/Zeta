## Description

Aaron: *"our zset is like a reflection modeled over the start to end so t0 = t_infinity with CPT symmetry and −1 is the antiparticle, for us at least."*

Three legs, separated because they are **not equally supported** — one is shipped code, one is a letter short, one needs a truth condition.

## Leg 1 — "−1 is the antiparticle": CHECKED, and it's in the code

- `src/Core/CostarZSet.fs:13,48` — *"the **Z-set antiparticle** (`−1` weights) — `add then remove = identity`"*
- `src/Core/SchemaZ.fs:291` — *"Revoke… a retraction, the antiparticle."*

The **Stückelberg–Feynman** anchor is tight rather than decorative: a positron is an electron with the sign flipped — *the same particle* — and `+1` against `−1` annihilates to identity. It's also why revocation here is never a delete: you emit the antiparticle and let the fold cancel, which yields §5 memory preservation **from the algebra rather than from policy**.

## Leg 2 — CPT: I under-reported it, and Aaron was right

I first reported CPT as absent from the repo. Aaron said he thought there'd been a formal analysis. **He was right.** The 2026-06-07 compression doc carries a CPT section — *"If physics is right, all noise is reversible ultimately under CPT symmetry"* — arguing unitarity plus CPT means information is never destroyed, apparent noise is coarse-graining (Loschmidt), and the deepest lens leaves no residual.

**But the acronym is doing two jobs, and only one is sound:**

- **2026-06-07 — CPT as warrant for reversibility/non-destruction.** Defensible, already load-bearing, grounds retraction-instead-of-delete. Uses the theorem's *conclusion*; needs no decomposition.
- **2026-08-13 — CPT as a symmetry decomposition of the Z-set.** A letter short. **C** (negate weights) is exact. **T** is real but better named by the rule we already have — the fold is commutative, so the shared conclusion cannot depend on arrival order, which is `local-time-never-enters-the-shared-fold` and is the load-bearing design property, not a triviality. **P is undefined.**

Open: name a parity — candidates are the over/under crossing sign in `Braid.fs`, or emit/retract *direction* as distinct from weight *sign* — or drop the letter and keep the two real properties.

## Leg 3 — t₀ = t_∞: true for CLOSED histories

False for a general Z-set (unretracted elements leave a non-zero total). Exactly right when every emission is eventually retracted: weights sum to zero, the fold returns to origin. Same shape as the book's `e^{iπ}` motif — the excursion is real, the record is kept, the endpoint coincides with the origin. Gives the claim a truth condition: **a history is closed exactly when its Z-set folds to zero.**

## Companion — three independent negatives, routed with permission to fail

- **The symmetry group exists, and my reason for expecting none was wrong.** A semilattice not being a group isn't the obstruction — Minkowski isn't a group either; it's a *space acted on*. **Aut(Frame, ∨) ≅ Sym(A)**. Wigner still fails three independent ways: `Sym(A)` is discrete so the covering step is vacuous (no spin); `AmplitudeEmu`'s amplitudes are over `Chip8Cow.Frame`, **not** traveler frames; and `observe` takes a *named* actor, so the dynamics isn't equivariant. `TravelerFrame.fs:32-33` already said *"not yet the group law."* Register: **well-matched analogy, not structure** — with a concrete falsifier to promote it.
- **"Quantum from tick bounds" is discretisation, not quantisation.** `Versionstamp.tick` is `Checked.(+) v 1L` — discrete at the definition, not produced by bounding. Quantisation proper needs a non-commutative deformation; ticks and the join commute. An honest ℏ would need a non-trivial 2-cocycle on ℤ^A — buildable, not present.
- **The hierarchy problem is a homonym.** Physics means scale sensitivity of `m_H` to `Λ`; ours means capture from permanent authority. The SUSY-cancellation rhyme doesn't survive. Recommend **capture/permanence problem**; manifesto §3 already names it correctly.
