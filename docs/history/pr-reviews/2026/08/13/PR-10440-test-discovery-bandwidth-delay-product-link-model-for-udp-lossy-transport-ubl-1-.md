## Description

## The ferry (Aaron, 2026-08-13, verbatim)

> udp prtocols are very hard to get correct with backoff, speaking of that is our backoff tested,
> can we saturate a network channel?

Two questions. Neither was answered by what existed, and the answer to the first turns out to
depend on a fact nobody had stated.

## What was missing

The Gilbert-Elliott chaos harness (#10417) measures **delivery ratio** and **goodput** against an
injected loss process. That is a channel-quality measurement and the right instrument for the
erasure-code question it was built for. It models **no capacity, no queue, no propagation delay**.
A model with no queue cannot produce congestion, so it cannot observe a congestion controller at
all. **Verified before building, not assumed** -- the chaos harness has no capacity, buffer, delay
or RTT anywhere in it, and its only time-like quantity is a reorder *depth* counted in packets.

This adds the physics: one bottleneck link with capacity `C`, a drop-tail buffer of `B` packets,
and a one-way propagation delay `D`. Loss then has **two structurally distinct causes**, counted
separately everywhere: **queue overflow** (congestion -- backing off relieves it) and
**Gilbert-Elliott corruption** (backing off relieves nothing). That separation is the module.

**No production file is modified.**

## Finding 0, which reframes everything else -- CHECKED

`LossyUdpChannel.flushBlock` broadcasts all 8 packets of a block in a tight loop and calls
`onSend` after each. **It never reads `gapMs`.** Repo-wide, `gapMs` is written in `updateAimd` and
read by `updateAimd` and two unit-test assertions -- by **no send path anywhere**.

The AIMD controller is an **open-loop estimator whose output is discarded**. The shipped transport
has no rate control at all. So everything below is measured in **two arms over one code path**,
differing in a single knob: `open-loop` (shipped -- the estimator still runs and we record the gap
it computes and throws away) and `aimd` (the same live `AimdState` functions, with `gapMs`
actually pacing). `UBL-14` pins the wiring fact so it cannot change silently.

## Can we saturate a channel? Yes -- and only because the controller is disconnected

Greedy source, clean channel, 10s, `util` = delivered / (C x duration):

| arm | C=200 | C=1000 | C=5000 |
|---|---|---|---|
| shipped (open-loop) | **0.99-1.00** | **0.99-1.00** | **0.99-1.00** |
| AIMD paced | 0.16-0.48 | 0.84 | **0.168** |

The shipped path fills every link tested -- by **overflowing the buffer** (the congestion-drop
column runs to half of everything sent). The paced arm has a **hard 1000 pkt/s ceiling on any
link** because `MIN_GAP_MS = 1` is one packet per millisecond; on a 5000 pkt/s link that is a
16.8% ceiling no channel condition can lift. On links *slower* than the ceiling it undershoots
instead. Full 54-row table: `bun src/Core.TypeScript/discovery/udp-bdp-link.demo.ts`.

## Is the backoff tested? It is now, and it does not converge -- it saturates

On a link the sender is filling to 99.9%, the gap the shipped code computes is pinned at
`MAX_GAP_MS` for **99.5%** of a 20s run and takes **two** distinct values. The queue makes the
#10417 estimator defect worse: with real propagation delay, NACKs arrive **clustered**, and
because `updateAimd` resets `sentCount` on every NACK, a cluster of k NACKs one send apart
computes a loss rate of **k**.

Chiu and Jain 1989 (CITED, not page-checked) prove convergence *under assumptions*; two fail:

1. **The control variable is a GAP, not the shared rate.** A constant -2ms step adds 0.4% of rate
   at gap 500ms and 25% at gap 10ms -- additive increase **in the wrong space**.
2. **The feedback must be a congestion signal.** Here it is a NACK, which this transport also
   emits for corruption and for reordering.

**Cost of (1), measured:** recovering from **one** spurious multiplicative decrease on a perfectly
clean link takes **4016 seconds -- 66.9 minutes** -- and the simulator agrees with an independent
closed form to **0.0%**, which also validates the model.

## The decisive experiment: congestion held at zero, corruption raised

Zero congestion is **structural**, not hoped for -- capacity is 4x the fastest rate either arm can
produce, buffer 4x BDP -- and `UBL-10` **asserts congestion drops are 0 on every row** before
reading any throughput number. Throughput relative to the same arm on a clean channel:

| corruption | congestion drops | shipped | AIMD paced | ideal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5% | 0 | 0.995 | **0.830** | 0.995 |
| 1% | 0 | 0.991 | **0.607** | 0.990 |
| 2% | 0 | 0.981 | **0.139** | 0.980 |
| 5% | 0 | 0.951 | **0.023** | 0.950 |
| 10% | 0 | 0.905 | **0.010** | 0.900 |

The shipped arm is the **negative control**: flat, tracking `1 - lossRate` within 1 percentage
point, because it cannot respond to the NACK stream at all. The paced arm collapses -- **7.1x less
throughput than a corruption-blind sender at 2%, 90x less at 10%, with no congestion anywhere.**

**The framing holds.** Balakrishnan et al. 1997 and BBR 2016 (both CITED, not page-checked) are
describing this transport.

### The reorder bug is the same defect through a different door -- was PROPOSED, now CONFIRMED

Zero loss, zero congestion, 5ms of delivery jitter: delivery is untouched (over 99% of the clean
run) but the paced arm falls **838.9 -> 70.6 pkt/s** on a link that dropped nothing. Reordering and
corruption are two doors into one defect -- **a non-congestion signal driving a congestion
response** -- so the fix is "separate the signals", not "tune the thresholds" and not "add a
reorder hold-down". That is larger than `081KZYN3D53087G0R0036XZSYM` and
`081KZYN37T4087G0R00181THA4` imply on their own.

### The control that keeps it honest

Under loss that **is** congestion -- drop-tail overflow, no corruption, no reordering -- two flows
reach **Jain 0.973 at 79% utilisation**. As a congestion controller it does roughly what Chiu and
Jain promise. It fails **specifically** when the loss it reacts to is not congestion.

## A correction to my own first reading, recorded rather than dropped

My first fairness run reported **total starvation** -- Jain 0.803, one flow at zero. That number is
an **artifact**. Deterministic pacing into a drop-tail queue is exactly the configuration Floyd and
Jacobson 1992 (CITED, not page-checked) identify as producing phase-lock, and 2ms of send-phase
jitter dissolves it to **Jain 0.972**. The harness now carries the phase-noise knob and `UBL-8`
runs both, because a fairness number measured only at zero phase noise is an artifact report.
Three other assertions were wrong on first run and were corrected against the measurement, never
the reverse.

## Bufferbloat, textbook (Gettys and Nichols 2011 -- CITED, not page-checked)

At a 64x-BDP buffer the shipped sender inflates mean one-way delay **30.0ms -> 1923.7ms (91.6x)**
for **0.0%** more throughput, monotone in the buffer the whole way.

## Model falsifiers before findings

`UBL-3` reproduces the empty-queue closed form `D + 1000/C` to **9 decimal places**; `UBL-5` pins
that a B-packet drop-tail buffer bounds p95 queueing delay by exactly B service times at B=1,5,50;
`UBL-5c` pins link utilisation at or below 1.0 after clipping service that spills past the horizon;
`UBL-1` pins byte-identical seeded replay; `UBL-4` pins byte-identical sweeps at DoP 1/4/8.

- **`081KZYQ8Q9V087G0R0013XR3ZX` (P2)** -- `gapMs` computed and never read, plus the 1000 pkt/s
  `MIN_GAP_MS` ceiling that only bites once pacing is wired.

**Ordering is the substance of the finding.** `081KZYN37T4087G0R00181THA4` is currently **latent**
because the saturated gap is discarded. Fixing the estimator or wiring the pacing without
separating the signals **activates** a defect that costs **86% of throughput at 2% corruption**.

## Honest limits

One bottleneck, one direction, no cross traffic. NACK channel assumed reliable (as the module
itself states). Drop-tail only -- no AQM/ECN. Fixed packet sizes. The receiver gap rule is
transcribed from `handleIncoming` rather than executed, and `UBL-14` pins the source so the
transcription cannot drift. **The paced arm is a hypothetical** -- it measures a controller that is
not currently connected; no shipped path behaves like those rows today.

## Disciplines

Sec.7 DST (one deterministic event loop; ties break on a monotone insertion counter, never a
clock) - Sec.13 noninterference (corruption and jitter on streams disjoint from the chaos harness;
`UBL-2` pins that raising jitter cannot perturb the corruption trace) - `async-all-the-way` (a run
is DoP=1 by construction, sweeps go through `runFerry`) -
`local-time-never-enters-the-shared-fold` - no binary in the proof lineage (every table is text).
