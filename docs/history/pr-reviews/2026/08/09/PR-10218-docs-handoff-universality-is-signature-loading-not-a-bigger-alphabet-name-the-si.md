## Description

Two corrections from Aaron, both material — and the first is me correcting my own understatement.

## 1. Universality = signature loading, not a bigger alphabet

I'd written *"'universal' is concrete for CHIP-8; generalising is an open claim."* **That understated the design.**

> *"We are designing an **Xbox-like interface** in mind to make it universal. The square lets you navigate search space in a predictable fashion by **loading different external signatures of the search space you are in**. We've generalized it in our zeta scheduler for .NET too, and our ferry throttler that can predict itself."* — plus the honest status: *"we just have not pulled it all together."*

The mechanism is a **fixed controller shape with swappable semantics**: an Xbox controller is universal across games not because 16 buttons encode every action, but because every game **loads its own mapping** onto an invariant shape. The alphabet is invariant; the *signature* is loaded per search space.

**Verified the claimed generalisations exist:** `FerryThrottler.fs` (+ `SoftThrottle.fs`, `FeedbackThrottle.fs`, TS `ferry-throttler/`) and `PredictionScheduler.fs` (+ Soft/Cell/VirtualTime/DarkHall/SoftChip8 schedulers).

So **the gap is integration, not design** — Round 4 is *assembly*, not invention. Materially easier and better-specified than my caveat implied, and worth saying plainly rather than leaving a false "open research question" in front of Lumen.

## 2. The signature detector exists — and its human anchor was unnamed

> *"our signature detector … I think it's called rainbow spectrum or something … I used to work at Itron and built **disaggregation over electric signal at 16 kHz**."*

Verified: `CoordinationSpectrum.fs` is *"the S-spectrum as a **soft-rainbow fingerprint**"* — the CHSH probe battery acts as a **prism**, so one source wearing many faces disperses into a characteristic pairwise-S spectrum. `FingerprintPrism.Rainbow` lives in `Optics.fs`.

The cited anchor was Pappu 2002 (PUFs, laser speckle). The **missing sibling anchor is NILM** — non-intrusive load monitoring (**Hart, Proc. IEEE 80(12), 1992**) — and Aaron built it **in production at 16 kHz**: high-rate transient/harmonic signatures, well past the ~1 Hz most NILM literature assumes. First-hand expertise, so **ask him rather than deriving from first principles**.

The framing transfers: signatures are **additive** in the aggregate, detection is **inference under overlap**, and the hard cases are *simultaneous* and *near-identical* loads — exactly the problem of distinguishing agents sharing one channel.
