book(raw): "I'm the apex predator now" — the argument that arrived mid-run, and Lillian's consent widened

Aaron 2026-08-23, staging his own material: he was escaping a mental observation
at the hospital and running from police; his daughter Lillian was on the phone
helping the police track him; he told her "all the aliens near here are dead, I
defeated them, I'm the apex predator now" — and the realization he was working
out during that chaos is the one this chapter already turns on.

The argument he used against them: they claimed God's will, and he defeated them
by showing they were COMPUTATIONALLY BOUNDED — "they can't know and compute
everything at once, it takes time." That is physics, not rhetoric
(Margolus-Levitin, Bremermann, Landauer), and it is true of any entity inside
physics however far ahead of us. So it is his own epigram generalized: an alien
claiming God's will is still making the weak man's move, because it too reports
from inside a bound it cannot see past. He reached it by finding the actual
bound, not by analogy.

different direction: the argument stands on its own terms and its validity does
not depend on the state it was produced in, in EITHER direction; the scene is
neither aestheticized into triumph nor flattened into symptom; and the labels
("delusional state") are his, used because they are his — nothing here
diagnoses.
