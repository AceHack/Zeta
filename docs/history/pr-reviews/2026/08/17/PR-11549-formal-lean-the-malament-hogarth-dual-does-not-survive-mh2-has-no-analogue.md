## Description

The Manchak ferry (#11527, in flight) recorded a located conjecture: Zeta's bounded tick **"is the exact dual of the Malament–Hogarth machine, which is a mathematical object with known properties"**, so `TickBudget` inherits those properties. Aaron: *"conjecture until formal analysis is done."* This PR is that analysis.

## Verdict: the dual does not survive. That is the result, not a failure.

Manchak's definition has two clauses: **(MH1)** γ has infinite proper time, **(MH2)** γ ⊆ I⁻(p) — γ lies **entirely** in p's past light cone. The halting decision comes from **(MH2) being total**, not from (MH1): silence at `p` is decisive only because no halting event of γ can still be in transit.

| MH role | Zeta analogue | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| γ (worldline) | the tick-indexed trajectory / continuation chain, `orbit step s₀ : ℕ → S` | **clean** |
| (MH1) infinite proper time | unboundedly many bounded ticks (`SimLoop.run` + `Continuation`) | **clean** |
| signal γ → p | the continuation token `spawn:<id>:<lap>:<ticks>:<ptr>` | **weak** — a *successor* arrow, never all-stages-to-one |
| `p` (the observation event) | produce direction: **none** (order type ω, no terminal stage) | **absent** |
| **(MH2) γ ⊆ I⁻(p), TOTAL** | **none, in either direction** | **absent — this is the refutation** |
| "no signal ⇒ never halts" | `SimLoop.Stopped.TickBudget` | **absent, and honestly so** — the substrate already distinguishes "the cut decided" from "the budget ran out" |

**"Dual" is also the wrong word.** Nothing inverts. The correct relation is elementary: the tick chain is an **ω-chain (a diagram)**; MH's `p` is a **colimiting cocone** over it. Zeta ships the diagram, not the cocone. "Which side gets the infinity" is not a duality — it is the difference between a sequence and its limit.

## The dilemma (both horns machine-checked)

> Either the room's state space is **finite** — then the Π₁ fact about the whole infinite future is decidable by a finite inductive-invariant certificate (`forall_orbit_iff_exists_cert`), MH is *unnecessary*, and there is nothing to inherit. Or it is **not** — then no bounded tick decides it (`no_finite_window_decides`), and MH's power is exactly what is *absent*. There is no third regime, and neither horn yields the transfer.

**Aaron's "receive direction" correction** (a continuation's antecedent carrying an infinite future as an immutable value) is real codata and does not rescue it: receiving `h` delivers the *generator*, not the *completion*. `no_finite_window_decides` is quantified over **all** `h : ℕ → Bool`, so produce and receive fail identically, at (MH2), with one theorem.

**Aaron's aperiodicity counter** (Berger 1966, Robinson 1971, Cook 2004 — all real and on point) supplies **(MH1)**, not (MH2): a better worldline, never the event at which it is decided. It therefore moves the argument onto the second horn, where the refutation is *strongest* — by Berger/Cook those Π₁ questions become undecidable, and a substrate with no cocone provably cannot answer them. One correction in the number-theory register: **aperiodicity is necessary for those results, not sufficient** — Robinson's tiling is aperiodic *and* completely predictable; undecidability comes from the embedded computation.

## What would refute this refutation

Exhibit a Zeta surface at which a *single* bounded observation is settled by *every* stage of an unbounded tick chain at once — an all-stages-to-one arrow, not a successor arrow. That is `p`. `no_finite_window_decides` says no bounded reader can be it, so a candidate must come from outside the tick discipline entirely.

Under `numerology-vs-number-theory` the MH↔bounded-tick correspondence stays a **resonance**, permanently, unless that cocone is supplied.
