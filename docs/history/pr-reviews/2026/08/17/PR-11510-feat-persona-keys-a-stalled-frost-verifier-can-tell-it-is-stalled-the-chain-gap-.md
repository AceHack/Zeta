## The defect, measured

`foldChain` reported `current` for two situations a verifier must not confuse:
it walked to the head of the chain, and it **stopped because the next link is
missing**.

A replica pinned at epoch 0, holding a genuine admissible **epoch-2** transition
for its own keyId with epoch 1 suppressed, folds to:

```
stalled: {"status":"current","epoch":0,"advanced":0,"retiredIndices":[]}
deaf   : {"status":"current","epoch":0,"advanced":0,"retiredIndices":[]}
```

Field-for-field identical to a replica that heard **nothing at all** — while it
holds, in its own ledger, signed evidence that its chain moved on. It keeps
accepting the retired key. That is the suppress-one-link attack: withhold a
single element and the target's "I am up to date" is indistinguishable from the
truth — a check that did not run looking like one that passed.

## The fix, and the honest limit on it

`chainGapProbe(pin, ledger)` reports the neutral fact and names the **one**
element to fetch (`stop + 1`, never a range).

**It deliberately cannot say "reject."** KL-16 demonstrates a party with **no
shares of the victim's key** minting an admissible ahead-element for any `keyId`
at any epoch — admission is a pure function of the element by design (header
property 1), which is what keeps merge commutative. Gating acceptance on this
signal would hand any attacker a free DoS on every listening verifier. So the
return type offers only `nothing-to-request` and `request-epoch`.

That is a **shape, not an enforcement**: a caller can still ignore the type and
refuse on its own, and nothing here stops that. What it removes is the vocabulary
that would make refusing look like the intended use. Fetching is safe under
forgery; refusing is not.

Pure in `(pin, ledger)` — no clock, no arrival order. It reports what to
*request* and never filters what enters the fold.

## What an old-share holder can still do (unchanged by this PR)

1. **Sign under `A`**, and anything still pinned to `A` accepts it. The key change
   is immediate; the knowledge is eventually consistent. This PR shortens that
   window by letting a replica know what to ask for — it does not close it.
2. **Reconstruct the OLD secret** with a threshold of other old shares. Delta
   rotation *retires* a key; it does not *un-share* one. Anything ever signed or
   encrypted under `A` stays within reach of an old-threshold coalition forever.
   **Rotation is not re-encryption.**
3. **Nothing under `A'`** — not with `t−1` old shares, not with all `n` (DR-6).

## Bookkeeping vs enforcement

**Enforced:** `A' = A + [δ]B` with `δ` jointly random and unknown to every party.
The revoked holder is revoked by **absence from `newIndices`** — asked for nothing,
sent nothing.

**Bookkeeping:** `retiredIndices` is a label plus an operator-error guard (DR-8,
DR-9, DR-10). It is not what kills the old share; the key change is.

**Newly measured and left open:** a participant index is a **slot, not a party**.
Verified — retiring index 3 at epoch 1 and re-issuing it at epoch 2 leaves the
fold reporting `retiredIndices: [3]` while slot 3 holds a live share. Nothing
depends on this today; `retiredIndices` must not be used as an identity blocklist.
Recorded in the work item rather than fixed, because the fix is a policy decision.
