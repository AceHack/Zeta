## Red-team pass — what the source note did not carry

I am not here to transcribe a good finding. Seven, in severity order:

- **HRT-1 — the issuer-DN observation is under-determined, and its class is wrong.** The note reports the emitted issuer became `CN=YubiHSM Attestation id:<ID>` and reads that as "the template's subject DN, copied". A *synthesize-from-object-ID* firmware produces byte-identical output, and `yubihsm-shell` generates templates with exactly that subject — the experiment cannot separate the two mechanisms. A matching string is not an identification. **And if it is a copy, the threat is Spoofing, not LANGSEC:** a caller chooses a string a verifier reads as provenance, and the attack needs no parser bug at all (Marlinspike / Kaminsky 2009, CVE-2009-2408). Verifiers pin the **key**, never the **name**.
- **HRT-2 — the attestation carries caller-authored text inside the signature.** Extension `.9` is the label, measured appearing **verbatim**. A signature authenticates the envelope, not the truth of what the subject put in it. Confused deputy for every downstream log viewer and policy engine.
- **HRT-3 — an attestation is not a proof of possession, and nothing in it says so.** No nonce, no challenge, `Not After 9999`, no clock, and reset is seconds. The bytes are **replayable forever by anyone who has ever seen them** — and they are meant to be published. Enables replay, and **reset-and-reintroduce**: an evil maid wipes the device, re-enrols genuine on-device keys, and looks exactly like a legitimate rotation unless re-registration chains to the previous key.
- **HRT-4 — `put-opaque` is transitively attestation-issuer-write.** The namespace is `(id, type)`, the template is read from the attesting key's own object ID, and storing it is a *different* capability from using it. Two narrow capabilities compose into one wide one, across a revocation boundary, below the repair boundary. The same shape almost certainly recurs at `put-template` × `sign-ssh-certificate` (S4), where the template carries the principal white-list — the higher-value instance.
- **HRT-5 — reachability is the perimeter, and it was never modelled.** "Bind the connector narrowly" is advice, not a control. The pre-auth self-description **selects the EUCLEAK-eligible node out of a fleet**; `Log used: N/62` is a credential-free activity counter; the **serial is an unrotatable linkability handle** present in every attestation — a privacy budget can frost a name, never a serial; and pre-auth session allocation is a plausible unmetered DoS whose *reclamation path* matters more than the exhaustion.
- **HRT-6 — the log's integrity is metered; its retention is zero.** A 62-entry hash-chained **ring**. An adversary hides one action by performing 62 unremarkable ones, and every remaining seal verifies perfectly across the gap. No exploit required.
- **HRT-7 — the anti-DVD-drive claim is about the artifact and silent about the consumer.** This is the one worth attacking, and the task asked me to. The artifact half is correct and measured. But **the DVD drive was never the bug — the console was.** Four doors the verdict re-enters: collapse at signature check (`if verify then trust`); trusting the **name** (HRT-1); trusting a subject-authored field (HRT-2); and reading `origin: on-device` as *independent* evidence when, on the custom-attestation path this design endorses, **the device and the signer are both ours** — a witnessed assertion, not third-party evidence. The honest statement now in the model: the attestation answers *which key, which capabilities, generated where*, and cannot answer *is this peer live*, *is this binding valid*, *what time is it*, or *who is this node*.
