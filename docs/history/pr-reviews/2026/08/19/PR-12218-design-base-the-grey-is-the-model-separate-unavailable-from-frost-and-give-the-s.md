## Description

## The finding, honestly

Three states named in `#12206` §7d look alike on screen and mean entirely different things.
Audited against the shipped tokens. **Two were fine; one was missing; and the channel carrying
all of them was worse than the missing one.**

| state | before | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **frosted** | `data-withheld="frost"` → violet + blur, with a test pinning blur-is-frost-only | **distinguished** |
| **absent** | no token, does not render | **correct** — but the rule was never written down |
| **unavailable** | *nothing* | **missing** |

With no token for "structurally impossible", an implementer had two places to put it and both
are wrong. `cold` (`#46506B`) means "watched, nothing there" — it implies something still could
arrive, silently promising a future the constraint system forbids. `sealed` /
`data-observed="false"` puts it in the **withheld** register, which is exactly the collapse §7d
forbids: violet saying *"this is not for you"* about something nobody is withholding.

**And the larger finding, which was not the assignment.** The DU's only channel was hue.
`Settlement.dc.html` carries `data-state` / `data-observed` / `data-withheld` and **not one aria
attribute**; its `◍` renders unconditionally, tinted by `var(--state)` — decoration, not a
discriminator. Strip the colour and all eight members read the same.

## Verifying the reasoning I was asked to verify

Both collapse directions are real, and the asymmetry holds. Collapsing *unavailable* into
*frosted* blames the reader's permissions for the model's constraint. Collapsing *frosted* into
*unavailable* is worse — a false statement about the world, made by the interface, over
something earned, permanent and inviolable
([`privacy-budget-is-hard-money-earned-by-others`](.claude/rules/privacy-budget-is-hard-money-earned-by-others.md)).
So the cascade fails in that direction: **withheld outranks unavailable.**

## What landed — extending the DU, not paralleling it

- **`--state-unavailable: #7F838B`** — near-neutral so it cannot join the blue-slate status
  family. Measured 5.07:1 / 5.13:1 on the two grounds and 4.57:1 / 4.87:1 on the two panels, AA
  at body size on all four. Legible rather than faint on purpose: a structurally impossible
  option you cannot *read* is the failure the token exists to prevent.
- **A single strike, never a repeat.** The withheld register owns the 45° repeating hatch. A
  repeating texture here would put both registers in one visual family at a glance whatever the
  hexes say. One rule through the middle survives greyscale and every form of colour-blindness —
  geometry, not hue.
- **`src/Core.TypeScript/cluster/state-du.ts`** — glyph, ASCII fallback, label, reason and
  required ARIA per member, one table read by the CSS *and* by any text/CLI renderer.
  `unavailable` → `aria-disabled` **plus a reachable reason**, never the bare `disabled`
  attribute (it drops out of the tab order, so the user cannot reach the element to learn *why*)
  and never `aria-hidden`. `frost` → **not** `aria-disabled`: it is not disabled, it exists and
  is withheld.
- **`absent` stays a non-member**, now as a written rule. The tempting error is `unobserved`,
  which files an inapplicable field under the withheld register.

## One thing the audit turned up that was not asked for

`filter: blur()` is paint-time only — it does not touch the DOM, the accessibility tree,
find-in-page, view-source, or a client with CSS disabled — and the stylesheet's own comment
*requires* the real content to be in the DOM. So frost as specified was cosmetic redaction.
Whether that is an actual leak depends on something the design language never said, and **the
silence is the bug**. Now stated at the definition site: frost is a *rendering* of an
already-enforced withholding; frosted bytes must not be delivered to a viewer not entitled to
them, and where the viewer is entitled the content node still carries `aria-hidden`. A blur a
screen reader speaks through is not frost, it is a smudge.

## Falsifiers

33 tests pass. Six mutations run against the new checks, **all six caught**: `unavailable`=violet
· `unavailable`=hatch · `unavailable`=cold · frost reported as disabled · an ASCII-mark collision
· and the sentence rewritten to blame the reader (`"you do not have access to this"`). That last
one asserts the carved consequence directly — **grey must say "this cannot be", never "this is
not for you."**
