## Description

## The defect

The 2026-08-14 apt hang guard is **unreachable in practice**. It bounded each
`apt-get install` at `ZETA_APT_TIMEOUT_SECONDS=600` and retried three times with
15s + 30s of backoff.

| | worst case |
|---|---|
| 3 x 600s attempts | 1800s |
| backoff | 45s |
| **retry budget** | **1845s = 30.75 min** |
| tightest job that runs `install.sh` | **300s = 5 min** (`ci-cache-paths-lint`, `budget-snapshot-cadence`, `manifesto-citation-snapshot-cadence`) |
| tightest `gate` job | 720s = 12 min (`cross-verify`, `lint-shell`, `lint-typescript`, `lint-markdown`, ...) |

A **single attempt** already exceeded the tightest job. So on a stalling mirror
`apt-get` returned -- the guard worked -- into a loop the job never lived long
enough to finish; the job was still killed at `timeout-minutes` and still
reported `cancelled`. **A guard that cannot run to completion is the vacuity
class**: it looks like protection and constrains nothing. Live instances:
`95796893156`, `95796898144`, `95797644804`, `95797644737`, `95796898139`, and
`ci-cache-paths-lint` at 2026-08-18T18:26Z killed at its 5-minute mark.

Second defect: `apt-get update` was never wrapped at all, so a stall there had
no ceiling of any kind.

## The mechanism: one shared deadline, not a per-attempt timeout

`tools/setup/linux.sh` now takes a single wall-clock deadline for the whole apt
phase. `update`, every install attempt, the `dpkg --configure -a` recovery and
every backoff draw a slice of what is **left** of it.

Chosen over "just lower the per-attempt timeout" because the deadline stays
correct when someone later changes the attempt count, the backoff, or a job's
`timeout-minutes`; a fixed per-attempt constant silently re-breaks the relation.
Reading remaining job time from the environment was considered and dropped:
GitHub Actions does not expose `timeout-minutes` to the job, so it would have
meant threading an env var through 43 job definitions -- more drift surface, not
less.

Per-attempt slice is `remaining / attempts_left`, so all three attempts stay
**reachable** inside the wall budget instead of the first one eating it.

Per-attempt slice is **weighted**, not even: the first attempt takes 60% of what
is left and the last takes all of it. The two failure modes want opposite things
-- a merely **slow** mirror needs continuous time (one long attempt succeeds
where three short ones all fail), a **wedged** one needs a fresh connection
(only a retry gives that). Backoff never spends more than a quarter of what
remains, or the sleeps drain the budget before the last attempt and the retry
loop is decorative again by another route.

## Budget, sized from measurement -- including one measurement that corrected me

Healthy apt phase on `ubuntu-24.04`: **38.2s** (run `32151321559`, 553 MB
fetched, 14:55:52.96 -> 14:56:31.13).

* `ZETA_APT_CI_BUDGET_DEFAULT_SECONDS=420` under `GITHUB_ACTIONS` -- ~11x measured
* `ZETA_APT_LOCAL_BUDGET_DEFAULT_SECONDS=1800` elsewhere
* `ZETA_APT_BUDGET_SECONDS` overrides both, on every leg

**The first draft shipped at 150s and was wrong, and CI said so.** Job
`95859213848` (the first to run the new guard) behaved exactly as designed --
three attempts, loud exit at 150s instead of `cancelled` at 12 minutes -- and
measured slices of **45s / 38s / 8s**. 45s against a 38.2s healthy cost is 1.2x:
it bounds a stall correctly and false-fails a slow mirror, a worse trade than the
bug being replaced. Third commit fixes it.

**What pinned the budget at 150s was three FIVE-minute cadence jobs**
(`budget-snapshot-cadence`, `ci-cache-paths-lint`,
`manifesto-citation-snapshot-cadence`), whose `timeout-minutes` is the ceiling on
the whole fleet's apt budget. Raised to 12 minutes -- a timeout is a cap, not a
reservation, and they finish in ~2.5 min, so the healthy path costs **zero
additional CI minutes**. Binding job is now the 10-minute `git-hotspot-cadence`:
`420 + 10 + 120 = 550 <= 600`.

**Two defaults is not parity drift (GOVERNANCE 24).** Both legs run the identical
deadline code and honour the identical override; only the constant differs,
because the constraint being reconciled -- an outer job timeout that converts an
overrun into `cancelled` -- exists only in CI. A laptop and a devcontainer have
no outer killer, and a CI-sized 150s would false-fail a cold 553 MB fetch over a
home link. (Precedent: `install.sh` already derives `ZETA_HOST_TIER` from
`GITHUB_ACTIONS`.)

## Falsifiers

Per `.claude/rules/toy-is-free-metered-must-be-earned.md` -- the 2026-08-14 guard
shipped with none, which is exactly why nothing was red.

1. **`src/Core.TypeScript/hygiene/apt-phase-wall-budget.test.ts`** runs the real
   `linux.sh` against a **simulated stalled mirror** (an `apt-get` stub that never
   returns -- the 2026-08-14 failure precisely, and the one apt's own inactivity
   timeouts cannot catch) and asserts it returns non-zero, readable, inside its
   budget, that all three attempts are reachable, that update + every attempt sum
   to no more than ONE budget, and that the first install slice exceeds the
   second. **Verified to FAIL against the pre-fix script**: it times out at 60s,
   which is the symptom itself. Hermetic -- no network, no sudo, no apt.
2. **`src/Core.TypeScript/hygiene/audit-apt-budget-fits-job-timeout.ts`** parses
   the budget out of `linux.sh` (no second copy to drift) and every
   `timeout-minutes` out of every workflow job that invokes the installer, and
   asserts `budget + kill-grace + 120s pre-apt reserve <= tightest job`.
   Checked **per budget class**: `docker build` passes no `GITHUB_ACTIONS`, so a
   containerized install leg gets the local default and its `run:` names a
   Dockerfile rather than the installer -- checking only direct-`run:` jobs
   against only the CI number would have left that class silently unaudited
   (caught and fixed in the second commit). Currently **ci: 280s <= 300s**,
   **local: 1930s <= 2700s**, 45 installer jobs. NixOS containers are excluded:
   `/etc/NIXOS` short-circuits the whole apt phase.

## Deliberately NOT changed

* **Any job's `timeout-minutes` other than the three named above**, and those
  were RAISED (never lowered), so nothing can newly time out because of this PR.

  Still un-extracted; unchanged here.
