**1. The Xbox root-of-trust precedent** — `2026-08-18-the-original-xbox-a-root-of-trust-below-the-update-boundary-*`

Aaron on the open YubiHSM firmware question: *"xbox the original did this and the memory resident version of the bios was hackable via a font overflow exploit."* The configuration we were treating as an open 2026 question was broken in public in 2002, and not via the crypto. Then: *"also xbox 360 was defeated by two different hardware models of security, the cdrom that could be faked into saying yes is this valid, and the cpu that was much harder until they came out with side channel attacks."*

Three failure classes, and the doc's value is that all three are one rule:

| | Xbox | what was trusted | what actually crossed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Parser in the trusted path | original — font overflow | a *format* | an undeclared **input** channel |
| 2. Trusted peripheral | 360 — DVD drive | a *component's verdict* | an undeclared **authority** channel |
| 3. Physical channel | 360 — glitch / timing | the *logic model* | an undeclared **physical** channel |

All three are §13 noninterference at different layers. Class 2 is `dual-use-detection-is-neutral` and `no-directives` (source ≠ authorization) realised in silicon, and it bites us directly on attestation: a verifier that accepts a boolean instead of a checkable quote has reproduced the DVD drive. Class 3 is why a capability list never settles a hardware comparison — you can revoke a key, you cannot revoke a data-dependent branch.

Plus §2a: a root of trust below the update boundary can only be replaced, never repaired — the same geometry as EUCLEAK, 22 years earlier.

Historical specifics are labelled in a **recall register**; §2 survives any of them being wrong, because the anchor is used for its structure. The 360 CPU-key extraction mechanism is explicitly marked as the least-certain item and is not load-bearing.

**2. Maxwell's demon** — `2026-08-18-an-unmetered-channel-is-a-maxwells-demon-*`

That is the exact dual of the coffee-cup point in the same conversation: entropy is a property of a description, never of how something looks.

The demon's resolution (Szilard → Landauer → Bennett) generalises to:

> **A Maxwell's demon is what an unmetered channel looks like from outside.**

Which makes §13 sharper — every undeclared channel is a demon, and it appears to give you something for nothing precisely because the ledger that would show the cost is the one you did not open. Same shape as the vacuity class.

A budget that meters retractions meters the reversible operation. The meter belongs at compaction, squash, snapshot-supersedes-log, and eviction.
