ferry(ip-questionable): five brain-evolution findings — neoteny, HAR folding, a saturated pathway

Aaron ferried an Anton Petrov video summarising five studies on human brain
evolution, with the note "this seem directly relevant to our BNNs."

Paraphrased notes, not the transcript. The source is a popular SECONDARY summary
of primary research, and the file keeps that distance visible rather than
laundering it into citations.

The anchoring pass is the part I want on the record. The transcript garbles
names, and four of five claims could not be anchored. Katherine Pollard is
correct for the HAR work (the transcript's "Katie Pard") and that line is solid.
The epiregulin organoid result is Dresden and the Huttner lab is the obvious
candidate, but the paper is unverified. The "Peter Pan" integration study is
attributed to a name I could not resolve and I did not guess. The sugar model is
attributed to Brand-Miller and Raubenheimer in Science, both real researchers in
the field, paper unverified. The de novo lncRNA gene has no author given.
Inventing confident references would have been worse than leaving gaps.

Four mappings to the BNNs, graded rather than listed.

The strong one: neoteny IS never-collapse. The de novo protein holds neural stem
cells immature so they divide more before committing; the integration study says
human brains keep juvenile plasticity into adulthood. That is SoftValue's stated
property in wetware -- resolve only above threshold, otherwise hold -- and
Aaron's own line that uncertainty is what gives us commutativity. A neuron that
differentiates is a distribution that resolved. Structural rhyme, not mechanism
transfer, and labelled as such.

The uncomfortable one: a saturated growth pathway is rho -> 1. More epiregulin
grows a GORILLA organoid and does nothing to a human one, because the pathway is
already at its stop -- the same shape as N clones pricing near one agent's worth,
which is exactly what the rho measurement that failed on main is claiming. Carries
an explicit caveat that I wrote that rho doc hours ago and am therefore primed to
see saturation everywhere today.

The third: HAR folding is structure-before-labels arriving from genomics --
function changed without the sequence meaning changing, because the spatial
arrangement moved an enhancer. Same tokens, different topology, different
behaviour. That is EVE ordering and the V8 hidden-class point.

The fourth is weak and marked weak: the energy bill (2% of mass, 20% of
metabolism) as a metering precedent.

What I would do with it: nothing yet, and that is the honest answer -- the strong
mapping corroborates a decision already taken rather than suggesting a new one.
The one testable question it raises is narrower and needs none of the biology: do
our BNNs collapse to a point estimate anywhere for convenience, and would holding
the distribution longer buy anything measurable?
