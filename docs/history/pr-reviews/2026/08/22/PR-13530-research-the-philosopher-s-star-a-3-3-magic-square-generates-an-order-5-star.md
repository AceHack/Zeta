## Description

research: the Philosopher's Star — a 3x3 magic square generates an order-5 star

Aaron ferried Gata Trigaza's "Hidden in plain sight" (Zenodo, DOI
10.5281/zenodo.7576665) asking to save the key concepts and lineage. Openly
published with a DOI, so an ordinary citation rather than an ip-questionable
ferry.

The paper is a clean dual-use instance: an alchemical reading (numerology, the
generator) wrapped around a real combinatorial result (number theory, the
conclusion). The author keeps the registers apart himself -- on whether an emblem
encodes the two magic sums he writes "We may never know."

CHECKED, not cited. The committed script builds a {5/2} pentagram from
coordinates rather than trusting a hand-drawn incidence table, then exhausts the
assignments:

  - 10 points, 5 lines of 4, every point on exactly 2 lines
  - pure 1..10 pentagram: 0 solutions at the forced sum 22 (impossibility holds)
  - 1..9 with the 5 repeated once, magic sum 20: 120 assignments
  - symmetry group order 10 (D5), every element permutes the line set
  - Lucas parametrization: 1670 distinct-entry squares, 0 violations, sum == 3*centre

One correction to the paper. It names "The Philosopher's Star" and treats its
second figure as a symmetry of the first. 120 / |D5| = 12, so there are TWELVE
inequivalent stars, and the two drawn are related by choice rather than by
symmetry. The construction is real; the definite article is not.

One anchor added: the paper says "Lucas parameterization" without citing Edouard
Lucas, Recreations mathematiques (1882-94).

What is worth taking is our own generate-from-the-irreducible rule arriving from
outside: the pentagram is not built directly, it is GENERATED from the smaller,
more constrained 3x3 square, parametrically rather than as a one-off. One scalar
(the centre cell) drives the invariant at both orders -- square sum 3c, star sum
4c. And the impossibility on 1..10 is beaten by the minimal PRINCIPLED relaxation
(one repetition, of the centre) rather than by widening the alphabet, which is
what the prior literature did.

The ECC reading of the magic constant is labelled a toy: no minimum distance
computed, no decoder.
