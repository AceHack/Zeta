## What this is

Aaron asked for two things, and the second sentence is the design brief, not a flourish:

> *"i also want a full writeup assuming no background on the subject so i can explain what is achieved to others like Max who has no concept of what the riemann hypothesis is … it would be even amazing if we could show some sort of css/gpu presentation … If we can make this transmissible in the visual cortex as well."*

Shape/visual agreement is named in `.claude/rules/anti-babel-preserve-reconcilability.md` as *"a carrier that does not route through words at all."* So the page is built as **an argument in that register**, not as an illustration of the prose one.

## Three regions, not two

The popular "verified vs unverified" split is **wrong**, and it makes the unreached third read as doubt. The paper's §1.4 is explicit: the remaining zeros are *"not shown to be off the line, merely not reached by the certificate."*

1. **proved on the line** — 2/3, grows over time
2. **proved OFF the line** — **empty. Always has been.**
3. **not reached by any certificate** — everything else

This is `.claude/rules/toy-is-free-metered-must-be-earned.md` rendered as geometry: unreached is its own state and must never read as either proved or disproved.

## How the empty region was made to read as information

This is the part that took the iterations, and the specific question asked back.

- **It is an addressed room, not blank space.** Both flanks carry a dashed border, an instrument hatch, a title, a 54px `0`, "zeros. ever.", a rule, and `detector armed 1859 · never fired`. A detector that has been on for 167 years and never fired is the loudest thing on the page.
- **It gets real estate proportional to its importance, not to its content.** The flanks are half the strip, permanently unoccupied.
- **Possibility crosses it; nothing lands in it.** Unreached zeros are drawn as horizontal bars spanning the whole strip — known height, *no committed horizontal position*, which is literally what the certificate constrains. So the flanks are **touched and never occupied**. That is the difference between "could be there" and "is there", drawn.
- **In the proportion bar it is a caliper whose jaws touch**, with a leader up to a full-size readout reading `0.0000 %` and `last changed: never`, plus a dashed ghost labelled as a scale reference showing how wide a 5 % off-line region *would* be. The apparatus is drawn at full size; the reading is zero.

## Two numbers the popular coverage gets wrong

- **2/3 is the theorem; 0.67250 is the Montgomery–Taylor *windowed* constant.** Both shown, distinguished, behind separate toggles.
- **The like-for-like jump is 25.0 points, not 25.6.** 2/3 − 5/12 = 1/4 exactly. The chart computes the delta from theorem constants only and labels it "like-for-like". 25.6 appears on the page **once**, in the sources table, identified as the mis-comparison it is (windowed new figure vs unwindowed old record).

## Honesty choices worth reviewing

- The ladder plots **only what the paper states** — Conrey at `> 2/5`, BCY at "more than 41%" from its own title. Finer constants circulate in the secondary literature (0.4077 for Conrey); they are named in the writeup as **unchecked** and are not plotted.
- **Selberg 1942 and Feng 2012** are real results whose constants the paper does not give. They are drawn as **hollow rings on the year axis with no plotted height** — plotting a number we cannot cite would be inventing one.
- **60 marks** was chosen because 60 divides by 3, 6, 12 and 5, so 1/3, 2/5, 5/12, 2/3 and 5/6 all land on a whole number of marks: counting the dots on screen returns the stated percentage.
- The **method's ceiling** (≈0.6818, §7.2, established by Easley, sharpened by McAleer) is drawn with a 16.7× inset so the 1.5 points of remaining room is visible at all.
