**The find.** *Samson Abramsky, "Relational Databases and Bell's Theorem" (LNCS 8000, 2013)* — a database instance admits a **universal relation** iff an empirical model admits a **local hidden variable model**. Same theorem, two vocabularies. Read in full; the correspondence table is quoted verbatim. It carries two dividends the CRDT framing does not: the graded **Bell < Hardy < GHZ** hierarchy of obstructions, and **Vorob'ev ⟺ database acyclicity** — a *structural* criterion for when local agreement forces global agreement, i.e. something we can carve schema/shard boundaries by.

**The correction.** The strong conversational claim splits. **⟹ is true and trivial** (it is the definition of the local polytope, not a fact about CRDTs). **⟸ is refuted** by a one-line counter-example: *"is `x` **absent** from the replicated set?"* is non-monotone — CALM says it needs coordination — yet factorises perfectly with the delivered state as λ, so it sits **inside** the local polytope and violates nothing. Locality constrains *where information came from*; monotonicity constrains *what survives more of it*. Two further quantifier mismatches are recorded (CALM is ∃-over-partitions, Bell is ∀-over-settings; Bell requires measurement independence, which replicated systems routinely violate).
