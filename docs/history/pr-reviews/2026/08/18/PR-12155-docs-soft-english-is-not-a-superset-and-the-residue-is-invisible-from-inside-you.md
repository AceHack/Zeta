## Description

docs(soft): English is not a superset — and the residue is invisible from inside your own base

Two more of Aaron's observations, and the first is the prediction confirming itself on data he
supplied independently of the algebra.

ENGLISH IS NOT A SUPERSET. §7a's "the choice of information set is the choice of source language"
could be misheard as English being the LARGEST base. It is not, and the algebra never said so —
every base has the same k. English does not obligatorily mark gender on nouns the way Spanish or
German do; Indonesian's `dia` does not split he/she/they where English forces the choice. Neither is
short of the other; their UNNAMED SETS DIFFER. That is §7b confirming itself: changing the base
changes WHICH, not HOW MANY.

CHECKED ANCHOR, and it is exactly on point — Jakobson, "On Linguistic Aspects of Translation"
(1959): languages differ essentially in WHAT THEY MUST CONVEY, not in what they may convey.
Obligatory grammatical marking IS the named set; what a language lets you leave implicit is the
residue that still acts. That his essay is about TRANSLATION is not a coincidence — same problem,
sixty years earlier, without the algebra.

GUARD, because the neighbouring overclaim is famous and wrong: this is about OBLIGATORY MARKING, not
a Whorfian claim about thought. Nothing here says a speaker cannot THINK what their language does
not force them to say. An unnamed colour still ACTS, and the algebra gives no licence for the
stronger reading.

THE DELIBERATE VERSION. Programming languages carry obligatory marking too — a language forcing
effects to be named, or relations instead of functions, or failure and supervision to be declared,
has put those in its named set; what another leaves implicit is its unnamed residue. So working
through Seven Languages in Seven Weeks (Tate, 2010) is the deliberate form of 7d's natural
experiment: seven bases, seven different unnamed sets.

AND THE LOAD-BEARING CONSEQUENCE, which is not a claim about anyone's ability: YOUR OWN UNNAMED SET
IS INVISIBLE FROM INSIDE YOUR BASE. You cannot notice what your language never makes you say —
there is no gap where the concept would be, because the concept has no slot. The only way to see it
is a second base where the same thing IS named. That is why multi-base exposure does something
deeper single-base expertise structurally cannot: it is not more resolution in one language, it is
more bases to difference against, and per §7c resolution and residue are different quantities. A
programmer with one deep language has excellent resolution and no view of their own k.

Same shape as §6b and that is the check on it: plurality cannot be verified by asking the lenses,
only by finding divergence; the residue cannot be found from within the base, only by contrast. THE
INSTRUMENT IS DIFFERENCE IN BOTH CASES — a reason to trust the pattern rather than a coincidence
between two readings of it.
