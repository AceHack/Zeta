## Description

## The headline

The brief asked for cross-repo resource/time-sharing planning, with the guard: *"different
resources have different conservation laws and a planner that conflates them will
misallocate"* and *"do not build a scheduler if the honest answer is that we do not yet know
what resource is scarce."* I took that literally and measured first.

**The obvious build — a runner-minute allocator across repos — would have rationed an
abundant resource and left the actual constraint untouched.**

| class | verdict | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **stock** (runner minutes, cache) | **not binding** | repo is public ⇒ Actions unmetered; `billable_*_ms` is `0` in **all 17** `docs/budget-history/snapshots.jsonl` rows since 2026-04-21 |
| **flow** (concurrent slots) | **not binding** | 100 runs / 10 min, 2026-08-18: queue delay **p50 = p90 = max = 0s** at 30-way concurrency |
| **mutex** (16 repo-wide singletons) | **unmeasured** | enumerated; wait-for-mutex time never measured. Says so rather than assuming it is fine |
| **window** (uninterrupted completion) | **BINDING** | see below |

## What is actually binding, and it is not a budget

Over 2h41m on 2026-08-18 the `gate` workflow — the **only** required check in the `CI Gate`
ruleset — produced **42 cancelled, 18 running, and zero conclusions**. Not one pass, not one
fail.

`gate.yml` keys concurrency per-ref with `cancel-in-progress`, so **a ref preempts itself**:

> When push interval < job duration, the ref can **never conclude — at any capacity.**
> Adding runners does not help. The work is destroyed by the lane's own next push.

| ref | median push interval | median duration | **ratio** | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `shadow/second-e8-tower-…` | 7.2 min | 9.1 min | **0.79** | 7 of 8 cancelled |
| `heartbeat/tick-metrics` | 15.3 min | 15.6 min | **0.98** | 10 of 11 cancelled |
| `heartbeat/society` | 31.2 min | 20.3 min | 1.54 | survives |

This is livelock, not starvation — and that distinction is the whole design.

## This bounds an existing repo belief rather than contradicting it

`docs/research/2026-08-14-cancelled-gate-runs-are-apt-stalls-…md` measured the same workflow
four days earlier and concluded, **correctly on that data**, that supersede-cancels were
benign — "the branch still gets a verdict". That population had **44 successes**. This one
has **zero**. Supersede-cancels are benign exactly while ratio > 1; that condition has now
been crossed. The prior doc is not wrong, it had a boundary condition it did not need to
state.

## What ships

**`resource-class.ts`** — the four classes as a closed DU, each with its conservation law
and a *witnessed* verdict. `verdictIsWitnessed` refuses a `binding` claim with no evidence,
so the evidence field cannot decay into decoration. The load-bearing consequence:

> **You can allocate a stock. You can only pace a window.**
> `isAllocatable(c) === RESOURCE_REGISTER[c].bankable`, pinned by test.

**`cadence-planner.ts`** — a pure fold over `(repo × ref)` lanes emitting `admit` / `pace` /
`hold`. No IO, no queue, no dispatch. `repo` is an **opaque string** it never interprets, so
it composes with Kenji's repo-topology work rather than pre-empting it.

Three DU choices worth defending:

- **No `defer` case.** Deferring implies the resource will still be there later — true of a
  stock, false of a window. The type does not have the case.
- **`unknown` and `incoherent` are first-class.** Fewer than three samples is not
  viable-by-default.
- **`preempted` ≠ `abandoned`**, because they differ in what they cost. This is the cut
  `SchedulerShedHeat` already makes: regenerable work is *pressure* and free; annihilated
  work is *loss* and pays. Booking preemption as backpressure is what makes a livelock look
  healthy on a dashboard.

**Livelock requires two independent signals to agree** — a rate signal (ratio < 1) and an
outcome signal (preemption streak, no conclusion). Either alone produces false positives,
and when they disagree the result is `marginal` rather than resolved by preference.

**Units are dimensionless on purpose** — ticks/ticks needs no cross-language treaty
(`MassPpm`/`TemperaturePpm` are the only metered units with an oracle). But a ratio is only
meaningful across one clock, so `tickSource` is carried and the fold **refuses** to mix two,
returning `incoherent` and producing no number at all. Mars Climate Orbiter guard, typed.

## Fairness — including what it does not give

`fairShareOrder` gives **selection fairness** over mutex-class turns (Deficit Round Robin,
the discipline already in `drain-scheduler.ts`, lifted to plan altitude; ties break on
canonical code-point order, deterministic without a seed).

> **It does not guarantee progress.** A livelocked lane starves no matter how often it is
> selected, because its work is destroyed by its own next push rather than by competition.
> **Selecting it more often is strictly worse.**

Fair share is right for `mutex` and wrong for `window`, so it is applied only to the former.
There is a test — *"selection fairness does NOT rescue a livelocked lane"* — whose entire job
is to tell a future maintainer this before they "fix" starvation the intuitive way.

## DST

Same evidence set ⇒ same plan, checked three ways: every **rotation** of the observation list
yields a byte-identical plan, **reversal** does too, and re-folding is **idempotent** (§12).

The no-clock claim is checked **mechanically** — a test reads the shipped source and fails if
`Date.now`, `new Date`, `Math.random` or `performance.now` appears outside a comment. Wall
time is not merely unused, it is **not representable in the input type**.

Register status: the *criterion* (ratio < 1 ⇒ cannot conclude) is **metered** — structural,
with the 2026-08-18 population as witness. The *thresholds* (1.25, streak 3) are judgement
calls **attributed in code** via a `ThresholdSource` DU mirroring `SimVerb.BudgetSource`, so
no bound is a hidden oracle.

## What this does not do

- **Does not act.** It emits a plan; nothing consumes it yet. Wiring is deliberately not here.
- **Does not repair the live red.** `gate` p50 is 18.7 min against heartbeat pushes every
  ~15 min. This diagnoses it; the fix is a longer push interval or a shorter gate.
- **Does not measure the `mutex` class** — named in the register as the highest-value
  follow-up, since it is the only class whose verdict is unknown.
- **The `window` class may not generalise** beyond preempt-on-newer-version schemes. That is
  a conjecture, labelled as one.
