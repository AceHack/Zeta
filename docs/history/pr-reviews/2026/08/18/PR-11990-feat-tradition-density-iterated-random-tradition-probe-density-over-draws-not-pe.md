## Description

feat(tradition-density): iterated random-tradition probe — density over draws, not per-draw confidence

Rebuild of #11687 (via #11986) as a single commit. That branch carried two AgencySignature
blocks whose `Action-Mode` fields disagreed — `human-directed` on the probe, an autonomous
value on a guard fix found on a later tick — and a squash concatenates both, so the merged
commit would have made two mutually exclusive claims about how much authority it carries.
The validator refused it, correctly. Rewriting either block in place needs a force-push,
which is not mine to take, so the branch is rebuilt instead. #11986 was the first attempt
and used the bare value `autonomous`, which is not in the enum; the validator caught that
too, which is the second time in one change that this check has been the thing that noticed.

One block, and it claims the WEAKER of the two: `autonomous-fail-closed`. The probe itself
was directed; the guard was not, and no human reviewed the combined artifact. Fail-CLOSED is
the accurate half of that pair rather than the modest-sounding one — the force-push gate is
exactly where this work stopped instead of proceeding past it, which is what the value
asserts. Under-claiming authority loses information, which is recoverable; over-claiming
manufactures a review nobody gave, which is not. This paragraph is where the detail the
field can no longer carry now lives.

THE PROBE. Draws are addressed by `(corpus, seed, iteration)` and each iteration walks its
own substream, so extending a campaign from 12 draws to 200 cannot renumber the first 12 and
iteration 7 replays without replaying 0..6. Draws are with replacement. Density is measured
over the draw sequence rather than per-draw, because a per-draw confidence would be a claim
about a single sample the sample cannot support.

THE GUARD. `uniformIndex` is exported and validated `n` but not `iteration`, so any caller
not arriving through `drawAt` had no guard — while `DrawError`'s docstring already promised
one, which made the gap read as covered. Unguarded, a fractional or negative index reaches
`BigInt(iteration + 1)` and dies with a RangeError naming BigInt rather than the caller's
mistake; an `undefined` index — forbidden by the type, permitted by the runtime, since the
annotation is erased before the call — yields `BigInt(NaN)` with nothing said about its
origin. This is what github-code-quality flagged on draw.ts:62 and :67; the finding was right
and the fix belongs at the exported boundary, not at the two arithmetic sites it surfaced
through. `drawAt` keeps its own guard: refusing before touching the corpus is a better error
than refusing four frames deeper.

Falsifiers. The guard test fails 1/13 with the guard removed and passes 13/13 with it, so it
measures the guard rather than asserting around it; its last case is the caller this surface
will meet — a JSON-parsed sheet with the field absent, not a hand-written NaN. Ledger
mutations: overwrite-instead-of-append 2 fail / 43 pass; self-report-sets-depth 3 fail /
42 pass; nulls-not-recorded 2 fail / 43 pass. Clean: 46/46.

n=14 supports nothing on its own, and the commit does not claim otherwise: both depth-2
targets carry zero self-reported "deep" marks, and every self-reported deep target sits at
depth 1.
