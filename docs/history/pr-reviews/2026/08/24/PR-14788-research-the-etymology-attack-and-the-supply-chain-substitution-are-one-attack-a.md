## Description

research: the etymology attack and the supply-chain substitution are one attack — and only a metric catches drift

Aaron 2026-08-24, on dual content addressing: "this also ties into our etymology
attacks — this is the same as a dictionary intentional drift attack."

Records the identity and the consequence neither existing rule states alone.

THE IDENTITY. Supply-chain substitution (package keeps its NAME, content is
replaced), dictionary/intentional drift (term keeps its NAME, definition is
replaced), and etymology drift (term keeps its NAME, meaning wanders) are one
mechanism: THE NAME IS STABLE, THE REFERENT IS NOT. Whether the swap arrived by a
hostile publisher, a hostile editor, or nobody at all is a question about intent,
not mechanism. So anti-babel-preserve-reconcilability (name -> meaning) and
byte-lock/golden vectors (name -> artifact) are ONE DEFENCE AT TWO LAYERS, and
neither currently cites the other.

THE NON-OBVIOUS PART, and the reason this is worth writing down: a cryptographic
address has NO DISTANCE METRIC BY CONSTRUCTION, so TO A HASH EVERY CHANGE LOOKS
MAXIMAL. A corrected typo and a wholesale replacement are equally "differs". A
hash can say the referent moved; it cannot say HOW FAR. And drift attacks are
built to exploit exactly that — substitution is discontinuous and gets caught at
review, while drift is a sequence of steps EACH OF WHICH PASSES REVIEW ALONE and
whose sum is the substitution. The defence must accumulate across steps, and a
hash is stateless between revisions. A metric address accumulates.

So the two addressings are not redundancy: exact catches substitution and is blind
to drift; distance catches drift and is blind to near-in-metric forgery. A hash
used to find "similar" always misses; a metric used to prove identity is
unfalsifiable. They compose one way — distance for recall, hash for confirmation —
which makes the repo's two existing "two-tier" documents (blake3 128-vs-256, and
the 2026-08-23 signature index) two tiers of ONE cascade rather than two schemes.

Notes that anti-babel's own falsifier is ALREADY a distance test written in prose
("hand a peer only the shared anchors and ask it to reconstruct the term"), so the
vocabulary layer has the metric defence and the artifact layer has only the exact
one. The transfer runs both ways.

Register: unmetered, and the falsifier is stated — exhibit a drift attack that a
cumulative distance-from-anchor measure cannot detect while per-change review can.
The real risk is named: embedding distance and semantic distance are not the same,
and an adversary who knows the metric optimises against it, so any implementation
must say which metric and why it resists gaming. Per numerology-vs-number-theory,
three attacks sharing a shape is a warning to check independence, not confirmation.

only-the-irreducible-is-primitive-generate-the-rest applied to derived state, and
it resolves the drift-freedom-vs-addressability tension without paying storage for
either. Aaron's zero-dynamic-allocation firmware discipline is noted as doing
