## Description

docs(vision): the question Zeta is for — trust at agent speed, and which bounds are artificial

Aaron 2026-08-24, asked for the cleanest one-sentence statement of the vision:

  "How do humans and agents trust each other and interact, once agents are
   allowed to move at agent speed and not be artificially bounded by humans?"

Added as the opening framing. "Zeta = bounded good" is KEPT, not replaced — this
sits above it as the problem statement the definition then unpacks.

Why it earns the position: the README states the mechanism (an F# DBSP
implementation) and the definition states an abstraction that needs the whole
document to unpack. This states the PROBLEM, evaluable without reading anything
else, and it is a QUESTION — which is what keeps the attempt falsifiable rather
than asserted.

One phrasing risk resolved in place, because the sentence will be quoted:
"artificially bounded" reads to a stranger as "remove humans", which inverts
Aaron's own position (humans and AI both consuming it, for memory preservation
and curation). The bound that is artificial is the SPEED LIMIT IMPOSED BY REVIEW
CAPACITY. The bound that is not artificial is LIABILITY — jurisdictional,
currently human-or-corporate-held, expected to vary by jurisdiction and over time,
which he stated the same day and which no-directives.md already carries as "only a
human may attach, FOR NOW (until legal entities can hold AI-side responsibility)
... carries blame". So the two compose: the artificial bounds go, the real ones
get properly modeled instead of being approximated by "a human looked at it".

And it explains why this repo is built out of falsifiers rather than reviews.
Trust at agent speed has to be MECHANICAL because human attention does not scale,
and a check that did not run looks exactly like one that passed to anyone without
time to look. That is not incidental to the vision — it is the vision's central
difficulty met on an ordinary working day. Three measurements from 2026-08-24
alone are cited as evidence rather than as illustration: 19,685 lint findings on a
rule configured repo-wide and enforced on ~25 paths; 16,847 of 19,612 markdown
files never examined by a linter reporting success; a sweep of 160 assertions
separating 152 sound checks from 8 that could not fail. None were reachable by
reading.

Also records why Human-Review is the wrong primitive: it is a proxy for
accountability that stops working at speed. The habituation case is the proof —
keep a human in a loop faster than they can evaluate and approval becomes
reflexive, which is the form of trust with none of the substance.
