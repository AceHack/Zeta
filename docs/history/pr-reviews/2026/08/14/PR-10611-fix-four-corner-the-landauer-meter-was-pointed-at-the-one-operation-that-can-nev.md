claim has no erasure metering, and had never identified which of its operations erase.**

## The finding: the meter was aimed at the one operation that can never pay

The chain in #10603 ends "and if the computation is reversible then Landauer is the
meter." That inverts. Landauer 1961 prices logical **irreversibility**; Bennett 1973 is
the converse — a bijection erases nothing and is free. A Landauer meter aimed at a
reversible operation is not a weak meter, it is a meter **with no signal**: it must read
zero for every input, forever.

`WSet.negate` **is** the retraction (WSet.fs: "negate — THE RETRACTION"). Measured: a
self-inverse permutation of the state space, 43/43 states distinct, **exactly 0 bits**.
The reversibility claim is *true* and carries no thermodynamic content — and that is the
structural reason a Landauer check placed there degenerates into a tautology.

## Where the erasure actually is

Exhaustive sweep, ℤ corner, keys {0,1} × weights {−1,0,1} × length 0..2 (43 states;
binary ops swept over all 1849 pairs). Bits = log₂(largest fibre) — **derived from the
operation, not asserted**:

| op | bits erased | class |
|---|---|---|
| `negate` (the retraction) | 0.000 | reversible |
| `copy`, `mapKeys id`, `apply`(injective) | 0.000 | reversible |
| `plus` (over the pair) | 1.585 | erasing |
| `bornProb` | 2.807 | erasing |
| **`consolidate`** | **3.459** | **erasing** |
| `discard` | 3.907 | erasing |
| `tensor` (over the pair) | 6.409 | erasing |

`consolidate` is the sharp case, because WSet.fs already calls it the place *"where
interference/retraction happens — opposite weights annihilate here."* It maps
`[(0,+1);(0,−1)]` and `[]` both to `[]` — two distinct states to one, **Landauer's own
example of an erasure**. The negation feeding it is free; the annihilation is what pays.
Those two are routinely read as one operation, and they are not.

## What fails when the claim stops being true

Not a counter bolted onto the hot path — an unread counter is the same defect one level
out, which is how this was found. Instead: every public `WSet` op carries a **declared**
thermodynamic class, an exhaustive sweep **measures** it, and the two must agree — plus a
reflection drift guard over `Zeta.Core.WSet`.

**Mutation results — all three planted mutants died:**

| mutant | result |
|---|---|
| `negate ... |> List.distinctBy fst` (reversible op made lossy) | **3 tests fail** |
| `consolidate := id` (erasing op made bijective) | **3 tests fail** |
| new public `WSet.truncate`, unclassified | **drift guard fails**, and names `truncate` |

The third is the one that keeps this from going stale: add a tenth operation and CI
blocks until it is classified.
