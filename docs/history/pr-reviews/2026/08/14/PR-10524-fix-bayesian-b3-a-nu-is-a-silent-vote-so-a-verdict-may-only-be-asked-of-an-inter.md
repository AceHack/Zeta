## Description

## What this is

B3(a) — the half of the double-counting work deferred until B3(b) (`Attested`,
#10474) landed. B3(b) removed **redundancy** (one source counted twice); this is
the other phenomenon, an **overconfident member annihilating a correct one**.

Reproduced live, exact configuration from the 2026-08-13 factor-graph design note:
modest (`mu=10, tau=1`) folded against overconfident (`mu=0, tau=1000`) gives joint
mean **0.009990**, precision **1001**.

## Why the obvious fix is not the fix (two new measurements)

**1. At N=2 the Student-t fold is bimodal and the wrong mode wins.** Two stationary
points, `0.000385` and `9.561575`; ranking them by likelihood does not rescue it —
the overconfident member's mode wins by **17.07 nats**, because a claimed precision
of 1000 makes its density peak `sqrt(1000)` times taller. Maximum likelihood under
a heavy tail still sides with whoever claims the most precision when nobody can
outvote them. This is exactly the reason (a) was deferred, now measured rather than
argued: robustness needs a quorum to *have* a majority, and no `nu` creates one.

**2. `nu` selects the verdict.** Five members agreeing at 10 plus one overconfident
at 0: the winner is the quorum's answer for `nu < 23.389306` and the overconfident
member's answer above it.

```
  nu =    4.0 -> quorum(10)          (theta = 9.919220)
  nu =   20.0 -> quorum(10)          (theta = 9.578799)
  nu =   25.0 -> overconfident(0)    (theta = 0.010046)
  CRITICAL nu (verdict flips): nu* = 23.389306
```

So `nu = 4.0` documented as *"robust but not too heavy-tailed"* is not a
conservative default — **it is a silent vote** by an unmeasured constant on which
member the society believes. Structurally the retired `* 1.2`.

## The change: remove the operation, don't add a field beside it

`BoundJustification`'s lesson applied to `nu`. The defect required **reading one
`nu` and returning a verdict**, so there is no such function. A verdict can only be
asked of a declared **interval**, and `Tail.declare` refuses `lo = hi` — a point
assumption is unfalsifiable; an invariance-over-an-interval claim is not.

Two publishing gates, both order-invariant (the fold sits on the `Attested`
join-semilattice, so it reads basin **sizes**, never index sets):

- **scheme-independence**, graded the way a physicist grades a regularisation-scheme
  dependence: the *systematic* (how far the answer moves across the interval) versus
  the *statistical* (the tightest standard error the fold would publish on it). Both
  in units of `theta`, so **no new free constant replaces the one removed**.
- **quorum**: the published mode must be carried by a strict majority of atoms,
  measured by basin size — not by an inlier threshold, which would be another free
  constant.

## Inferred or declared?

**Declared, with falsifiers — and route 1 wired but gated on evidence.**

`tryInfer` estimates `nu` by profile likelihood and returns an interval **only when
the boundary LRT rejects the Gaussian** (Chernoff 1954 / Self–Liang 1987: the null
`1/nu = 0` is on the boundary, so the statistic is a 50:50 chi-square mixture,
critical 2.706). Measured, 400 replicates per cell:

| nu_true | N | median nu-hat | p10 | p90 | LRT power |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 2 | ceiling | ceil | ceil | 0.000 |
| 3 | 6 | ceiling | 1.00 | 200 | 0.080 |
| 3 | 30 | 3.58 | 1.83 | 200 | 0.502 |
| 3 | 300 | 3.13 | 2.39 | 4.09 | 1.000 |
| 8 | 6 | ceiling | 1.14 | 200 | 0.040 |
| 8 | 1000 | 8.00 | 6.54 | 11.96 | 1.000 |

At N=6 the estimate spans the entire admissible range and the test has no power
over its own 5% size. Inference needs O(100) atoms; a society has O(10). So route 1
is **refused at society scale on the evidence**, and the gate opens by itself as
the atom count grows. Both routes return the same `TailInterval`, differing only in
the `Justification` register — so `isChecked` reports which you have.

## Does it fit `BoundJustification`?

**The `Justification` discriminator: yes, exactly, and it is used.** **The `Bound`
type: no.** A bound is a *sum of additive terms*; a tail index is a shape parameter
that does not decompose additively, and "widening `nu` by adding a named term" is
meaningless. Forcing it would have been rung 4 wearing a type, so it was not forced.

## What a fold over deduplicated-but-possibly-correlated atoms may claim

- **Entitled:** bounded influence *per atom* — a property of the t score function,
  holds unconditionally.
- **Entitled only conditionally:** majority-robustness, under the *additional and
  unverified* assumption that the atoms are not commonly caused.
- **Not entitled:** independence. `Deduplicated` is a bookkeeping fact, never a
  certificate of independence. **k correlated atoms are one piece of evidence
  wearing k basins**, so a basin majority is a majority of *members*, not of
  *evidence* — and under a shared hidden cause this fold converges to their answer
  **robustly**, which is worse than noisily. That half still needs an external
  observer over repeated rounds (`DecorrelationExcess`, the CHSH oracle in
  `AntiSybil`), both of which convict without ever acquitting.

## TypeScript

`nu` is now **required, first, and validated** — a tail assumption can no longer be
made by saying nothing. `tailSensitivity` is the local form of the scheme gate, and
it **declines** rather than grading when the EP variance is on its `1e-10` clamp,
which it is for small `nu` (the factor-variance update goes negative and the floor
catches it, reporting a precision of `1e10` that nothing measured).
