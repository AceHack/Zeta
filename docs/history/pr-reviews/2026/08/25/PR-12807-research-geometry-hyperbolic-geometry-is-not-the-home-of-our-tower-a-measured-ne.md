## 1. The tower is the wrong shape for the argument

Sarkar (2011) and Nickel & Kiela (2017) are about **trees with branching factor > 1**, where node count grows exponentially with depth and Euclidean space runs out of room. Both our named towers branch **not at all**:

- algebraic tower: ℝ → ℂ → ℍ → 𝕆 — a **4-node path**
- Futamura tower: interpreter → compiler → compiler-generator — a **3-node path**

A path is 0-hyperbolic (test) and embeds in R^1 isometrically (standard metric geometry, not instrumented). Distortion 1 is the theorem; the test pins only δ=0. **`tower` ≠ `hierarchy`**, and only the second is the shape the geometry argument is about.

## 2. And the graphs that DO branch are not measurably hyperbolic

## 3. The intended payoff has a better, non-geometric answer

"Routable-around (oracle) vs must-route-through (hub)" is **vertex connectivity** — Menger (1927) + Hopcroft–Tarjan articulation points, **exact, linear-time, no embedding**. A hyperbolic embedding would give an *approximate* answer where an exact one exists.

Measured: **`docs/BACKLOG.md`** (deg 1140), **`src/Core/DynamicValue.fs`** (69) and **`src/Core.TypeScript/observe/observe.ts`** (43) **are articulation points** — hubs in the strict sense of our own rule. Meanwhile the grid anchor's highest-degree node is **not** one. So **degree does not predict hub-ness**. *Whether that concentration is acceptable is an architecture call, not mine.*

## 4. What survives, theorem-level

`Isom(H^n) = O⁺(n,1)` ⇒ the usual model algebra is **`Cl(n,1)` (or `Cl(1,n)`)**; a **definite** form has no null cone, which is precisely why `Cl(3,0)` (×171) and `Cl(8,0)` (×56) cannot host it.

And the cheap consequence: `evenSubalgebraClass` already encodes `Cl⁰(p,q) ≅ Cl(p,q−1)`, so **`Cl(3,0) ≅ Cl⁰(3,1)`** — the algebra we use most **is** the rotor algebra of H³ (`Spin(3,1) ≅ SL(2,ℂ)` double-covers `PSL(2,ℂ) ≅ Isom⁺(H³)`). If we ever want hyperbolic geometry the cost is **one generator** (`e₄² = −1`), not a rewrite. `ConformalGA.fs` is already `Cl(4,1)`.

**The guard (numerology-vs-number-theory, applied to my own claim):** real dimension 16 identifies nothing — `Cl(4,0)`, `Cl(3,1)`, `Cl(2,2)`, `Cl(1,3)`, `Cl(0,4)` all have it. `Cl(1,3) ≇ Cl(3,1)` separates by **Brauer class**. But the sharper fact is **`Cl(3,1) ≅ Cl(2,2)`**: *the Morita class does not determine the signature.* So a future claim "X is M₄(ℝ), therefore X is Lorentzian" is **invalid** — `Cl(2,2)` is the counterexample.

## 5. Two bugs my own calibration anchors caught

1. **Runaway loop bound** in the CSN refinement — the condition read `bestAlpha` while the body assigned to it, so on a degenerate tail it ran to float saturation: **α = 112.25 from a grid capped at 6.0**. Degenerate tails are now refused: their MLE diverges and their **KS goes to zero**, i.e. *a fit that cannot fail* — the vacuity class. Three regression tests, each verified to fail with the bug restored.
2. **δ/diam is not a graph-vs-null comparator.** Randomising collapses the diameter (grid 56 → ~9), so my first verdict rule declared the **grid** "more hyperbolic than its null". The verdict now **refuses** when diameters differ >1.5× or when the extreme-value and bulk statistics disagree. **It refuses more often than it concludes** — which is why this is a negative rather than the positive my first pass produced.

Also corrected: I first wrote "rerunning reproduces every number exactly." **False.** The estimator replays exactly on a *fixed* graph; the input is the *live repo*. Runs are pinned to a SHA.
