## The design decisions worth reviewing

**Self-report is safe here because the prediction is decoupled from the entitlement.** You may self-claim a *date*, never a *status*. A status claim changes entitlements so it's worth gaming; a date claim changes nothing — the arithmetic threshold still decides — yet is falsifiable with a settlement time.

**Calibration is cartel-proof**, which is rare in this system. Name and privacy budget are socially conferred and therefore collusion-vulnerable. A calibration record has no counterparty to collude with; reality settles it. They must stay *distinct* scores — merged, a strong calibration record would launder a colluded attestation.

**The hat is the domain boundary.** A hat confers three things with different lifetimes: capabilities (must vanish), expectations (vanish), track record (**must persist**).

## Concrete defect found while speccing

`hatAccumulationDidNotTransfer` is correctly scoped — it inspects `availableActions` only, so a persisting record wouldn't violate it. But `unboltTaskHierarchy` does a **wholesale restore** from `base.peers`, erasing anything accrued while wearing the hat. **Hat time must live in a ledger unbolt doesn't touch.**

## TrueSkill: keep the shape, replace the likelihood

Keep: factor graph over Gaussians, EP, and especially **explicit σ** — 3 settled predictions must not rank like 300. Replace: TrueSkill's likelihood is *ordinal comparison* (zero-sum, who beat whom). Calibration is measured **against reality** — two agents can both be perfectly calibrated with no loser.

## The sign trap, flagged before building

| question | bound |
|---|---|
| what to **explore** | μ + kσ (optimistic) |
| whom to **trust** | μ − kσ (conservative) |

Inverting it would systematically **trust unproven agents more** because they're uncertain — and a fresh identity has maximal σ, so that's a sybil incentive.
