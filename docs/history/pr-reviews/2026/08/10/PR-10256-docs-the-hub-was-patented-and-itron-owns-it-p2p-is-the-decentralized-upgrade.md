## Description

**US20180109563A1 → granted US10834144B2, "Hub and Agent Communication Through a Firewall"** — inventors Aaron Stainback & Christopher Higgins, **assignee Itron Inc**, live to 2038.

The migration-operator-as-hub wasn't a metaphor this thread reached for. Aaron played the role at Whitfield's org and then **formalized it as a claimed mechanism** — and the title says *hub*. The tension recorded earlier is a granted patent with his name on it.

## What survives decentralization

- **Outbound-initiated contact** — agent dials out (WSS/443), no inbound port, no firewall change. Works peer-to-peer unchanged.
- **The closed command set** — the far side may *name* a command, never *define* one. Least-privilege at the protocol level, and it matters **more** peer-to-peer than hub-and-spoke: a gossip peer deserves exactly as little trust as a hub.

## What doesn't

The hub as **sole mediator** — the single-migration-particle shape, which fails precisely when the carrier is partitioned or captured.

## The licensing fact, which isn't philosophical

**Itron is the assignee. Inventorship conveys no license, and coworker sign-off is not assignee authority.** Citing is free (public document); practicing the claims isn't ours to choose. So the decentralized design is the *legally clean* path, not merely the manifesto-preferred one — and a genuinely P2P architecture with no mediating node doesn't read on a hub claim. *(Not legal advice; claim construction is for counsel.)*
