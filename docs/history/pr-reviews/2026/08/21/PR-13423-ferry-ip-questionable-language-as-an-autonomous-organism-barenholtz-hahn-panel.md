## The Beacon pass is the substance

Most of the panel's core **restates named prior art**, and saying so isn't a demotion — the prior work carries the counter-arguments too.

| panel claim | anchor it restates |
|---|---|
| "meaningless squiggles", ungrounded symbols | **Harnad**, *The Symbol Grounding Problem* (1990) — "ungrounded" is his word |
| language's structure suffices to generate language | **Harris** (1954), **Firth** (1957) |
| form alone can't yield meaning ⇒ LLMs don't understand | **Bender & Koller**, ACL 2020 — same conclusion, five years earlier |
| the brain is a prediction engine | **Rao & Ballard**, **Friston**, **Clark** |
| language as an organism using minds as substrate | **Deacon**, *The Symbolic Species* (1997) |
| mind as virtual machine | **Sloman & Chrisley** (2003) — Hahn's framing, already developed |

So *"obliterates 70 years of cognitive science"* overshoots: predictive processing **is** 70 years of cognitive science, and it agrees with him. **Date correction:** LSTM is Hochreiter & Schmidhuber **1997**, not 1991 (1991 is the vanishing-gradient thesis that motivated it).

**What survives as genuinely new** is narrower, and Barenholtz says it himself: not that prediction is cognition, but that long-range coherence can be **compressed into** next-token prediction — an existence proof that didn't exist before the models were built.

The corroboration doesn't share a mechanism, which is the good kind.
