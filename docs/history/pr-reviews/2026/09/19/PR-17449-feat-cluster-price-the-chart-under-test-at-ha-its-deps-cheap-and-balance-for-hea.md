## Description

The lane split was built to answer *"what is the fewest lanes this fits in"*. That is the wrong question, and answering it well is why **nothing is tested at HA today**.

## Measured over 49 charts

| rung | CPU | lanes | covered | HA? |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| everything at `dev` | 4650m | 2 | 47/49 | **no** |
| everything at `metal` | 12365m | 4 | 45/49 | yes, but 3 charts no longer fit alone |

Neither says whether a chart *works with replicas and redundancy on*, which is the only thing a deploy lane is for.

## What changed

**`priceTwoTier`** charges the **subjects** at the HA rung and everything they drag in at the cheap one. The asymmetry is the point: a lane exists to test its subjects; their dependencies only have to be present and serving, so paying HA prices for a dependency buys nothing and spends the budget that would have bought HA for a subject.

**`packBalanced`** minimises the *fullest* lane instead of the lane *count*. First-fit returned 4 lanes holding 30/2/5/9 subjects with lane-1 pinned exactly at the 2125m cap — every one of those 30 charts one replica from not fitting, and a chart is a thing that grows. Headroom is the property being optimised for, not slack left over by accident.

## Where the width comes from

Six was an estimate until now. Three measured constraints bracket it:

| | |
|---|---|
| **floor** | **4 lanes** — the budget, under the two-tier rule |
| **ceiling** | **~6** — observed concurrency (six started within a 4–5s spread) |
| **economics** | `p > 0.24` — the `F/(2F+S)` stopping rule, `F` at 55–67s |

| target | max util | headroom |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 96% | 4% |
| 5 | 78% | 22% |
| **6** | **67%** | **33%** ← the knee |
| 7 | 65% | 35% |

4→5 buys 18 points, 5→6 buys 11, 6→7 buys 2 — and 7 is past the concurrency ceiling anyway.

**Real tree at six:** 46/49 covered, lanes at 63–67%. Three named exclusions: `gitlab` (2650m) and `mimir` (2760m) are over CPU **alone**, so they want a self-hosted runner rather than a redesign; `hat-system` is unpriceable because its image is literally `...hat-system-operator:placeholder` and is currently counted as zero.

`METAL_FIRST_BOOT_BASE` is exactly the `*-install.yaml` entries metal applies at first boot — a lane without them tests a cluster shape hardware never has — kept honest by a falsifier that derives the list from the directory rather than by its comment.

## Witnessed, including one that wasn't

| mutation | result |
|---|---|
| charge a subject at `dev` when the HA catalogue lacks it | caught |
| drop a chart from the base | caught |
| replace the balance objective with first-fit | **SURVIVED** |

The balance test asserted only "lanes within 25 points" — true of both algorithms, so it witnessed nothing. Rewritten to give one lane per feasible subject and assert each gets exactly one; first-fit crams lane-1 and leaves the rest empty. Now caught. **57 pass.**

**Known and not fixed:** balance is by resource utilisation, not subject count. Lane-4 holds 23 subjects at 63% because they share a closure. Correct for fitting, unproven for wall-clock — a different objective needing a different measurement.

**Nothing is wired into CI here.** This is the pricing and packing; the matrix still comes from `packLanes`.
