## The constraint, as a falsifier rather than a promise

Every vertex of every filled region carries the index of the E8 root it is the snapped
projection of. The test asserts the eight vertex-index sets **are** the eight orbits of
the bipartite Coxeter element, together accounting for all 240 roots. A hand-placed
vertex has no root index and fails. Mutant M2 injects exactly one and the provenance test
dies.

The regions need no hull algorithm: rung 3 measured each ring to be one Coxeter orbit at
equal angular spacing `2π/30`, so each is a regular 30-gon and sorting by angle is the
entire step.

## Where the irrational is allowed in

`src/Core/Tsirelson.fs` keeps `S² = 8` in integers so the irrational appears only at
**readout**. A renderer runs the other way, so the boundary is stated the other way:

> **The irrational appears only at INTAKE.**

One `snap` converts the eigen-derived projection to lattice integers; everything
downstream is integer with no tolerance. Coverage is the sign of a grade-2 blade, area is
its magnitude — the same outer product that generates the roots. The exactness claim is
**bounded** (`2^20`, eleven bits under the `2^53` ceiling) and the bound **refuses**
rather than degrades. The scale floor is measured, not decorative: the smallest ring
loses convexity at scale 64.

## Falsifiers and measured numbers

Measured at `PROOF_LATTICE_SCALE = 4096`:

| ring | 2·area | B | I | covered | Pick residual |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | 23,757,568 | 972 | 11,878,299 | 11,878,784 | 0 |
| 7 | 543,567,036 | 3,944 | 271,781,547 | 271,783,518 | 0 |

(all eight rows are in the doc)

- **Pick's theorem (1899)** residual exactly `0` on all 8 regions and all 224 fan
  triangles, computed from three unrelated routes — a shoelace blade sum, the
  rasterizer's own interior classification, and a gcd edge count. An *external* theorem,
  not a program compared with itself.
- **The partition.** 240 distinct triangulations (8 regions × 30 apex choices) each yield
  the identical count of the identical point set, **0 overlaps and 0 cracks** — the
  oldest rasterization bug caught in both directions, counted separately so neither hides
  behind the other. The apex is a gauge, as rung 3's z-axis was, so "it does not matter"
  is asserted, not assumed.
- **The fill rule is exclusive** over all 6,560 direction pairs in ±40.
- **Exact translation invariance** at 1,038,576 lattice units (DST with teeth, rather
  than `f(x) = f(x)`).
- **Two refusals** — the exact bound and the scale floor — each killed by disabling
  exactly it.
- **Noninterference** by source scan with comments stripped and the call form matched.

**Coverage equals area exactly** on all eight regions. Recorded as a *measurement*, not
promoted to a law: the same test exhibits the unit triangle, `2A = 1` and coverage `0`.

## On the Variational Stokes anchor

Larionov, Batty & Bridson (2017), *Variational Stokes: A Unified Pressure-Viscosity
Solver for Accurate Viscous Liquids*, ACM TOG 36(4):101, doi
[10.1145/3072959.3073628](https://doi.org/10.1145/3072959.3073628) — **cited as an anchor
and a comparison point, not reimplemented.** Its discretization is a staggered grid, and a
grid is an authored discretization, which is precisely what Aaron's constraint forbids. So
porting it is not a smaller version of this project; it is a different one.

The genuine connection is its own sentence: the method *"treats difficult boundary
conditions naturally through simple volume weights"* — a per-cell **coverage measure**,
and the quantity whose inaccuracy it blames for wrong free-surface behaviour. Rung 4
builds an exact one. The gap is the derived discretization that does not exist yet, plus
the step from an integer count to a rational fraction. **No solver is implemented; that
paragraph is `unmetered`.**

I worked from the paper's published abstract and metadata (read this session at the
UWSpace record). I did not read `github.com/elrnv/stokes-houdini` — someone else's
implementation we hold no rights to.

**Prior art split honestly.** Geometric algebra for graphics is decades old and is cited,
not claimed: Dorst, Fontijne & Mann (2007); edge functions are Pineda (1988); the lattice
identity is Pick (1899); the E8-from-Clifford lineage is Dechant (2016/2017), already
anchored by `src/Core/CliffordE8Roots.fs`. Four searches turned up nothing on the
*combination* — exact integer coverage over geometry admissible only because the algebra
generated it. That is recorded as a **negative search result at four queries**, not a
novelty claim.
