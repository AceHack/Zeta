## Description

Aaron challenged the representation layer for the combined graphics/physics engine, then upgraded the ask: *"route the PGA evaluation and let's see the numbers."* `Cl(3,0,1)` is implemented far enough to **measure** against the three rivals a real engine picks between.

## The indictment is right; its stated mechanism is not

> *"if we are paying for extra dimension and then projecting down that's going to be slow"*

The projection is **one-time at load**. Per frame it costs nothing — the browser animates the already-projected 3D result with an ordinary camera. What costs is **the surface the projection produces**, and that is a theorem rather than a defect:

> A 2-complex is in general position — embedded, no self-intersection — only in dimension **2k+1 = 5**. Three is below five. Self-intersection is the generic outcome of *every* rank-3 map. (Whitney 1936.)

An independently written implementation reproduces rung 7's **738** overdraw and **29.3%** bbox exactly, and the best of 24 seeded random frames buys **1.16×**. A sibling task measured the same over 116 embeddings: span 598–834.

## Per vertex, a PGA motor and a quaternion are the same computation

Derived before measured. Symbolic expansion of `M X ~M` gives `e123 = s² + e23² + e31² + e12²` — the **rotor norm alone** — with the result degree-1 in the point. Linear-plus-constant is affine, its linear part is the rotor, so after a once-per-motor decomposition the degenerate half does no per-vertex work.

Motors **are** dual quaternions (Clifford 1873, Study 1903), and rotors in 3D GA **are** quaternions. So "should we use quaternions" resolves to *we already do* — and PGA earns its place everywhere except the inner loop: one object for rotation and translation, 8 floats vs 16, uniform join/meet, screw motion for the physics half.

## Exactness survives, and the boundary improves

`Z` throughout rigid motion, **zero square roots**, one division at homogeneous readout — a projective algebra never has to normalise. Named cost, measured: **~6.1 bits of coefficient growth per composition**, linear, so renormalisation is a policy question rather than a blocker.

## Aaron's "different dimensions for different parts" — measured, and the cost is CONSERVED

| parts | per-part overdraw (own frame) | **SUM** |

Per-part cost falls only because each part holds 1/512 of the triangles. **The ink is conserved.** And parts in different 3D subspaces have no comparable depth, so it yields N pictures, not one scene. It is a legitimate *visualisation* technique — which is what the Coxeter plane and the eigenlayer tessellation already are — and not a route to an occlusion-correct scene.

## The verdict

**PGA is a performance and uniformity answer, never a correctness one.** 27 faces per edge is computed from the combinatorial face list with no coordinates in it — no algebra can alter it, and the surface stays two-sided under every representation.

The two jobs separate cleanly: **PGA `Cl(3,0,1)` for the engine**, **E8/`Cl(8)` still the candidate for cross-domain reasoning** (240 directions, `696,729,600`-order Weyl group). They compose cheaply — the in-tree finding is that all three GA towers reduce to `Cl(3,0)`. The real cost of separating is that the engine then has no content: a 3D-native derived source is needed, and the strongest candidate is already anchored in-tree (H3 in `Cl(3,0)`; Dechant's chain runs rank-3 → rank-4 → E8, so rendering should read the generator at rank 3).

**0 bytes of geometry survives** — the property is derivation-not-output and does not depend on the content being 8-dimensional; an H3 seed is *smaller*. **Derived-not-authored survives** — PGA is a Clifford algebra and H3 roots come from versor reflection.

## Exactness survives at rank 3 — run, not argued

The `toy` claim that the 8D's exactness comes from the roots being *algebraic integers*, not from the dimension, is now half-measured. H3 — the icosahedral Coxeter group, and the rank-3 generator of the chain that produces E8 — built in `Z[φ]` and closed under its own reflections in exact `bigint`:

| measured | H3 (rank 3, `Z[φ]`) | E8 (rank 8, doubled `Z`) |
|---|---|---|
| roots | **30**, closure adds none | 240 |
| distinct squared norms | **one class — the rational integer 4** (`φ` component exactly zero) | one class: 8 |
| reflections leaving the ring | **zero** | zero |
| rank of span | **3 — no projection anywhere** | 8, then projected |

`1 + φ² + (φ−1)² = 4` under `φ² = φ + 1`, so every root has the same rational-integer length and `x − ((x·α)/2)·α` lands back in the ring on every reflection. Same property E8 has, one ring over, in three dimensions. The test carries a control that fails on a root of the wrong norm.

**Still `toy`, narrowed:** whether H3-derived geometry reproduces rung 6's exact *shading*. This measured the ring, not the light.

## Honest limits

Single-threaded scalar JS; no SIMD, no GPU. Nothing here implements an engine, no dynamics, no solver. The H3 claim is registered `toy` with a named falsifier — argued, not run. The fixed-point/rational/float decision for time integration is unaddressed and is the first wall an engine proposal hits. Several citations are carried from in-tree documents that recorded their own verification state; I did not open those papers and the doc says which.

**Recorded against myself:** the first unrolled fast path had a sign error in the rotor-to-quaternion map, and the benchmark had already produced plausible timings with it. Only the oracle-agreement test stood between that and this document. The correct convention was then *measured* — the right assignment agreed to `3.8e-15`, the nearest rival was wrong by `3.2`.
