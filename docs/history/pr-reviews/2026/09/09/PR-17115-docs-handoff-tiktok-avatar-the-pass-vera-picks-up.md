## Description

docs(handoff): TikTok avatar — the pass Vera picks up

Aaron asked for a writeup to hand to Vera, and has installed Effect House.

THE SHORT ANSWER TO HIS QUESTION: yes, face rigging is possible, and it is the
thing Effect House is built for -- he is right that it is in their samples. Face
tracking driving blendshapes on a 3D head is the core supported case, runs
entirely on-device, and none of the network limits touch it. The blocker was
never capability.

His "we don't need two characters at once" removes the binding budget case: two
4.3 MB meshes would be 8.6 MB with zero textures and do not fit; one leaves
3.7-6.5 MB for everything else.

WHAT THE HANDOFF CARRIES, with status on every claim:

  * VERIFIED -- ARKit-52 blendshapes by exact name (the most favourable fact
    here: an ARKit-authored head binds with no remapping; Snap has 51 in
    PascalCase); the 8 MB package limit on two pages; visible per-effect refusal
    on unsupported devices ("This effect doesn't work with this device") with a
    WebGL floor and no published OS minimum; no account or region gate.
  * SETTLED from the dated release notes -- 60,000 triangles is a PER-MESH cap
    for blendshape meshes (v4.2.0), and bones are 100 not 50 (v3.7.0). Carried
    with the trap: NEITHER official page is uniformly authoritative. Technical
    Optimization is right on triangles and stale by two and a half years on
    bones, and it is the page the in-tool test links to on failure.
  * INFERRED -- no runtime network beyond CloudDataManager's 1 KB cap, so no
    live-progress plugin. Stated as an inference from a complete API
    enumeration, not an explicit prohibition.

THE ARITHMETIC THAT REDIRECTS THE WORK: a 60k-triangle mesh with 52 ARKit targets
carries ~18.7 MB of morph deltas alone, over twice the whole budget. So the
triangle cap is UNREACHABLE and irrelevant; the real ceiling is ~4-5k vertices on
the blendshape-bearing mesh. The lever is splitting head from body -- only the
head needs blendshapes -- and that lever is legal ONLY because the cap is
per-mesh, which is what the research settled.

FLAGGED FOR VERA AS EXPECTED-BUT-NOT-VERIFIED: her rigged GLBs are skeleton-
rigged, which drives body motion; face tracking needs ARKit-52 morph targets,
which a MakeHuman-derived head will not have unless authored. I did not open the
files and say so.

THE FORK IS AARON'S AND THE HANDOFF REFUSES TO RESOLVE IT BY BUILDING: there is
no Clifford derivation of jawOpen. Option A is substrate-pure (the E8 figure on
the derived Coxeter flow, 100% generated, 11,854-byte OBJ = 0.14% of budget) and
is NOT a likeness; Option B is a likeness and off-substrate by construction.

Also carries the one experiment worth doing first (import the rung-3 OBJ, read
one blendshape coefficient -- collapses four unknowns), the note that the
documented MorpherComponent gap may sit on a path we never take since the editor
graph can bind the driver directly, and a policy question that is cheap now and
expensive late: avatar-driven-by-your-own-face is clean, likeness applied to
OTHER users' faces is not.

Honest limit stated in the doc: nothing here was measured by running Effect
House. No install, no build, no submission.
