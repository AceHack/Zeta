## Description

> Stacked on #17174 (the Constraint-existence check). Merge that first.

## The finding

Six of the seven Hat ConstraintTemplates are **referential**: their Rego reads other cluster objects out of `data.inventory` rather than deciding from the admission review alone. Gatekeeper populates that inventory **only** for kinds named in a `config.gatekeeper.sh` Config's `spec.sync.syncOnly`.

**No such Config existed anywhere in the tree, and the gatekeeper chart does not create one.**

So the inventory was empty, every comprehension over it produced the empty list, and every `count(...) >= threshold` compared **0 against a positive number**. Six policies that compiled, applied, and **could not deny anything on any input**:

`01-cooldown` · `02-max-bindings` · `03-conflict-of-interest` · `04-quorum` · `06-max-new-hats` · `07-no-supervisor-cycles`

Only `05-warmup` is unaffected — it decides from the admission review alone.

**The policies were not wrong.** Their Rego is correct and their inventory paths match the CRD scopes exactly: `Hat` is `scope: Cluster` and is read from `data.inventory.cluster`; `HatBinding` and `HatSwap` are `scope: Namespaced` and are read from `data.inventory.namespace[ns]`. The data they query was simply never delivered.

## Three layers of one vacuity — and every one reconciles perfectly

| layer | caught by | when |
|---|---|---|
| the template did not **compile** | "Gatekeeper compiled every ConstraintTemplate" | earlier today |
| the Constraint was never **applied** | "Every Hat Constraint is applied" | #17174 |
| the Constraint **enforces nothing** | **this PR** | nothing could see it |

This is the whole argument for post-deploy assertions over reconciliation verdicts. `Synced + Healthy` is ArgoCD's opinion about **reconciliation** — and a policy engine holding seven policies that cannot fire is exactly what perfect reconciliation looks like.

## The fix

One object: a Gatekeeper `Config` syncing `Hat`, `HatBinding`, `HatSwap`.

`HatPolicy` is **deliberately absent** — no template queries it, and `syncOnly` is an allowlist whose breadth is a real memory cost on gatekeeper's audit pod.

## The falsifier

`referentialFailures` ties the two halves together in **both directions on three axes**, because each is separately satisfiable while the system does nothing:

1. a kind the Rego **reads** that no `syncOnly` entry populates — **the defect**;
2. a kind `syncOnly` **caches** that no Rego reads — memory paid for nothing, and a cached kind reads as evidence a policy is wired up when nothing queries it;
3. a read whose inventory **scope** disagrees with the CRD scope — well-formed, wrong sub-tree, forever empty. A third silent way to read nothing, knowable from the text.

The scope map is derived from the committed CRDs and the `syncOnly` expectation from the committed Config, so an eighth referential policy cannot land without either extending the Config or turning this red.

## Plus a live post-deploy assertion

The static check cannot see one thing: whether the Config the tree declares actually **reached the cluster**. ArgoCD gives a `Config` no health, exactly like a `Constraint`, so `hat-system` stays `Healthy` without it.

The new step derives its expected kinds from the committed Config (a hardcoded list of three would keep passing when a seventh policy adds a fourth) and **refuses an empty expectation** rather than comparing against it trivially.
