## Description

## What this is

An assessment of **3DGUT** (Wu, Martinez Esturo, Mirzaei, Moenne-Loccoz & Gojcic, 2025) as a transfer candidate for the Clifford/E8 rendering ladder — and a **decline**, backed by a measurement rather than an opinion.

Aaron's framing: *"we may be able to pull in some of our bayesian stuff and learn from this paper ... we have a lot of advanced bayesian inference stuff that ties directly to our clifford too."*

## The load-bearing result is negative

Zeta's 8D→3D/2D projection is a **linear map** — dot products against orthonormal Coxeter-plane / eigenplane frames (`projectRoots`, `embed3d`). For a linear map the Unscented Transform and the EWA linearisation it replaces are provably and measurably **identical**, because linearising something already linear discards nothing.

Measured on the real 240 E8 roots:

| quantity | measured |
|---|---|
| worst \|mean\| discrepancy, UT vs EWA | **6.661e-16** |
| worst \|cov\| discrepancy, UT vs EWA | **7.105e-15** |

Machine epsilon. And it holds across **every** UT parameterisation, so it cannot be tuned into a benefit.

The boundary where the UT *does* pay, measured against a 200k-sample deterministic Monte-Carlo reference:

| projection | KL(UT) | KL(EWA) | EWA/UT |
|---|---|---|---|
| pinhole f=500 | 3.90e-5 | 5.94e-5 | **1.52x** |
| fisheye f=300, off-axis | 2.66e-4 | 1.71e-3 | **6.43x** |

This reproduces the paper's own qualitative finding — and our EWA baseline is **harsher than theirs**: they compare against a Jacobian of the wrong map, we give EWA an exact numerical Jacobian of the map under test.

## Three further findings

- **The half that would deliver reflections is not the Bayesian half.** Primary rays are splatted; secondary rays are **traced by 3DGRT**. The paper's reflective sphere is an *inserted synthetic object* — inadmissible under *"if we cant generate it from clifford that's not our research"*.
- **The paper's declared limitation is our geometry.** §7: *"unable to render overlapping Gaussians accurately."* We have 27 incident 2-faces per edge. A particle representation would also delete nearest-hit occlusion, which is the only reason rung 7 renders at all — depth complexity is a property of the geometry, not the primitive.
- **A fitted Gaussian has no E8 root index**, so it fails the provenance test before any question of exactness arises. Fitting is the inverse of deriving.

## On UT ↔ EP

The bridge is **real but narrower** than "both match moments": ADF is the common parent (EP is ADF iterated — Minka 2001; the UKF is ADF with unscented quadrature). Five invariants separate them, tabulated in the doc. It is **currently unpaid** — this repo's probit site has an exact closed form (GPML 3.58), so sigma-point quadrature there would be a downgrade.

3DGUT uses a Bayesian-derived algorithm in a thoroughly **non-Bayesian** way: the Gaussian is a shape primitive, not a belief.

The **Clifford tie is the strongest part of the instinct** and is structural, not numerological: an anisotropic Gaussian *is* a versor-transformed ball, and sigma points are versor-transformed frame axes. Operationally inert here (linear projection), but it names the one genuinely live direction — what it would mean for our geometry to carry uncertainty. Registered `toy`.
