READING IT CORRECTED ME TWICE, which is why the ferry is worth its length.

1. MY SURVIVORSHIP-BIAS CRITIQUE WAS RIGHT BUT MISLOCATED. I implied an oversight.
   It is a declared constraint: §3.1 builds the carrier on `D+`, and A.18 says
   "failed trajectories are excluded from training (hard project constraint)". The
   sharper form survives -- a hole means "no SUCCESSFUL trajectory went here", which
   conflates "no solution exists" with "nobody tried" -- and that is precisely where
   Zeta differs, because this repo KEEPS its failures. That is now the lane's only
   original hypothesis; the rest is application.

2. THE WINDING NUMBER IS DEPLOYED, AND ITS RESULT IS A NULL. I said "they compute
   what we assert". They do more: A.12 runs it as a route lock in the decision loop.
   It works exactly as designed -- ZERO route switches on every seed of every task,
   against the geometric lock's 17 and 26 -- and buys NO success-rate improvement
   (94.8 vs 96.4, p=0.46). They keep the cheaper geometric lock and say so. That null
   is worth more to us than a win: our monodromy clause makes the same commitment for
   the same structural reason, so this is the first evidence about what it costs and
   what it buys, and it argues against expecting a performance win from formalising it.

onto the Eikonal equation, which makes three mutually conflicting regression targets.

properties of one object, and the paper's own words are "the Eikonal constraint is
thereby converted from a regression target into an identity."

borrowed it from the other, which is the interesting part.
