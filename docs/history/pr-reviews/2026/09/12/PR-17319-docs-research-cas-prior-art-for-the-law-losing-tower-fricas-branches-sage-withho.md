## Description

## What Aaron asked

> "can we look up **CAS** (computer algebra systems) from calculators and try to see **what humans think about this**, or is that system too simple?"

"This" = work item `081M29N1AX9087G0R001GSJD6G`: `IRing<Sedenion>` compiles with zero compiler objection and is not a ring. `IStarRing.cs` discloses the gap in prose — *"law profile carried as documentation, not types."*

## Verdict

**Both halves of the prior were right, and the split is sharper than expected.** Pocket calculator CAS is too simple — the structural question does not arise, because a TI-89 or HP-50g models *expressions and polynomials over a field*, never *a structure with laws*. **Research CAS is a direct hit, and three systems solved this independently.**

## The structural answer

FriCAS/Axiom, read from `src/algebra/catdef.spad`:

```spad
Algebra(R : CommutativeRing) : Category ==
  Join(Ring, NonAssociativeAlgebra(R)) with
```

**The associative algebra is the DESCENDANT of the non-associative one.** `QuaternionCategory` joins `Algebra R`; `OctonionCategory` joins `FramedNonAssociativeAlgebra(R), NonAssociativeRing`. So the lattice *branches*, and losing a law is **moving toward the root** — which subtyping expresses natively. The two arrows in the problem statement are not opposed; the algebra chain was drawn upside down. That insight costs no new mechanism.

## The refutation half, which matters as much

`SemiGroup() : Category == Magma` — **identically**. The whole difference between "has a binary operation" and "has an *associative* binary operation" is the category's name and a `++` comment. Sage's own docs say an axiom's semantics are *"specified in the documentation."* Both are the disclosure `IStarRing.cs` already makes, reached independently by purpose-built algebraic type systems. That is evidence this is a hard boundary, not a Zeta oversight.

Each pays for it with a **shipped falsifier**: FriCAS `associative?()` and GAP `IsAssociative` both enumerate triples and check the associator — which is the measurement this work item independently reproduced 35 years later.

## Sage is the cleanest existing answer, in one line

```python
cat = MagmaticAlgebras(R.category()).Unital().WithBasis().FiniteDimensional()
```

Capabilities added; `.Associative()` sitting right there and **deliberately withheld**. Limits verified in source: **no `Alternative` axiom exists** in `all_axioms`, so the octonions' positive law is inexpressible there — while `NoZeroDivisors` **is** present, so the sedenion step has a name and the octonion step does not.

## What does NOT transfer

Stated explicitly, because "someone solved this" is not "we can use their solution": SPAD's compile-time category checking, its conditional category membership (`if R has OrderedSet then ...`), and Sage's runtime axiom algebra have **no F#/C# equivalent**. And **no surveyed system models sedenions at all** — Zeta is past the prior art at the top of its own tower.

What does transfer: FriCAS's `unitsKnown`, a **nullary marker category** joined into `Ring`, which is a members-free marker interface in C#; plus the shipped falsifier that keeps such a marker from being unmetered. Both are proposals, marked `toy`, with the combinatorial risk named.
