## The premise expired before anyone acted on it

The draft's headline was that the two archive trees are **64% of Zeta's 30-day accretion**. That window ended 2026-09-06 and is almost entirely *before* the 2026-08-29 batch disable of the cadence workflows:

| window | files/day into the archive trees |
|---|---:|
| 2026-08-12 → 08-29 (before the pause) | **1,974** |
| 2026-08-29 → 09-11 (after) | **120** |

Sixteen-fold, on that date. **The draft isn't wrong — it accurately described a regime that ended a week before it was read.** The header now says so, and tells a future reader to re-measure rather than revive.

Second correction recorded there, from the same day's growth-register work: **rewrite count is a poor proxy for on-disk cost.** `data/tick-history.json` has 1,090 versions summing to 189.3 MB raw and occupies **241 KB on disk** — 805×, because git delta-compresses canonically-ordered appends. What survives is the **file count** (28,757), a tree/scan cost rather than a byte cost — which #17283/#17285 addressed directly with dated buckets.

## The docstring

`pr-manifest-shards.ts:74` said `pr-reviews` *"already holds ~6000 in ONE flat directory."* It held 14,378, and it is no longer flat. The paragraph above it also argues **date directories are wrong for this data**, which now reads as contradicting its sibling store.

It isn't a contradiction, and the docstring now says why — the two stores are keyed differently **because they answer different questions**:

| store | key | why |
|---|---|---|
| shards | **PR number** | path computable from the number alone → lookup is one O(1) read; this is what `resolveArchivePath` uses |
| records | **merge date** | a merged PR *is* an event with a timestamp — the exact condition that paragraph names as justifying dates |

Date buckets on the shards would destroy the O(1) lookup; number buckets on the records would bound fan-out without recording when anything happened. Keeping both keys is the design, and the resolver is the bridge.
