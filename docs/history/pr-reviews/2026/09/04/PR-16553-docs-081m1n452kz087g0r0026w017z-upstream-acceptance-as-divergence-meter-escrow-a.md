## Description

Aaron 2026-09-03, on the whole k8s stack: replace it with our own over time, but *"only the community will decide if our version is better"* — until they do, support **both**, contribute upstream what we learn, and treat PR rejection (after earning credit) as the signal for where our product is most needed.

## The divergence meter, and why the control is the whole idea

The naive form — *they rejected a PR, so fork* — measures nothing. Every project rejects PRs, and most rejections are scope, bandwidth, style or timing. **"After earning credit" is a control**, and it separates two hypotheses a raw count cannot:

| | reads as |
| --- | --- |
| rejected, no track record | **we are strangers** |
| rejected, with a record of accepted fixes | **we want different things** |

Standing conferred by an outside community is the strongest form available here — a fleet that rated its own alignment with upstream would be measuring nothing.

### One sharpening, flagged as mine

**Grade by change class, or the meter fires on every healthy project.** Accepting small fixes and declining architectural changes is the *normal* state of every mature upstream, so an ungraded meter reports divergence from Kubernetes, from Linux, from everything — and a meter that fires on everything discriminates nothing.

| small fixes | architectural | reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| accepted | accepted | aligned |
| accepted | declined | **normal** — a project with a scope |
| accepted | declined *naming our requirement as out of scope* | **divergence, and located** |
| declined | declined | strangers, or bad citizens — fix that first |

Only the third row is actionable, and it needs **the stated reason**, not just the outcome. That's what locates *which part of the stack* the product is for.

And it stays a **meter**: report `ArchitecturalChangeDeclined(reason)`, never `ForkJustified`. Two parties reading the same history must be able to disagree, or an oracle got in upstream of the measurement.

### It's the ρ band, run against humans

`anti-babel`'s falsifier is *"can a diverged peer reconstruct your meaning from anchors you both already hold?"* **An upstream PR is exactly that test, executed against a human community, with a recorded verdict.** Merged = reconcilable. Declined naming our requirement = diverged, and locatable. Declined with no engagement = we failed to make ourselves reconstructible — our defect, not theirs.

## Escrow is `clone-at-tag-stays-sufficient` pointed outward

The threat model is broader than the name. Nation-state takedown, a maintainer deleting a package, a registry unpublishing, a licence change, an acquisition, a repo going private, a CDN that stops — **the events differ and the recovery is identical.**

| have | without it you have |
| --- | --- |
| source at the pinned revision | a binary you cannot patch |
| the transitive dependency closure | a build that stops at the first missing edge |
| the build toolchain, pinned | source you cannot turn into the artifact |
| **a reproduced build, not a stored one** | **a claim that you could rebuild it** |
| the licence, recorded | bytes you may not be allowed to use |

The fourth row decides whether this is real. **An escrow nobody has rebuilt from is the storage form of a check that cannot fail.** So the deliverable is a mirror *plus* a periodic reproduce-from-escrow run that's loud when it fails.

**The honest limit, on the page:** escrow preserves the **code**, never the **community**. Hold every byte of Kubernetes and you still don't have the people who review its security patches. It buys the ability to keep running and to fork under duress — not maintenance.

## Fork ratings need several oracles

All three of your constraints are already carved rules:

- **"not just one but a few"** — a single rating authority is an appointed hub at the package layer. That's what download counts on one registry already are: one number, one authority, a popularity contest standing in for a judgement.
- **"boring metrics we can all agree on mostly"** — the good-meter test verbatim: anyone can inspect it and agree to the rules *in advance*, not after seeing the result.
- **"canonical vs experimenting"** is a **reading**, and the metrics must support both without preferring either:

| release cadence | change-failure / revert rate | honest reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| low | low | **canonical** — mature, or finished |
| high | high | **experimenting** — moving fast, and saying so |
| high | low | unusually good, or under-measured |
| low | high | **abandoned or in trouble** |

No cell says "better". Picking the experimental fork on purpose is a choice, not a mistake.

DORA analogues for a fork (release cadence, issue-to-release, revert rate, CVE-to-patch) anchored to *Accelerate* (2018), with the borrowing stated precisely: the **shape** transfers; the original validation was about delivery teams and does not transfer by assertion. `dora-metrics.ts` already folds DORA over work-item events — it measures **us**; measuring a third-party fork is the increment.

## Open, named rather than hand-waved

- **Who runs the oracles.** All hosted by one party and the plurality is decorative.
- **Goodhart.** Release cadence is trivial to inflate; deriving from public artifacts is a likely mitigation, not yet a design.
- **Whether upstream engagement scales to an agent fleet** — genuinely unknown, and upstream's call, not ours.
- **What "the community decides" means operationally** — and it shouldn't be settled by us alone, since that would be scoring our own exam.
