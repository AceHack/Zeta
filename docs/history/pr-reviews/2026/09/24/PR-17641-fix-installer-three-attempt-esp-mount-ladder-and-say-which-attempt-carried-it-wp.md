## Description

Third on `081M39CJP96087G0R001T4J2R3`, after #17638 (capture the mount error) and #17640 (log the ESP offset and its provenance). **This is a mitigation, not a fix** — it explains nothing and the work item stays open.

## Why three attempts

blkid parses the FAT superblock in **userspace**; `mount -t vfat` additionally needs the **kernel driver and its NLS charset modules**. "Label readable, mount refused" — which is what run 36044770870's guest reported for *every* candidate, `/dev/disk/by-label/EFIBOOT` included — is the signature of a kernel-side capability problem rather than a data problem. That reading is what survives every measurement taken so far:

- both ISOs put the ESP at **LBA 268** (#17640);
- the pre-WP29 detector resolves **137 216 through the MBR branch** on both;
- replaying the bake on the failing ISO yields a clean, mountable, byte-exact ESP (#17638).

The bytes were fine. Only the *first* attempt can be defeated by the kernel being unable to do the thing.

Two causes fit, and the ladder tells them apart on sight:

| cause | what you will see |
|---|---|
| NLS charset unavailable | `FAT-fs: IO charset iso8859-1 not found` / `codepage cp437 not found`, as `-EINVAL`. **Attempt 3 names a charset explicitly and may succeed**, turning a lost install into a completed one plus a diagnostic. |
| device-level read error | all three fail, each with the kernel's own words. Three errors instead of one is strictly more information. |

## The guard on attempt 2

Attempt 2 drops `-t vfat` and lets the kernel autodetect — which makes it the **only** attempt that could mount something that is *not* FAT, since these probes walk iso9660 partitions too. So its success is accepted only after `findmnt` confirms `vfat`/`msdos`; an unconfirmable mount is unmounted and counted as a failure. A probe that read a firstboot conf off the wrong filesystem would be a worse bug than the one being mitigated.

## Applied to all three read-only FAT mount sites

`zeta_pf_probe_esp`, the iter-4.2 pubkey probe, and the iter-5.2 / iter-5-wifi re-mount of the remembered ESP — plus the sibling ladder in `zeta-first-boot.sh`. iter-4.2 mounting via the mitigation while a later reader still gave up on attempt 1 would produce exactly the split-brain this work item is about: one reader finds the ESP, the next reports `(no-vfat)`. The `rw` ledger mount is untouched — different mode, different question.

**And it says when it fires.** `via=vfat` is the healthy shape; anything else prints a NOTE naming the attempt that worked and the refusals before it, so a run the mitigation carried is never mistaken for a healthy one.

## Falsifiers

`src/Core.TypeScript/zflash/esp-mount-ladder.test.ts` extracts the **shipped** shell functions and drives them under bash with stubbed `mount` / `findmnt` / `umount`:

- attempt-1 success issues exactly one mount, sets `via=vfat`, records nothing;
- attempt-1 refused + autodetected FAT mounts, and the refusal is **kept**, and `via` is *not* `vfat`;
- an autodetected **iso9660** is unmounted and refused;
- an **unconfirmable** type is refused rather than trusted;
- the charset attempt is last and byte-exact;
- all-three-refused names every attempt and the reason stays **space-free** — `espConfScanOutcome` parses `tried=(\S*)`, so a single space would truncate the field and lose the rest of the device list;
- a stderr-less failure still records something falsifiable;
- the two scripts' ladders must not diverge.

The extractor **throws** rather than returning an empty string, so the suite cannot quietly pass against nothing.
