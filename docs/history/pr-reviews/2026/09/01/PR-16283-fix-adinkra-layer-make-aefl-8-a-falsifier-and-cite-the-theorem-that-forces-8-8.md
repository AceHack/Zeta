## Description

Acting on two independent adversarial reviews of #16274, neither by its author.

## AEFL-8 was the vacuity class — and worse than the review found

Its strongest assertion was `Assert.Contains("independent Gaussian roots", descriptor.Exactness)` — string-matching a prose literal against **the same literal hardcoded in the code**. A claim of exactness tested in place of an exactness checked.

Reading the function to fix that surfaced the sharper problem: **`tryToFactorDagLayer` ignored its `sectorized` argument entirely.** Every field was a constant of `variableBase`, including a hardcoded eight `"+"` then eight `"-"`. The descriptor was identical for a correct sectorization, a swapped one, or any other — so **no sectorization defect could make that test fail, whatever it asserted.**

## The fix

The descriptor now **derives** from the sectorization: `SectorLabels` reports the measured eigenvalue of the feature actually routed to each node, and `PlusInputs`/`MinusInputs` carry the original indices so a consumer can check *where* a belief landed instead of trusting that the plus sector is "the first eight" by convention.

AEFL-8 is rewritten around that dependence and **mutation-checked**:

| honest implementation | 453/453 passes |

## The first draft of my control was wrong, and the test caught me

It varied `trySectorize`'s first argument expecting different sectors. That argument is the representative **seed**, and membership is deliberately seed-invariant — the independent recomputation confirmed identical sectors at seeds 0, 1 and 255. So the control now varies the sectorization itself, and the seed-invariance it accidentally probed is **asserted explicitly** rather than left as a background belief.

## The anchor, added where the claim is made

The recomputation confirmed 8+8 is structurally **forced**: `Cl(0,7) ≅ R(8)⊕R(8)`, with (16,0)/(0,16) excluded by the measured inequivalence of the eigenspaces, and a different signature excluded by the checked `γ² = −1`.

But the file **stated the conclusion without naming the theorem that forces it** — Atiyah–Bott–Shapiro (1964). The anchor was already in this directory in `mod8-clock.ts`; it was missing *here*, which is where the 8+8 claim is actually made. Competitors and their exclusions are now written beside it, per `numerology-vs-number-theory`.
