## Description

Aaron: *"wire the DuplexEndpoint over a real WebSocket next — just one of our many transports."* The socket-backed fill of the **same** `DuplexEndpoint` port that `duplex-transport.ts` lit in-process.

- **`DuplexSocket`** — the injected network door (noninterference §13: no `new WebSocket` in the module).
- **`jsonFrameCodec`** — `Frame` ⇄ wire-string; validates the channel tag, returns null on garbage (bad message skipped, never crashes inbound).
- **`webSocketEndpoint(socket, codec?)`** — a `DuplexEndpoint` over the socket.
- **`platformWebSocket(ws)`** — thin real fill wrapping a WHATWG `WebSocket`.
- **`fakeDuplexSocketPair()`** — deterministic double for the whole serialize→cross→deserialize path.

**14/14 tests** across the three four-corner files. Includes a **real ws:// loopback** (`Bun.serve`, ephemeral port): a normal frame crosses a genuine WebSocket, the peer replies with a **feedback** frame, and it surfaces on the client's `feedbackIn` (`ack:ping`) — mutual empowerment over an actual wire, not just the fake. Lint clean.

**Anchor (Aaron's own prior art — he pointed me to it):** `AceHack/MultiplexedWebSockets` (C#, 2023). Many logical request/response pairs multiplex over **one** physical socket, Guid-correlated, under an envelope (version byte, 32-byte header, `MessageType` Request/Response, max 0xFFFF); the send path is a **DoP=1 ferry-throttle** (`ActionBlock` MaxDegreeOfParallelism=1 over `System.IO.Pipelines`) — the exact "beautiful on one thread" pattern the async rules cite; **~16× over HttpClient** (115,309 vs 7,075 req/s). Lineage: this `DuplexEndpoint`/`Frame` is the **single-channel** case; the Guid multiplexing is the **multi-channel** generalization; four-corner **adds the feedback corners** to his Request/Response. A future `MultiplexedDuplexTransport` = N four-corner channels over one socket, Guid-keyed.
