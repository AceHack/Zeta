## Description

refactor(flake): the root flake stops declaring machines — blocking consumers reach ZERO

The last two `blocking` entries on the stale-cluster-tree roster were
`flake.nix` and `.github/workflows/root-flake-check.yml`, and the recorded
reason for both was WRONG in a way that made them look harder than they were.

The roster said "full-ai-cluster/flake.nix already declares control-plane and
worker-gpu, on nixpkgs 25.11 against the root flake's 24.11. Repointing crosses
a nixpkgs boundary." Measured 2026-09-07: the root pins nixos-26.05 — NEWER than
full-ai-cluster's 25.11 — so the version gap runs the other way and was never
the obstacle. What the root actually held was the THINNER declaration: 3 hosts
and 5 modules against full-ai-cluster's 4 hosts and ~30.

And nothing installed from it. `full-ai-cluster/PROVISIONING.md` runs
`nixos-install --flake .#<host>` against full-ai-cluster's flake, which is what
`zeta-install.sh` uses. Checked: no `inputs.self.nixosModules` consumer exists,
and the three TypeScript files mentioning `nixosConfigurations` all refer in
comments to full-ai-cluster's `installer`, not to these hosts.

THE ONE THING THE REPO COULD NOT ANSWER, and how it was answered. A 2026-08-26
research doc named the blocker precisely: "whether any off-repo machine tracks
`.#control-plane` from the root flake is UNKNOWABLE." No amount of measurement
inside the tree could settle it, because the evidence was outside. Aaron,
2026-09-07: "there are no machines out there tracking anything yet, I'm waiting
to reformat the machine once we get everything working."

That is worth naming as a method rather than a detail: the unknown was resolved
by ASKING the one party who could see it. Inferring it would have been guessing
about state the repo cannot observe — and the cost of guessing wrong was
breaking a real machine on its next rebuild.

Removed: `nixosConfigurations` (control-plane, worker-gpu-01, worker-gpu-02),
`nixosModules` (4 entries), and the now-unused `mkSystem` binding rather than
leaving dead scaffolding. The flake keeps its developer surface — devShells,
packages, checks, darwinConfigurations.

`root-flake-check.yml`: the anti-vacuity roster expected
`nixosConfigurations.control-plane` to be evaluated, and its own error text
demands that any narrowing be "a reviewed diff" rather than a silent omission.
Narrowed deliberately, with the reason in the step: `packages` replaces it so
the roster still names THREE outputs and cannot shrink to nothing unnoticed. The
`infra/nixos/**` path filters are dropped because this lane no longer reads that
tree.

ROSTER: blocking 2 -> 0. Six `derived` entries remain, and several of those need
no edit at all — they record historical paths on purpose. Deleting
`infra/k8s` + `infra/nixos` is now provably safe by the roster's own criterion.
THE DELETION ITSELF REMAINS GATED on maintainer sign-off and is not performed
here; what changed is that it is now a decision rather than a scavenger hunt.

Verified: `nix flake check --no-build --all-systems` on the ROOT flake exits 0
with 33 outputs checked; all three roster outputs appear in the log; zero
mentions of nixosConfigurations remain; the workflow parses; and the
cluster-tree-consumers audit is green.
