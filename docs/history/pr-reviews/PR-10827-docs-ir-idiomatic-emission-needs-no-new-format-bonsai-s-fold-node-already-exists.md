## Description

## The question

Aaron 2026-08-15: *"our IR may have to come up with our own version of bonsai — we may want language specific hints … so it can be performant and idiomatic for that language and not just an instruction-for-instruction rewriting … **This is the same problem when I translated my book from English to other languages: at first it didn't sound like a native speaker, it sounded like an English translation.**"*

**Determine whether that is true.** The honest answer is **no — and Bonsai already carries the node**, which is a better result than a new format.

## The finding, in four claims

**1. The fold node exists in the shipped format, today.** `Bonsai.Expr.Call of string * Expr list` serializes as `{"kind":"call","fn":"<any string>","args":[…]}`, and **no oracle whitelists the `fn` name** — read in all four (F# `Bonsai.fs:306-311`, TS, C#, Rust; `rg 'UnknownFn|allowedFn|knownFn'` → nothing, while `UnknownKind`/`UnknownConstTag` *are* implemented, so it is a deliberate extension point). `Call("map",[Lambda…; Param "xs"])` round-trips byte-identically now. **Byte-lock cost: zero** — a new `fn` is a new *value*, not a new node *kind*; every committed golden vector stays byte-identical and no version tag moves.

**2. The gap is a missing EDGE, not a missing format.** `ZetaIrV1`–`V4` (six lowered scalar ops, six emitters, no expression level) and `Bonsai` (an expression level with lambda + application, four oracles, **zero emitters**) are two rungs of one nanopass ladder that were never joined:

```
rg -l 'Bonsai' src/Core/ZetaIr*.fs src/Core/MixIr.fs src/Core/Cogen.fs src/Core/GrammarIr.fs  → empty
rg -l -i 'bonsai' tests/cross-verification/_harness/                                          → one comment
```

#10774 concluded "the IR is too low" — correct, and it was reading the bottom rung. **The high level was already built and never wired in.**

**3. Language-specific hints cannot live in the IR — structurally, not by preference.** A target-indexed field makes the same program's bytes a function of the *project's target list*, which is N forks sharing a brace, and it destroys the four-oracle byte-lock the IR exists for. The carved test:

> **Would this field change if you deleted a target from the project?** Yes ⇒ target preference ⇒ **target descriptor**, beside the emitter. No ⇒ program property ⇒ may live in the IR.

`associative`/`ordinal-collation` pass; `prefer_iterator_chain` does not. And even the passing ones cost nothing: spell them as a wrapper `Call("associative",[e])` rather than a node attribute.

**4. Branch-free is load-bearing — and Aaron unified his own two claims in June.** The brief framed this as an open finding. The record already has it, and *not* as a hardware claim: *"avoid `if` — it is a **composition-killer**"* (2026-06-06) and *"avoid ifs everywhere so it's easy to **switch between parallel soft and serial sharp** algebra"* (2026-06-07). That is **instance-passing**, and read at *emission* time instead of *run* time it **is** the anti-N-forks mechanism — the target descriptor is the instance, the single emitter is the branch-free call site. The 50 hand-written `emit*` functions #10774 counted are precisely the anti-pattern the 2026-06-07 doc names, one layer up.

## Measured (the falsifiers, not assertions)

**The corpus question is answered: ZSetMerkle is non-degenerate, and it already has six hand-written controls.** `cargo clippy -W pedantic -W nursery` on `src/Core.Rust.Algebra` (cache defeated by `touch`, because a 0.87 s silent "Finished" is the check-that-did-not-run trap):

| | classes | note |
|---|---|---|
| u64 corpus (#10774) | **3** | two are literal formatting |
| ZSet/Bag/GSet | **9** (62 findings) | incl. **`clippy::option_if_let_else` — "use `Option::map_or` instead of an `if let`/`else`"** |

That last row is Rust's own linter asking for the combinator over the branch: the idiom axis and Aaron's branch-free preference turn out to be one mechanical check, on the exact corpus proposed.

**A weakness in #10774's bar that only measurement exposes:** the hand-written control carries **25 `use_self`** findings of its own, so under `lints(generated) ⊆ lints(hand-written)` a generator emitting non-`Self` is *excused*. The bar's discriminating power is bounded by the control's cleanliness; it should be reported as two numbers, not one.

**A defect found by a third instrument** (cross-lane differential reading — neither #10774's linter bar nor any byte-lock has it): `ZSetMerkle.byteCompare` hand-rolls 8 lines of mutable loop where the BCL has `MemoryExtensions.SequenceCompareTo`. Rust spends `a.0.cmp(&b.0)`; Go spends `sort.Strings`. **200 000 randomized cases, 0 sign disagreements.**

## Corrections flagged

- **`codegen-zset-merkle.ts` generates nothing** despite its name and docstring (*"generate cross-language ZSet Merkle verifiers"*). No writer, no emitter — it verifies the TS impl against golden vectors. It is the file a reader would consult for "is ZSet generated?", and it answers wrongly. *(Not fixed here — a harness-owner's one-line header change.)*
- **The Venuti anchor inverts on checking.** He introduces domestication/foreignization **as a critique** and advocates *foreignization*; domestication is his term for *"an ethnocentric reduction of the foreign text to target-language cultural values."* Citing him for "make it native" asserts the reverse of his thesis. Kept for the axis — which yields a real design output: **foreignize the cross-verification oracles** (their job is to be *diffed*), **domesticate shipped SDK code**. So the descriptor needs a *register* field, not just a capability list.
- **To the brief:** "a branch-free expression tree is freely re-associable" is not the repo's property. 2401 `if` occurrences in `src/Core/*.fs`, and Bonsai's own grammar has `Cond` (golden vector `factorial`). True of a *layer*, not the codebase. The narrower true statement — branch-freeness keeps a program in the **expression** category, and idiom lives at the term level — is what does the work.
