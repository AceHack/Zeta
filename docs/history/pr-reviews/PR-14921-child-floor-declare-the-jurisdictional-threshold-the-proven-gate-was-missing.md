## Description

## The gap

`src/Core.Lean4/Safety/ChildFloor.lean` proves `denied_never_executed`: **an effect the policy denies is never executed, at ANY depth (`fuel`)** — `source ≠ authorization` made structural. It quantifies over `policy : Nat → Verdict` and says nothing about *what* the policy denies. That is deliberate, and it is also the limit: **the theorem is satisfied by `fun _ => .admit`**, under which nothing is denied and it holds vacuously.

The gate was proven. The policy it enforces was never declared. `lucent-ksk`'s `docs/ksk_architecture.yaml` names `red_lines: [no_minors, …]`, and nothing connected a jurisdiction's age parameter to the proven gate. Identified as the one piece of new work in `docs/research/2026-08-24-ksk-is-the-kinetic-rung-*.md` §6.

## The design, as stated

Aaron 2026-08-24:

> *"the fixed moral floor is always protect children and disagree on their age around 16-21."*

Two different kinds of thing, and separating them is the whole point:

- **The predicate — protect children — is INVARIANT.** Not a competing morality submitted to the Multi-Oracle Principle (§11); the floor every oracle stands on, which §11's own text carves out as the *default* oracle for morally-relevant entities.
- **The threshold — roughly 16 to 21 — is JURISDICTIONAL.** A parameter. Disagreement is expected and legitimate.

Where jurisdictions disagree or the jurisdiction is unknown: **the protective bound**, per *"unknown include is better than unknown exclude"* — an unknown that halts is recoverable, one that ships is not.

**`ChildFloor.lean` is unchanged.** Every theorem here *instantiates* `denied_never_executed`; none restates or weakens it.

### The shape a policy must have to plug in without weakening the proof

Nothing constrains it — `denied_never_executed` is total over `policy : Nat → Verdict`. So the *proof* imposes no requirement, and the parameterisation is a pure addition: `policyOf reg js classOf subjectOf : Nat → Verdict` is just such a function. What *is* required — and what this PR supplies — is that the concrete policy be non-vacuous in **both** directions, which the theorem cannot see: a policy that denies everything satisfies every safety statement in the file and is useless. §7 of the Lean module carries executable witnesses that the policy **admits** (an adult executes; the boundary sits exactly where the reading put it; a child inside an *admitted* parent's proposed children is still refused).

### (a) No jurisdiction can disable the floor

`accepted` rejects any reading outside `[bandLow, bandHigh]`, and the resolver then falls through to the protective bound. `Reading` has no field that could express "off".

Demonstrated, not asserted — the hostile entries are written out and handed to the real resolver, in **both** oracles:

```lean
def sabotageDisable : Reading :=
  { jurisdiction := "world/xx", threshold := 0, attributedTo := "hostile", dated := "2026-08-24" }

example : resolve [sabotageDisable] "world/xx" = bandHigh := by decide
example : floorVerdict (resolve [sabotageDisable] "world/xx") (.age 3) = Verdict.deny := by decide
```

`no_registry_lowers_the_floor : ∀ reg js a, a < bandLow → floorVerdict (resolveAll reg js) (.age a) = .deny`

And end-to-end against the **shipped** file: putting `threshold: 0` into `db/child-floor/jurisdiction-readings.json` and running the real lint gives `rc=1`, `threshold 0 is below the floor 16; the predicate is not a parameter`.

### (b) An unknown jurisdiction denies rather than admits

`resolve_unknown` → `bandHigh`; `resolveAll_upper_bound` → every known resolution is `≤ bandHigh`; `deny_monotone` → denial grows with the threshold. Composed: `unknown_denies_superset` — the unknown case denies everything every known case denies, and strictly more. `resolveAll [] = bandHigh` — naming no jurisdiction is the unknown case, not a free pass. `unknown_age_denies` — unknown *age* has no admitting branch at any threshold.

### The connection, which is the point

```lean
theorem under_bandLow_never_executed … : id ∉ executed (policyOf reg js classOf subjectOf) fuel t
```

A child-gated effect on a subject under `bandLow` is **never executed, at any depth, under any registry, for any effect tree** — by instantiating the existing `denied_never_executed`.

## What is NOT claimed — read this part

- **NOT LEGAL ADVICE.** `readings` entries are one named party's reading of one jurisdiction at one date, revisable, possibly wrong. `attributedTo` and `dated` are required fields precisely so an entry can never read as an anonymous statement of law.
- **`readings` ships EMPTY, deliberately.** No attributed legal review exists, so every jurisdiction resolves to `21` — the most protective answer. That is the correct fail-closed state for an unreviewed registry, and inventing readings for jurisdictions I have not had reviewed would have been exactly the register violation this work exists to avoid. `candidates` records where a reading is *wanted*, carries no threshold, and is never consulted.
- **The classifier is NOT proven.** `classOf` (is this effect child-gated?) and `subjectOf` (whose age?) are the deployment's decoders. Everything here is conditional on a classification and says nothing about whether one is correct. **This is the real remaining gap and it is larger than this was.**
- **NOT deployed.** `src/Core.FSharp.ObserveBridge/Effects.fs`'s `Effect` taxonomy has no subject and no age, so wiring the policy into the live gate means extending that taxonomy — a design change, not a plug-in. The policy is **declared and proven**, not **deployed**.
- **The band's numbers (16, 21) are declared**, from Aaron's stated range. Nothing proves them.
