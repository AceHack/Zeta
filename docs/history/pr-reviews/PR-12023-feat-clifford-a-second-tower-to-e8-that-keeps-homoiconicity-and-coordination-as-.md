## Description

feat(clifford): a SECOND tower to E8 that keeps homoiconicity, and coordination as the velocity

Aaron 2026-08-18: "we can have more than one way of doing it and see what else pops out -- we don't
have to have one 'the' adinkra. Maybe some E8 or other Lie groups can be generated from the
homoiconic version, and maybe it would have some other logical mod-8 like clock that falls out. We
are never locked into one tower because it's tall already."

He is right, and the second route is standard rather than speculative:

    e8 = so(16) + half-spin(16)        120 + 128 = 248

The so(16) half is the BIVECTORS of Cl(0,16) -- bivectors span so(n) under the Clifford commutator
-- and the 128 is one chirality half of its spinor module. No code, no quotient, so the uncoded
tower's homoiconicity survives intact. Construction A over the [8,4] code was never the only door.

And the clock is not a different clock; it is the SAME clock doing a second job. Cl(0,16) sits at
s = 0, so its even subalgebra Cl(0,15) sits at s = 1 -- the split real row -- giving M128(R) +
M128(R). The two 128s ARE the even-subalgebra splitting, exactly as the two 64s were at (7,7) and
the two 8s at (0,8). One clock, three rungs, and a test pins all three together.

The two routes do NOT produce the same object, which is worth being precise about rather than
blurring: Construction A yields the E8 LATTICE, the spinor route yields the E8 LIE ALGEBRA. They are
two faces of E8 -- the lattice is the algebra's root lattice -- reached by constructions sharing only
the periodicity. That is a stronger statement than "two ways to get E8" and it is what Aaron's
"different ways of generating the same object that seem unrelated at first" actually looks like when
it is true.

f4 = so(9) + spin(9) = 36 + 16 = 52 is included so the recipe is tested at more than one point.
Anchors: the standard spinorial constructions (Adams, Lectures on Exceptional Lie Groups; Kostant) --
not ours and not new, which is exactly why they are checkable. 26 tests green.

COORDINATION IS THE VELOCITY. Separately, Aaron supplied the first concrete candidate for the metric
the Lorentz scoping doc had to leave empty: "anything that is not embarrassingly parallel slows down
causal phase time in that local space, like reverse time dilation, where things look like they are
moving in slow motion from the outside where things are embarrassingly parallel."

That fills exactly the missing axis. We had the causal cone (Lamport 1978) and no interval; a
partial order says WHETHER two events are causally separated, never HOW FAR. Coordination is
measurable, dimensionless, local to a region, and varies between regions -- which is what a metric
needs to be non-trivial. CALM makes it decidable rather than a judgement call: coordination-free iff
monotone. Amdahl supplies the ceiling.

The rhyme with gamma is recorded as a rhyme: 1/sqrt(1-v^2) diverges, 1/(s+(1-s)/N) saturates --
different functions sharing the shape "an irreducible fraction sets an asymptote", which licenses an
investigation and not a claim.

The encouraging part is that curvature and dilation turn out to be one parameter seen twice: a
reordering that costs nothing is exactly a region that needed no coordination, so flat/undilated/
embarrassingly-parallel is one case and curved/dilated/coordinating is the other.

Four falsifiers are recorded, and the fourth is the one to run first because it is cheap and can
kill the Lorentz framing while leaving the insight intact: in special relativity dilation is
SYMMETRIC, each frame seeing the other's clock run slow, whereas Aaron's phrasing privileges the
parallel region. If the relation is asymmetric it is not a boost at all -- it is closer to a
gravitational potential, where the deeper region really is slower and both parties agree. That is
decidable, and it determines whether O(1,1) is even the target.
