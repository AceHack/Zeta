## Description

Aaron, correcting the "community moderation" placeholder in #10462:

> *it's really just the authors of the game who decide the cannon, they will have owners where the game orginated from with the write keys to that game*

I'd flagged "the community decides" as an unbuilt mechanism with its own attack profile — whoever rules bug-vs-canon shapes which strategies are viable. **That framing was wrong, and the correction removes the problem instead of solving it.**

## Canon is authorship, not governance

No standards body, no vote, no adjudication. The author decides canon for their game, and the mechanism is **cryptographic rather than social**: ownership is holding the write key, anchored to origin.

Better than community moderation on three counts: **no permanent authority over the space** (each author rules exactly one artefact, so there's no general seat to capture — §3 weight-free); **no adjudication procedure** to design, attack, or staff; and **it's already the mechanism** — mods and ROM hacks have always worked this way.

## Disagreement forks — and a fork is a version row, not a dispute

If you think the author ruled wrongly, you **fork**. There's nobody to appeal to. Your fork is a new artefact with your key and **a different content hash** — so it lands in the *substrate version* partition (#10462) automatically, derived rather than declared.

**So the version partition IS the dispute-resolution mechanism.** Nobody has to be overruled, and no authority has to exist to overrule them. Disagreement produces a new row rather than a verdict — the same move as everywhere else here: report the distinguishing fact, refuse the judgement. And the social signal survives without any voting apparatus: **which fork people actually play** is the measure, accrued rather than polled.

## Immutability is key liveness, not substrate age

| write key | behaviour |
|---|---|
| held and used | mutable — canon is a live decision |
| held, unused | frozen in practice |
| **lost or abandoned** | **immutable — "it just is what it is"** |

Old Atari games aren't immutable because of their era — they're immutable because **nobody holds a live write key**. That unifies the two cases #10462 treated as distinct, and sharpens the governance argument: **load tracks key liveness, not age.** Starting frozen means starting where no owner exists who *could* rule.

Corollary: **key loss is what turns an artefact into a fixed measurement instrument.** The same event that's a disaster for a wallet is what makes a game canonical. Both readings honest — dual-use again.

## Limits, stated

- **A stolen key hijacks canon.** Scoped authority is still authority. Handle with existing apparatus, including the reunion-vs-sybil reading — an owner returning after key loss produces the same evidence a hijacker does.
- **Origin is a claim needing an anchor.** First-to-publish ≠ authorship. This is the one genuinely unbuilt piece.
- **Abandonment isn't observable.** A key merely unused is indistinguishable from one lost, so "frozen" can be *observed to date*, never proved. Same one-way discipline as the CHSH oracle.

**Withdraws open item 4 of #10462** — there's no community-moderation mechanism to name, because there's no community moderation. Replaced with the narrower real gap: anchor the origin claim.
