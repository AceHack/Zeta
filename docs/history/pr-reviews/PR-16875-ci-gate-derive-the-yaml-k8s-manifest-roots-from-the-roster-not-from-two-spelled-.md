## Description

`lint (yaml/k8s)` named `infra/k8s` twice as a literal argument — once to `yamllint`, once inside the `kubeconform` `find`. Two reasons to derive it, and **the second is a live fragility, not tidiness**:

1. The tree is scheduled for deletion. Spelled literally, this job is a `blocking` consumer the deletion commit must edit; derived, it needs no change at all.
2. **`find` errors on a missing directory.** If the tree were deleted without editing this file, the job would break inside the **kubeconform** step — reading as a schema failure rather than a missing path. A red that misnames its own cause.

A `Declared cluster manifest roots` step emits the declared k8s roots **that exist on disk**; both consumers read them through `env:` and `read -ra` rather than interpolating into the shell. `agentic-organization/deploy` is not a cluster tree and stays literal.

**Checked against the literal it replaces, before wiring:** the derivation emits exactly `full-ai-cluster/k8s infra/k8s`, and bash expands it to two arguments.

## Why this one degrades where the parity guards refuse

`declared-cluster-trees` exposes both shapes on purpose, and the choice per call site is a judgement rather than a default:

| call site | missing declared root | why |
|---|---|---|
| two-tree **parity** guards | **refuse** | scanning one tree while claiming to compare two is the vacuity class |
| this **lint** job | **degrade** to one fewer tree | it is not comparing; it lints whatever manifests exist |

This job's refusal is placed where it actually matters instead: **empty roots exit 1**, because scanning nothing would pass silently.
