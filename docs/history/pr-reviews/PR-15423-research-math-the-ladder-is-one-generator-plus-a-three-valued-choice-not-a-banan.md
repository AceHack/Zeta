## Description

Applies Meijer/Fokkinga/Paterson's **banana split law** to the Cayley–Dickson lattice ladder, as PRs #15415 (Kira, adversarial) and #15417 (Lumen, adjudication) left it. Read both in full first; this answers the open question both converged on.

## The verdict

> **One irreducible object, plus one thing that is not an object of the same kind.**
>
> Cayley–Dickson doubling is a genuine catamorphism. The maximal-order completion is **not a catamorphism, not a functor, and not a natural transformation** — at the quaternion rung it is a unique lub, at the octonion rung a **three-valued choice with no canonical member**, and at the sedenion rung it does nothing at all.

Aaron's "N irreducible objects plus one" is right about *where* the plus-one is (the glue) and wrong about *what* it is: N = 1, and the plus-one is a **selection**, not a join.

## On the methodology (`generator + join`)

Read strictly — `join` = the semilattice `∨`, which is what `2026-08-14-verlinde-*.md` §7b says it means — the framing forbids merges that **require a choice**. Both of its own exemplars violate that:

- the ladder needs a choice at the octonion rung (three maximal orders, **no top**);
- `ace` needs one too — its resolver is deterministic backtracking (pubgrub-shape), and genuine conflicts escalate to a human. **That is why `ace.lock` exists.** A lockfile is a confession that resolution was not a join; if it were, you could always recompute it.

Repair: **generator + selection + ledger**, which makes the half-integer glue vectors and `ace.lock` the same object. Also corrects an in-repo framing: generate+join is *not* the dual of map+reduce — both end in a fold, and the difference is **idempotence + commutativity**, not direction and not associativity.
