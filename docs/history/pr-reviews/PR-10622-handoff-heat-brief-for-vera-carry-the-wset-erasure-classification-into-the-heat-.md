## Description

handoff(heat): brief for Vera - carry the WSet erasure classification into the heat types

Routes the heat type integration to Vera, who owns src/Core/Heat.fs. Self-contained
because Vera runs on a different harness and will not have the originating conversation.

The measured gap, CHECKED: src/Core/WSet.fs contains zero references to Heat, HeatSignal
or MassPpm. So the path carrying the reversibility/CPT claim has a measurement and a
vocabulary that do not touch. An earlier and broader claim - that the reversibility
surfaces meter nothing at all - was WRONG and is retracted in the brief: ComputeReceipt,
ReceiptScheduler, SoftChip8Scheduler, ToffoliGate, AdinkraCode, SoftThrottle and
LandauerFloor.lean are real, and ComputeReceipt has five production call sites.

What is waiting to be carried (PR #10611): an exhaustive sweep over the Z corner, 43
states / 1849 pairs, giving bits erased per operation. negate - the retraction - costs
0.000 bits because it is reversible; consolidate costs 3.459 because it annihilates, and
WSet.fs already calls it the place "opposite weights annihilate". The negation is free,
the annihilation pays, and those two are currently read as one operation. MassPpm is the
field that bit count belongs in and Forgotten is the signal it should ride.

The brief also carries the structural reason this was missed, because it constrains the
design: Landauer prices irreversibility, so a meter aimed at a reversible operation must
read zero forever. That is why verifyLandauer had degenerated to `x >= x` - not only a
coding slip but structurally guaranteed by where the meter pointed. A heat meter must be
attached to the erasing leg or it is a tautology wearing a physics name.

The decision procedure is already in the repo, in SchedulerZeta.fs: "Because the fixed
points are derived, drop-and-recompute is lossless." Regenerable from the seed means
pressure or nothing; unrecoverable means loss. That is why SchedulerZeta correctly emits
no heat today, and it is the same test as negate-vs-consolidate.

One question is left explicitly undecided because it is a treaty change across four
languages and belongs to Vera and Aaron: does "pruned by a complexity bound" need to be
policy-distinguishable from Forgotten? There is a real argument either way and the brief
states both rather than choosing.

Also recorded: TemperatureBand's warm and hot were structurally unreachable on the
TypeScript side against a comment advertising the full four-band ladder (fixed in
#10575). Noted because the treaty did NOT catch it - per-case reachability is the
property a load-bearing treaty is currently missing.

Docs only. No code, no workflow, no golden vector touched.
