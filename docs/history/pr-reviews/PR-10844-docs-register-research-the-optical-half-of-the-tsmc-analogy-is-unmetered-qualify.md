## Commit 1 — the register correction (`4a40d8afc`)

Filed after the maintainer's 2026-08-15 observation that *"we do care [about optical] — we want TSMC lithographic precision over our lensography."* Nothing is overturned; two claims are qualified to what the code supports, and the missing quantity is stated as an open requirement rather than filled with an invented number.

**`docs/VISION.md` — the 5D-voxel table.** The row read *"Multi-patterning (compose filters for resolution) | Multiple lenses/schemas composing for precision."* That imports the workaround and drops the constraint that motivates it.

Anchor, checked (ASML, *The Rayleigh criterion for resolution*): half-pitch = k1·lambda/NA, and **k1 cannot go below 0.25 for a single exposure**. Multi-patterning exists *only* to obtain an effective k1 under that floor by splitting one dense layer across sparser exposures — and the price is that **overlay error moves into the critical-dimension budget** instead of being a separate control.

What the code has: `src/Core/PolarityFilter.fs` is 53 lines — Malus's law `cos^2(dtheta)` plus an `n`-step sweep over `[0, pi)`. It has **no notion of resolution, limit, or smoothness**. `n` is an arbitrarily refinable *sampling* step, not a lower bound on distinguishability. No lambda, no NA, no overlay budget — and **no composition operator**, so the "composing" in that row is either absent or means `Optic.compose`, which composes functional lenses *exactly* and has no tolerance to spend either. Also flagged: *"the shadow IS the image"* / *"the projection IS the data"* are **losslessness** claims, and losslessness is a resolution question, so they depend on the quantity we do not have.

**`docs/pitch/funding-thesis-tsmc-in-time.md`** declared *"structural, not metaphorical"* while carrying no quantified precision on the Zeta side. The heading now splits the two halves, the moat row distinguishes a **measured tolerance** from **exact pass/fail**, and a disclosure states that we publish no nm, no error budget and no overlay analogue **because we do not have one**.

## Commit 2 — the F1 pricing, landed (`3608b4267`)

**Why this commit exists.** The derivation below was produced alongside commit 1 and then existed **only in a task transcript**. A sibling agent building the F2 falsifier went to inherit it and found nothing. That is precisely the failure this repo spent 2026-08-14 fighting (PR #10821 — one transcript degrading into a wrong recollection in two days). It is now in `docs/research/`, with a **tracked, runnable probe**, because a scratch path is a dangling pointer.

**The Lipschitz constant is free, and tight.** `cos^2(Delta) = (1 + cos 2Delta)/2` gives `d/dDelta = -sin 2Delta`, so **`L = 1`** — and the supremum is *attained* at `Delta = ±pi/4`, so it is **tight, not a loose over-estimate**. (Independently re-derived from scratch in PR #10847 and measured at `1.00000000003`.)

**But `L` is not the constant that binds.** `findOrientation` localises a **maximum**, where the first derivative vanishes and `transmit ~ 1 - Delta^2`. So the throughput difference encoding sub-grid position is *quadratic*, and the floor is **curvature-limited at `sqrt(sigma)`, not `sigma`** — strictly worse, and it governs the operation the module actually performs.

Measured by `src/Core.TypeScript/research/polarity-filter-resolution-floor.ts` (deterministic seeded LCG, no ambient entropy; `--check` asserts both claims). RMS orientation error in radians, 400 trials, seed 42:

| sigma | n=64 | n=1024 | n=16384 | floor / sqrt(sigma) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | 1.42e-2 | 8.81e-4 | **5.41e-5** | — (no floor) |
| 1e-6 | 1.42e-2 | 8.99e-4 | 5.04e-4 | 0.50 |
| 1e-4 | 1.40e-2 | 5.49e-3 | 4.00e-3 | 0.40 |
| 1e-2 | 5.57e-2 | 4.49e-2 | 3.74e-2 | 0.37 |
| 1e-1 | 1.60e-1 | 1.27e-1 | 1.18e-1 | 0.37 |

**At `sigma = 0` the error keeps shrinking with `n` without bound — the model currently has literally infinite resolution.** That is the defect, stated exactly: not "the limit is wrong" but "there is no limit." At `sigma > 0` a floor appears that `n` cannot beat.

*Correction to how I first reported this:* the prefactor is **0.37–0.43 over sigma in [1e-4, 1e-1]**, rising to 0.50 at 1e-6 where the sweep grid (`pi/16384 ~ 1.9e-4`) is itself comparable to the floor. The `sqrt(sigma)` **scaling** is the checked claim; the prefactor is a measured range, not the flat "0.37" I first said. Seed-independent (42 and 1337).

**Smoothness and resolution are one constant seen from both sides** — `L` bounds how fast output can change; `sigma/L` (or `sqrt(sigma)` at a peak) is what that makes unresolvable. The 2026-05-26 deferral did not defer two research programmes; it deferred one constant and one declared noise floor. And this is the same fact PR #10847 measured from the other side: its `sin(pi/n)` true constant for `findOrientation`'s throughput **vanishes** as the grid refines — a vanishing throughput sensitivity and a `sqrt(sigma)` angle floor are two readings of one curvature.

### Costing, with two corrections from PR #10847

- **F2 — smoothness. Cheap, and built** in PR #10847 (open; its test file is not on `main` yet, so this doc cites the PR and not the path). Its two recorded breaks — the piecewise-constant argmax, and the exactly-constant objective at `|R| = 0` where a `1e-6` perturbation flips the answer by `pi/2` at n = 180, 1001 and 4001 alike — are cited, not restated.
- **F1 — ~1 day, and the cost is not the test.** The test is three assertions the probe already runs. What blocks it is sharper than "we need an RNG": **`transmit`'s signature has no channel through which noise could enter.** Adding one *changes the signature* — which is exactly what §13 noninterference requires (injected `Source`, never ambient). F1 is a design decision about a declared entropy channel, not a research problem.
- **F3 — repriced to "do not build yet."** My earlier estimate assumed a composition operator would serve a pipeline. **`PolarityFilter` has zero downstream consumers** — only its own tests, plus two doc-comment mentions that call nothing (`src/Core/Orbit.fs:24`, `src/Core/Optics.fs:19`); confirmed independently and re-verified here. Building it would be speculative API design with no caller to validate it. The prediction it *would* test is recorded instead: composing filters **spends** budget to buy coverage, exactly as multi-patterning does.

The zero-consumer fact also reprices the analogy itself: both tables map onto a module that nothing calls.
