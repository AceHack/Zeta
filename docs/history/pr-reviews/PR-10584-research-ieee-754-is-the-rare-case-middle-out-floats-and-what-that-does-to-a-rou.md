## Description

research: IEEE-754 is the rare case - middle-out floats and what that does to a routing call

Ferries Aaron's observation (verbatim) that our carriers are middle-out floats that
track their own precision and expand as needed, middle-out bool, minimal expanding
quantum amplitude emulation and bayesian emu - and that IEEE-754 is a rare case for
us, with Q# and other exotic substrates the target.

It lands on Soraya's Z-EPS routing call, now on main via #10548. That call's internal
reasoning is sound and its parenthetical - that an exact carrier makes the threshold
defect INVISIBLE - is exactly right. What it did not do is ask which carrier the
substrate is heading for; it treated IEEE-754 as the ambient given.

The confirmation arrived as code the same hour. PR #10570 landed Z[zeta_16] as an
exact amplitude carrier: 1.9x float on a 1000-member depth-1 fold, ~5x with
denominator-exponent spread handled, coefficients inside Int32, and the amplitude
layer byte-lockable for the first time. zeta_8 gives sqrt(2), hence Clifford+T's
Z[1/sqrt2, i] - the ring Q# substrates speak.

The part worth carving is the reciprocal, not the correction. An exact carrier is
blind to the Z-EPS defect BY CONSTRUCTION - EPS exists because a finite mantissa
forces a cutoff. #10570 measured it: setting EPS to 0.0 kills exactly the three float
arms and leaves all 24 exact arms green. So #10570 kept the float path with a
differential assertion holding both carriers at once. Deleting the float instrument
would delete the evidence along with the defect.

Conclusion is therefore NOT "IEEE was the wrong carrier" but that two carriers answer
two different questions and must be named apart - the same move as join vs interfere.
The Z-EPS witness stands with its scope stated: it establishes signalling ON THE
IEEE-754 CARRIER. A witness is a claim about the carrier it ran on.

Four consequences carried, one of them explicitly marked CONJECTURE: a
resolution-tracking number may have no EPS at all, only a budget - which would move
the threshold from a numerical artefact to a §13 noninterference crossing.
