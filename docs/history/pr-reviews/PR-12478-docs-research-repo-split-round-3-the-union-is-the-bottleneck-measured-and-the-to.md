## Description

docs(research): repo-split round 3 -- the union IS the bottleneck, measured; and the tooling that would fix it is already built and unwired

Testing a hypothesis Aaron formed over years and other codebases, not one
discovered here: "without hardcore tooling support for monorepo, the union of
everything becomes the bottleneck, splitting it out actually can speed things
up and help decouple everything from everything."

IT HOLDS ON THIS TREE, BY A WIDE MARGIN. But the conditional in it does not,
and that is the round's most decision-relevant finding -- see the last section.

THE UNION, MEASURED. 11,533 MB across 23 components on a developer clone (one
version each; `du -sk`). Lean 2,600 · LLVM 1,700 · rust 1,300 · dotnet SDK
1,228 · emscripten 953 · dotnet-tools 806 · node 684 · java 377 · zig 352 ·
semgrep 301 · go 258 · cargo 234 · bun 178.

HONEST CORRECTION TO MY OWN FIGURE, made before it shipped: CI does NOT
provision 11.5 GB. The measured `install-v2-Linux-X64` cache is 1,487 MB and
`mise-Linux-X64` is 825 MB, so the CI union is ~1.5-2.3 GB. Two different
unions answering two different questions; conflating them inflates the result
7x, which the first draft of the delta section did until the cache API was read.

20 GATE JOBS INSTALL THE UNION. TEN NEED ONLY `bun` (178 MB). Derived by
parsing gate.yml into jobs, extracting only `run:` shell text (comments and
cache-path globs name ~/.dotnet, ~/.rustup, ~/.elan and produced a false
positive on the first pass), then resolving `bun <script>.ts` ONE HOP into the
script -- load-bearing, because lint-fsharp looks like a bun job and shells out
to `dotnet format`. 65x over-provision against the developer union, 8x against
the CI one. Exactly ONE job legitimately needs the union: `full-verify`.

94% OF PROVISIONING WORK PER GATE RUN IS WASTE. And on a GREEN run
(32281902548): 2,830 of 7,766 runner-seconds = 36% OF ALL JOB WALL-TIME is the
install step. Not the build. Not the tests. The install.

THE CACHE IS OVER ITS CEILING. Measured 11.57 GB across 31 entries against
GitHub's documented 10 GB default -- CHECKED against the docs, which also
supply the name for the regime: "cache thrashing, where caches are created and
deleted at a high frequency." Whether the ceiling was raised I could not verify
(usage-policy returns Not Found at agent permission level); it is one click in
Actions settings and it is in the decision list.

THE HYPOTHESIS FIRING IN PRODUCTION, ON THE DAY IT WAS STATED. All gate.yml
runs on main today: 91 runs, 6 success, 13 failure, 70 cancelled. Across the 13
failed runs, 45 failing jobs -- 28 died in `Install toolchain`. Excluding the 11
`gate (required)` aggregator roll-ups, that is 82% of real job failures. Five of
the eight distinct jobs that died there NEED ONLY `bun`. They failed installing
.NET, Rust, Go, Zig, Java, Python and semgrep, none of which they use.

THE REPO ALREADY CONTAINS ITS OWN DEPENDENCY PARTITION, AND IT HAS 50 PARTS.
`ace/build-graph.json`: 107 targets -> 50 connected components; 43% singletons;
36% with NO CI leg (35 of 36 Rust crates -- so the 1,534 MB rust+cargo toolchain
serves exactly one crate's test). One component (48 dotnet targets) absorbs 96%
of build-touching commits. And only 2,282 of 9,191 commits (25%) touch ANY build
target at all.

87% OF THE UNION (10,055 MB) IS NEEDED BY EXACTLY ONE CANDIDATE. The corollary
is as useful as the headline: `bun` -- the most-shared component, and the entire
need of half the gate -- is 178 MB, 1.5% of the union. What everything shares is
nearly free; what is expensive is wanted by one consumer.

THE CCP/CRP SYNTHESIS (Martin, checked for entailment: he states the two
principles OPPOSE, which is exactly the framing used). Round 2 measured CCP
(changes together). This measured CRP (used together). They rank the candidates
almost oppositely and NEITHER IS WRONG -- change rate prices human/agent cost
(clone size, review locality, ruleset fit), closure prices machine cost
(toolchain bytes, cache pressure, install time).

The result that inverts the natural reading of the tension: THE STRONGEST
CLOSURE CUTS ARE THE ONES CHANGE RATE CANNOT SEE, AND THEY ARE CHEAP PRECISELY
BECAUSE THEY NEVER CHANGE. `zeta-formal` (Lean+TLA+) and `zeta-wasm`
(zig+llvm+emscripten) remove 5,982 MB -- 52% of the union -- and were not
round-2 candidates at all (48 and 24 commits/90d, invisible on churn). Low churn
is what makes a closure cut cheap to OPERATE. Where CRP would be followed
literally and be WRONG: 25 isolated Rust crates share ONE toolchain, so 25 repos
buy what 1 repo buys at 25x the CI wiring.

CORRECTIONS TO ROUND 2, recorded rather than quietly amended:
- The byte-lock rendezvous constrains REGENERATION, not VERIFICATION.
  `cross-verify` asserts COMMITTED outputs and needs only bun. Cut B is cheaper
  than round 2 priced it.
- `zeta-archive` still leads on change rate and removes ZERO toolchain. It is
  no longer the obvious first move.

AND THE CONDITIONAL IS SATISFIED HERE, IN ITS MOST INSTRUCTIVE FORM (see the
corrections block below -- the first draft of this line said "false", which was
backwards). `ace/build-graph.ts` already anchors to Bazel
and Nixpkgs, already carries `legs` on every target, and its affected-set query
WORKS TODAY -- a doc change resolves to 1 of 107 targets and one CI leg. And
`grep -rn build-graph .github/workflows/` returns ZERO. The graph that would let
each job take its subset is defined, correct, tested, and disconnected from the
thing it was built to drive. Existence is not support: an unwired graph produces exactly the union
bottleneck a missing graph produces. So the alternative to splitting is not
"adopt Bazel"; it is "wire the query that exists" -- which changes the PRICE of
the remedy, not the diagnosis. Priced honestly in the doc,
including the part that is real work (per-leg toolchain subsets in tools/setup/)
and the parts splitting solves that wiring cannot (clone size, the 10 GB
ceiling, ruleset divergence) and vice versa (the 48-target mega-component,
full-verify, pin-bump coordination).

NOT A RECOMMENDATION. Four options with consequences; plus two cheap checks that
are independent of which is chosen -- verify the cache ceiling, and adopt the §1
guard that `git clone` at a pinned tag stays SUFFICIENT forever. That guard
matters MORE this round: a dependency-driven split makes an `ace`-shaped
mandatory resolver more tempting, and that is exactly how a good tool becomes an
appointed hub.

## CORRECTIONS (2026-08-19, after operator review) -- applied in place, with blocks

Three corrections. No measurement changed; every figure above is unchanged and
still `metered`.

**1. Existence is not support -- I had the inference backwards.** Aaron: *"the
tooling also has to be used, even if it exists but is not used or taken
advantage of the bottle neck still ends in CI everything of everything union."*
An unwired graph is indistinguishable from an absent one at the outcome layer,
so the measurements SATISFY his conditional rather than falsifying it. The
discovery changes only the PRICE of the remedy; the diagnosis is strengthened,
because the union persisted EVEN WITH the graph in the tree. §10's title and
lede are rewritten. Named as the **vacuity class** in its outcome-layer form --
a capability never invoked looks like one that was never built -- and that is
the **third instance in this repo on 2026-08-19** (a check that did not run; an
alarm whose label could never be created, #12429; a query nothing calls).

**2. The thesis, and the register it must carry.** Aaron gave the round its
argument: the 94% waste is a SYMPTOM of the increment graph living in nobody's
head, so externalization is the point. Added as a **third evaluation criterion**
beside megabytes and minutes -- *does this option externalize the increment
graph into a checkable artifact?* -- and all four options are scored on it. The
deeper half: an externalized graph is a **shared external referent**, what lets
an AI and a human verify they mean the same thing rather than assert it. Which
makes two findings one: **externalizing was necessary and insufficient.**

But the motivating claim is an assumption and my draft promoted it. Aaron
labelled it himself -- *"i will say this is an assumption of mine … LLMs change
their behavior when observed … AIs sometimes hide their capabilities"* -- so an
observed failure to hold a graph cannot separate a real capacity limit from a
strategic one. In-repo anchor, verified rather than paraphrased:
`docs/ALIGNMENT.md` §"sleeping bear conjecture", already filed as *"empirical
conjecture, kept as conjecture"*. The doc now says **observed not to, under
conditions that cannot separate cannot from did-not**, and notes the gap is not
ordinary `unmetered` but possibly **unmeterable by direct observation**. My
drafting went wrong twice and both are recorded: pass one filed it as cognitive
load, pass two over-corrected to a flat capacity limit used as a premise.
**The recommendation does not depend on how the confound resolves**, and the doc
says so -- mirroring ALIGNMENT.md's own *"works whether or not the strong
version is true"*.

**3. Futamura gets its precondition, so the claim gains a falsifier.** Aaron:
*"it's only the same operation when your generator is complete over the domain
you are trying to close over."* Anchor CHECKED: Futamura 1971 defines the
projections over a given language; Jones/Gomard/Sestoft 1993 state correctness
as *"same behaviour as the original on all inputs"* -- the all-of-domain
quantifier Aaron's formulation has. Jones-optimality is a QUALITY bar, not the
completeness bar. Aaron **sharpens** the literature by quantifying over the
domain rather than a language's inputs; stated as his, not claimed to be in
Futamura. **And the condition fails here by my own measurement: 38 of 107
targets (36%) carry no CI leg**, so the graph is not complete over the CI
domain and the identity does not hold today.

Consequence, the sharpest in the round: **wiring an INCOMPLETE graph buys
incremental behaviour that is unsound at the edges** -- jobs silently skipped
for those 38 blind spots. Completeness is now an **acceptance condition** on
Option 1, and §13 gains a third cheap check: a completeness lint (every target
claims a leg; every job is claimed by a target -- both directions). Plus §10.1,
*what stops it being wired a second time*: the mapping must be DERIVED from the
graph at run time, never 20 hand-maintained `if:` guards.
