## Description

docs(081M23QZTS3087G0R003T4JP4S): graphics/physics roadmap — read the generator at rank 3, and price the fast path

Synthesises the day's measurements into a roadmap and answers the three questions
Aaron named: quaternions or something else; whether to run a "correct" and a
"performance" build; and where the Bayesian work fits.

Four measurements constrain the rest, and two of them refute things I asserted
earlier today.

1. EVERY rank-3 projection of a 2-complex self-intersects — Whitney (1936)
   general position, since a rank-3 map from R^8 has a 5-dimensional kernel.
   Measured: overdraw spans 598-834 across 116 embeddings (1.39x total), best of
   24 random frames buys 1.16x. I had claimed the pathology "lives in the
   projection" and built a roadmap claim on it. It is a theorem about the object.

2. 27 faces per edge is combinatorial — computed from the face list with no
   coordinates in its call graph. No algebra alters it, so PGA is a performance
   answer and never a correctness one.

3. Exactness comes from ALGEBRAIC INTEGERS, not from dimension 8: H3 closes at
   30 roots in Z[phi], one norm class, rank 3, no projection. What 8D uniquely
   buys is 240 directions and a Weyl group of order 696,729,600, and a 3D
   renderer needs neither.

4. The projection is one-time at load and free per frame. Animating IN 8D costs
   25.2x a 4x4 matrix. A PGA motor and a quaternion are the same computation per
   vertex (3.03 ns each) -- derived before measured, since the weight reduces to
   the rotor norm alone, so the degenerate half does no per-vertex work. Motors
   ARE dual quaternions (Clifford 1873, Study 1903), so "switch to quaternions"
   resolves to "we already do".

On the correct-vs-fast split: yes, with the rule that the exact build is the
ORACLE and every fast path ships with the measured fraction of pixels it moves
against it. Three prices are already measured -- subdivision 7.4x faster and
moves 2.9% of pixels; SAH/SBVH move zero; and a one-ulp scene perturbation moves
2.40%, which is not a choice but a property. 100% correctness is already not on
offer at float32, so the honest question is which approximations are declared.
A fast path whose divergence is unmeasured is a slower correctness claim.

On Bayesian: not rendering -- our 8D->3D map is linear, so UT and EWA agree to
6.7e-16 across every parameterisation. Physics, because the UT exists for
NONLINEAR maps and rigid-body dynamics is one. And the reasoning thesis, where
the fidelity meter's random-carrier control is what decides whether Clifford is
doing any work.

Phases: read the generator at rank 3 (H3, and the 0-bytes-of-geometry property
improves because the seed is smaller); find one renderable object, since 4_21
cannot be an occlusion-correct 3D scene and everything downstream presupposes a
surface with a side; the F# core; GPU RASTERISATION first, because GPU ray
tracing hits the same acceleration-structure wall we did; then physics, whose
first wall is the fixed-point/rational/float decision rather than a solver
choice, because time integration brings division and roots everywhere.

Register: measurements metered, sequencing toy until each phase has a falsifier.
Section 6 lists what the roadmap does not know, including whether H3 yields a
usable scene rather than merely an exact one.
