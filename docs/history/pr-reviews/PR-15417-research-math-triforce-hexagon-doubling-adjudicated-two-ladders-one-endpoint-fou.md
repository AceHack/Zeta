## The one sentence that resolves most of it

There are **two ladders** from dim 1 to dim 8. They land on the same lattices at dims 1, 2, 4, 8 — because those are exactly the normed-division-algebra dimensions (Hurwitz 1898) — and they pass through **different dim-2 rungs**: Cayley–Dickson goes through **Gaussian ℤ[i]** (square, 4 units), laminated/densest goes through **Eisenstein ℤ[ω]** (hexagon, 6 units). Aaron's hexagon is on the laminated ladder. Naming which ladder you are on dissolves most of the remaining tension.

## Where Aaron is right, with the theorem

- **"first or second doubling in Clifford form"** — exactly the scope where the identification holds, and it is not a limitation of our work: **Frobenius (1878)** says the associative real division algebras are ℝ, ℂ, ℍ, every Clifford algebra is associative, so the Clifford picture **provably cannot reach 𝕆**.
- **"triforce middle out is somehow fundamental"** — the "somehow" is `φ(6) = 2`. Computed both ways over n=1..59: `{n : φ(n) ≤ 2} = {n : 2cos(2π/n) ∈ ℤ} = {1,2,3,4,6}`. One theorem chain runs from there to hexagonal shells, densest packing, kissing 6, and ℤ[ω].

## The refutations

**The ladder does not commute with doubling.** Computed: CD-doubling ℤ[i] gives Lipschitz ℤ⁴ at **index 2** inside Hurwitz; doubling Hurwitz gives D₄⊕D₄ at **index 4** inside E₈, holding **exactly 48** of the 240 roots. The maximal order needs half-integer glue every time.

**Alexa's associativity-boundary prediction fails, informatively.** The in-tree split is indexed by code dimension `k` (`dim M = 2^(N−k)` vs `dim A = 2^N`), not by doubling level `N`; the uncoded tower is homoiconic at **every** `N` forever; and **nothing on either side is non-associative**. The two "3"s do not even line up — at `N=3` the adinkra is still homoiconic while CD is already in 𝕆. **But the intuition was good and it paid off**: the associativity boundary *is* visible in this synthesis, one level up — the 240 unit Cayley integers form a **Moufang loop, not a group**, while 2/6/24 are genuine groups. Recorded as a generator that worked.

**`FourCorner`'s C₄ claim is a label, and the refutation is harder than V₄.** The type is a record with three **idempotent** field setters. No transition function, no element of order 4; the reachable-state structure is a join-semilattice with **no inverses**. And every finite subgroup of ℂ\* is cyclic, so V₄ could not be a unit group even if the setters were involutions. `C₄` / `i-rotation` occur in exactly one place in the whole repo — that docstring.
