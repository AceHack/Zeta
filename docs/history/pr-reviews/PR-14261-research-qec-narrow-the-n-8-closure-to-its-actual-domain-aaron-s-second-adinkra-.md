research(qec): narrow the N=8 closure to its actual domain -- Aaron's second adinkra family checked

Aaron raised, after #14224 shipped: "we also have a 2nd type of adinkra -- a non-coded
adinkra that keeps homoiconicity. For our regular coded adinkra it's not homoiconic
except in its colored sub-algebras... We generate the E8 Lie group from one and the E8
algebra from the other type."

HE IS REMEMBERING WORK, NOT AN INTENTION, and to a level of precision worth recording.
Two of the three claims are in-tree theorems with a mechanised falsifier
(tests/Tests.FSharp/HomoiconicSeam.Tests.fs); the third is off by exactly one rung. He
hedged with "from what I remember" and his filed caveat is that he indexes memory by
resonance -- on this one the index retrieved correctly.

WHAT IS PROVEN IN-TREE (Lumen, 2026-08-14 and 2026-08-18, both predating my doc):
the uncoded N-cube IS homoiconic -- M is the regular representation of Cl(0,N), 2^N
vertices against a 2^N algebra. The coded one is NOT: quotienting drops the vertex module
to 2^(N-k) while Cl(0,N) stays 2^N, defect = 2^k, quantised, so there is no "nearly
homoiconic". And "except in its colored sub-algebras" is V4 almost verbatim: M IS free of
rank 1 over a Cl(0,N-k) subalgebra generated by N-k of the colours, verified exhaustively
(20,248 tested, 0 free at the wrong size). Exactly k colours act without being named, and
WHICH k is a convention (56 of 70 valid choices at N=8), not a fact.

DOES IT NARROW MY CLOSURE? YES -- BUT NOT WHERE THE CONCERN POINTED. The worry was that a
non-coded family sits outside the enumeration's domain. It does not: under DFGHIL the
uncoded N-cube IS the C = 0 case, dim C = 0, inside the domain. I omitted the row as
degenerate, which was a presentation error, not a gap. Computed: dim 0 -> C^perp = F_2^8
-> [[8,8,1]]. Distance 1, corrects nothing. The row is now in the table and it STRENGTHENS
the closure -- the uncoded family is not an escape hatch, it is the worst row on the same
ladder. Same shape as the k=0 theorem: homoiconicity is bought by declining the quotient,
and the quotient is exactly where a quantum code's protection lives. The two trade against
the same coordinate in opposite directions.

WHERE I DID OVER-CLAIM, and it is a different place: my enumeration ranges over
CSS(C^perp, C^perp) only. It does not cover asymmetric CSS, and it does not cover non-CSS
stabiliser codes -- under CRSS most isotropic subspaces do not split into X and Z parts.
That gap is not empty: [[8,3,3]] (CRSS 1997) encodes 3 and corrects 1, beating every row in
my table. CITED, NOT COMPUTED -- I did not enumerate the 8-qubit stabiliser space and do
not claim to have. So "the direction is permanently retired" is replaced by "closed over
the adinkra category". The routing verdict stands, because the reason was never "no 8-qubit
code exists"; it was "no code in our lineage at N=8 is useful", which is what was computed.
[[8,3,3]] does not come from a binary code at all, so it is not an adinkra object.

THE E8 CLAIM -- TWO OF THREE. src/Core/CliffordPeriodicity.fs:172-231 answers it directly
and was written 2026-08-18 to almost this question. Coded ([8,4], Construction A) yields
the E8 LATTICE / root system, 240 minimal vectors. Uncoded (bivector/spinor tower,
quotients nothing) yields the E8 LIE ALGEBRA, e8 = so(16) + spinor half, 120 + 128 = 248.
The split is real and is exactly Aaron's; the file itself says the two routes "do not
produce the same object". His ALGEBRA half is exactly right and on the right family. His
GROUP half is off by one rung -- the coded route gives the LATTICE, not the Lie group, and
nothing in the tree constructs the E8 Lie group. Group, algebra and lattice are three
objects, so that is a correction rather than a quibble. The bridge is in-tree too:
248 = 8 + 240 = 120 + 128, two decompositions of one algebra against two maximal
subalgebras (Cartan, a Z^8 grading, versus so(16), a Z_2 grading).
