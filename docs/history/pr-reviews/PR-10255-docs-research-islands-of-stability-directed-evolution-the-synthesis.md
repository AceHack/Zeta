## James's contribution — islands of *stability*

I'd recorded that the island-model GA framing governs exploration/exploitation but says nothing about **stale authority**. The nuclear reading closes it, because those islands are islands **of stability** — and stability is the missing quantity.

| nuclear | substrate |
|---|---|
| half-life | how long a configuration persists **without external correction** |
| sea of instability | configurations needing constant coordination to survive |
| island of stability | a configuration that holds together **while partitioned** |
| predicted-but-unreached islands | the frontier — what does not reduce |

**R8's stale-authority concern was never a separate axis** — it's the half-life of the configuration. The two island framings measure the same thing from opposite ends.

And the valuable islands are the **unreached** ones, inferred from shell structure rather than observed — the same claim as `DoesNotReduce`.

## Directed, not random

A classical GA assumes undirected variation, which is why it needs enormous populations. Each derivation here is a reasoning agent **aiming at the spec**. Hence: **n = 2 sufficed** for twelve spec defects; **a losing island is still informative** (B lost on substance and still gave the deferred-half type sketch and the only R11); and **selection can act on the reasoning**, not just the artifact — a signal undirected evolution doesn't have, and one generation 0 nearly threw away.

**Anchor kept honest:** Arnold's directed evolution is the closest name, but her *mutations* are random and only her *selection* is directed. Ours are directed on both sides. Claiming her method wholesale would overstate it.
