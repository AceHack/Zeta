## Description

Aaron recalled a "hall of famous mathematicians and their special numbers" he thought was lost. It was never lost — it was **never minted**: produced 2026-07-10, offered as a durable doc, and the thread moved on. It survived only in his personal paste archive.

## What this adds beyond the original

**The list has a proper name:** the **special values of the Riemann zeta function** ("zeta values"; MZVs in the multi-variable case). The organizing principle — and the better recall handle — is **Bernoulli numbers**:

- `ζ(−n) = −B_{n+1}/(n+1)` → B₂ = 1/6 gives **−1/12**; B₄ = −1/30 gives **+1/120**
- `ζ(2n)` has its own B_{2n} formula → B₂ again gives **π²/6**
- `ζ(3)` has **no closed form**, which is exactly why Apéry 1979 was a sensation

One constant, B₂ = 1/6, generates both π²/6 and −1/12.

Aaron's near-miss ("I kept wanting to say Euler's numbers") is recorded as a genuine sibling, not a slip: Euler numbers stand to the **Dirichlet beta** function exactly as Bernoulli numbers stand to ζ.

## The finding runs deflationary on our own code

`src/Core.TypeScript/oracle/berry-keating-spectral-check.ts` notes the tick-sampling operator's `B₂/2! = +1/12` against `ζ(−1) = −1/12`, opposite signs, held at §B. Section 1 **explains** that match — both are Bernoulli expansions of the same constant — and explaining a coincidence **removes its evidential value for the deeper claim**. Status unchanged (§B); support weaker than the correspondence made it look, not stronger.

That direction is worth recording because the instinct runs the other way.
