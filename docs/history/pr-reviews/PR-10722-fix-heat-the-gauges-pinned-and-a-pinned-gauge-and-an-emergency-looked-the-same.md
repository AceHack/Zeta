## Description

Applies PR #10695's principle — **an encoder must be a total, injective function from its data's actual domain onto its channel, or non-injective only in a declared, bounded way** — to the four sites #10695 measured and did not fix.

All four defects were **reproduced against `origin/main@f63307c17` before anything changed**. Every number below was produced by running the code.

## The one that mattered most, and why it ranks first

`temperatureBand` pinned at `critical` from 11 units upward. On the live darkhall path, units **11, 100, 1000 and 1,000,000 rendered as one picture**.

That is the failure class Aaron named **"correlated coincidence over time"**: it varies plausibly, it correlates with what you care about, it measures the wrong thing, and repeated observation *strengthens* false confidence rather than exposing it. A stuck gauge and a genuine emergency were indistinguishable, and each extra reading made an operator more sure, not less.

```
                    BEFORE                          AFTER
units | ppm       | band         units | ppm       | band     | fidelity

  100 |  1000000  | critical       100 |   416138  | hot      | exact
 1000 |  1000000  | critical      1000 |   622951  | hot      | exact
1000000| 1000000  | critical    1000000|  1000000  | critical | saturated

distinct (ppm,band) pictures for {11,100,1000,1000000}:  1  ->  4
```

## Per-site choice — the sites are not alike

The deciding fact is **who else byte-locks the values**. `temperatureBand` and `blackBodyRadiancePpm` have F# counterparts in `src/Core/Heat.fs` (the identical integer double-floor, in `int64`) plus treaty cases in `heat-signals-treaty.json`. `heatReceiptPpm` has **no F#, Q# or treaty counterpart at all** — which is what makes widening available there and nowhere else.

| site | option | why this one |
|---|---|---|
| `heatReceiptPpm` | **1 — widen** | TypeScript-only, values free to move. `log1p` with a declared **65,536**-unit ceiling (was an implicit linear **16**). Injectivity verified exhaustively: image 65,537 of 65,537, strictly increasing, tightest realised gap exactly 1 ppm. `131,072` is **not** injective (image 121,755) — the ceiling is measured, not guessed. |
| `temperatureBand` | **3 — refine input** + **2 — declare ceiling** | The four band tokens are a four-oracle treaty and cannot be widened unilaterally. But `number` is wider than the treaty domain (`int` in `[0,1e6]`), so the branch relocates into `TemperatureBandReading.verdict`. **Tokens unchanged.** |
| `blackBodyRadiancePpm` | **2 — declare the bound** | Values byte-locked **and** the floor is mathematically forced: a fourth-power law over six decades of input needs twenty-four decades of output; the channel has six. At `T=31,623` the true radiance is exactly 1.0 ppm; below that it is genuinely sub-ppm. Naming the floor is the honest move; faking resolution is not. |
| `SocietalDoraSvg.pct` | **3 — refine input** | New `UnitInterval` + smart constructor. `percent` is now total and branch-free; the branch moved to one site in `render`, where out-of-range draws a **visibly broken dial** instead of a plausible bar. |

Nothing was "fixed" by removing branches. `blackBodyRadiancePpm` is branch-free and destroys information anyway — `floor`/`round`/`trunc` are branch-free destroyers, which is exactly why the principle is injectivity and not "no `if` statements".

## Found, unreported, and worse in the fail-dangerous direction

The clamps made **missing measurements reassuring**:

```
temperatureBand(NaN)      = "cold"      heatReceiptPpm(NaN)      = 0
temperatureBand(Infinity) = "cold"      heatReceiptPpm(Infinity) = 0
temperatureBand(-1)       = "cold"      F#: pct(nan)      -> confident 0% bar
                                        F#: pct(infinity) -> confident 100% bar
```

The reported defects **over**-alarm; these **under**-alarm — a dead sensor rendered as a calm room. Also found: `heatReceiptPpm` collided at the bottom (`0`, `-5`, `0.5`, `0.999`, `NaN`, `Infinity` all → `0`), and `blackBodyRadiancePpm` is non-injective across its *whole* range, not just the dead zone (1,000,001 temperatures → 515,562 distinct radiances).

## Corrections to my own work

- My **first probe reported "lie factor 62.5"** for `LF(16→1000)` by dividing the ratios in the wrong order, which would have made the brief look mislabelled. Tufte's orientation is `[e(b)/e(a)] / [b/a]`, which reproduces the reported **0.0160 exactly**. The report was right; my measurement was wrong. The corrected probe agrees with all three published lie factors.
- A **first version of the log-protocol test** asserted that equal ratios of *units* give equal ppm distance. False for `log1p`, where the invariant is in `(1+units)` — and the test failed. The encoder was faithful; my claim about it was not.

Both are recorded in the commit rather than quietly fixed. No defect in the brief turned out to be misreported — all four reproduced, with one nuance: for `temperatureBand`, units 11–15 differ in ppm but share a band, while from 16 up the ppm is identical too, so two mechanisms compose.
