## Description

## The lever is images, not PVCs — and the disk under both was a vendor number

### 1. Shrinking PVC declarations frees no runner disk (verified, not assumed)

Both dev/CI StorageClasses (`zeta-local-path`, and the `longhorn` alias) sit on
`rancher.io/local-path`. Its node-side provisioner script, in this repo at
`full-ai-cluster/nixos/modules/local-storage.nix`, is:

```sh
mkdir -m 0777 -p "$VOL_DIR"
```

A directory. No quota, and no reference to the requested size at all — the
provisioner exports the size and the script never reads it. A 10Gi PVC consumes
the bytes written and nothing more. Those GiB are scheduling reservations on
Longhorn (metal); on a runner they are not bytes. Recorded in the two values
files carrying the largest declarations, which is where someone goes to
"shrink the PVCs".

### 2. The runner has 77 GiB free, not 14 — MEASURED on run 32585090483

| runner | total | used | **available** | reclaimable extra |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ubuntu-24.04` | 144.26 GiB | 67.18 | **77.06 GiB** | 20.72 GiB |
| `ubuntu-24.04-arm` | 144.26 GiB | 45.23 | **99.02 GiB** | 4.52 GiB |

One filesystem each. **No `/mnt`.** Images land on `/var/lib/docker`, on `/`.

`runnerEnvelope.freeDiskGib: 14` was GitHub's published figure, carried because
`--measure-runner` reads `/proc` for CPU and memory and read no disk. It was
wrong by more than 5x, and every "2.2x over / 5.5x over" verdict divided by it
was wrong in the direction that under-reports what fits. The row now reads
**70** — a conservative floor beneath both measurements — and
`runner-disk.ts --check-envelope` is **armed** in both live jobs. Green on this
PR, on both runners.

The 20.72 GiB of preinstalled toolchains (android 10.81, ghcup 3.66, swift 3.29,
CodeQL 1.69, powershell 1.27) is measured and **not deleted**: nothing needs it
yet, and reclaiming costs wall clock on every run that does it. `--reclaim`
exists, times itself, and refuses `/usr/share/dotnet` by name.

### 3. Five apps are 84% of the image bytes, and four are already excluded

`image-footprint.ts --report`, measured 2026-08-22 from `helm template` at each
pinned targetRevision plus the registry's linux/amd64 manifests:

| cohort | apps | images | compressed | est. on disk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| asserted | 33 | 71 | 3.05 GB | 8.15 GB |
| applied | 37 | 80 | 12.84 GB | 34.29 GB |
| all | 46 | 126 | 29.83 GB | 79.66 GB |

Top 5: `vllm` 9.11 · `hindsight` 9.05 · `gitlab` 4.42 · `ollama` 1.59 ·
`platform` 0.82 GB. **Only `hindsight` is applied to the dev lane.** So the
applied cohort's 34 GB is 24 GB of hindsight and 10 GB of everything else.

**Units are load-bearing here** and are now computed in bytes rather than left to
the reader: image sizes are decimal GB, disk is binary GiB, and 77.06 GiB is
82.74 GB — so all 46 apps' images fit the measured x64 runner by ~3 GB, and are
~8.8 GB over the conservative declared budget the report prints against.

### What this changes

Disk is no longer the binding constraint anyone thought it was. A 23 GB image is
testable on a hosted runner today. The partition work stays the structural
answer for CPU (8106m of requests against 4000m — CPU is the real ceiling), but
it does not need to be sized around a 14 GiB disk that does not exist.

Not taken, recorded instead: upstream ships `hindsight-api` 0.5.1 at 1.38 GB and
0.5.1-slim at 0.69 GB against the pinned 0.1.1's 8.81 GB, there is no slim
variant at the pin, and chart 0.3.0 does read `api.image.tag` — verified in the
render. Whether app 0.5.x runs under chart 0.3.0's templates is untested, so it
is an option someone can take, not one this PR takes.
