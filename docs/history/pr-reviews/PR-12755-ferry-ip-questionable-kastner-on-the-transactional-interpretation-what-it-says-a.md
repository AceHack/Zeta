## The link to our meter

Kastner's complaint is that the conventional formalism has **no internal means of saying a measurement occurred** — an outcome is inserted from outside at an arbitrary boundary (Heisenberg's cut). Her empirical claim is correspondingly odd and she says so plainly: both theories agree on Born probabilities, so the distinction isn't a new number. **Measurement itself is the anomaly.**

Stripped of the physics, that's the shape we spent today inside:

> The failure is not a wrong answer. It is the **absence of an account** — and the absence reads as a pass.

A golden vector nothing read. A guard whose removal no test could detect. A package-index `200` taken as proof of installability. "Main is green" from a query that excluded the failing job. **Nothing produced a false value; something failed to produce a value at all.**

And the `db/uncertainty/` ledger already made the transactional-shaped choice before anyone here read Kastner:

| Kastner | our metering |
|---|---|
| an offer wave alone yields no outcome | a self-asserted ΔU is not a measurement — `measure.ts` refuses it |
| the absorber must actively confirm | the ΔU needs its falsifier — the test that fails without the fix |
| the **transaction** is the measurement | the **pair** is; neither half is |
| observer ≠ measurer | metered by the falsifier, not by anyone watching |

Same reason privacy budget is credited only by others' attestations, and a capability is a derivative of a **witnessed** self-claim.

## Filed as coincidence, not identification

Per `numerology-vs-number-theory`, the note names five other structures with the same two-sided-handshake shape — 2PC, interactive proofs, Byzantine quorum, Merkle receipts, double-entry bookkeeping. **The shape does not pick out TI.** TI is an anchor for an intuition we already implemented, dated to Wheeler–Feynman 1945 rather than to us. It is not evidence for our design.
