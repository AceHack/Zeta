## 1. The claim, and why it splits

Intel's VP of advanced technology programs, quoted: *"These are the most advanced semiconductor
facilities not only in the US but in the world."* The video's own finding is that **this is
simultaneously true and false depending on which axis you measure**, which is more useful than
either verdict alone.

**Two new technologies in 18A:**

- **RibbonFET** — gate-all-around. The gate wraps **all four sides** of the channel rather than
  three (FinFET), as four stacked horizontal ribbons per transistor. The manufacturing step that
  makes it hard: grow alternating Si/SiGe layers, then **selectively dissolve every germanium
  layer** so the silicon ribbons hang suspended, held only at their ends — then grow gate material
  into gaps a few nanometres tall, uniformly, across a 300 mm wafer, billions of times. A defect
  there is *buried under the structure where inspection can barely see it*.
- **PowerVia** — backside power delivery. Move the entire power network to the **back** of the
  wafer, freeing the front side for signal routing alone.

**The reported gains** (Intel's VLSI figures, vs Intel 3): +25% frequency at equal power, or −36%
power at equal frequency; >30% density. From PowerVia specifically: 8–10% density purely from the
reorganisation, via resistance −24 to −49%, and **voltage droop improved up to 10×**.

That droop number is the one worth understanding. When a chip demands a sudden current burst, the
voltage sags, and **chips must be designed to work at the bottom of that sag** — so droop forces
lower frequency or higher voltage than the silicon could otherwise sustain. Killing droop hands that
margin back.

**Where it wins and loses, measured by different parties:**

| axis | verdict |
|---|---|
| **density** | **TSMC N2 wins, not close** — 313 Mtr/mm² vs 238 for 18A and 231 for Samsung (TechInsights, high-density standard cell). ~31% more transistors in the same area. SRAM tells the same story |
| **perf/watt** | analyst scoring puts 18A ahead of N2 and SF2 — *though the video flags that these composites extrapolate from older nodes and the methodology is debated* |
| **backside power** | **Intel is alone in production.** TSMC's Super Power Rail arrives with A16, moved from late 2026 to **2027**; Samsung has talked about it for years without shipping |
| **volume** | **Intel loses by an order of magnitude** — ~30k wafers/month on 18A against TSMC targeting 100k/month N2 by end-2026 |

## 2. Register — what is checkable and what is not

This is a **popular secondary summary**. The density figures are attributed to TechInsights and are
the kind of thing independently measured; the perf/watt claims are **Intel's own** VLSI numbers, and
the composite analyst scores are flagged *by the source itself* as methodologically debated. The
financial figures (foundry revenue $5.8B against a $2.1B quarterly operating loss; **external**

## 3. Why Aaron is right that this is worth tracking

His framing — *"one of the most geopolitical electronics in the world"* — is the load-bearing part,
and it connects to a thesis already on file.

**`project_tsmc_precision_bet_honest_meter_is_the_financial_thesis`** records the position that
**vendor differentiation shrinks**, so an honest decorrelation/contribution meter becomes the scarce
good — *"the moat is legitimacy not capex, and checkability keeps it an oracle rather than a hub."*

This node is a **live test of the first half of that**, and the split verdict is the evidence:

> Two leading-edge processes now exist, each genuinely better on a *different* axis, and **neither
> dominates**. Density says TSMC; backside power and perf/watt say Intel; volume says TSMC by 10×.
> A buyer cannot rank them on one number — they must decide **which axis their workload lives on**.

That is differentiation shrinking *in the specific sense the thesis predicts*: not that the vendors
converge, but that **no single scalar orders them**, so the choice moves from "who is ahead" to
"what are you optimising." Which is exactly the condition under which an honest meter is worth more
than a benchmark.

**And the exit/hub distinction applies directly.**
`itron-hub-patent-boundary-p2p-is-the-upgrade` holds that the discriminator is **exit, not degree** —
concentration is fine where you can route around it, and a hub where you cannot. For a decade there
was **one** place to get the most advanced silicon, in *"the most contested strait on Earth."* That
is concentration with **no exit** — the definition of a hub in that rule's sense, at civilisational
scale. A second leading-edge source does not reduce concentration much (30k vs 100k wafers), but it
**creates an exit**, and under that rule the exit is what matters. The video reaches the same place
independently: *"competition at the leading edge is the single healthiest thing that could happen to
computing."*

**One caution, in the register this repo uses.** Backside power's ~10× droop improvement is a real
architectural gain and it is *not* a scoring artifact — but the perf/watt lead rests on composite
scores the source itself calls debated, while the density loss is a direct measurement. **A measured
loss and a scored win are not the same evidence class**, and any use of this material should keep
them apart.

## 4. What would actually be worth tracking

Not the marketing. Three checkable things, on a schedule:

1. **Does TSMC's A16 ship in 2027?** That is when Intel's sole structural lead ends, and it has
   already slipped once.
2. **External foundry revenue.** $293M on a $5.8B base is the number that says whether anyone
   besides Intel is buying. Internal products do not make a foundry.
3. **14A customer commitments**, expected H2 2026 → H1 2027. The video is explicit that 18A is not
   the exam; 14A is.
