## Spent codemods

`patch-deregister.js`, `patch-observe.js`, `patch-register-node.js`, `patch-registry.js` are one-shot scripts that read a source file, string-replace, and write it back. They were **applied** — their targets are present in the committed sources — and **nothing references them** (checked `package.json`, `.github/`, `src/`, `docs/`). They aren't part of the build and sat loose at the repo root. Git keeps them in history if anyone needs to re-read one.

## Thrash guard

It refused with a bare `return world`, which is indistinguishable from a retraction that simply had nothing to undo. That ambiguity is exactly how a bare `|| true` kept a dead CI job invisible in this repo for months — and it's the same defect the mutation runner's `unresolved` outcome exists to prevent: **a thing that did not happen must not look like a thing that succeeded.**

**Announced, not appended — and that distinction is load-bearing rather than stylistic.** The counter walks *backward* over trailing `retract_time` entries and stops at the first entry of any other type. Recording the refusal in the ledger would break that trailing run, reset the count to zero, and **re-arm the very thrash the guard exists to stop**. The refusal is real, but it must not become an event. A `console.warn` says so without touching state.
