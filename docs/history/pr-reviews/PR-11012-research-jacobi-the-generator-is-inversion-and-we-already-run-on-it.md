## Description

Aaron noticed that the **Jacobian conjecture** and our **J-space / global-workspace** work carry the same name, and asked the right question about it:

> *"i'm trying to 0 in on his generator function. i like to understand mathematicians and physicists brains in their entirety not just bits and pieces because this is often where unnoticed things live."*

My first answer was a correction — *surname collision* — which is true at the level of **objects** and misses the level he was working at. He was not claiming the objects are the same; he was asking for the **generator** that produced both. Better question, and it has an answer.

## The generator

> **"Man muss immer umkehren"** — one must always invert. (Jacobi's own motto.)

- **Elliptic functions (1829)** — Legendre studied elliptic *integrals*; Abel and Jacobi **inverted** them into *functions*. Double periodicity falls out of the inversion.
- **Jacobian determinant** — the **local invertibility criterion** (inverse function theorem), not change-of-variables bookkeeping.
- **Jacobian conjecture** — that same question pushed **local → global**.
- **Hamilton–Jacobi theory** — invert the dynamics into solvability.

## The unnoticed thing, exactly where he predicted

**Hamilton–Jacobi theory is where "generating function" is a technical term** — Jacobi formalised the function whose derivatives generate a canonical transformation. Aaron asked for Jacobi's *generator function*; Jacobi formalised the concept of one, and it is the least-famous of his four, so neither "Jacobian" headline would ever surface it. Recorded as a validated instance of his method, not as evidence for a mathematical claim.

## Why it keeps landing near us

A shared **method**, not a shared name: Meijer's `IEnumerable ⇄ IObservable`, `reify`/`apply`, emit/retract, Z-set retraction as the inverse element, and generate-from-the-free-object are all inversions. That licenses a **falsifiable prediction** — we should keep rediscovering his objects *structurally* rather than by name-match — which is what makes it a claim rather than an appreciation.
