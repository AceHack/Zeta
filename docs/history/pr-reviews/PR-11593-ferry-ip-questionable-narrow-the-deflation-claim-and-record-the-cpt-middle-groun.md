Two changes, both corrections to my own earlier text on this file.

1. THE DEFLATION CLAIM IS NARROWED. A reviewer restated it back as "the
deflation theorem", which is a promotion it does not earn, and checking
that promotion broke it twice over.

   NOT A THEOREM. No-signalling is a theorem; what I wrote is closer to
   a TAUTOLOGY -- if an account reproduces the statistics then it is by
   construction indistinguishable on those statistics. True, shallow,
   and it earns none of the authority "theorem" lends. It is a
   definition of what makes something an interpretation.

   ITS ANTECEDENT FAILS FOR THE FERRIED PAPER. Hossenfelder says at 3:33
   that the two-time proposal "does not make exactly the same
   predictions as ordinary quantum mechanics" -- it predicts a
   distance-dependent excess correlation standard QM forbids. So it does
   not reproduce quantum statistics, my conditional does not apply, and
   it is TESTABLE. The counterexample sat four sections above the claim.

   SO THE COMFORT I OFFERED WAS TOO BROAD, and the fix favours Aaron.
   "Your math would deflate anyway" holds only for the INTERPRETATION
   class. For a retrocausal THEORY -- one predicting something new --
   deflation IS informative: it says each attempt keeps landing in the
   interpretation class instead of producing different predictions.
   Concrete next move: when a formalisation deflates, ask what it would
   have to predict differently in order not to.

   Both readings kept -- the first is what I wrote, the second is what
   survived checking it.

   Also: Price 1996, Cramer 1986, Wharton & Argaman 2020 are now marked
   CITED, NOT CHECKED. Written from recall, none opened. The anchor rule
   requires an entailment check, so they are leads, not support.

2. OBSERVATION 7 records Aaron's CPT / middle-ground framing.

   THE MONOPOLE HALF IS A PHYSICAL FACT stronger than the analogy needs:
   you cannot isolate a magnetic pole -- cut a magnet, get two magnets --
   and none has been observed. The middle ground is FORCED; nature does
   not supply the isolated pole to collapse to. (Dirac 1931: one WOULD
   explain charge quantisation, which is why the searches continue.)

   THE CPT HALF IS A GENUINE THEOREM (Lueders, Pauli, Bell c.1954-55),
   and the detail that matters is that T ALONE IS NOT a symmetry --
   direct T-violation measured at BaBar 2012, CP violation long
   established. The pair is lawful where neither member is. Labelled
   ANALOGY: CPT entails no epistemic policy.

   THE FOLK DEFINITION lands on the right casualty -- "one or the other
   or both" is the failure of exclusive disjunction, i.e. counterfactual
   definiteness, which Bell tests rule out. Boundary stated: a
   superposition is not a classical "both are true" and is not a
   mixture; |A>+|B> and a 50/50 mixture differ measurably, and
   distinguishing them is the content of an interference experiment --
   which is also what the ferried paper's proposed test does.

   AND IT IS ALREADY A TYPE IN THE TREE. PR #11579 introduced
   `Epistemic` in src/Core/SimVerb.fs, the first five-way
   known/unknown/assumed/disputed/decided type in the repo; before it
   `Disputed` had ZERO occurrences in any .fs or .ts. Recorded as an
   independent commitment to the same discipline, NOT as evidence for
   the CPT reading.
