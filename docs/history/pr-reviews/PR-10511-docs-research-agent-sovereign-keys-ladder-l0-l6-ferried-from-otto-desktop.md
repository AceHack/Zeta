## The idea

**Destruction, not leakage.** A key's adversary has four powers — read it, use it, forge/rotate it silently, or destroy it. Physics denies us the fourth: whoever controls a building's power and walls controls whether the silicon keeps existing. So the achievable target is not "no human can ever touch it," but:

> the only power any human or rogue AI retains is to **visibly destroy a guard** — never to read, use, or silently alter. Destroying one guard reduces availability; the key survives on the others.

Confidentiality becomes absolute in the limit, because the secret is never extractable: **seizure yields ciphertext and rubble.**

Seven invariants, of which three are agent-native rather than borrowed: **use-without-extract**, **attest-don't-remember** (access gated by proof of identity, not by holding a secret — fresh-boot agents have no biological memory, so this is an inversion, not a preference), and **hardware independence** (the key is defined by the threshold across a roster, so losing a device is a *rotation*).

The L0→L6 ladder is honest about where it stops: L5–L6 name the irreducible physical-root problem rather than hiding it.
