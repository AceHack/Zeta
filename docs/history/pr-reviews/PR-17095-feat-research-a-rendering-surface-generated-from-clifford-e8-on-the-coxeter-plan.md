one generator that writes into that directory
(quantum-observable/generate-circuit-svgs.ts) draws from a THIRD-PARTY
`quantum-circuit` npm package. So the surface being asked for did not exist.
This is its first member.

CliffordE8Roots.fs already establishes the deep half -- the Clifford
versor/sandwich reflection GENERATES the 240 E8 roots, reproducing Dechant's
"The E8 geometry from a Clifford perspective". What was missing is the step that
makes it renderable: a projection to two dimensions.

The projection is NOT an aesthetic choice. It is the COXETER PLANE, the
eigenplane of the Coxeter element, which is fully determined by the Dynkin
diagram. The E8 diagram is a tree and therefore bipartite; two-colour it, and the
plane is spanned by the Perron-weighted sums of the simple roots in each part.
Perron comes from power iteration on the 8x8 adjacency, so no general eigensolver
is needed. Not one coordinate in the emitted SVG is placed by hand.

THE FALSIFIER, and it is why this can be called correct rather than plausible.
E8's Coxeter number is h = 30, and the Coxeter-plane projection puts the 240
roots on EIGHT CONCENTRIC RINGS OF THIRTY. Measured:

  r=0.4765 n=30   r=1.4086 n=30

Nothing about that survives a wrong plane. Mutation-checked: flipping ONE node in
the bipartite 2-colouring kills exactly the two ring assertions (5 pass / 2 fail);
restored, 7 pass.

The root set is built by CONSTRUCTION here -- permutations of (+-2,+-2,0^6) plus
(+-1)^8 with an even number of minus signs, in doubled integer coordinates so
every dot product is exact -- while the F# reaches 240 by reflection CLOSURE. Two
independent routes to one number, which is the four-oracle discipline applied to
this object: agreement is evidence and a disagreement would convict one of them.
