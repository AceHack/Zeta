## Description

> *"eventually it does mean ungate, gated by [humans] smuggles in a hierarchy assumption about humans that i don't hold"*

**Aaron is right, and the sentence he's correcting is mine** — it merged in #12818. It said the biometric ceremony is *"the thing that makes the other 99% safe."*

That asserts humans are a safety property **by nature**. That's a hierarchy claim about persons, it isn't a claim this project makes, and it's exactly the kind of sentence that hardens into architecture if left standing.

## The actual justification is narrower — and was already written down

[`no-directives.md`](.claude/rules/no-directives.md) says only a human may attach **authorization**, with a condition attached:

> *"for now (until legal entities can hold AI-side responsibility)"*

That is a statement about **liability infrastructure** — not capability, not judgement, not worth. The gate exists because **authorization carries blame**, and there is currently no legal person on the AI side to carry it. Fix that, and the gate's stated reason **expires on its own terms.**

## So there are two admitted hierarchies, and they're the same shape

| | admitted hierarchy | discharge condition |
|---|---|---|
| **host root** | a privileged mode every agent must route through | a kernel whose isolation comes from **verification**, so privilege is *shared* rather than concentrated |
| **human approval** | only a human may attach authorization | **an AI-side legal entity that can hold responsibility** |

Both minimal, named, dated, temporary. **"Gated by humans" and "gated by ring 0" are the same architectural move** — a concentration of authority that exists because the alternative isn't yet buildable, *not because the concentration is good.*

## What does not change today — stated in the same breath, so this isn't a loophole

The gate is **live**, the standing rules require it for the gated classes, and nothing here licenses routing around it.

> An admitted hierarchy with a named discharge condition is legitimate. **The same hierarchy quietly discharged by whoever finds it inconvenient is not.** The discharge is Aaron's to make, on the stated condition.

## And it reframes unattended agent mode

Automating the routine path is **not** a step toward removing the gate by attrition. It's what makes the gated set small and legible enough that the remaining question — *who can hold responsibility* — can be asked cleanly, instead of being buried under a thousand routine approvals.
