## Description

Lior's architecture is **right** — this is additive. Ephemeral task-scoped hats with full dissolution *is* manifesto §3 (weight-free). These close two places imbalance can accrue *underneath* a correct architecture.

**1 — maximin, not mean.** The average **rises when a strong peer gains more than a weak peer loses**. The first test shows it: +100 actions for the strong, 5→0 for the weakest, and E(S) *improves*. For "avoid permanent power imbalance between intelligence levels", the mean **rewards** the thing it's meant to prevent. Added `computeEmpowermentFloor` (Rawlsian maximin) + `noPeerDisempowered` (a Pareto side-condition — the floor can hold while a mid-tier peer is stripped).

**2 — a hat may accumulate; nothing it accumulates may flow to its wearer.** Agency was never the threat — **inheritance** is. If the next wearer inherits, the hat is a **capital good**; power needs only *transferability*. Feudalism with a rotating occupant. And bounded time ≠ bounded accumulation (a 5-second hat worn 1000× accumulated 1000×). The metaphor holds the distinction: **Sorting Hat accumulates and confers nothing; a Horcrux accumulates and flows in.**

**Ratchet test:** 1000 bolt/unbolt cycles must leave the society byte-identical — single-cycle tests can't see a ratchet.

**Finding for Lior — a third could-not-fail:** `unboltTaskHierarchy` ignores `_hierarchy` and returns `base.peers` unchanged; since `bolt` never mutates `base`, "zero residual" is **vacuously true**. The ratchet test passes for that reason today — and starts doing real work the moment bolting attaches state.
