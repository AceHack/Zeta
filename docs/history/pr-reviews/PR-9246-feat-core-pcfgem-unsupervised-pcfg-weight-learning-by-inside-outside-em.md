## Description

The **"where do the production weights come from"** answer: the grammar **learns its own weights from a corpus** — no hand-tuning. Classic inside–outside EM (Baker; Lari–Young; Dempster–Laird–Rubin):

- **E-step** — `Sppf.expectedCounts`: for each production, its expected use across all parses of an input (family posteriors from inside–outside), summed over the corpus. **The inside–outside expected counts ARE the EM E-step.**
- **M-step** — `PcfgEm.step`: re-estimate each production's weight = expected count normalized over the productions sharing its LHS.

Iterate; corpus likelihood increases monotonically to a local max.

In your **SSAS** frame: **training the decision forest** — the weights become *learned* `NodeDistribution`s, fit to data rather than set.

## Proofs (4/4)
- **frequency recovery** — `S→a|b` on a **3a:1b** corpus ⇒ learned **0.75 / 0.25**;
- **valid distributions** — per-LHS weights sum to 1;
- **EM improves likelihood** — learned beats uniform (the monotonicity guarantee);
- **E-step** — `expectedCounts` gives the right expected usage.

Self-contained (inside–outside only). Next (resume): **parses → ISA lowering** (distribution over *executable* programs) or the **loopy/EP + emotional-propagation** rung (math-team).
