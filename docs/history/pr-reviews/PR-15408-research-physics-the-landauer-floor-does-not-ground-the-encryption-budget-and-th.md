## The physics does not carry it

`kT ln 2 = 2.871 zJ` at 300 K — the brief's 2.75 zJ is the value at **287.4 K**. A DRAM write (Horowitz, ISSCC 2014) is **10^9.8** above the floor. Erasing a **zettabyte** at the floor costs **23 J**, a few percent of an AA battery.

The structural objection is worse than the magnitude: **Landauer lower-bounds the *defender's* `erase`; hard money needs a bound on the *attacker's* `un-erase`.** Landauer's arrow runs `logical irreversibility ⟹ dissipation`; the claim needs the converse. The physics is the *receipt*, not the ground.

## The algebra does carry it — but not where it was claimed

Zeros are **dropped**, not retained — `ZSet.fs:211,216,239,251`, `z-set.ts`, `indexed_zset.rs`, **all three agree**. But uniqueness fails: `{(k,5),(k,−1)} → {(k,4)}` is equally non-injective, and on canonical Z-sets `a ↦ a+b` is a **bijection** for known `b`. Retain the delta and everything is free (Bennett 1973).

What survives is stronger: **the zero-crossing is the only operation that removes its own witness** — absence is indistinguishable from never-having-existed. It is the one act that can **blind the meter**, which is exactly why a ledger must instrument it.
