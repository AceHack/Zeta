THE HUMAN ANCHOR. Pali kilesa / Sanskrit klesa -- "defilement",
"affliction" -- a mental state that OBSCURES CLEAR SEEING and thereby
distorts what follows. Three roots in the Theravada Abhidhamma: lobha
(greed), dosa (aversion), moha (delusion); systematised canonically by
Buddhaghosa, Visuddhimagga (5th c. CE). The Sanskrit form also carries
five klesas in Patanjali's Yoga Sutras 2.3, rooted in avidya.

WHY THE IMPORT IS STRUCTURAL, NOT DECORATIVE -- the load-bearing part. A
kilesa is not a debt to repay or a fault to punish; it is a distortion
removed BY BEING SEEN CLEARLY, never by force. That is exactly Aaron's
own argument about the four-corner trace in the same session: the remedy
for hidden influence is a trace that makes hiding impossible, not a
filter that suppresses. Tradition and mechanism agree on the METHOD,
which is what separates a real anchor from physics-as-metaphor.

THE ENGINEERING PEER TERM, so the concept is not import-only.
Integrator windup in control theory -- an integral term accumulating
error while saturated, then distorting subsequent response -- is the
closest standard object. Adjacent: DC offset/drift; confounding. In
Zeta's own vocabulary a kilesi is an UNDECLARED ACCUMULATED INFLUENCE,
precisely what §13 noninterference forbids and what the four-corner
trace surfaces.

PROPOSED GLOSS, MARKED AS PROPOSED IN THREE PLACES (row, Sources table,
§4.6): accumulated undeclared distortion carried on the interrupt (ISR)
path -- bias that accretes on the arrow and colours what the next
observation sees; dissolved by being seen, not by force.
