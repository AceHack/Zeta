## Description

## What

`cross-verify (trust-core oracles + ace suite)` ran **31 distinct audits under one check
name**. When it went red it named none of them. It is now a **31-leg matrix**, each leg
publishing `cross-verify (<id>)`.

Measured live on 2026-08-26: a red `cross-verify` was one audit — `task-zetaid-resolves`,
convicting on a work-item filed under `docs/backlog/P1/` while that audit indexes only
`workitems/`. A false positive in the cheapest audit on the floor held the byte-lock
oracles hostage, and from outside the job log it looked like the oracles had broken. **One
red standing for 31 audits is the same defect class as a log that does not name its failing
step, relocated into the gate's structure.**

> *"if it's a shared gate we want to reduce those to smaller individual ones rather than
> widening one that's monolithic already, we are trying to split our monolith bit by bit."*
> — Aaron, 2026-08-26

## The floor is not weakened, and that is structural rather than promised

`gate-required.needs:` is **byte-identical**. The matrix job keeps the id `cross-verify`,
and GitHub collapses a matrix into one `needs.<job>.result` that is `success` only when
every leg succeeded — so *any audit red ⇒ the gate red* survives the split **by
construction, with no roll-up job to get wrong**. A roll-up job was written first and then
deleted: it cost a job, it changed the declared name the floor resolves through, and it
added a second place the aggregate could be made to fail open.

The job also declares no job-level `if:`, so `gate-skip-verdict.ts` refuses even the state
where it never runs. `cross-verify-roster.test.ts` §"the floor is not weakened" calls that
verdict logic against the committed `gate.yml` for every result GitHub documents.

A decomposition that quietly made 31 audits non-blocking is the worst available outcome
here and an easy one to reach by accident, so it is proved rather than asserted — see the
live demonstration below.

## The roster is derived, not hardcoded — and the two views cannot diverge

The roster lives in `src/Core.TypeScript/ci/cross-verify-roster.ts` and carries each
audit's command plus, **verbatim**, the comment its step carried. That prose is the only
record of why several of these sit on the pre-merge floor at all, and moving the step
without the reason would have been the expensive half of this change.

There are two views of one list — the ids in `gate.yml`'s matrix and the entries in the
roster — and **every leg asserts they are equal as sequences before it runs its own
audit**. Add a roster entry with no leg and all 31 legs go red naming it; add a leg with no
entry and that leg goes red naming it. They can disagree for at most one CI run. That is
the mechanical answer to the divergence that bit `audit-dep-currency.ts` (one hardcoded
path, a root flake invisible for months).

**A dynamic matrix was rejected on purpose.** `fromJSON` of a roster job's output removes
the second list, and it also means the leg names do not exist until that job succeeds. Once
any of them is a required context, **a name that never reports does not fail a PR — it
wedges it**, silently and forever. A static list cannot do that, and it keeps the names
greppable in `gate.yml` for the ruleset promotion and for the `merge_group` coverage lint
the research doc's §6.5 recommends.

|---|---|---|---|
| jobs | 1 | 31 | 31 |
| **runner-seconds** | **58** | **572** | **597** |
| wall-clock | 58 s | 53 s | 61 s |
| `actions/cache` steps in the job | **0** | **0** | **0** |
| cache uploads (`Post` step > 2 s) | 0 | 0 | 0 |

Two samples, because a runner-second figure from one observation is a number rather than a
measurement. Mean **584 against 58**: **Δ = +526 runner-seconds ≈ +8.8 runner-min ≈ +9.3%**
on the research doc's 94-runner-min push run.

**That is higher than the doc's +6.6% estimate, and higher than this PR's own first draft,
which is the more useful fact.** I priced the split at +7.3% from the pre-split job's 13 s
setup. Measured, the legs take **8–20 s each (mean 19 s)** because `checkout` and job
start-up both slow down when 31 jobs schedule at once — so a single job's setup measurement
does not predict 31 concurrent ones. That is the general lesson for the next split, and it
is corrected in `gate.yml`'s comment and the doc rather than left as the guess. The class-0
classification itself I verified rather than took on trust: a leg's full step list is set-up
job, checkout, setup-bun, `bun install`, run the audit, post-setup-bun, post-checkout,
complete — no `actions/cache` step anywhere, and `Post Setup pinned Bun` measured **0 s**,
i.e. a key hit and no upload.

**Cache bytes written: zero.** The split adds 31 cache *restores* of one pinned Bun key and
no writes, so it does not feed the eviction loop the cache work is fighting.

**Wall-clock is flat to slightly better** (53 s / 61 s against 58 s): the legs run in
parallel and the longest (`ace-suite`, 46 s) finishes around when the old serial job did.
The spread between samples is scheduling noise, not signal.

The pre-split job spent **13 s of setup for 44 s of audits**, and 29 of the 31 audits took
**≤ 2 s**. That ratio is why this job was the one to split and a class-1 job (`install.sh`,
46–149 s of setup plus a cache participant) is not — the doc's rule: *a job may be split for
free along a setup boundary it does not cross.*

## The floor falsifier, demonstrated live — and unplanned

I did not need a sabotage commit. Run `32963212370`, the split's first run, produced the
demonstration by itself:

* **`cross-verify (ace-suite)` concluded `failure`** — one leg of 31 — because
  `build-graph.json` had drifted (the two new files are tracked witnesses of the
  `cross-oracle-bytelock` rule, count 10 → 12);
* **the other 30 legs concluded `success`**;
* **`gate (required)` concluded `failure`**, annotating `cross-verify: the job failed`
  beside `gate scope=full: 8/8 floor jobs ran`.

`needs.cross-verify.result` collapsed the matrix to `failure` exactly as the design
requires, the required check went red, and — the actual product of this change — **the red
named the audit.** Pre-split, that identical failure would have been one anonymous
`cross-verify` red standing for 31 audits, and finding the cause would have meant opening
the log.

## Two audits caught this branch, and both were right

Recorded because they are worth more than the fix:

1. **`audit-linter-coverage-vs-invocation.ts`** went red naming five package.json checks —
   `hygiene:dotnet-pin-parity`, `mise-toolchain-couplings`, `no-check-then-use-file-races`,
   `stage0-independence`, `tech-radar-claims` — as invoked by no workflow. Moving their
   commands out of `run:` bodies made them *look* wired to nothing, which is exactly
   backwards: all five run on the required floor of every PR. The audit's model of "where a
   check is invoked from" is what needed updating, and it now includes the roster's
   **command strings and nothing else** — never its prose, because the roster carries every
   replaced step's rationale verbatim and those paragraphs name scripts they do not invoke.
   A whole-file grep would launder a mention into an invocation: the vacuity class arriving
   through the fix for the vacuity class. Three falsifiers added, including one that proves
   a prose-only mention still does not count.

2. **`audit-cluster-tree-consumers.ts`** went red because one line of the
   `image-source-provenance` rationale lists `infra/k8s`. Registered as `prose` with a
   migration target rather than reworded — the verbatim carry-over is the point.

## Falsifiers

23 tests. **8 targeted mutants applied, byte-`cmp`-verified before reading each result, 8
killed:**

| mutant | killed by |
|---|---|
| `fail-fast: true` on the matrix | "the matrix does not fail fast" |
| a job-level `if:` added (a licence to skip) | "every non-success result BLOCKS" + "declares no job-level `if:`" |
| one matrix leg deleted | 6 tests, including parity end-to-end through `--run` |
| a per-audit `run:` step pasted into the job | "no audit can be added except through the roster" |
| `cross-verify` removed from `gate-required.needs` | "`gate (required)` still names `cross-verify`" |
| `assertGateParity` forced true | "every leg checks parity before running its own audit" |
| unknown id treated as a no-op pass | "an unknown id fails loudly" |
| the event gate swallowing its own event | "on its own event it actually runs" |

The first `fail-fast` mutant **survived** and that is recorded rather than quietly retried:
`fail-fast: false` appears three times in `gate.yml` and a first-occurrence replace hit a
different job. Re-targeted by line number, it was killed. A mutant that misses its subject
is a check that did not run.

All 31 audits were also executed locally through the new runner: 30 exit 0, and
`task-zetaid-resolves` exits **2** because the local `PR_BODY` is empty — its documented
refusal, and the control proving the runner really invokes the audit.

## Not done here, deliberately

**Nothing is promoted into `required_status_checks` and nothing is added to
`gate (required)`'s `needs`.** That is a ruleset edit and a gated-class call.
`docs/ci/CROSS-VERIFY-CHECK-NAMES.md` carries the 31 exact strings, the two hazards to
check before promoting (a never-reporting required context wedges rather than fails; a
required context must come from a `merge_group`-triggered workflow under the pre-merge
matrix), and an honest account of what a promotion would and would not buy — it buys
**legibility of the red**, not coverage, because all 31 already block today.

**AH006 is untouched.** Whether `task-zetaid-resolves`'s scope is deliberate migration
pressure is a separate pending decision. This change makes its failure legible, which is the
point.

## Stated limit

Adding 31 jobs per `gate` run takes the workflow from 37 jobs to 67. At the doc's measured
peak of ~6 concurrent gate runs that is roughly +186 concurrent standard jobs. It should sit
inside an enterprise plan's ceiling, but **I did not measure the ceiling** — and the
+9.3% overshoot above is partly this effect showing up already, since the per-leg setup got
slower under 31-way concurrency than the doc's single-job measurement predicted. If the
ceiling is ever exceeded the failure mode is **queueing delay, not failure**.

Second, smaller: the `ace-suite` audit produced one unexplained `exit 1` with `724 pass /
0 fail` during a local back-to-back sweep, and did not reproduce in four subsequent runs or
in CI. It is a pre-existing flake in that suite, not something this change introduces, and
it is recorded rather than dropped because a suite that reports all-pass and exits nonzero
is worth someone's attention.
