## The actual finding

Not the lensography mapping — that is recorded as **Mirror**, untested, no metering test run.

The load-bearing item is that **optical-clock practice patches the entangled-sample proper-time problem by using the proper time at the centre of the trap.** That is a hardware instance of the exact defect [`local-time-never-enters-the-shared-fold`](../blob/main/.claude/rules/local-time-never-enters-the-shared-fold.md) forbids: each atom has its own proper time, the clock's report is the collective conclusion, and the patch promotes one locality's clock to arbiter of it — silently, since the clock keeps returning a number.

The rule was written 2026-07-11 on the argument that this mistake would be easy to make before the fold existed. Independent corroboration: the people whose profession *is* measuring time made it, and describe themselves as not worried about it. Fuentes' own resolution moves the rule's direction — her quantised light clock defines clock time from **collective oscillation across slices**, not a privileged slice.
