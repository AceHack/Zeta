## The ask

Aaron 2026-08-14: *"there are many bulk head, jitter, exponential backoff, circuit breaker, many many techniques for 'backpressure' — our back pressure should be more **algebraic and not so ad hoc**. we have some work on this already over tiny bandwidth channels so **bandwidth isolation leads to true decorrelation over time**."*

Design study + one measurement. **Do not arm auto-merge.**

## 1. Is there an algebra? No — a taxonomy, and one composition law

The four techniques constrain four different objects, which is *why* they feel ad hoc:

| technique | constrains | structure | composes? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bulkhead | a **resource** | a partition; commutative monoid of shares | yes, associatively |
| Backoff / AIMD | a **rate** | monoid of monotone clamped maps | weakly (composite is monotone; nothing about its fixed point) |
| Jitter | a **joint distribution** | symmetry-breaker (Floyd–Jacobson 1992) | not a control law at all |
| Circuit breaker | a **belief** | 3-state automaton over an error estimator | **no** |

The breaker's failure is an *instrument* failure, not a missing theorem: **when an outer breaker opens, the inner breaker stops seeing traffic, so its error rate falls to zero and it reads healthy.** Same class as the eleven blind instruments found this month.

**The one genuine unification is not the one asked for:** jitter and bulkheading are the same operation on different coordinates — jitter destroys a shared *phase*, a bulkhead destroys a shared *capacity*. Both are decorrelation operators; neither is a feedback law.

### The composition law that does hold

An operator `m(offered) = (admitted, deferred)` is **conservative** when `admitted ⊎ deferred = offered`.

- Conservative operators **compose**: a monoid, with the deferred sets joining as a **semilattice** (idempotent, order-independent, replay-safe).
- Lossy operators compose into **nothing** — the composite depends on application order.

Not a coinage. **Kahn (1974)**: a network of monotone stream functions has a unique least fixed point *independent of scheduling*; blocking-write on a bounded FIFO — exactly conservative backpressure — preserves it (Kahn–MacQueen 1977). **Brock–Ackerman (1981)**: for nondeterminate dataflow the input–output relation is *not* compositional. So conservative backpressure is a Kahn process network and its determinacy is why DST replays the throttle path at all.

**#10640 reached the same boundary today from reversibility** ("derived / handed back ⇒ PRESSURE, free; annihilated ⇒ LOSS, it pays"). Two independent derivations of one boundary is the strongest evidence available that it is real — so yes, `HeatSignal.isPressure` is the right base type. `SoftThrottle` already satisfies the law: every shed path hands the tail back (CHECKED, read end to end).

## 2. The measurement — bandwidth isolation → decorrelation

New: `src/Core.TypeScript/discovery/bandwidth-isolation-decorrelation.ts` on the existing D/D/1/B link. Three arms differing in **one** structural fact; per-flow entropy **identical across arms by construction** (`udp-bdp-link` indexes draws by `flow * 1000003 + seq`), so an isolated arm is the same experiment with the coupling removed — which is what lets it serve as the null. The textbook i.i.d. null is *not* used: a queue series is autocorrelated, the exact defect `DecorrelationExcess` had to fix with a block null.

Offered load **fixed**, capacity **raised**. 24 seeds, Pearson r on per-100 ms delivered-throughput trajectories:

| capacity | utilisation | **shared** r | sign | **isolated-split** r | sign |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 700 | 0.74 | **−0.240** | **24/24 −** | +0.021 | 11/24 |
| 1000 | 0.52 | −0.142 | 23/24 − | +0.002 | 9/24 |
| 1400 | 0.37 | −0.064 | 19/24 − | −0.027 | 17/24 |
| 2000 | 0.26 | −0.031 | 17/24 − | −0.003 | 13/24 |
| 5000 | 0.10 | −0.007 | 12/24 − | +0.013 | 11/24 |
| 20000 | 0.03 | +0.004 | 11/24 − | −0.001 | 12/24 |

At 74% utilisation with **zero congestion drops**, two flows sharing only a queue correlate at **−0.240**, same sign on **24/24** seeds (sign test, p = 2⁻²⁴ — chosen because it assumes nothing about i.i.d.). Bulkheaded at **conserved** capacity (BID-10 pins total throughput matching to within 1 pkt/s — a bulkhead, not an upgrade) the sign is a coin flip.

**The falsifier passes (BID-11):** the shared-arm coupling decays *monotonically* to the null as contention is removed, no threshold moved. Had it not, the number would have been an artifact of running both flows in one simulation and the study would be void.

**So: yes, measured.** With the honest refinement — what decorrelates is the *absence of contention*, and isolation is the route to it that does not depend on the neighbour's restraint.

## 3. The limit, also measured — and it contradicts the unqualified claim

Two flows on **separate** links, same control law, same boundary, **no channel between them**:

| arm | gap-trajectory r | usable |
|---|---:|---:|
| shared | +0.258 | 16/16 |
| **isolated-split** | **+0.710** | 16/16 |

Isolation made them **~3× more correlated**, both guards passing. Reichenbach (1956): a correlation has a common cause *or* a channel. **Isolation removes the channel; it does not remove the shared design**, and a shared design at a shared boundary is a common cause. Concretely: *the more perfectly you isolate identical agents, the more perfectly correlated their failures become*, because contention was doing the decorrelating. Consistent with the decorrelation capstone's independent finding on repo history (benign shared *buses* as the surviving common cause).

## 4. How the instrument fails — three modes, all observed, two guarded

- **F2 one-sample leverage.** Two saturated isolated links: series took **exactly two distinct values, 50 (×189) and 51 (×1)**, and r = **+1.000** off one bucket in 190. Guarded by `covarianceLeverage`; BID-7 pins that all 8 seeds are refused and `meanR` is `null` — *refusing to answer is the answer*.
- Together F1+F2 would have reported **that isolation makes flows maximally correlated** — a complete inversion, from two accounting identities.
- **F3 shared attractor** — §3. No guard, and cannot have one: it is not an artifact.

Also stated: Pearson sees only linear coupling; the lag search inflates |r|; one queue discipline only.

## 5. Derived vs correlated control inputs

**The razor: name the conservation law, or mark the input correlated.**

Derived — queue occupancy (`arrivals = departures + occupancy + drops`), deferred-set size (`offered = admitted ⊎ deferred`), tank charge (`heatSpent = Capacity − Charge`), bulkhead share (`Σ ≤ C`), standing-queue-from-delay (**Little's law**).

Correlated — loss rate (corruption/reorder/silence give the same quotient), latency percentile, breaker error rate (**and an upstream open breaker drives it to zero**), throughput (pinned by capacity when saturated), threadpool depth.

**CHECKED and load-bearing:** `CongestionEvidence` is **unconstructible**, so the transport has **no derived congestion input at all** — every congestion decision is taken on `unknown`.

**Generous tit-for-tat lands exactly here:** generosity is the price of a correlated input; `retractLoss` is generous TFT with the generosity replaced by *evidence* (recompute without the retracted loss; reverse only if it would not have fired). **Attribution is what lets you stop paying for generosity.**
