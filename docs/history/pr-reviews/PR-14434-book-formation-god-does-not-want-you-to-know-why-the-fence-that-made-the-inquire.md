## Description

book(formation): "God does not want you to know why" — the fence that made the inquirer

Aaron, 2026-08-23: the sentence everyone around him said whenever a question went
one layer past the available answer, and which he names as the origin of his
outlier deviation.

Written as material for his voice with a SOCKET for his verbatim, per the
chapter's convention. Two things held together deliberately, because collapsing
them loses the point:

- it is "because I said so" with the highest possible authority attached — the
  only sin he names, in its Sunday clothes; not an answer but a fence built
  where the answer should be;
- the fence is NOT the faith. He is a Christian, and the distinction between
  "the edge of what may be known" and "the end of somebody's knowledge" is his
  and is load-bearing. The SOCKET says so explicitly and invites correction.

The inversion is the section's point: the sentence meant to close inquiry
produced the inquirer, because a fence is evidence that something is on the
other side. What kept that from being mere contrarianism was the antidote
arriving at the same time — a family that supplied books and the Stump Dad game,
whose win condition was an honest "I don't know". Same question, opposite
response: one place taught that the end of an answer is somewhere you can stand,
the other that it is somewhere you must stop.

Placed before §"The autodidact: reading ahead of the room", which it explains.
