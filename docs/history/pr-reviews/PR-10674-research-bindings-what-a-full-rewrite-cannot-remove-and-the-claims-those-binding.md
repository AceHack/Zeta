## The short list — what we still won't control after rewriting everything

1. **The OEM UEFI Platform Key** — only the vendor can authorize replacing it.
2. **NVIDIA GSP firmware + CUDA userspace** — even the *open* kernel module requires the closed GSP blob.
3. **The silicon vendor as the root of every attestation** — AMD ARK, Intel PCK, NVIDIA NRAS, TPM EK.
4. **PKCS#11 as a standard** — no mechanism composes a FROST partial (already on file, Nazar).
5. **The crypto hardness assumptions** — not code, so not rewritable in any sense.
6. **The frontier model doing the rewriting** — local serving is written and explicitly `DEFERRED`.

Items 1–3 share a shape: the deepest bindings are all **silicon vendors as roots of trust** — the Microsoft-CA-in-`db` tension, one layer deeper and until now unnamed.

## I corrected the lead I was asked to verify

The suspected finding — *"a proprietary GPU driver permanently caps measured/attested boot"* — is **wrong**, and it mattered. The driver is not in the initrd (CHECKED: `worker-gpu/hardware-configuration.nix:16` carries only `virtio_*`), not in the measured chain, and signing out-of-tree modules under lockdown is routine on other distros. What ends the chain at the UKI is `MODULE_SIG=no` + no lockdown LSM — which applies to *everything*, not to NVIDIA.

The real cap is one layer lower and worse: **the node can be attested; the GPU cannot.** NVIDIA CC/attestation is Hopper/Blackwell datacenter only — the cards in hand are RTX 4090 + 3090 — and where it exists it verifies against NVIDIA's own service.

## Which claims these cap

- **"Attest, don't remember"** (invariant 3, ladder L3/L5) — strongest available claim is *"AMD says this is genuine AMD silicon running this measurement."*
- **Manifesto §1 scale-free** — holds at the software layer, cannot extend to the metal.
- **§13 noninterference** — RDRAND is an entropy channel we cannot declare or meter (effective `RANDOM_TRUST_CPU` value is UNMEASURED, needs a node).
- **"We are rewriting everything"** — the rewriter is rented; the price is the capability gap, not engineering.
