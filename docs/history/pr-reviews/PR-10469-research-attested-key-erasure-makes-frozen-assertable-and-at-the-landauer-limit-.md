## Description

## The gap it closes

An unused key and a **destroyed** key are different physical states, and the second is attestable. So "nobody holds a live write key" stops being an inference from silence.

## The construction needs both halves — and the birth half is the one that gets skipped

**A deletion attestation proves *this* element destroyed *its* copy. It cannot prove no copy exists elsewhere.** Erasure of a key that was ever extractable is one copy fewer, not erasure. So: attested **non-extractable generation** → attested destruction → frozen. Without the first, the second means nothing.

Real mechanisms (existing tech, not in-repo work): TPM 2.0 non-migratable keys with attested creation; Secure Enclave / StrongBox attestation with non-exportable flags; and strongest — **OTP eFuses**, where a burnt fuse is a *changed device*, not a policy assertion.

## Erasure *at* the limit makes the energy the receipt

I initially wrote that Landauer was "the right conceptual anchor and the wrong security argument." **That's wrong for the design Aaron is describing**, and the doc carries the correction.

Ordinary CMOS dissipates 10⁴–10⁶ `kT` per op, so the Landauer term is buried and carries no information. **Operating near the limit makes the erasure energy the dominant measurable term** — that's the engineering point, and it's about making the receipt *legible*, not about power saving.

The inference is one-way, which is the right direction here:

- **No erasure without the energy** — you cannot erase `N` bits dissipating less than `N·kT ln 2`.
- **Energy without erasure is possible** — so it cannot confirm.

**Metered dissipation below the bound falsifies an erasure claim.** Convicts, never acquits. And unlike attestation, a thermodynamic receipt **is not an assertion by anyone** — where both exist they're independent evidence with independent failure modes, and a discrepancy between them is itself a detection.

| act | irreversibility | enforced by | forgeable by |
|---|---|---|---|
| `frost` | one-way by rule | the substrate | a substrate bug |
| attested deletion | one-way by device | vendor chain | a compromised trust root |
| **erasure at the Landauer limit** | **one-way by thermodynamics** | **physics** | **nobody — but falsifiable only** |

## It also makes freezing deliberate

Under #10465 an artefact froze by **abandonment**. Now an author can *choose* to freeze. Which gives the "fun glitch stays" preservation commitment (#10462) a mechanism stronger than a regression test: the author doesn't promise not to patch it away — **they destroy the ability to.**

## Severe limits, stated

- **The energies are minuscule** — 256 bits ≈ 7 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. **The measurement, not the erasure, is the hard part.**
- **Approaching the bound needs slow quasi-static erasure** — a "fast secure erase" cannot be at the limit by construction.
- **This is frontier experimental physics** — Bérut et al. (Nature 2012) is a laboratory result, not an orderable component.
- **The hardware trust root is a §1 dependency.** Attested-frozen must be a *revocable credence booster over* observed-frozen, not a replacement — per `081KTHY32YQ08QG0R000JWHJYN`. Emit both readings.
- **Coerced or mistaken erasure is now possible and unrecoverable.**

**The cheap half is buildable now regardless:** if erasure is ever to carry a thermodynamic receipt, the erasure path must be **metered from the start** — retrofitting a measurement to an unmetered path isn't possible. `entropy-tracker.ts` already exists for it.
