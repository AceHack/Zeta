## The anchor — your mom moving the furniture

Some people walk into a room and just *see* it: where the energy pools, where it stalls, why nobody talks
in that corner, why everyone crowds the kitchen. Aaron's mom is one of them — an interior designer who
sees the **chi** (the flow) of a room and the people in it, and moves the furniture until the room *works*.

That skill has a two-thousand-year-old name: **feng shui** — literally "**wind-water**." Both words are
**flows**. Feng shui is the practice of arranging a space so the flows through it serve the people in it.

**It is not an analogy for what Zeta does. It is the thing.**

## What a room really is

A room is a **boundary** (walls, doors, windows) with **flows crossing it** — people, conversation,
light, attention. The furniture is the **joints and membranes inside**: it routes the flows.

- A couch facing the door = an invitation (a wide-open channel).
- Two chairs angled toward each other = a collaboration joint (the flow couples).
- A table in the wrong place = a blocked channel (the chi stalls; so does the conversation).

Good feng shui is **minimal-action flow design**: you don't push people around — you arrange the space so
the flows *carry themselves* where they should go. (The same tradition says this directly: **wu-wei** —
effortless action. Arrange, then don't push.)

## Zeta's rooms are the same craft

Zeta builds computational **rooms**: bounded spaces (Markov boundaries) with flows crossing them
(messages, data, attention), furnished with joints that route the flows (queues, channels, links). The
design questions are *identical* to the ones Aaron's mom asks:

| the interior designer asks | the room designer asks |
|---|---|
| where does the energy enter and pool? | where do the flows cross the boundary? (the doors/IEffects) |
| does this furniture invite collaboration? | do these joints couple the flows? (the links/four corners) |
| why does this corner feel dead? | why is this channel starved? (backpressure/budget) |
| can I fix it WITHOUT pushing people? | minimal action — let one flow's energy drive the other |
| does the room *feel* right? | the aesthetic IS engineered (ÆSTHETIC ENGINEERING — the neon cells) |

So yes: **Aaron and Max are interior decorators now** — of computational space. The metaspace floorplan
(salon, darkhall, bowling alley, skatium, the dev room) is interior design; the boundary-flow architecture
is feng shui with types.
