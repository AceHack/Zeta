Every popular account of this result draws two regions — verified and unverified. That is the wrong shape. There are three, and the most interesting one is the one with nothing in it.

The critical strip

Riemann’s zeros live inside a vertical strip. The claim is that all of them sit on the line down its middle. Drag the year and watch the certificate grow. The two flanks are the detector for zeros off the line. It has been armed since 1859 and has never once fired.

How much fuel is left in this tank

The authors computed the ceiling of their own family of techniques: approximately 0.6818. The theorem sits at 0.6667. Being able to draw this at all is rare — it is a paper telling you where its own method stops.

Why the empty region is the point

  • Region 2 is not small. It is zero. Not “none found yet in this window”, not “a negligible fraction”. Across every zero anyone has ever computed — more than ten trillion — the count of zeros shown to be off the line is exactly nought.
  • Region 3 is not doubt. The paper’s own words, §1.4: “the remaining third of the zeros are not shown to be off the line, merely not reached by the certificate.” Merging 2 and 3 into one grey “unverified” band throws away the distinction the paper opens by insisting on.
  • The method cannot tell the difference between two thirds and all. §7.2(a), verbatim: “Nothing in the method distinguishes between ‘two thirds’ and ‘all’.”
  • And the honest killer, volunteered in §1.4: the method’s inputs also hold for the Davenport–Heilbronn and Epstein zeta functions, for which the analogue of the hypothesis is false — those really do have zeros off the line. A technique whose conditions are satisfied by objects that violate the conclusion cannot, by itself, establish the conclusion. The author put that in section 1.4, before the proof.
  • Even 100% would not be the hypothesis. These bound a density. A set can have density zero and still be infinite, so a hypothetical 100% would still leave room for infinitely many exceptions. The hypothesis says every zero, and density arguments do not reach the word “every”.
