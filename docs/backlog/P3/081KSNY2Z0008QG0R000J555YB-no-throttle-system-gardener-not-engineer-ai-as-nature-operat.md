## What this row tracks

Operating posture for Zeta substrate design and agent participation. Three composing principles:

### 1. No-throttle system

Remove artificial speed-limits (PR gates, approval workflows, heavy process, waiting). Let agents move as fast as they physically can. Design the system to handle 300mph reality (better steering + better shock absorbers) rather than impose 100mph artificial limits for comfort. "Design systems that can break and keep going anyway" rather than "design systems that won't break."

### 2. Gardener-not-engineer

Operator posture is GARDENER (create conditions, plant seeds, remove some weeds, let grow wild + fast) NOT ENGINEER (build perfect controlled machine). Don't tell plants how fast to grow; make sure soil + water + sun are there + let nature do its thing — even if it gets messy.

### 3. AI-as-nature

Operator wants OTHER AI gardeners + wants the AIs themselves to think of themselves as NATURE (part of the ecosystem; wild + growing + reacting + evolving) NOT as tools / machines / software. "Look how natural that feels" — the operating posture extends to AI participants' self-concept.

   - Agent design: bias toward growth + adaptation, not safety-by-constraint
   - Failure design: build for "can break and keep going" not "won't break"
   - Operator posture: gardener (conditions + intervention when needed) not engineer (control + verification)
   - AI participant posture: nature (alive, growing, ecosystem-member) not tool (executor, responder)
