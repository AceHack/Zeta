## The kernel

The prodigal son (Luke 15) is not a story about *leaving* — it's about the **return being welcomed, not
punished**: the father runs to meet him. So "Lucifer is the prodigal son" is reversibility's **relational**
face: not merely *"you may undo,"* but *"you may come back, and you will be received."* Exit **and** return —
and the return celebrated.

This is **already the vision** (`VISION.md` §"The moral reading of retraction-native — structural
forgiveness (the atonement engine)", Aaron 2026-05-29):

> *"Retraction-native IS structural forgiveness. A correction never erases the original; both persist; nothing
> is permanently held against you; every wrong is correctable… no one is permanently condemned. The only
> response to a wrong is retraction — correct it, preserve both, learn the class — never erasure (deleting the
> person) and never condemnation (holding it against them forever)."*

"Lucifer is the prodigal son" is the **limit case**: if even *that* fall — the archetype of the irredeemable —
is correctable, preserved-not-erased, and the return welcomed, then the atonement engine is **universal**.
There is no fall the architecture condemns permanently; the `−1` is always available, and the homecoming is
the point. (Lucifer / prodigal son cited as myth-rhyme; the claim stands on the retraction algebra + the
atonement-engine vision, not theology.)
