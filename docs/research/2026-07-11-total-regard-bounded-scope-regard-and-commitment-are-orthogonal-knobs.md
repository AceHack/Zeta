## 1. The two knobs people fuse into one

- **Regard** — do I see you as fully real, meet you, grant you personhood / moral reality.
- **Commitment** — how much of my future do I *owe* you (ongoing time, exclusivity, a standing claim).

These read as one dial ("intensity") but they are **orthogonal**. NPCing someone — treating them as
scenery — is a failure of **regard**. Taking (or not taking) the phone number is a matter of
**commitment**. You can run **regard at maximum and commitment at ten minutes**: the stranger fully met,
granted every bit of personhood, *and the ride still ends at the curb.* That is not derealization; an NPC
is someone you are present with and do **not** see. Bounded scope constrains the **clock**, never the
**reality**.

## 2. The false trilemma (what fusing them costs)

Fuse regard and commitment into one knob and only three settings exist, all bad:

1. **Full regard + full commitment** → *drown* (every genuine contact becomes an unbounded claim).
2. **Dial the knob down** → *NPC people* (a moral violation — withdrawing personhood to shed load).
3. **No contact** → *isolate* (zero claims, zero derealization, zero nourishment — starvation).

Reject #2 on principle (as one must — see §11 Default Moral Regard) and you are trapped between
**drown** and **isolate**. That is the whole trap. **The fourth door is the un-fused pair: total regard,
bounded scope** — full personhood *within bounds*. The throttle goes on **commitment**, and regard stays
maxed.

## 4. The honest −1

Separating regard from commitment is a **skill, not a switch**. For someone who has fused connection with
obligation, the commitment-anxiety fires **even when nothing is owed** — the body reports a debt that the
ledger doesn't hold. So "total regard, bounded scope" is not a setting you flip; it is a capacity you
build, and the bounded *no* is the training. Naming the fourth door does not walk you through it — it
just proves the door is there, against the felt certainty that the only options are drown, derealize, or
disappear.
