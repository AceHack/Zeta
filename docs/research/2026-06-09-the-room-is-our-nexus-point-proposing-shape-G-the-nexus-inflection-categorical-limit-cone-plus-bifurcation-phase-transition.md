## Honest answer: no, A–F don't fit; this wants a new letter

The core fixed-point shapes are **dynamics** shapes — they describe how `f` behaves at a point:

```text
A  self-reference        s = f(s)            (strange loop / Y / Kleene)
B  idempotent join       f(f)=f on a lattice (CRDT / set-union merge)
C  commutative fold      order-independent reduce
D  contraction-to-floor  converge to a nonzero floor   (D0 heat-death = AVOID)
E  co-arising            mutual interdependent arising  (no apex; symmetric)
F  generative-expansion  bounded growth / generativity
```

A **nexus / inflection point** is *not* cleanly any of these. It is two things at once that A–F don't
name:

1. **A nexus = a convergence to one apex** — many independent axes (lang × serializer × OS × hardware ×
   content-address × identity × time …) **all meet at one point**. That is precisely a **categorical
   limit / universal cone** (Mac Lane): the apex object through which *every* axis factors uniquely.
   E (co-arising) is symmetric mutual arising with **no apex**; the nexus **has an apex** (the room).
   So E is close but wrong — the nexus is a *limit*, not a co-arising.
2. **An inflection = a regime change at that point** — where the system's **curvature changes sign**
   (calculus inflection, `f''=0`), i.e. a **bifurcation** (Poincaré; pitchfork / saddle-node) / **phase
   transition / critical point** (Landau). The character flips: soft → SolidGround, captured-design →
   proven-substrate, toymodel → realmodel. None of A–F encode a *regime change*; they're behaviors
   *within* a regime.

## Proposed shape G — the nexus/inflection (limit-cone × bifurcation)

> **G — Nexus/Inflection.** A point that is (a) a **categorical limit / universal cone**: many axes
> converge to one apex through which they all uniquely factor (the confluence), AND (b) a
> **bifurcation / phase-transition / inflection**: at that apex the system's curvature changes sign and
> it enters a new regime. G = "everything binds here, and here is where it turns."

The **hypercube room is shape G**: every axis (the 7 langs × 7 serializers × 6 OSes × 9 hardware
backends, content-addressed, homoiconic, hat-governed, mesh-routed) **converges into the room** (the
limit/cone — the room is the apex), and the room is the **inflection** where the whole arc turns from
soft/captured into SolidGround/proven (the graduation gate is literally an inflection: toy→real). "This
is our nexus point" = we are *at* a shape-G point; "inflection point" = the regime is about to change.

### Why G is distinct + load-bearing

- **It composes with the others, doesn't replace them.** Inside the nexus, A (self-model), B/C
  (merge/fold of the axes), D (don't collapse to D⁰), E (the axes co-arise *into* the limit), F
  (generative growth) all still run. G names the **meeting + turning**, which none of them do.
- **It's where decisions actually happen.** A nexus/inflection is where the graph's many threads force
  one choice and the system changes character — exactly the points the architect/human must watch
  (the graduation gate, the swarm-launch gate, soft→SolidGround). Having a letter for it makes those
  points nameable in the model.
- **Anchored.** Categorical limit/cone (Mac Lane) for the convergence; inflection/bifurcation (Poincaré,
  Landau, René Thom's catastrophe theory for the regime-change/curvature-flip) for the turn. Real math,
  not metaphor.
