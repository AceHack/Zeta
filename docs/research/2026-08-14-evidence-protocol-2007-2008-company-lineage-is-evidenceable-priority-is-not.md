## 1. The boundary that governs this entire document

**Lineage is evidenceable. Priority is not, and is not pursued.**

| | claim | status |
|---|---|---|
| **Lineage** | "Zeta's decentralisation instincts are first-hand — these people built a peer-to-peer money system in 2007–2008" | checkable, defensible, valuable |
| **Priority** | any claim relative to the October 2008 Bitcoin whitepaper, or to Hashcash / b-money / bit-gold | **out of scope; not to be written in any form** |

Out of scope means *all* of its forms: not "predated," not "pre-Bitcoin," not "contemporaneous
with," not "in the same cohort as," not "independently arrived at the same idea," not "months
before." A softened priority claim is still a priority claim.

**Why the ban is strict rather than cautious.** A date coincidence is a **generator**, never a
conclusion (`numerology-vs-number-theory`). The specific harm is not that a priority claim would be
*wrong* — it is that an unevidenced priority claim **discredits the true material sitting next to
it**. The lineage claim is strong, evidenceable, and useful; attaching it to an unfalsifiable
priority framing converts a defensible record into a dismissible one.

And the lineage claim is the one with actual value to Zeta. It sits alongside a fact already on
file: Aaron's Itron patents are **centralised**
(`.claude/rules/itron-hub-patent-boundary-p2p-is-the-upgrade.md`). He has both lineages, from
opposite directions, first-hand. That is the interesting thing, and it needs no date race.

### 1a. The operational rule that keeps the boundary from drifting

> **State dates absolutely. Never relative to another project's date.**

"Described as peer-to-peer money and logistics by August 2008" and "months before the Bitcoin
whitepaper" assert the *same fact*. Only the second one drifts, because the comparison supplies a
frame the evidence does not support. The first is a dating claim; the second is a priority claim
wearing a dating claim's clothes.

This is the single mechanical guard. Any sentence in which the 2008 date appears next to another
project's name has crossed the line, regardless of how it is hedged.

### 1b. A prior surface already carries the framing — flagged, not edited

`docs/research/2026-05-05-claudeai-…-functional-tree-2007-pre-bitcoin-…-aaron-forwarded-preservation.md`
already carries "pre-Bitcoin" in its filename, title, and body, and its body states cohort
membership as a conclusion ("valid pre-Bitcoin-cohort provenance claim").

That document is a **courier-ferry** — a verbatim preservation of an external Claude.ai
conversation, with its own confidence calibration attached (the priority passages are marked

## 2a. The business — the peer-to-peer money was a *derived requirement*

Aaron 2026-08-14:

> "The peer to peer system was based off Movable Cubicle a competitor to Pods. Pods had their own
> truckers, Movable Cubicle has independent truckers — this is why we needed peer to peer money for
> cross state paying of truckers to move containers."

**This is the strongest thing in the record, and it is stronger than any date.** It converts
"they were into peer-to-peer money" from a *chosen technology* into a *forced consequence of a
business model*:

| | delivery capacity | paying the driver |
|---|---|---|
| **PODS** | its own container fleet and delivery trucks, with the proprietary PODZILLA lift operated by trained drivers — delivery is **internal to the PODS system** | internal payroll |
| **Moveable Cubicle** | **independent truckers** — contractors, not employees | **cross-state settlement to independent counterparties, per container move** |

Payroll does not solve the second row. A central processor is awkward and costly for small per-move
amounts across state lines to counterparties who are not employees. So the settlement problem is a
**structural necessity of the independent-contractor logistics model**, not a preference — and it is
also why "money **and** logistics" was one system rather than two. That phrasing in the earlier
record now has a mechanism behind it.

**And it is a real-economy use case, not a crypto-native one.** Actual containers, actual drivers,
actual state lines. This is worth naming explicitly because it is a **better claim than any priority
story**: it is checkable against an industry whose structure is public knowledge, and it does not
depend on what anyone else was doing in 2008.

**Beacon anchor (checked):** PODS (Portable On Demand Storage), founded 1998 by Pete Warhurst in
Clearwater, Florida; the patented **PODZILLA** hydraulic lift is truck-mounted and driver-operated,
and the company's scale is described in terms of its own container fleet and facility network
([pods.com 20th-anniversary release](https://www.pods.com/resource-center/news/pods-reaches-new-milestone-celebrating-20th-anniversary);
[Wikipedia, PODS (company)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PODS_(company))). *Precision:* PODS also
franchises, so the honest form is that delivery capacity is **internal to the PODS system**, not
that every driver is an employee of one corporation. The contrast with an open network of
independent truckers holds either way.

### The Zeta parallel — structural, and safe to state

> **Independent truckers are to Moveable Cubicle what independent agents are to Zeta.**

Non-employee participants, doing discrete units of work, needing settlement without a central payer.
Same problem shape, different substrate. That is a concrete lineage for Zeta's agent-economy design
— wallets, budget transfer, conservation invariants, no central mint — and it is far more specific
than "they were into decentralisation early."

Stated as **system shape**, which is observable, and deliberately not as motivation, which is not.
The motivational version is left as a question in §7.

## 3. The artifacts, ranked by evidentiary strength

The ranking axis is **who attests the date**. An artifact whose date is asserted by a participant is
weak regardless of how detailed it is; an artifact whose date is recorded by an uninvolved third
party is strong regardless of how thin it is.

### 3.1 The founder licence document — not evidence, and not currently a blocker

Listed here only so it is not mistaken for part of the evidence chain. It is not evidence of
anything about the company; it gates **publishing the old code**, which nobody has proposed. Detail
and current status in §5.

### 3.2 Domain registration — the strongest date attestation

**What it is.** WHOIS/RDAP records the domain's creation date. Neither Aaron nor Houman controls
the registry, so the date is **third-party attested**. Nothing else in this list beats it for
date-strength.

**What it proves.** *That a domain of that name existed on that date.* That is the whole of it.

**What it does not prove.** Nothing about what was built, what the company did, who was involved,
or whether the domain was ever used. A parked domain and a running product are indistinguishable in
WHOIS.

**Practical caveats, stated because they routinely surprise:**

- **Current WHOIS shows the *current* registration.** If the domain lapsed and was re-registered —
  even by the same person — the creation date typically resets to the re-registration. A 2007
  creation date visible today implies **continuous** registration, which is itself informative.
- **Historical WHOIS is a paid service** in most cases (DomainTools, WhoisXMLAPI and similar). The
  free current-record lookup may be sufficient if registration was continuous; it will not be if it
  lapsed.
- **GDPR-era redaction** hides registrant identity in most public WHOIS output. Creation and
  expiry dates are generally still visible; the *registrant* usually is not. The registrant link is
  better established from the **registrar account** Aaron controls than from public WHOIS.
- **`.com` creation dates are authoritative from the Verisign registry**, not from the registrar —
  query the registry record if the two disagree.

**Time-sensitive, and the one genuinely urgent item in this document:** if the domain is currently
held, **do not let it lapse.** Expiry destroys both the continuous-registration record and control
of the name, and neither is recoverable. Confirm auto-renew and the expiry date before anything
else on this list. Capture the registrar account's own record (creation date, order history) while
access exists.

### 3.3 Archived web content — the strongest artifact for *what it said it did*

**What it is.** The Internet Archive's Wayback Machine holds dated snapshots of page content
captured by a crawler neither founder controls.

**Why it is unusually strong here.** It is the only artifact class that can attest **the described
purpose** — "peer-to-peer money and logistics" — **on a date**, from a source outside the founders'
control. The domain gives a date with no content; the code gives content with no date; Wayback
gives *content and date together*, which is why it carries most of the weight of the defensible
claim in §7.

**How to check it (Aaron runs this):**

- Calendar view: `https://web.archive.org/web/2008*/<domain>` — shows which dates have captures.
- Full capture index (this is the one that actually answers the question, since it lists every
  captured URL under the host with its timestamp):
  `https://web.archive.org/cdx/search/cdx?url=<domain>&matchType=domain&from=2006&to=2011&output=json`
- Also try `archive.today` / `archive.ph`, and the domain **with and without `www.`**, and any
  alternate spelling (one word vs two, `.com` vs `.net`).

**Two precision points that are easy to get wrong:**

1. **The third-party attestation covers the timestamp, not the truth of the text.** A Wayback
   snapshot proves *"they publicly described it as peer-to-peer money and logistics on date X"* —
   not *"they were building it."* Self-authored marketing copy is still self-authored; what the
   Archive supplies is that it was published by that date. This distinction is load-bearing for §7,
   where the brief's proposed wording ("they were building…") is stronger than the artifact
   supports.
2. **Absence is not evidence of absence.** Wayback holds only what a crawler happened to fetch at
   the time. A 2008 site with no inbound links may simply never have been crawled. A missing
   snapshot falsifies nothing.

**Do not "Save Page Now" the current domain and file it as evidence.** A capture made today is
dated today and says nothing about 2008. This is an easy self-deception and worth naming out loud.

### 3.4 Corporate records — third-party attested, and now actionable (§2)

**What they establish.** A filing gives the formation date, entity name, registered agent, status
(active / administratively dissolved), and — on annual reports — officers and often a stated nature
of business. Formation date is the strongest possible attestation of *the company's* existence,
because the state recorded it.

**What they do not establish.** What was built, or that it was peer-to-peer money. A registered
entity is a registered entity.

**Where to look, with the known blocker already on file:** the **NC Secretary of State** business
entity search (`sosnc.gov`). The 2026-05-05 ferry already records the obstruction: the search is
**interactive-only, explicitly forbids automated/scripted searches, and returns 403 to programmatic
fetches.** An agent cannot run this; Aaron can, in a browser, in about a minute.

Secondary, all free and public: county **assumed-name (DBA)** filings at the Register of Deeds if
the trading name differed from the registered name; **USPTO TESS** if a trademark was ever filed.

Note the annual-report "nature of business" field is a **third-party-filed description** — the same
shape of evidence as a Wayback snapshot, and it corroborates the purpose claim independently.

### 3.5 The August 2008 email — already in-repo, and undervalued

The brief's artifact list omits this class. It should not.

A verbatim email body from **August 5, 2008** is already preserved in the repo (CEO/investor
structure discussion; Houman named as a partner). Its evidentiary strength depends entirely on
**which object we mean**, and the two are far apart:

| object | strength | why |
|---|---|---|
| the **pasted body** now in the repo | weak | retyped/copied text with no headers; participant-attested; trivially alterable |
| the **original message** in Aaron's mail account | **strong** | full `Received:` header chain written by intermediate mail servers — third parties — at delivery time |

**Retrieving the original with headers intact is a high-value, low-cost action**, and it is the
action the repo copy cannot substitute for. Export as `.eml`, preserving all headers.

Two honest limits:

- **A 2008 DKIM/DomainKeys signature will almost certainly not verify today.** The verifying public
  key lives in the sender's DNS and rotates; the selector from 2008 is long gone. So the signature
  is not a cryptographic proof of date. The `Received:` chain is still meaningful — several
  independent servers stamped it — but it is corroboration, not cryptography.
- **The email is about business structure, not technical content.** It evidences that a named
  project with partners and investor negotiations existed by that date. It says nothing about the
  architecture, and it must not be cited as if it did.

**The highest-value thing to look for in that mailbox is not the email itself — it is an
attachment.** A source archive, spec, pitch deck, or diagram attached to a dated 2008 message
**inherits that message's third-party-attested date.** That would be the single strongest possible
dating of the code, and it is the only path by which the code acquires a date it did not assert
itself.

### 3.6 The code — the only artifact that establishes *what was built*, and the weakest at dating

The code is the only thing on this list that can substantiate the **content** claims: that it was
peer-to-peer, that logistics was in scope, that it used F# functional tree structures. Domain,
archive, and filings cannot reach any of that.

Its **dating** strength ranks as follows — and the brief's ranking needs one correction, marked:

| rank | form | dating strength | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | attached to a dated third-party email (§3.5) | **strong** | inherits a `Received:`-chain date |
| 2 | published on a third-party host of the era — CodePlex, Google Code, SourceForge, a mailing list, the hubFS F# forum | **strong** | the host recorded receipt; archived copies of all of these exist |
| 3 | a repo whose history was **pushed to a third-party host** that logged the push | moderate–strong | the host's log is the attestation, not the commit |
| 4 | a local source-control history — SVN, VSS, TFS, Mercurial, git | **weak on its own** | see correction below |
| 5 | a folder of files with filesystem mtimes | very weak | trivially alterable, and altered accidentally by ordinary copying |

> **Correction to the brief.** The brief states that "a git history with signed or at least dated
> commits is strong." **Dated commits are not strong.** A commit date is a self-asserted header
> field written by the committing machine's clock; `GIT_COMMITTER_DATE` sets it to any value, and
> rewriting an entire history's dates is a one-line operation. Signing does not fix this: a
> signature attests **who**, not **when** — it binds a key to the content, and the date inside the
> signed object is still whatever the signer put there. A commit history carries a date only when
> something *outside* it attests the date: a host's push log, a third-party timestamp authority, or
> a cross-reference to an independently dated artifact.
>
> There is also a period detail worth noting: git was released in 2005 but was rare in .NET work in
> 2007–2008. **Subversion, Visual SourceSafe, or TFS are the more likely forms**, and if the code is
> in git today it was probably imported later — which means the git dates are import dates, not
> authorship dates, and would be actively misleading if cited.

**The language-version check convicts, never acquits.** F#'s surface changed a great deal in this
period (pre-1.0 `Microsoft.FSharp.*` namespaces; `#light` not yet the default before 1.9.6; no
`async` workflow syntax in the earliest releases; nothing from F# 3.0 type providers onward). So:

- Code using post-2010 F# features **cannot** be from 2007–2008 — the check falsifies.
- Code whose surface is period-consistent **is not thereby dated** — a period-consistent codebase
  can be written today by anyone who knows the era.

One-way inference: it can refute a 2007–2008 claim and can never confirm one. Run it anyway; a
falsifier that only convicts is still a falsifier.

## 4. The honest ceiling

The brief proposes, and asks me to test rather than adopt, that domain + archived snapshots + code
together could support *"they were building a peer-to-peer money and logistics system, described as
such, by date X."*

**Tested: nearly right, and one clause is stronger than the artifacts support.**

Decomposing the proposed sentence:

| clause | supported by | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| "described as peer-to-peer money and logistics" | Wayback snapshot; annual-report nature-of-business | **supported** — third-party timestamp on self-authored text |
| "by date X" | WHOIS creation date; snapshot timestamps; filing date; email `Received:` chain | **supported** |
| "**they were building** [it]" | code only — whose date is participant-asserted | **overstated as one conjunct** |

The third clause is the problem: it silently borrows the date attestation from the first two and
applies it to the code, which has none. The strongest honest form keeps the two attestations
**separate** and says so:

> A company of that name existed and **publicly described itself as a peer-to-peer money and
> logistics system by date X** — attested by third-party records (domain registry, web archive,
> state filing). **Source code implementing it survives**, in F#, held by the founders; its date is
> participant-attested and period-consistent, not independently timestamped.

That is two facts with two different warrants, honestly labelled. It is not weaker in practice —
it is the version that survives a hostile reader.

**And here is the result that matters most, which cuts against doing more work rather than for it:**

> **The lineage claim needs far less evidence than this protocol can produce.**

Lineage is *"these people have first-hand experience building a peer-to-peer money system in that
era."* It needs the era, not the month. The Wayback snapshot alone very nearly discharges it; the
email plus a filing discharges it comfortably. Everything past that buys **date precision**, and
date precision is only load-bearing for a **priority** claim — which §1 rules out.

So the protocol's own conclusion is that **most of the available evidencing is unnecessary for the
claim we actually want to make.** That is worth stating plainly, because the temptation once
artifacts start arriving is to keep sharpening the date, and the only thing a sharper date buys is
the claim we have agreed not to make.

**The failure mode to watch while collecting.** Motivated evidencing: assembling artifacts *toward*
a conclusion and reading each one as confirmation. Aaron's own named risk applies directly
(`user_aaron_stores_long_term_memory_by_coincidence_index_…`) — and `numerology-vs-number-theory`
states the guard: **too many correlations is a warning, not a confirmation signal.** If every
artifact seems to line up perfectly, check whether they are independent or one fact wearing several
costumes. The domain, the site copy, and the code all trace to the same two people; only the
registry, the crawler, the state, and the mail servers are independent.

## 5. IP — the negative claim, and what it forecloses

> — Aaron states, 2026-08-14: *"it was code that we both contained the license to, private code, we
> don't use any of it here."*

Recorded as a **founder's attestation, dated and attributed** — which is the appropriate register
and the strongest one honestly available. It is explicitly **not** a verified audit finding: nobody

It is worth stating explicitly rather than leaving implicit because **it forecloses a diligence

### 5.1 The licence — settled enough, and gating nothing current

Facts from the same statement:

- The licence was **symmetric between the two founders** — both Aaron and Houman held a licence to
  the code. It is a **founder-held licence, not an OSS-style dual licence.**
- **The code is private.** Not published, not open-sourced.
- **Open, and now with a third party in it:** Moveable Cubicle **funded** Functional Tree (§2b), so
  it plausibly holds rights too. The "dual" may name a Functional-Tree/Moveable-Cubicle pair rather
  than a founder pair — **hypothesis to check against the document, not asserted.** Whichever it is,
  the licence has **potentially three parties** (founders, investors, funder), not two.

Consequence: the licence terms matter **only for publishing the old code**, and nobody has proposed
doing that. So the IP strand is a **contingency note, not a blocker**. It was ranked first in an
earlier draft of §7; that ranking is withdrawn.

The contingency, kept short in case it becomes live:

- **Rights are separable.** *Use*, *modification*, *publication*, *sublicensing*, and *relicensing*
  are distinct, and a licence can grant some and not others. A founder licence permitting **use**
  says nothing about permission to **publish** or to **relicense** as open source.
- **The repo already models this.** `src/Core/DerivationProtocol.fs` — `Wall.Whitebox` /
  `whiteboxPermitted` — encodes that **an unknown or unheld licence blocks; unknown is not
  permissive.** `.claude/rules/itron-hub-patent-boundary-p2p-is-the-upgrade.md` cites that mechanism
  for this class of problem: **inventorship is not a licence, and coworker sign-off is not assignee
  authority.** *Knowing a licence exists does not move the state from unknown to permitted. Reading
  it does.*

### 5.2 Ownership structure — dilution and licence rights are different things

Recorded as structure, with no conduct claim and no named parties (§6): Aaron and Houman were
**co-investors** as well as founders, and the founders' equity was **substantially diluted** through
dilutable stock.

Aaron's own compression of the condition, 2026-08-14: *"investors took advantage of asymmetric
information."* That is a structural description of the transaction, not a claim about anyone's
character, and it has a standard anchor. **First-time founders facing repeat-player investors is an
information asymmetry**, close to the base rate for the situation.

**Beacon anchors (checked, not merely cited):**

- George A. Akerlof, *"The Market for 'Lemons': Quality Uncertainty and the Market Mechanism"*,
  **Quarterly Journal of Economics 84(3), August 1970, 488–500** — the founding treatment of
  asymmetric information and adverse selection. *Entailment note:* Akerlof's subject is quality
  uncertainty in goods markets; it anchors the **mechanism**, and does not itself analyse
  founder–investor terms.
- Akerlof, A. Michael Spence and Joseph E. Stiglitz, **Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences
  2001**, *"for their analyses of markets with asymmetric information"* — Spence on signalling by
  the better-informed side, Stiglitz on what the worse-informed side can do.
- The claim's nearest **direct** anchor: Steven N. Kaplan and Per Strömberg, *"Financial Contracting
  Theory Meets the Real World: An Empirical Analysis of Venture Capital Contracts"*, **Review of
  Economic Studies 70(2), 2003, 281–315** — VC contracts separately allocate cash-flow, voting,
  board, liquidation and other control rights, which is the specific mechanism by which a cap table
  and a control position come apart.

The distinction that matters, and it runs opposite to intuition:

- **Dilution reduces ownership, and therefore control** over what the company does with its assets.
  Control likely sits with the investors — so "we will just ask the company" is not a fallback the
  founders can rely on.
- **Dilution does not, by itself, extinguish a licence.** A licence is a granted right and survives
  a change in the cap table unless its own terms say otherwise. Given the founders were diluted,
  the founder-held licence may be **the most valuable thing they retained** from that company.

### 5.3 What is gated, and what is not

**Gated by the licence:** publishing the old source, open-sourcing it, relicensing it. None of these
is proposed.

**Not gated by anything:** citing that the company existed, naming what it did, and **the entire
evidence path in §3**. Those are facts, and **facts are not owned.** A domain registry date, a web
archive snapshot, and a state filing cannot be licensed, assigned, or diluted.

Stated structurally, because it is both correct and the point: **nobody can dilute you out of having
built something.** The lineage claim — the thing of actual value to Zeta — does not depend on the
cap table at all.

**Not legal advice.** Dilution terms, licence survival, and what a dissolved entity's IP does are
precisely the questions counsel exists for.
