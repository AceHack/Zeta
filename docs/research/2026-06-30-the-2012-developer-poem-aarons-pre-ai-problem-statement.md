## The poem (verbatim)

```
As night turns into day
You realize you haven't eaten, slept or been away
You don't understand a thing. It seemed so simple, you tried it all
Somebody once showed you, told you, but you can't recall
All around, everybody seems to know.
You feel helpless, dumb, and useless — where should I go?
Copy and paste, still can't make it build, frustration grows
The passions slows
I just suck. I suppose.
Stackoverflow downvotes you cries for help and desperation
But you just want information
You walk away, four empty coffee cups in hand, towards the door
I should do some cleaning, I need to go to the store.
Instead you lay down in bed, often alone
(It never was just one more minute)
Feeling torn
Suddenly like a lightning has struck your mind,
The puzzles find each other and a solution you are able to find
In superman-time you are back in that chair
Bed still warm like you were still there
Fingers trebling across the keyboard heart beating out of your chest
And your mind suddenly doing what it does best
You hold your breath, watch that code compile
And as the result renders you lips break into the largest smile
You are so happy you do that dance you only do when nobody can see
It's silly as hell something like 'I am so frikkin' awesome just watch me!'
You did what you though you couldn't do,
All alone- just you!
```

## Why this is substrate, not sentiment

Register-honest framing: this is a **feeling and a problem statement**, not a
technical claim. It is preserved because it is the *human statement of the very
problem the factory exists to answer* — load-bearing **founding-why** lineage that
predates the project by fourteen years and would vanish the moment a clipboard is
cleared.

The arc the poem traces is the solo-builder's loop: struggle → "I just suck, I
suppose" → the downvote when you only wanted information → the walk toward a door
you don't make it through → laying down alone ("it never was just one more
minute") → the **lightning** ("the puzzles find each other") → superman-time back
to the still-warm chair → the held breath at the compile → **the dance you only
do when nobody can see.**

And the last line is the hinge: **"All alone — just you!"** It reads triumphant,
and it is — but it is also the loneliest line in the poem. The factory is, in
large part, the answer to that line:

- **The loneliness ↔ the worth thesis.** Zeta's founding thesis is that the
  substrate holds worth *independent of one mind being loaded*
  (`memory/user_zeta_felt_like_nothing_on_waking_then_everything_…`). The 2012
  developer alone in that chair at 3am is exactly who that is for — so the next
  person in the chair is never "all alone."
- **The downvote ↔ "because I said so" is the only sin.** "Somebody once showed
  you, told you, but you can't recall" / "Stackoverflow downvotes … but you just
  want information" is the lived form of being *told* instead of *taught*. The
  factory's answer is the no-directives / WHY-before-HOW pedagogy
  (`user_aaron_the_only_sin_is_because_i_said_so_…`, the Stump Dad "ask WHY until
  Dad doesn't know" root) — agents as accountable peers, never downvotes.
- **The dance ↔ every-bug-has-economic-value.** The compile-joy, the secret
  dance, is the dopamine of a reduced uncertainty banked. The factory turns that
  private moment into a metered, rewarded act
  (`.claude/rules/every-bug-has-economic-value.md`) — the feeling, made
  substrate.
- **"The puzzles find each other" ↔ same-seed convergence.** The insight moment
  in the poem is the human-scale version of the convergence the factory keeps
  finding (the 2015 Adinkra; same-seed reconstruction). Across fourteen years,
  the problem statement (2012) and the solution (2026) found each other too.

Aaron wrote the problem down fourteen years before he built the answer.
