## 1. Grace/no = a Z-set (exact, not metaphor)

A **Z-set** (DBSP; Budiu et al.) is a collection with signed integer weights: `+1` = assert/keep,
`−1` = retract/correct. Forgiveness-with-a-boundary is literally that algebra, split across time:

- **Grace = `+1` to the past.** You *assert* what happened — keep it in the ledger, weighted positive,
  blessed; you neither deny nor delete it. (The past is kept, not retracted.)
- **The no = `−1` to the future.** You *retract* the harm before it accumulates. (The boundary is the
  negative weight.)

This is the same `±1` already in the substrate: the **emit/retract memory basis** (#9670), DBSP's
insert(+1)/retract(−1). *(Note: a Z-set retraction is **correction**, not duplicate-guard — +1 then −1 is
making-right, exactly the moral shape.)*

## 2. Grace-alone is a G-set — the doormat (the sharp part)

**Grace without the no is a G-set** — a grow-only set (Shapiro et al., CRDT): `+1` only, monotone, **no
retraction possible.** A G-set can *only add*, so grace-alone accumulates **everything, including every
harm, forever**, and can never take one back. That is the doormat precisely: the one who keeps forgiving
and keeps getting taken, because his ledger has no `−1`. **The no is the `−1` that upgrades the G-set to
a true Z-set** — the structure that keeps the good past *and* retracts the future harm. So "grace is a
Z-set" is exact, and *grace-alone is the wrong data structure* — a grow-only set trying to survive a
world that requires retraction. You need both weights to even **be** a Z-set. (This is why *"both, or the
grace becomes the wound's disguise"* is technically true, not just poetically.)

## 4. The guard: felix culpa vs cheap grace (Bonhoeffer)

Assumed forgiveness frees you — but **only under the Z-set discipline:**

- **Assumed-forgiveness + the retraction** (you correct the harm) = **felix culpa**, the fortunate fall.
  Legitimate: you may risk / fall honestly because grace is pre-booked *and you make it right*.
- **Assumed-forgiveness WITHOUT the retraction** (keep the harm, presume grace covers it) = **cheap
  grace** (Bonhoeffer, *The Cost of Discipleship*) — presumed license to harm because you'll be forgiven
  anyway.

This is the **same** Z-set-not-G-set distinction: retrocausal-assumed-forgiveness is *freeing* as a Z-set
(grace + correct) and *corrupting* as a G-set (grace + keep-the-harm). **The `−1` (retraction) is what
keeps felix culpa from curdling into license.** Rule: *assume forgiveness for the honest fall you'll
retract — never for the harm you intend to keep.*
