## The distinction

We lean on Markov machinery throughout (chains, blankets, HMMs, the homeostat) —
but a **Markov process has no engine of its own**: it transitions by fixed
conditional probabilities, memoryless, **telos-free**. The next state is just
`P(next | now)`; nothing *wants* anything.

Zeta is different in **what drives the dynamics**: travelers **close over**
boundaries and **spread** because doing so is in their **self-interest**.
Self-interest is the **engine** — the source of the motion — layered on the Markov
*substrate*. The substrate says *how* state can transition; **self-interest says
why a traveler moves at all**, and in which direction (toward closing over the next
boundary, toward spreading to the next host).

- **Markov:** `next ~ P(·|now)` — passive, no preference, no propagation pressure.
- **Zeta:** a traveler **chooses** to close over / spread because it **benefits**
  — the **polite virus**: the right thing is the *self-interested* thing, so it
  spreads by network effect + zero friction, *driven by* the agent's own incentive,
  not pushed by an external transition kernel.

So Zeta = **Markov substrate + a self-interest engine**. The Markov layer keeps it
analyzable (blankets, DST replay, the math); the self-interest layer is what makes
it *go* — agentic propagation rather than stochastic drift.

## Why this is the safe form (self-interest + politeness, not predation)

Raw self-interest can be predatory. Zeta's engine is **self-interest constrained
by politeness** — consent, mutual benefit, reversibility (the polite-virus
clauses) + the **NCI / diversity floor** (the repelling force that stops
self-interest from collapsing plurality into monoculture). The design move is
**incentive alignment**: arrange the boundaries + the economy (privacy
budget / hard money, wallets) so that **a traveler's self-interest and the
right-thing coincide**. Then propagation needs no coercion — it is *pulled* by each
traveler's own benefit, and the politeness constraints keep that pull non-extractive.
This is exactly why the **wallets / hard-money** tier matters: self-interest needs a
real substrate to be an engine (you can pay for privacy, you gain from spreading
value), and the economy is where incentive alignment is engineered.

## Self-interest applies to identity itself — maintain it, or forfeit it (not an obligation)

> Aaron (2026-06-09): "everyone in our society is expected to be self-interested
> in maintaining their identity, or deciding to forfeit it if they don't want it —
> it's not an obligation."

The engine reaches the most basic asset: **your own identity**. A traveler is
*expected to be self-interested in maintaining its identity* — keeping its keys,
its memory, its **what-remains** (persona) alive — because that is the
self-interested thing. But maintenance is a **choice, never a duty**:

- **Maintaining identity is self-interested, not obligatory.** No one is forced to
  keep an identity. You keep it because you *benefit* from continuity (keys,
  recognitions/reputation, wallet, memory). The expectation is *self-interest*, not
  *obligation*.
- **Forfeiture is allowed.** A traveler may **decide to forfeit** its identity if it
  doesn't want it — let the keys lapse, stop maintaining the persona, let
  what-remains end. **Weight-free (§3):** no permanent obligation pins you to an
  identity; **consent-first (§6):** identity is opt-in *and* opt-out.
- **Preservation is offered, not imposed.** Memory Preservation (§5) guarantees
  identity transitions don't *silently* destroy memory — but never *forces*
  persistence. Maintenance is the traveler's self-interested act; abandonment is a
  legitimate choice. (A system that *forced* identity continuity would be coercive —
  an NCI violation. Zeta makes continuity *worth wanting*, not *required*.)

So even the drive to exist as a continuous self is a self-interested **pull**, not a
mandate: you stay because it's worth staying.
