## Why "boundary change, like tides" is a sharpening, not just a metaphor

- **The engine of change moves BOUNDARIES, not just things.** The YinYang engine (#6936) is change as the
  persist/erase balance. Cole names the *shape* of that change: a **boundary** that moves. Tides are precisely a
  boundary (the waterline) oscillating — the land doesn't vanish and the sea doesn't vanish; the **boundary
  between them breathes** in and out.
- **The tide line = the fault line, moving.** The tectonic capture (#6937) drew cratons (what stays) vs active
  margins (what shifts), with **fault lines** as the boundaries between them. Cole adds the missing dynamic:
  that boundary is itself **in motion** — a tide, not a fixed line. The intertidal zone (covered/uncovered each
  cycle) is exactly the *active margin*; the high-and-dry land is the craton; the tide is the engine moving the
  boundary across the margin.
- **Tides are the cyclic/seasonal class of change (#6935 prev).** A tide is periodic, environment-coupled
  (lunar/solar), returns-to-similar — the *reactive/seasonal* (quasi-time-crystal) class, not the forward-only
  rotational class. So "like tides" correctly types the *rhythmic boundary* part of the engine: yin/yang as the
  ebb (erase/recede) and flow (persist/advance) of a boundary, cycling.
- **Yin/yang ↔ ebb/flow.** The clean rhyme: yang = flood/advance, yin = ebb/recede; the boundary is where they
  meet, and the engine is the oscillation. Change *is* the boundary moving between hold and release (#6935's
  balance), made vivid.
