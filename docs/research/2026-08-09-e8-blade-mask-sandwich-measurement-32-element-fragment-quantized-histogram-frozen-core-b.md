`src/Core.TypeScript/algebra/e8-blade-mask-sandwich.ts` replicates, in

s_A(x) = −A·x·Ã / ⟨A·Ã⟩₀.

Baseline for construction fidelity: the classical ℝ⁸ reflection

## The measurement (golden numbers, asserted in the test)

Over all 240 × 240 = 57,600 ordered pairs (A, x):

1. **Versor-normed elements: exactly 32 of 240.** A bridged root A is
   versor-normed when A·Ã is scalar. The 32 sit on exactly 10 supports:
   the 8 single blades (the ±2·blade roots), plus the only two weight-4
   codewords whose supports align with Cl(3,0)'s own structure —
   {1,2,5,6} = {e₁, e₂, e₁₃, e₂₃} and its complement
   {0,3,4,7} = {S, e₁₂, e₃, e₁₂₃}. Note {0,3,4,7} is XOR-closed: it is
   the subalgebra generated by e₁₂ and e₃, and {1,2,5,6} is its coset.
   Of the 14 weight-4 codewords, precisely this complementary pair is
   Clifford-aligned.

2. **Each of the 32 preserves ALL 240 roots** — 7,680/7,680 pairs. A
   perfect root-symmetry fragment lives inside the bridge.

3. **The other 208 quantize.** Per-A preservation histogram:

   | roots preserved | # of A |
   | --- | --- |
   | 0 | 160 |
   | 64 | 32 |
   | 128 | 16 |
   | 240 | 32 |

   Totals: 33,024 images have integer coordinates; 11,776 are roots
   (20.4%); 352 pairs are identity-fixed.

## Interpretation

The route-B disclaimer upgrades from an argument to a theorem-shaped
measurement: **the blade-mask sandwich implements exactly a 32-element
Clifford-aligned symmetry fragment of the E8 root system, and nothing
more.** It is nowhere near a reflection action (baseline 100% vs 20.4%),
so Cl(3,0) is confirmed as the basis/metric bridge only — but the
fragment is real, and its membership has a reason: an element acts as a
root symmetry precisely when its support is compatible with the algebra
that is doing the sandwiching. The two special codewords are where the
adinkra code and the Clifford blade structure agree.

## Part II — both minted questions answered (same day)

### The fragment group is D8 × C2

The 32 versor-normed elements induce sandwich permutations of the 240
roots; A and −A induce the same permutation, and closing the 16 distinct
maps under composition terminates at a group of **order 16**, non-abelian,
with element-order histogram {1: 1, 2: 11, 4: 4}, center of order 4, and
commutator subgroup of order 2 (`fragmentGroup()`, asserted in the test).
Eleven involutions occurs in no other group of order 16: the fragment is
**D8 × C2** — the dihedral symmetries of a square times a reflection. The
geometric reading writes itself: the dihedral group of the (e12, e3)
frame — the very subalgebra whose codeword survived as a versor — doubled
by the pseudoscalar sign.

### The quantization follows subalgebra signature

`strataBySupport()` (asserted in the test) resolves the {0, 64, 128, 240}
histogram completely:

| support class | signature | strata |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ⟨e₁₂,e₃⟩ + coset | Cl(1,1)-like, e₁₂² = −1 | 8 versors → 240; 8 → 128 |
| ⟨e₁,e₃⟩, ⟨e₂,e₃⟩ + cosets | Cl(2,0), squares +1 | 8 → 64; 8 → 0 |
| eight non-subgroup-coset codewords | none | all 16 → 0 |

Within every algebra-aligned support the 16 sign patterns split evenly
8/8 between the two tiers — sign parity relative to the subalgebra
decides which half. The remaining open (now much smaller): prove the
predicate *tier = f(subalgebra signature of the support, sign parity)*
from the Cl(3,0) product rules — the table above is the complete truth
set a proof must reproduce.
