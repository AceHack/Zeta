## The fifth oracle

The treaty discipline has four LANGUAGE oracles (F# locks the golden vectors; C#/TS/Rust conform
byte-identically). Those oracles speak hex. A human cannot ratify hex — until the same math grows
perceptual projections:

- **pictures** — BoundaryLight/AdinkraViz/WaveSim render the golden math onto the planes;
- **audio** — ChipAudio renders the same tick arithmetic as waveforms (hear and see ONE math);
- **animations** — StrokeAnim/AnimFlow play the math unfolding over generated time.

With all three, **human perception becomes the fifth oracle**: a person ratifies the treaty by
looking and listening — "the spiral reads as a clock face; the fringes land on the lines; the
downbeats hit together" — no code-reading required. And because every projection derives from the
SAME golden vectors and common-cause seed, perceptual agreement IS agreement about the bytes: the
render-is-the-oracle doctrine (yesterday's capture) run in reverse — the overlay truths let the eye
falsify, the derivation chain lets the eye's yes count.

Why this matters for the society: treaty ratification stops being gated on programmers. ANYONE —
Addison, Max, a kid at the cabinet — can serve as an oracle, because the question put to them is
perceptual ("does this match?") with known-answer overlays marking what "match" means. Consensus
widens from four compilers to every traveler with eyes and ears. (Beacon anchors: this is acceptance
testing made perceptual — the demo-as-acceptance tradition; and dual-coding/redundant-display from
human-factors práctice — the same value shown two ways catches what one way hides.)

The honest bound, kept: the human oracle ratifies the PROJECTION (does the render/audio match the
declared intent); the byte-lock still belongs to the language oracles. Five oracles, two registers —
machines agree on bytes, humans agree on meaning, the seed ties them.
