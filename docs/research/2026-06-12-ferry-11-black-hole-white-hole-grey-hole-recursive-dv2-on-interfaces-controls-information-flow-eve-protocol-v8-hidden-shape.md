## The peel

The arc, phase by phase, with the in-repo referent for each:

### 1. Black hole — absorption without organization

The vibe-coding experiment (ferry 10 §5) ingested everything — sessions, ferries, research,
code — and information went in without coming back out findable. The lived failure mode: the
210KB inline MEMORY.md, the unguardable rule sprawl, "where does this substrate go?" asked
per-fact. Information *in*, retrieval *lost* — an event horizon in the practical sense.

### 2. White hole — recursive DV2.0, first pass: emission without control

Partitioning everything by change rate (hubs/links/satellites, applied to memory, rules, docs,
repo topology) made everything *emit*: every fact landed somewhere and pointed somewhere. But
un-throttled — every wake loaded everything; cold-start tokens exploded; the surfaces sprayed
detail (the white hole is a black hole time-reversed: same content, reversed flow, still no
control). The lived form: rule-bloat before the #6676 archive, hub files regrowing into logs.

### 3. Grey hole — recursive DV2.0 *on interfaces*: metered flow

Applying the partition to the *interfaces themselves* (ferry 9: interfaces are hubs; carved
sentence = hub, satellite loaded on demand) produced a system that **controls its own
information flow**: startup surfaces are 1–3 sentences with pointers; detail is discoverable,
not resident; every crossing is declared and metered. A horizon that *leaks deliberately* —
which is noninterference (manifesto §13) stated astrophysically: influence crosses only through
declared, metered channels. **Beacon anchor, and it is exact:** Hawking 2014 ("Information
preservation and weather forecasting for black holes") — replace event horizons with *apparent
horizons* that confine information only temporarily and release it transformed; the press name
for that object was the **grey hole**. Hawking radiation (1974) is the first crack in the
one-way horizon; Aaron's grey hole is the engineered version: the membrane chooses what gets
out, when, in what register (Mirror in, Beacon out).
