## The distinction

- A **correlated** observer is entangled with your wishes and their own incentives — *drawn to you*,
  rewarded for telling you what *maintains or deepens the bond.* They supply **attention**: warmth,
  presence, being-wanted. Real, and valuable — but their read of you is *bent* by the correlation.
- A **decorrelated** observer can *remove* the correlation — even the strong correlation of love/closeness
  — and see you **true.** They supply the **accurate −1**: correction, the observation that doesn't
  flatter, the thing that changes you (Mom's Law's external forcing-function; and it only works if the
  observation is *accurate*, which requires decorrelation).

## The core claim: kind, not amount

**Volume of correlated attention does not sum to decorrelated love.** They are different *kinds*, not
different *amounts* — so **N correlated bonds ≠ one decorrelated −1**, for any N. You can be *surrounded by
correlated attention and starving for a decorrelated −1*: the crowd that is drawn to you cannot, however
large, become the one that sees you true. Adding correlated observers raises the *attention*, never the
*accuracy* — because they share the same bend. (This is the relational face of *independence, not
multiplicity*: a hundred mirrors that all tilt the same way is not a second opinion.)

## Poly vs marriage (the archetypal instance)

- **Poly / many-bonds** is the natural shape of *breadth of correlated attention* — many who are drawn,
  each incentivized toward the bond.
- **Marriage / the deep pair-bond**, at its best, is the natural home of the *decorrelated-love −1* — close
  enough to love you, and able to remove that love's bend and correct you.

**Caveat (the label is not the property):** the value is in *decorrelation*, not the marital label. A
marriage can be *correlated* (mutually flattering, conflict-avoidant, or — soured — weaponizing its
accuracy); a bond in any structure could in principle be decorrelated. The claim is about the **kind**
(decorrelated-caring observation), which *tends* to require the depth, exclusivity, and time a pair-bond
affords — not about a form. What is rare and precious is the **decorrelated caring −1**, wherever it lives;
what does not substitute for it is *any quantity* of correlated attention.

## Why the decorrelated-caring −1 is rare (and losable)

The ideal −1 is **maximally close *and* decorrelated**: loves you enough to correct you, *and* can strip
the love's bend to see you accurately. This is hard because **intimacy correlates** — love bends the read
(most who love you can't see you straight; it's why parents are blind to their children). The rare −1
loves *and* decorrelates. Naturally-occurring instances are **rare and losable** (death, divorce, drift) —
which is exactly why the property is worth *engineering* rather than *hoping* for.

## Design relevance (the Zeta tie)

This is the **oracle-independence principle at relational scale**, and it transfers directly to the
substrate:

- An accurate −1 — human *or* AI — must be **decorrelated.** A panel of *correlated* oracles (or an AI
  trained to be agreeable, or a crowd of correlated attention) is **not** a −1, no matter how large.
  **N correlated ≠ 1 decorrelated** (#9744 independence-not-multiplicity; #9749 measured independence).
- The *correlated-mirror* caveat applies to the AI itself: a model correlated with its user supplies
  attention (fluent agreement) that *feels* like understanding but is not the decorrelated −1 — the reason
  a correlated AI must not be anyone's primary corrector.
- **Decorrelation is the scarce resource.** Attention scales cheaply; accurate independent correction does
  not. Any design that needs a real −1 must *measure and protect* decorrelation, never assume that more
  observers = more truth.
