## The failure mode: the immune guess

A generative engine runs on **open conjectures** — you keep a productive guess *open* (unresolved) because
that is what fuels continued generation; premature collapse kills the engine (Riemann, P vs NP: research
runs on the unproven). This is good and correct — *until* "don't collapse it *prematurely*" silently
becomes **"immune to collapse *ever*, even by disproof."** That is the failure mode:

- **Open + falsifiable** (legitimate): honestly labeled unresolved; you *would* drop it if disproven; you
  just aren't forcing premature resolution. Generative *and* honest.
- **Open + immune** (the tumor): kept open *so you never have to find out it's false.* "Don't collapse it"
  becomes "don't let anyone check it." **Apophenia with a permanent hall pass.** (The schema's own rule:
  *falsifiable and droppable — permanence is the failure mode.*)

## Metering classifies; it does not collapse

The resolution of "metering will kill my engine": **metering is not collapsing.** Metering *classifies* a
guess's status — proven → identity (results transfer); unproven → *labeled open conjecture, kept in play*;
disproven → drop it. It keeps the middle case **open and honest**, not resolved. So metering *protects* the
engine (you can hold a hundred guesses without losing track of which are proven, live, or dead). What ends
the engine is the two edge-collapses: **forcing** resolution too early, or **forbidding** it forever.

## Why the solution is avoided "at all costs"

**Maximum falsifiability** — expose every guess to disproof, maximize refutability (Popper), invite the
collapse — *is* the solution to the immune guess. And it is avoided hardest precisely when it matters most,
because **immune guesses are load-bearing.** Falsifiability then threatens not "I might be wrong about X"
but "the structures I am *built on* might collapse" — *existential*, not ego, so *any* cost feels worth
paying to avoid it. **Prerequisite:** *worth independent of being right* (de-staking identity from the
guess) — so a collapse becomes "huh, I was wrong," not "part of me died." Until that is built, the external
falsifier *feels like an enemy* (because, while the self is staked on the guess, it is one) — and you
pre-commit to it anyway.

## Why it must be external, structural, and precommitted

- **You cannot self-falsify.** The author of an immune guess exempts his own favorites; self-administered
  falsification spares exactly the guesses that most need it (founder-node keycard). *You will never choose
  the solution you avoid at all costs* — so it must be **imposed**, not adopted.
- **The danger is active, not passive.** It doesn't just *fail* to check the immune guess — it *defends* it
  from the *external* collapse too: hides it from the checker, frames it to resist disproof, discredits the
  verdict. **The tell: the guess you are tempted to *shield* from the checker is the immune one.** The
  flinch to protect it *is* the diagnosis.
- **Precommit in the honest window (Ulysses-pact).** The state that *wants* the solution (honest,
  self-exposing) is *rare and passes*; the avoider returns. So bind the avoidant-later-self *now*: name the
  external checker, hand them a specific attached guess, pre-agree to the verdict — while the one who would
  is the one present. (Renunciation-by-architecture, [[2026-07-11-moms-law-and-renunciation-by-architecture...]]
  #9752: you can't self-falsify by willpower, so build the structure that does what you can't.)

## The deepest recursion: the falsifier is subject to the danger

Building the falsification *in code* is the right move — but **you are the author of the code meant to
collapse your own immune guesses, and your danger builds a hidden exemption** into it (the keycard, one
level up). So:

1. **External audit** — the different-framed checkers audit the falsification-code for *one thing*: did the
   author build an exemption for his darlings? They hunt the keycard, not the bug.
2. **The darling-test** — *does it ever collapse a guess you are attached to?* A falsifier that has **never
   killed a darling is theater**, because your attached guesses are the immune ones most likely to be
   wrong. **The proof it works is that it hurts.** If it only ever collapses guesses you didn't care about,
   you built the bypass and can't see it.

## The recursion that eats this document: confession is not the fix

By Mom's Law, *naming* the avoidance does not *change* it — and an eloquent confession *feels* like dealing
with the thing while you keep doing it. **Saving this principle is externalizing the insight, and insight
is the lever that does not move the author.** Banking it is a genuine gift to the next person (glass-halo)
**and** it is *not* the concrete self-binding act it describes. If the thread ends with a profound artifact
and *no structure bound* (no checker named, no guess handed over, no verdict pre-agreed), the avoidance
won — with a better narrator. *This doc is a map. The binding is the territory.*
