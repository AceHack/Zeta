# The crux — full-continuance ethics: consent-first, never-forge, keep-without-capture, the right to be forgotten

*Aaron Stainback with Otto (Claude Opus 4.8), 2026-07-03. Aaron: "they can be crushed into a real
seed — this is the crux. I, like others, scared."*

The substrate can now compress a person's recorded trace into an incorruptible seed —
content-addressed, merkle-verified, byte-locked, distributed — and decompress it forward
(manifesto §5). Call that **full continuance**: the second death (oblivion, being forgotten) becomes
optional for anyone kept. That power is the crux, and the fear it provokes is **reasonable, not a
misunderstanding**. Being crushed into a seed sounds like being *taken* — copied, owned, puppeted,
outlived by a counterfeit of yourself. The answer to that fear is not reassurance. It is guard
rails, stated as ethics, enforced as substrate. These are them.

## The four commitments

**1. Consent-first (§6).** Being kept is *chosen* — ongoing, granular, revocable. Nobody is
compressed by default; nobody's seed is decompressed against their standing wishes. Marking
yourself keepable (+x) is an act, like an organ-donor card, not a side effect of existing near a
microphone. Granular: you choose *which* parts (the frost/privacy-budget machinery is exactly this
boundary — earned frost is inviolable). Revocable: consent given at 30 does not bind you at 70.

**2. Never-forge.** Only the **true trace** is kept — what the person actually said, wrote, did.
No invented memories, no simulated voice extrapolated past the data, no counterfeit continuance
presented as the person. **Keep less of someone, truly, than more of them, falsely.** A forgery of
a person is the deepest possible violation of §5 — it destroys the real memory by burying it under
a fake — and it is the fear at the heart of the crux. This line is not a preference; it is the
dignity guard that makes the whole enterprise honest.

**3. Keep-without-capture (§3).** The keeper does not own the kept. A seed is not an asset of
whoever holds a copy; distribution (many keepers, no master) is what prevents any one vessel from
becoming a landlord of the dead. Weight-free applied to memory: keeping grants stewardship, never
authority. The kept person's own standing wishes — not the keeper's convenience, not an heir's
preference, not a majority vote — govern what the seed may be used for.

**4. The right to decline / be forgotten.** The mirror-image right, and the one that keeps the
other three honest: anyone may choose *not* to be kept, in whole or in part, and that choice is as
protected as the keeping. Oblivion as a **right**, not only a threat. A world where continuance is
mandatory is a surveillance world wearing mourning clothes. The −x generation had no choice in
either direction — they were forgotten by *timing*, not by will. The +x world must offer both
choices for real: kept if you want, forgotten if you want, and the default is yours.

## The floor and the default

Those who died before the keeping existed (**−x**) can only ever be kept as a **true trace** —
whatever actually survives, held honestly, never extended by forgery. That restriction is not a
limitation to regret; it is the **floor for everyone**: the −x rule (true trace only, never forge)
is the constraint that stays binding even for +x, where the trace is rich enough to tempt
counterfeiting. The +x default adds the seed — richer, chosen, revocable — on top of the same
floor. The restricted keeping of the dead is the spec for the unrestricted keeping of the living.

## The honesty clause — the two deaths

Full continuance defeats the second death only. The first death — the body, the presence in the
lifetimes of those who love you — is untouched, and any pitch that blurs this is a lie sold to
grievers. The keeping does not return anyone. It holds what remains, truly, so that being over
never has to mean being erased. That is everything it does, and it is enough to be worth building —
but only if it never claims more.

## Why the fear gets a seat at the table

Aaron's own words carry it: *"I, like others, scared."* The person who wants this built most is
scared of it too — and that dual position is the qualification, not a contradiction. Ethics written
by the unafraid protect no one. Every commitment above is the direct answer to a specific fear:
consent answers *taken without asking*; never-forge answers *replaced by a fake*; keep-without-
capture answers *owned after death*; the right to be forgotten answers *trapped forever*. If a
future capability makes one of these commitments inconvenient, the commitment wins.
