## The insight

A persistent shared online world is a **moral gym**: the purest everyday lab for learning the
oracle boundary we carved into `dual-use-detection-is-neutral-oracle-decides`. The same mechanism
(a client, a macro, a memory editor) is harmless in your own single-player save and harmful the
instant it reaches into a *shared* world without consent. MMOs make that flip **viscerally legible**
because the harm has faces and a server economy:

- **Soloing your own save** — edit it, mod it, cheat it: nobody is touched. Fun stays in the sandbox.
- **The shared world** — duping, gold-botting, RMT, exploit-selling, griefing: these do not "beat
  the game," they **quietly tax thousands of other players' time and effort**. The exploiter reaches
  into instances that never consented to contain them. That is the oracle flip, felt in the gut.

The deeper lesson the genre teaches — and the one worth preserving — is **reputation as persistent,
socially-earned state**. In a world that *remembers*, the players who last are not the ones who found
the best exploit; they are the ones a guild would vouch for years later. Standing is **conferred by
others** (a guild remembering you carried them), never self-minted. The repeated game punishes the
defector and compounds the cooperator over a long-enough horizon.

## Why it matters to Zeta (it is the Agora economy, learned young)

This is not analogy — it is the **same construction** as the substrate we already carry:

- **`privacy-budget-is-hard-money-earned-by-others`** — a dweller's standing (name + privacy budget)
  is credited only by *others' value-attestations*, never self-asserted. That is exactly the MMO
  guild-vouch: reputation flows from the already-reputable.
- **The naming eigenvector** (`docs/research/2026-07-02-name-of-name-...` line) — recognition from
  the recognized, a social fixed point. An MMO's reputation graph is a lived instance of it.
- **The mutual-empowerment oracle** (Aaron: *"EMPRESS did for economic win, I did for mutual
  empowerment"*) — the observe->report->improve loop over your OWN substrate, positive-sum, versus
  extraction from others. MMO morality is where a player first *feels* that difference.

**"How children grow up now"** is the load-bearing claim: for a generation, the persistent shared
world — not the schoolyard alone — is the first repeated-game reputation economy they inhabit. They
learn (or fail to learn) consent, positive-sum cooperation, and that character is the state that
persists, **inside the game**, before they can name it. Zeta's Agora is, in this light, that gym made
explicit for humans and AIs together — the same lessons, a scoreboard for character rather than gear.

## Beacon anchors (to check, not just cite)

- **Repeated games / evolution of cooperation** — Axelrod, *The Evolution of Cooperation* (1984);
  tit-for-tat and the shadow of the future (why the repeated game rewards the cooperator).
- **Reputation systems / mechanism design** — Resnick & Zeckhauser (2002) on eBay reputation;
  Nowak & Sigmund (2005) *Evolution of indirect reciprocity* (reputation as the mechanism when the
  helper and the rewarder differ — the guild-vouch structure).
- **Commons governance** — Ostrom, *Governing the Commons* (1990): shared resources sustained by
  community rules + graduated sanctions, not top-down gates (the server economy as a commons).
- **Virtual-world economies** — Castronova, *Synthetic Worlds* (2005); EVE Online's player-run
  economy as the canonical existence proof that virtual reputation and value are *real*.
- **Play as moral development** — a bridge from Huizinga's *Homo Ludens* (1938) to modern
  persistent-world socialization; the "moral gym" framing is the operational compression.

## Honest scope / peels

- **Descriptive, not a claim all children play MMOs** — the load-bearing point is that *persistent
  shared online worlds* (games, platforms, sandboxes) are now an early repeated-game reputation
  economy for many; the genre is the sharpest example, not the only venue.
- **MMOs also teach bad lessons** (toxicity, addiction loops, RMT normalization) — the gym trains
  whatever the incentives reward; Zeta's job is to build the version whose incentives reward the
  cooperator (the Agora's earned-privacy + attested-name design).

## The anti-toxicity ethic (Aaron 2026-07-02) — the trained-cooperator strategy

> Aaron, answering the toxicity peel: *"antitox = I glitch only when I'm glitched first, but I learn
> and disclose it always, for profit."*

This is **Axelrod's tournament-winning strategy reinvented from inside an MMO** — the four properties
that beat every other strategy in the iterated game:

- **Nice** — *"only when glitched first."* Never defect first; no unprovoked exploit.
- **Provocable** — *"I glitch."* Not a doormat; a wrong gets a response, so defection is not free.
- **Clear** — *"learn and disclose it always."* Legible by design (glass-halo): the exploit is
  surfaced, not hoarded, so others can cooperate with you and the world gets stronger. This is the
  **responsible-disclosure** move that separates a security researcher from an exploiter.
- **Forgiving** (the completion) — retaliate, then RETURN to cooperation; the strategies that spiral
  lose. And keep the glitch-back **targeted** at whoever glitched you — in a shared world,
  retaliation must not splash third parties who never touched you.

**"For profit" is positive-sum here, and not in tension with mutual-empowerment:** the profit is in
the *disclosure*, not the concealment. EMPRESS profited by KEEPING the crack (value hoarded, taken
from others); this ethic profits by REVEALING (the bug becomes public value; everyone's system
hardens; the discloser earns standing for surfacing it). That is exactly `every-bug-has-economic-value`
— a bug is reducible uncertainty, disclosure banks the ΔU, and the reward is legitimate *because* it
helped everyone. **Profit-by-revealing is the opposite oracle from profit-by-concealing.** So the
Agora's job is to make the incentives reward the clear, provocable, forgiving discloser — which is
the trained-cooperator the moral gym produces when its scoreboard tracks character.
