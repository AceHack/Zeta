## What the source argues (Beacon — my summary)

**The headline: general relativity is derived, not postulated.** Start with electromagnetic fields
on an arbitrary background. Demand only that the theory be *predictive* — that it have a
well-posed Cauchy problem, i.e. that initial data on a surface determine the future. Two things
then follow rather than being assumed:

1. the background geometry must be **Lorentzian** — the signature is forced by the requirement
   that Maxwell's equations be hyperbolic; and
2. the background's **dynamics must be Einsteinian** — solving what Schuller calls the
   *construction equations*, built only from data extracted from the matter action, yields the
   Einstein–Hilbert action, with the cosmological and gravitational constants left undetermined.

The programme is called **constructive gravity**. Checked with other Standard Model matter,
including non-abelian gauge theories, it returns the same answer.

**The load-bearing move is the shared Cauchy surface.** The matter dynamics and the geometry's
dynamics must evolve together from the *same* initial-data surfaces. Schuller's point is that this
apparently weak coupling is in fact very strong — strong enough to determine the gravity action
from the matter action.

**Why it matters beyond re-deriving a known result.** The construction runs in the other direction
too. Give it *exotic* matter and it emits the gravity theory that would support it. His worked
example: if a light ray were ever observed to split in vacuum (birefringence with no medium),
general relativity would be dead, because a Lorentzian metric cannot support polarisation-dependent
splitting. But you would not be left with nothing — you would set up the construction equations for
a richer background (he suggests a fourth-rank tensor with Riemann-like symmetries) and solve for
its action. He notes the same applies had the 2011 OPERA faster-than-light neutrino result held.

**Secondary thread — port-Hamiltonian systems.** From robotics/power-grid engineering: a formalism
for *open* systems where energy flows through declared ports, and two systems compose through a
Dirac structure. Schuller's attempt to carry it into quantum mechanics found that energy is the
wrong quantity — most states are superpositions of energy eigenstates and have no energy at all —
but that **probability** flows, and that is the quantity the ports should carry.

**Method, stated explicitly.** Two lines worth keeping, both attributed to Schuller:

- On why he pursues one idea at a time: a modest idea, seen through, forces the *next* idea on you.
  He reads Einstein's road to GR as exactly this — taking Maxwell seriously enough, for long
  enough, that spacetime had to give.
- On generalising a theory: *"Formal generalizations typically fail."* You must first understand
  why the thing you are generalising had the form it had; only then can you ask under what
  circumstances that same *why* would answer differently.

## Addendum — which "arrow", precisely (Aaron, 2026-08-13)

Aaron: *"i was talking about A Kleisli arrow … i've never heard of Hughes Arrow, are they the same?"*

**They are not the same; Kleisli is a special case.** A Kleisli arrow for a monad `m` is a morphism
`a → m b` — the Kleisli category has types as objects, `>=>` as composition, `return` as identity.
A **Hughes Arrow** (*Generalising Monads to Arrows*, 2000) is the weaker typeclass `arr`, `>>>`,
`first`. Every monad induces an Arrow (`Kleisli m` is the canonical instance), but not every Arrow
comes from a monad — stream/signal transformers are Arrows with no underlying monad. So
**Kleisli ⊂ Arrow**, strictly.

The repo's vocabulary is **Kleisli** (80 files, including `IsrLift.fs`, `IntrCtx.fs`,
`FerryThrottler.fs`, `Meno.fs`); "Hughes" appears 9 times and mostly incidentally. `IsrLift.fs`
already writes the bridge itself: *"the unit of the Kleisli structure — `arr` in arrow terms"*, and
`Meno.fs` defines `Meno<'a,'b>` as a computation expression, i.e. a Kleisli arrow.

**The distinction is load-bearing for the predictivity claim, which is why it is worth the pedantry.**
The precise boundary is a theorem: **`ArrowApply` ≅ `Monad`**. With monadic bind, `x >>= f` lets
`f`'s *structure* depend on the runtime value of `x`, so the shape of the remaining computation is
not knowable before running it. With a plain Arrow (no `app`), `f >>> g` is fixed at construction —
the pipeline is static and therefore analysable ahead of execution. Static analysability was
Hughes' original motivation.

So the light/dark axis acquires an exact type-theoretic boundary: **the `app`-free fragment is where
cost is statically computable (light); the moment `app`/bind is needed, structure depends on values
not yet in hand (dark).**

**This was not a suggestion — it is the recorded design.** Aaron, on reading the above:
*"yes this is the key insight i had when working on chip8 with you and we created the predictor."*

**And it holds in the code, checked rather than taken on report.** `src/Core/IsrLift.fs` exposes
exactly two constructors — `ofPolicy` and `ofPure : ('a -> 'b) -> ISR<'a,'b>`, the latter being
`arr` under another name (its own docstring: *"the unit of the Kleisli structure — `arr` in arrow
terms"*). Grepping the module for `app`, `bind`, `Bind`, `>>=` returns **nothing**. The ISR arrow
sits inside the `app`-free fragment by construction.

That matters because it converts the CHIP-8 prediction claim from empirical to structural: the
predictor can compute cost ahead of execution **because the pipeline's shape cannot depend on
values flowing through it**. Had `app` been available, the shape would be value-dependent and no
amount of engineering would recover static cost. The prediction substrate around it is real too —
`Chip8Cow.fs` (the copy-on-write frame DAG), `Chip8Observer.fs`, and the throttled batched
predictor over that DAG.

This is the anchor-taxonomy discipline working as intended: a **checked** anchor rather than a

## Addendum — echolocation, and not drowning out the quiet (Aaron, 2026-08-13)

> "we've built a lot on echolocation and making sure we can debound the self the I too so one does
> not drown out quite others"

**This names a real failure mode of the echo-state framing, and it has hard prior art.** A reservoir
whose determinacy depends on fading memory only stays useful while it remains *sensitive* — drive
one input hard enough and the reservoir saturates, dynamic range collapses, and weak signals become
unrecoverable. In echolocation the same problem has two classical forms, both with named solutions:

- **Self-masking.** Your own emission deafens you to a faint return. Bats solve it with the
  **stapedial reflex** — the middle-ear muscle contracts during the call, attenuating the animal's
  own signal so the echo is audible. That is precisely *"debound the self, the I too"*: turn your
  own amplitude down so you can still hear what is quiet.
- **Mutual masking.** Several emitters jam each other. The named phenomenon is the **jamming
  avoidance response** (Watanabe & Takeda 1963; Heiligenberg, on *Eigenmannia* electric fish; also
  observed as frequency shifting in bats) — each individual moves off the shared frequency so the
  others remain legible. That is *"so one does not drown out quiet others"*, as a mechanism rather
  than an aspiration.

**Why it belongs in this file rather than as a separate note.** It is the same light/dark axis at
signal level. A drowned-out agent is not merely unheard — its contribution becomes *unrecoverable*,
which is exactly the dark condition: information that now requires coordination to reconstruct, if
it can be reconstructed at all. Preserving dynamic range is what keeps a region light.

It also constrains the naming/privacy eigenvector directly. Recognition accruing from the
already-recognised is a PageRank-shaped process, and PageRank-shaped processes concentrate. Without
something playing the stapedial role, the loud get louder and the quiet fall below the floor —
which would make the eigenvector a drowning mechanism rather than a memory. In-tree the concern is
already live: echolocation appears in 13 files, including `sensor-fusion-the-identity-eigenvector-
and-multi-oracle-proof` and the photophone piece (Bell's light-carried audio — electromagnetic
again, which is the thread this whole ferry runs on).

**The metering question this raises, stated so it can be answered:** the light/dark axis was given a
dimensionless measure above (fraction of computation that cannot be made monotone). Dynamic range
wants its own: *the ratio between the loudest and the faintest contribution the fold can still
distinguish.* If that ratio is bounded and measured, "not drowning out the quiet" is an invariant
the substrate can hold rather than a virtue it hopes for.

### The nesting: classical is the real-axis slice, not a separate layer

Aaron's second clause sharpens something the addendum above got slightly wrong by implication. I
described the classical substrate as *underneath* the amplitudes. The truer relation is **containment**:

`SoftEmu` (real weights, no interference) is not a different system from `AmplitudeEmu` — it is
`AmplitudeEmu` **with the phases set to zero**. Take the complex-amplitude ensemble, drop every phasor
onto the positive real axis, and the merge stops cancelling and starts behaving as a plain probability
mixture. The classical emulator is the **real-axis slice** of the quantum-like one.

That is why *"you model on classical when using qubits"* is the right description rather than a
concession. The classical model is not scaffolding you discard, and not a floor you build on — it is
the **fixed sub-object you return to**, present inside the larger structure the whole time. Which is
exactly `e^{iπ}` again: the real axis is not somewhere else, it is where the rotation starts and where
it comes back.

It also tightens the argument against quantizing the base. If the classical layer were merely
underneath, you could imagine replacing it. If it is the slice you return to on measurement, replacing
it removes the destination — and an amplitude with nowhere to land is not an amplitude.
