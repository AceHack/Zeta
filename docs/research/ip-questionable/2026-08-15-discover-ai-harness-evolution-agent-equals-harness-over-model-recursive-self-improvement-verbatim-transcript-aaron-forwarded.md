## Framework-composition analysis (what this means for Zeta)

### 1. The harness decomposition is the capability-interface principle, one layer up

The content decomposes the control shell around a model into eight independently-varying components —
instruction content, skill activation, tool selection and routing, memory storage/retrieval, per-call
context assembly, control flow (planning / retry / delegation / stopping), verification and scoring, and
resource limits (tokens, compute, permissions, safety).

That is not a new idea here. `docs/research/2026-06-14-zeta-language-ir-compiler-v2-capability-interface-principle-fsharp-host-csharp-contracts-self-hosting-futamura.md`
§0 already states the governing principle: *everything is a declared interface capability*, resolved by
**multiple dispatch over a capability graph** (anchors: CLOS, Bobrow et al. 1988; Julia, Bezanson et al.
2017; and the negative anchor — Aaron's Itron collection engine, four injected interfaces over ~15 years and
~1M lines *"without good composability, so it was plumbing everywhere"*).

So the eight components are **capabilities at the agent layer** rather than the codegen layer. Aaron's
DU reading follows directly, and it is the stronger claim: a **closed** discriminated union makes a new axis
a compile error at every consumer, which is exactly the property that lets `ace-cli` and
`mux-transport-bridge` close over the same abstraction without special cases.

### 2. Three uses of one dispatch mechanism — and conflating them is a defect

PR #10774 supplied the middle row, which did not exist as a distinct category before:

| layer | what the dispatch decides | failure mode if wrong |
|---|---|---|
| codegen over source types | **soundness gate** | Merkle roots diverge — a real break |
| target-native emission (#10774) | **preference over semantically equal emissions** | still byte-locks; merely unidiomatic |
| agent-control layer | **optimization axes** | scores worse; nothing breaks |

One mechanism, three jobs. A "capability" whose wrong resolution cannot fail anything is the vacuity class
wearing a dispatch table.

### 3. Where the formalism is standard, and where it hides the difficulty

The objective — expected discounted return over a trajectory, then `argmax` over the control-shell
parameters — is the ordinary reinforcement-learning objective (Sutton & Barto). The symmetry the content
draws between optimizing the shell and optimizing the model weights is real and is just the observation that
both are `argmax` over a parameterization.

Two peels, in the Mirror→Beacon register:

- **`argmax` over an 8-tuple whose components are natural-language strings and executable code is not a
  computable search.** There is no gradient, and the space is not a well-defined set. Writing the `argmax`
  *names* the problem; it does not supply a method. "It is just a mathematical optimization problem" is
  precisely where the difficulty is hidden.
- **The recursive step is a definition, not a demonstration.** "The artifact produced in round *t* becomes
  the optimizer in round *t+1*" asserts recursion. Nothing in it argues the sequence converges, improves, or
  fails to drift — and an optimizer improving its own scorer is the classic way a metric stops measuring.

### 4. The one place we are already ahead — correlated runs

The content's expectation is taken over probabilistic runs to average out noise. That step assumes the runs
are independent observations.

They are not, for a fleet of near-identical harnesses, and this repo already carries the correction in two
places:

- `.claude/rules/numerology-vs-number-theory.md`: *"N correlated observations are not N observations … if
  everything corroborates, you may have one observation counted N times."* Stated there as a warning about
  confirmation; it applies verbatim to averaging over correlated trajectories.
- `src/Core/SocietyUsefulWork.fs` already ships the ΔU aggregation theorem with the **analytic expected value
  under pairwise correlation ρ**, plus a Gaussian copula for heterogeneous agents — so N clones price near
  one agent's worth rather than N agents' worth.

So our scoring substrate is strictly ahead of a bare expectation on exactly the axis that matters when the
thing being scored is a population of similar agents. **Any harness-optimization scorer built here must not
regress to an independence-assuming average.**

Adjacent, already built: `src/Core/TravelerRankLedger.fs` (TrueSkill-style per-(traveler × hat-domain)
rankings, held by others, with the whitewash window closed by construction) and `db/uncertainty/` (the ΔU
ledger a measurement commits to). The propose → apply → evaluate → accept/revert loop the content describes
is our existing `sim` (ephemeral, `db/sims/`) vs `measure` (commits ΔU) split.

### 5. What we already have, so it is not re-derived

We have the start of our own harness — `src/Core.TypeScript/cluster/harness/bootstrap.ts`,
`docs/HARNESS-SURFACES.md`, `.claude/hooks/harness.ts` with `src/Core.TypeScript/claude-hooks/harness.test.ts`,
`tests/Tests.FSharp/Plugin/Harness.Tests.fs`, and three `docs/research/harness-run-2026-04-20-*.md` runs.
The open question is which of the eight axes are already explicit there, which are implicit, and which are
genuinely absent — not what a harness should look like from scratch. Where our existing decomposition differs
from the eight, **ours wins**; the list is a requirement set, not a target shape.

Also relevant and already shipped: `src/Core/ChaosEnv.fs` (FoundationDB-style seeded `Buggify` fault
injection) is the replayable-perturbation channel any harness experiment should draw from, rather than
ambient randomness — see `.claude/rules/local-time-never-enters-the-shared-fold.md` and §13 noninterference.

### 6. Register

Everything above is **toy** in the sense of `.claude/rules/toy-is-free-metered-must-be-earned.md`: a
harness-optimization framework with no measured improvement is a toy, and saying so is the point. The
falsifier for any design that comes out of this is a measured score improvement on a fixed task set that
survives the correlation correction in §4 — not the existence of the objective function.
