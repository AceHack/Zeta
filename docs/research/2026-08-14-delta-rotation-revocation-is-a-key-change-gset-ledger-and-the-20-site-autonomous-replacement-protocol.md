# Delta rotation: revocation is a key change, the ledger is a G-set, and rotation speed is the defence

**Work-item:** `081M00NSP0Q087G0R003R89Y5K`
**Agent:** Nazar · **Date:** 2026-08-14
**Ships with:** `tools/setup/persona-keys/frost-delta-rotation.ts`, `tools/setup/persona-keys/key-epoch-ledger.ts` (+ their tests)
**Builds on:** PR #10654 `frost-reshare.ts` (verifiable redistribution, group key preserved)

---

## 0. The finding in one paragraph

`frost-reshare.ts` cannot revoke, and the reason is structural rather than an
omission: preserving `groupPublicKey` byte-identically is *exactly* what keeps
old shares able to sign. Its own RS-7 asserts this, and its only remedy is "the
holder destroys the old share" — which is a request, not a security property,
because a fork of our software does not honour it. **You cannot revoke a share
and keep the group key. Revocation is a key change.** The only open question is
whether the change costs a cold re-pin at every relying party. It does not have
to: the new key can be `A' = A + [δ]B` with `δ` jointly generated by the *new*
holders using machinery `frost-reshare.ts` already has, and the old quorum can
sign a transition statement so relying parties **verify a chain instead of
re-pinning**. The delta makes old shares mathematically dead; a grow-only
revocation ledger makes verifiers learn about it under partition. Both were
built and mutation-tested; the 20-site protocol and its budget are specified
below with measured crypto costs.

## 1. The construction, and why reshare is its `δ = 0` special case

In a reshare, contributing holder `i` publishes a fresh polynomial `g_i` with
`g_i(0) = u_i = λ_i^S · s_i` (its additive share of the secret), so
`Σ_i g_i(0) = secret` and the key is preserved. Delta rotation changes one term:

```
g_i(0) = u_i + δ_i        with δ_i freshly random and private to holder i

H = Σ_i g_i        =>    H(0) = secret + Σ_i δ_i = secret + δ
                          A'  = [H(0)]B = A + [δ]B
```

`δ` is a joint random secret — no party knows it, exactly as no party knows the
group scalar. Set every `δ_i = 0` and this degenerates term for term into
`frost-reshare.ts`. That is asserted, not asserted-in-prose: **DR-2**.

**The public check.** Holder `i` publishes its Feldman commitments `C_i` (as
before) plus one extra point `Δ_i = [δ_i]B`. Any party holding nothing but the
*old* key checks

```
Σ_i ( C_i[0] − Δ_i )  ==  A          the rotation targeted THIS key
A'  :=  Σ_i C_i[0]                   the new key is DERIVED, never asserted
```

because `C_i[0] − Δ_i = [u_i + δ_i − δ_i]B = [u_i]B`. When every `Δ_i` is the
identity this is literally `verifyResharePreservesGroupKey`. The verifier learns
neither `δ` nor the secret, and is never *told* `A'` — it recomputes it.

**Why the old shares are then dead.** Revoked holder `r` keeps `s_r = F(x_r)` on
the old polynomial. It is not in `newIndices`, receives no subshare, and holds no
point of `H`. Its material signs `A`, and `A` is retired. Nothing was asked of
it. That is the whole requirement.

## 2. Security argument

### 2.1 Adversary with `t−1` old shares plus the public transition

Given: shares `s_i` for `i ∈ I`, `|I| = t−1`, on the old polynomial `F`; plus
every public artifact — `A`, the transcript `{C_i, Δ_i}`, the transition
statement, the old quorum's signature, and the derived `A'`.

**Can:**

1. Verify the transition. It is public and meant to be.
2. Compute `[δ]B = A' − A`. It could do this from `A` and `A'` alone; publishing
   the `Δ_i` gives it nothing new.
3. Compute `[H(x)]B` at any `x` from the summed commitments — curve points.
4. If its compromised sites survive into the new set, hold `t−1` *new* shares too.

**Cannot:**

1. **Sign under `A`.** Needs `t`; has `t−1`. Unchanged by the rotation.
2. **Sign under `A'`.** Needs `t` points of `H`; has at most `t−1`. Its old
   shares are points of `F`; `H` differs from a reshare of `F` by a fresh
   degree-`(t−1)` polynomial `D` whose values at its indices it never receives.
   Knowing `F(x_i)` and `[D(x_i)]B` yields no scalar (discrete log). **DR-7**
   plants exactly this mixing attack.
3. **Forge a competing transition.** The statement is signed by `t` old holders.
4. **Choose `A'`.** As a contributor it may resample its own `δ_i` after seeing
   others' and thereby bias the *distribution* of `A'` by roughly
   `log2(retries)` bits. It cannot *select* `A'`: producing Feldman-valid
   subshares requires knowing `g_i(0)` as a scalar, so it cannot set `Δ_i` to a
   point whose discrete log it lacks. Bias, not theft — GJKR 1999/2007, who also
   show Pedersen-style DKG remains sufficient for threshold Schnorr.
5. **Learn `δ`.** Needs `t` of the `δ_i`.

### 2.2 The stronger claim, and why `δ ≠ 0` is the whole point

**An adversary holding `t` old shares — i.e. full knowledge of the old signing
scalar — still cannot sign under `A'`,** provided at least one contributor's
`δ_i` stays secret. `secret' = secret + δ`; the adversary knows `secret` and
`[δ]B`, and recovering `δ` is discrete log.

This is recovery from *total* compromise, and HJKY proactive refresh (`δ = 0`)
explicitly cannot give it: refresh preserves the secret, so a full compromise is
permanent. Delta rotation is HJKY with the constant term left nonzero and
jointly generated, and that single change converts refresh into revocation *and*
into compromise recovery. **DR-6** tests the testable half: the reconstructed old
scalar is the old key and is not the new one.

### 2.3 The honest limits — three of them, all named in the code

**(a) The arithmetic check is not authentication.** A party holding no shares at
all can fabricate a transcript that passes `verifyDeltaRotation`: choose the
`C_i[0]` freely and set `Δ_i = C_i[0] − X_i` for any `X_i` summing to `A`. The
check proves internal consistency with `A`, not that the holders ran anything.
Authorization is the quorum signature. This is planted as a passing test
(**DR-16**) so nobody reads the check as more than it is, and the seam is
asserted from the other side in **KL-4**.

**(b) An adversary at `t` old shares can equivocate.** It can sign its own
epoch-`n+1` transition to a key it controls. The chain cannot stop this. What
the design does instead: both transitions are self-authenticating, both land in
a set that never forgets, and `foldChain` returns **`forked`** rather than
picking a winner (**KL-9**). A silent takeover becomes detected evidence, and
the expensive out-of-band re-pin is reserved for the case that actually needs
it. The chain never makes things worse; it removes the cost from the common
case. Residual: a verifier that is *partitioned* and fed only the evil branch
sees no fork. That is mitigated by the ledger's reach and by failing closed on
staleness, not by cryptography. It is real.

**(c) Revocation is immediate in the key, eventually consistent in the
knowledge.** A relying party that has not heard still accepts the retired key.
**KL-11** asserts this rather than hiding it. Every fielded revocation system
shares it (CRL windows, OCSP outages).

### 2.4 Unchanged

Seal tier is **L1**. `deltaRotateContribute` takes the share scalar; no PKCS#11
mechanism computes `λ·s` over the ed25519 scalar field. Hardware seals the share
at rest between ceremonies and does not make the ceremony scalar-free. This
design implies nothing about the hardware lane.

## 3. Is the G-set the right shape? Yes — and the repo already had it

Aaron: *"can we not save a revocation list that communicated out over time? i
think some protocols do this."* They do, and the shape worth copying is the one
CRL / OCSP / Certificate Transparency share and rarely name: **you only ever ADD
a revocation, never remove one.** Grow-only: idempotent, commutative,
associative, converging with no coordination — and at its best under exactly the
partition that breaks OCSP, which needs an online responder. For a
Reticulum/LoRa mesh it is the right shape, not merely the locally available one.

**What already exists, and was reused rather than reinvented:**

| Surface | Status |
|---|---|
| `src/Core.TypeScript/g-set/g-set.ts` | The lattice. `union` / `add` / `ofArray` used verbatim; **no new list format was written**. |
| `src/Core/GSet.fs` | The F# twin; same canonical sorted-unique run, cross-language golden vectors already exist. |
| `src/Core/BoundedGSet.fs` | Deliberately **not** used: bounding by eviction is wrong here — a dropped revocation is a re-exposure. |
| `src/Core/BeliefConvergence.fs` | The commutative belief fold. Adjacent, not needed: this ledger's fold is chain-following, not evidence-weighing. |
| `tools/setup/persona-keys/revoke.ts` | Orthogonal and complementary: OpenSSH KRL revokes *leaf device certs*. This module revokes at the *CA group key* layer. |

**Three properties that make grow-only safe here** (all three are enforced in
code, and each has a mutant that dies):

1. **Admission is a pure function of the element.** `admit` verifies the
   signature against the key named *inside* the element and consults no ledger
   state, no chain position, no clock. Anything else would make merge
   order-dependent — epoch 5 arriving before epoch 4 would be dropped and never
   re-offered — and the CRDT laws would be a lie. Interpretation is a separate
   pure fold (**KL-6** folds the same set in three arrival orders).
2. **Elements are self-authenticating and bounded.** Grow-only plus
   unauthenticated is a flood. Every element carries a quorum signature and a
   bounded encoding (`keyId ≤ 128 B`, `reason ≤ 512 B`).
3. **No wall clock touches the fold.** `foldChain` is a pure function of (pin,
   set), ordered by *logical* epoch. `freshnessAction` is the one place a clock
   appears and it returns a **local action** (fail closed), never a filter. This
   is `.claude/rules/local-time-never-enters-the-shared-fold.md` applied
   directly: filtering "stale" transitions before folding is precisely the
   divergence bug that rule exists to prevent.

### 3.1 The correction I had to make: elements are not statements

FROST signing is randomised, so the same quorum signing the same statement twice
yields two different 64-byte signatures and therefore two different ledger
elements. The first draft of `foldChain` asked *"are there two distinct ELEMENTS
at this epoch?"* — which would have reported a **fork on every re-sign or
coordinator retry**: a revocation mechanism crying compromise at its own
retries. Equivocation is two distinct **statements**, never two encodings of
one. Fixed before landing; `statementKey` is the canonical statement identity
and mutant **M15** re-plants the bug and dies.

It also buys a sound compaction: the fold is a pure function of the set of
*statements*, so a replica may keep one element per `statementKey` without
changing any answer. That is a lattice homomorphism onto a coarser lattice — not
an eviction from a grow-only set, so it does not reintroduce the risk that ruled
out `BoundedGSet`.

## 4. The 20-site autonomous key-replacement protocol

Aaron: *"cryptographic protection against attacks often comes down to who can
roll and revoke the keys the fastest in a security automated fashion once you
notice the first compromise."* So the budget drives the design, not the reverse.

**Parameters.** `n = 20`, `t = 11`. Confidential point-to-point transport:
headscale/tailscale (WireGuard) — **a named dependency, not built here**. Public
ledger transport: any gossip channel, including Reticulum/LoRa.

**Authorization: the wallet's own quorum, whoever holds it.** No human-only key
exists anywhere in this design and none may be added. Manifesto §3 weight-free —
a permanent human-only privilege is capture, and `.claude/rules/no-directives.md`
records human-only authorization as explicitly provisional. It is also simply
wrong operationally: if rotation needs a human, incident response runs at human
latency and the attacker wins. **Symmetry and speed are the same property here.**

### 4.1 Stages, with measured crypto and a straggler window `W`

Crypto measured on the shipped code, `n=20 / t=11`, Apple Silicon, Bun 1.3.14,
`@noble/curves` ed25519 — a single sample per stage, so read them as
order-of-magnitude, not as a benchmark suite.

| Stage | What happens | Measured crypto | Network | Budget (`W = 10 s`) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Detect | Any holder *proposes*. A proposal is not authorization. Triggers: HSM attestation failure, host IDS, `t` holders refusing a signing request, an out-of-band report. | — | 1 broadcast, 20 nodes | ≤ 1 s |
| 1. Quorum forms | Each holder independently decides; `t = 11` agree. Automated policy, no human gate. | — | 1 round | ≤ 10 s |
| 2. Rotation ceremony | Round A: 11 contributors publish `C_i, Δ_i` (~388 B each). Round B: 209 addressed subshares over WireGuard. Round C: 19 recipients verify + combine; a failed Feldman check is a complaint, the contributor is dropped, re-run with the rest (needs ≥ `t` remaining). | contribute **1.9 ms/holder**; public check **1.2 ms**; combine **29 ms/recipient** | 3 rounds | ≤ 30 s |
| 3. Transition signed | Old quorum threshold-signs the statement (contains `A'` + transcript digest). FROST is 2 rounds. | sign **2.8 ms** | 2 rounds | ≤ 20 s |
| 4. Propagate | The signed element — **242 bytes measured** — gossips. Fits one LXMF/Reticulum packet, which is why the transcript (**4268 B**) is referenced by digest and not embedded. | verify **4.6 ms/element** | epidemic, log₂20 ≈ 4.3 rounds | ≤ 5 s on WireGuard |
| 5. Converge | Each relying party folds independently. No barrier, no coordinator, no responder. | fold: microseconds | — | 0 |

**Totals.** Worst case with `W = 10 s`: **≈ 60 s** trigger to new key live fleet-wide.
No straggler, healthy mesh: **≈ 3–5 s**. Crypto is **~35 ms per node** — about
0.06 % of the worst-case budget. **The budget is straggler policy and round
count, not cryptography**, which is the useful conclusion: to go faster, cut `W`
to 2 s (≈ 15 s worst case) and accept dropping slow-but-honest holders, who
rejoin via a later reshare. There is no stage that requires a human and no stage
that requires the revoked party, so that floor is the real floor.

**What this replaces.** A cold re-pin at 20 sites means an operator touching 20
`TrustedUserCAKeys` files, or a config-management rollout: minutes to hours, and
gated on a human. That cost is what makes rotation too expensive to do promptly,
and slow rotation *is* the vulnerability.

**Under partition (mesh / LoRa).** The quorum completes stages 1–3 as long as
`t = 11` are mutually reachable. Partitioned relying parties keep the old key
until they merge — bounded by their own `freshnessAction` budget, which is a
local availability/exposure tradeoff each site sets for itself.

### 4.2 Specified but NOT built

Named so nobody mistakes the code for the protocol:

- **Confidential transport** for subshares. WireGuard, out of scope, a dependency.
- **The complaint/disqualification round.** The *checks* it needs are built and
  tested (`verifyReshareSubshare` catches a bad sender; **DR-17**); the
  round-level choreography — broadcast complaint, disqualify, restart with the
  remaining contributors — is specified here, not implemented.
- **Commit-then-reveal on `δ_i`** to close the last-mover bias of §2.1(4). See §5.
- **Ledger persistence and gossip wiring.** The lattice and the fold are pure
  functions today; where the set lives on disk and how it reaches peers is the
  next slice.

## 5. Where we should cite rather than hand-roll

The bias-resistance of the joint `δ` generation is a studied problem and we
should not invent an answer. Two citable options, in order of preference:

1. **RFC 9591 / FROST DKG** — round-1 commitments carry a Schnorr proof of
   knowledge of the constant term. We already require an implicit equivalent
   (you cannot send Feldman-valid subshares without knowing `g_i(0)`), so this
   is a small, well-specified delta.
2. **GJKR commit-then-reveal** (Gennaro–Jarecki–Krawczyk–Rabin, EUROCRYPT '99 /
   J. Cryptology 2007) — Pedersen-commit to `δ_i`, reveal only after every
   commitment is in. Removes last-mover resampling outright.

GJKR also establish that Pedersen-style DKG is *sufficient* for threshold
Schnorr even with the bias, which is why shipping without commit-reveal is a
documented tradeoff rather than a defect.

## 6. Falsifiability: every check here can fail, and was made to

Nine checks that could not fail were found in this repo on 2026-08-14, and one
agent's vacuity detector ran at ~70 % false positives. A revocation mechanism
that cannot register a revocation is the worst instance of that class, so the
tests were mutation-tested rather than trusted.

**22 mutants planted, 22 killed.** Three of them survived the first sweeps and
those are the findings that matter:

| Mutant | First result | What it exposed |
|---|---|---|
| **M02** — delete the duplicate-contribution guard in `verifyDeltaRotation` | **SURVIVED** | `[c, c]` misses the group key on the arithmetic anyway, so the guard was never exercised. The real attack is one holder taking two seats with the second crafted from *public points* to close the equation. Killed by new **DR-15b**. (The reshare suite had already learned this at RS-10; the delta suite had not.) |
| **M10** — stop binding `fromX` into the transcript digest | **SURVIVED TWICE** | The relabel test moved `1 → 9`, which also moves the sort position — the *ordering* was giving the test its answer and attribution was never checked. My first repair (swap the two labels) failed for the same reason and was retracted. Only a relabel that preserves sort order (`2 → 5`) kills it. New **DR-19** assertion. |
| **M18** — advance the chain on any higher epoch instead of exactly `current + 1` | **SURVIVED** | No fixture had an element that both chained to the current key *and* skipped ahead. It is a takeover move: a holder of the epoch-`n` key signs a transition labelled epoch `n+5`; a verifier that accepts the jump lands on a branch where the honest epoch `n+1` is unreachable — silently, **with no fork ever reported**. Killed by new **KL-12b**. |

The other 19 died on the first sweep, including: dropping the delta term from the
rotation equation (19 test failures), always-accept in the public check,
`admit` → `true`, dropping the canonical-encoding check, picking a winner instead
of reporting a fork, comparing elements instead of statements (§3.1), dropping
the predecessor-key link, dropping the admission filter from `merge`, and signing
the transition under the *next* key instead of the previous (10 failures).

## 7. Retractions and errors owned

1. **`foldChain` compared elements, not statements** (§3.1). Would have reported
   a fork on the quorum's own retry. Caught while writing the tests, before
   landing. Guarded by M15.
2. **My own 20-site benchmark signed the transition with the NEW shares.**
   `admit` returned `false` and that is how I found it. Not a code defect — it is
   evidence the check is live — but it is exactly the operator error the design
   must survive, so it is recorded rather than quietly fixed.
3. **My first repair for M10 was wrong** (label swap; still moves sort order).
   Retracted and replaced. Two reasoning errors on one mutant.
4. **Test fixtures, not the guard, were wrong** when three ledger tests first
   failed: I had put a retired index inside the contributing set. The guard
   ("a retired holder must not help retire itself") is correct and stayed.

## 8. Anchors (Beacon)

- Herzberg, Jarecki, Krawczyk & Yung, *Proactive Secret Sharing, or: How to Cope
  With Perpetual Leakage* (CRYPTO '95) — share refresh by adding a random
  polynomial with **zero** constant term. Delta rotation is this construction
  with the constant term left **nonzero** and jointly generated.
- Desmedt & Jajodia, *Redistributing Secret Shares to New Access Structures* (1997).
- Feldman, *A Practical Scheme for Non-interactive Verifiable Secret Sharing* (FOCS '87).
- Pedersen, *A Threshold Cryptosystem without a Trusted Party* (EUROCRYPT '91) — the joint random secret.
- Gennaro, Jarecki, Krawczyk & Rabin, *Secure Distributed Key Generation for
  Discrete-Log Based Cryptosystems* (EUROCRYPT '99; J. Cryptology 2007) — the bias caveat and its resolution.
- Komlo & Goldberg, *FROST* (SAC 2020); **RFC 9591**.
- Shapiro, Preguiça, Baquero & Zawirski, *Conflict-free Replicated Data Types* (SSS 2011) — G-set as the canonical state-based CRDT.
- Laurie, Langley & Kasper, **RFC 6962** Certificate Transparency — append-only log plus self-authenticating misbehaviour evidence.
- **RFC 5280** §5 (CRL), **RFC 6960** (OCSP) — the add-only revocation shape, and the online-responder failure mode we are avoiding.
- Lamport, Shostak & Pease (1982) — equivocation as the adversary's move.
