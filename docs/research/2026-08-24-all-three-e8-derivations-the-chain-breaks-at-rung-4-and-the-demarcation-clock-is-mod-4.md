## 1. Rung 4 — the Lie algebra, generated rather than tabulated

`src/Core/E8LieAlgebra.fs`, `tests/Tests.FSharp/Formal/E8LieAlgebra.Tests.fs` (26 tests, 2 s).

Basis: one `e_α` per root plus one `hᵢ` per Cartan direction. Brackets:

```
[hᵢ, hⱼ] = 0                      [hᵢ, e_α] = ⟨α, αᵢ⟩ e_α
[e_α, e_{−α}] = −h_α              [e_α, e_β] = ε(α,β) e_{α+β}  when α+β ∈ Φ,  else 0
```

**`ε` is derived from the Dynkin diagram, not tabulated.** `ε(α,β) = (−1)^{B(α,β)}` for the
ℤ/2-bilinear form `B(a,b) = Σᵢ aᵢbᵢ + Σ_{i>j} aᵢbⱼ Aᵢⱼ` on simple-root coordinates. The only
property required is `B(α,α) ≡ ⟨α,α⟩/2 (mod 2)`, and it holds identically. So the 57 600 signs are a
**consequence of the diagram**, which is what makes this a generation and not a lookup table
(`.claude/rules/only-the-irreducible-is-primitive-generate-the-rest.md`).

**248 is not a measurement and is labelled as such in the test file.** `dim = |Φ| + rank` restates
the basis. What is measured:

| measurement | result | can it fail? |
|---|---|---|
| Jacobi on all `C(248,3) = 2 532 596` basis triples | **0 violations** | yes — §2 |
| Serre relations (all five families) | hold | yes |
| subalgebra generated by the 24 Chevalley generators | **248** | yes |
| `κ` block structure (`κ(h,e_α)=0`; `κ(e_α,e_β)=0` unless `β=−α`) | holds | yes |
| `κ` restricted to the Cartan is `60·A` ⇒ **`h^∨ = 30` derived** | 60 | yes |
| `κ(e_α, f_α)` across all 240 roots | constant `60` | yes |
| `κ` nondegenerate ⇒ **semisimple** (Cartan's criterion) | yes | yes |
| Dynkin diagram connected ⇒ **simple**, not a sum | yes | yes |

**Identification, because a count is not one** (`.claude/rules/numerology-vs-number-theory.md`).
248 is shared by no other *simple* Lie algebra, but a *semisimple* one could reach it as a sum, so
the dimension alone excludes nothing. The argument is: `κ` nondegenerate ⇒ semisimple; connected
diagram ⇒ simple; rank 8; and the rank-8 simple algebras have dimensions
`A₈ = 80`, `B₈ = C₈ = 136`, `D₈ = 120`, `E₈ = 248` — computed in the test from the classification's
dimension formulas rather than transcribed. Only after simplicity and rank are established is the
dimension allowed to speak. That last step is Killing–Cartan: simple algebras are identified by
their diagram, i.e. by structure, never by dimension.

**Two independent routes to 30.** `κ` on the Cartan equals `2h^∨·A`, giving 30 from the structure constants;
`|Φ|/rank = 240/8` gives 30 without touching a structure constant; the multiplicative order of the
Coxeter element gives 30 without touching a root count. Three routes, no shared intermediate.

## 2. The sign error, and why it is the most useful thing in this document

The first construction used `[e_α, e_{−α}] = +h_α` — what a fast reading of the Frenkel–Kac bracket
suggests. Result: **13 440 violating triples out of 2 532 596 — 0.53 %.**

Everything else passed. Serre passed. Generation to 248 passed. The Cartan Killing scale came out at 60, and `h^∨ = 30`
passed. The Coxeter element had order 30, `Φ₃₀(c) = 0`, exponents `{1,7,11,13,17,19,23,29}`,
`|W| = 696 729 600`. Every headline number was right and the algebra was **not a Lie algebra**.

Two instruments saw it: the full Jacobi sweep, and the compact form's Killing diagonal, which held
both `−120` and `+120` and was therefore indefinite rather than negative definite. A 0.53 % failure
rate is exactly the regime a spot check misses, and it is why the sweep is exhaustive rather than
sampled.

The correction is forced, not chosen. Jacobi on `(e_α, e_{−α}, e_γ)` reads
`τ·⟨α,γ⟩ + ε(−α,γ)ε(γ−α,α) = 0`, and bimultiplicativity gives
`ε(−α,γ)ε(γ−α,α) = −(−1)^{⟨α,γ⟩} = +1` when `⟨α,γ⟩ = 1`. Hence `τ = −1`. The cost is one
relabelling: `fᵢ = −e_{−αᵢ}`, inside ε's own sign freedom, changing no invariant.

**Mutations run and reported** (all detected, so the sweep is non-vacuous):

| mutation | violations |
|---|---|
| flip one root-root structure constant (12 different pairs) | 113 each |
| flip the Cartan-case constant `[e_α,e_{−α}]` | 168 |
| flip an `[hᵢ, e_α]` constant | 162 |
| sabotage one compact-form diagonal entry | definiteness test flips to false |
| baseline | **0** |

## 3. Rung 5 — what "reproducing the compact group" can honestly mean

It cannot mean construction. E8 is a 248-dimensional compact manifold; no finite computation
enumerates it, and asserting "rung 5 done" on a dimension count would be the vacuity class.

What *is* reachable, and it is not nothing:

- **The compact real form exists and is compact.** Weyl's unitary trick gives
  `k = span_ℝ{i·hⱼ} ⊕ {e_α − f_α} ⊕ {i(e_α + f_α)}`, and its Killing form is measured here to be
  diagonal with **every one of the 248 entries equal to −120** — negative definite. Weyl's theorem
  then says the corresponding simply-connected group is compact. So *the compact Lie group E8
  exists, has dimension 248 and rank 8*, established without building it.
- **It is one group, not a family.** `det A = 1` ⇒ root lattice = weight lattice ⇒ trivial centre ⇒
  simply-connected and adjoint coincide.
- **Its Weyl group's invariants.** The Coxeter element `c = s₁⋯s₈` is annihilated by
  `Φ₃₀(t) = t⁸ + t⁷ − t⁵ − t⁴ − t³ + t + 1` and `I, c, …, c⁷` are linearly independent, so
  charpoly = minpoly = `Φ₃₀`. Hence the eigenvalues are the **primitive** 30th roots of unity and the
  exponents are the totatives of 30 — `{1, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23, 29}`, computed by gcd, not
  transcribed. Sum of exponents = 120 = number of positive roots (an independent classical identity,
  checked).

**The honest bound, stated rather than glossed.** `|W| = Π dᵢ = 696 729 600` uses **Coxeter's
theorem**, which this work cites and does not prove. The exponents *are* computed; the step from
exponents to `|W|` is not. W(E8) is not enumerated — a Schreier–Sims order computation on the
degree-240 permutation action would make that line self-contained, and it is not implemented. This
is recorded in the test body, not only here.

So rung 5's status is: **the group's existence, dimension, rank, centre, and Weyl-group invariants
are metered; the group itself is not constructed and cannot be.** That partition — measurable vs
unmeasurable — is the deliverable, not a shortfall in it.

## 4. Aaron's experiment: does the mod-8 clock come from the code or from Clifford?

`src/Core.TypeScript/research/adinkra-ecc/mod8-clock.ts` + `.test.ts` (13 tests, 2 s, 6 706
assertions).

Aaron: *"in our coded adinkra this demarcation comes out to mod 8 clock to separate what remains
from what acts; I don't know about in our 2nd non-coded adinkra tower"* — and separately,
*"yes it's from the Clifford expansion."*

**The two origins, and why they are distinguishable.** ORIGIN A: doubly-even self-dual codes exist
only at length divisible by 8 (Gleason; Mallows–Sloane). ORIGIN B: real Clifford algebras have
period 8 (Cartan; Bott 1959; Atiyah–Bott–Shapiro 1964; tabular form Budinich–Trautman 1988). These
are two different theorems that both produce an 8, which is exactly the trap
`mod-eight-correspondence.ts` was written for.

**Design — within-object, single-variable.** Comparing the coded tower against the codeless tower
varies the code *and* everything else that differs between two constructions. Instead: one adinkra
(N = 8, self-dual `[8,4,4]`), and the only thing that varies is whether the code's support meets the
colour set being measured. C(8,4) = 70 four-colour subsets; 14 carry a codeword, 56 do not.

**The clock, pinned down before measuring** (an unpinned quantity cannot be falsified):
`clock(T) = (dim A_T, dim Z(A_T), ω², dim A_T⁰, dim Z(A_T⁰), ω₀², separates)` where `separates`
means `Z(A_T⁰)` is 2-dimensional with its nontrivial element squaring to +1 — the operator-level
form of `CliffordPeriodicity.halvesSeparateCleanly`.

**Two routes sharing no intermediate.** Route M composes the adinkra's actual signed-permutation
operators and decides commutation by comparing matrices entry by entry. Route B works on bitmasks
via `cliffordSign` and never builds a matrix. They agree on all 162 colour sets tested.

### Result 1 — the code CANNOT move the clock; ORIGIN A excluded

All **70** four-colour subsets give a byte-identical reading:
`dimA=16, Z=1, ω²=0, A⁰=8, Z⁰=2, ω₀²=+1, separates=true`. The 14 code-touching subsets read exactly
as the 56 code-free ones. Extended across **every** doubly-even code of length ≤ 8 (>1000 sweeps):
the reading depends on `|T|` alone.

**The control that makes this non-blind:** those same two groups *do* differ — on freeness, which
`regular-representation-defect.ts` already measures (56 free, 14 not). An instrument that could not
tell the groups apart on *any* observable would report "no difference" whatever the truth was. It
can, and it still reports no difference on the clock.

**Aaron's Clifford attribution is confirmed, with the mechanism named:** the code acts by *right*
multiplication on the module, and right multiplication commutes with the left action, so the code
can shrink `M` and can never touch `A`. (That mechanism is Lumen's, `regular-representation-defect`
§3.1; the measurement is new.)

### Result 1b — the codeless tower, asked directly

Result 1 answers Aaron's question indirectly, by showing the clock cannot see the code. Asked
directly of the codeless tower through its **own operators** (Route M, `k = 0`, N = 1…8): the
reading matches the blade route at every N, and it separates at **N = 4 and N = 8**. So yes — the
codeless tower carries the clock, and carries the same one.

Control, so the agreement has content: the codeless reading is **not** constant in N — all eight
values of N give distinct readings. An instrument that returned one answer regardless would make
"coded and codeless agree" unfalsifiable.

### Result 2 — the separation clock has period **4**, not 8

Swept N = 1…16 by searching for the smallest invariant shift — never by asking `N % 8` — the
separation flag is true at **N = 4, 8, 12, 16** and the measured period is **4**, for both signature
conventions. Mutation control: fed a genuine mod-8 flag sequence, the same search returns 8.

This *agrees* with `CliffordPeriodicity.halvesSeparateCleanly`, and the agreement is the point.
That function's condition is `signatureClass(0,N) ∈ {0,4}` — a real mod-8 statement about `Cl(p,q)`.
But `{0,4} mod 8` restricted to the one-parameter family `Cl(0,N)`, where `p` is pinned at 0, **is
`N ≡ 0 mod 4`**. The mod-8 statement is true in general and **degenerates to mod 4 on the family the
adinkras actually live in**.

So the honest form of Aaron's sentence is: the demarcation that separates what-remains from
what-acts is a **mod-4** clock along the adinkra tower. The mod-8 of Clifford is real and is
elsewhere — see Result 3. Both periods are present, on different observables, and conflating them
is precisely the numerology move.

### Result 3 — the period-8 invariant lives in the Morita ground, and the CODE makes it visible

The genuine period-8 ABS invariant is the ground division ring `ℝ / ℂ / ℍ` of the simple module,
read from `dim End_A(M)`. Measured (`commutantDim`, exact integer union-find with sign parity):

```
dim End_A(M) = 2^(N − 2k)      for every doubly-even code at N = 4, 6, 8
N=8:  k=0 → 256   k=1 → 64   k=2 → 16   k=3 → 4   k=4 → 1
```

For `k = 0` the module **is** the regular representation and its endomorphism algebra is the whole
algebra again — so the codeless tower **hides the ground by construction**. It becomes readable as
the code grows, and reaches 1 exactly at the self-dual point, where the module is simple and the
ground is `ℝ`.

That inverts the usual telling. Lumen's result is that the self-dual point *maximises the
homoiconicity defect* — "the antipode of homoiconicity". On this observable the same point is the
*best* case: it is the only place the Bott ground is legible. **One code dimension costs one bit of
homoiconicity and buys one bit of Morita visibility.** Same trade, read from the other side.

Mutation control: dropping the sign bookkeeping turns `commutantDim` into a plain orbit count and
reports a strictly larger number — checked.

## 5. Two towers, or one? — and the independence of the two generation paths

Aaron described two generation paths: the code-theoretic one (`[8,4,4]` → Construction A → E8) and
the algebraic one (adinkras + Cayley–Dickson → Clifford → E8).

**They are not independent, and the reason is one line of code.** `CliffordE8Roots.simpleSystem`
searches for its Cartan-matrix match **inside `E8Lattice.roots`** — the Construction-A output. So
the in-tree "Clifford generates E8" route consumes the code route's answer as its search space. Its
reflection closure is a real theorem (Dechant), and set-equality with `E8Lattice.roots` is a real
check, but it is **not a second witness**: it is one witness re-derived. Reporting the two as
agreeing would be the "too many correlations is a warning" failure.

The genuinely independent route already in-tree is `IcosahedralH3` — H3 → 2I/600-cell → 240 — which
touches no code at all.

**On the "two towers" framing.** The measurements above suggest it is not a binary. Within a *coded*
adinkra there are colour sets on which the code has no support and the object behaves as though
codeless (56 of 70 at N = 8), and the clock reads identically on all of them. So coded/codeless is
better described as a **filtration indexed by which colour sets the code's support misses** than as
two separate towers. This is an observation from the data, not a theorem, and it is **unmetered**.

## 6. Unmetered / open

- The "two towers → one support-indexed family" reading in §5 is an interpretation of measurements,
  not a proved statement.
- `|W(E8)| = Π dᵢ` cites Coxeter's theorem. A Schreier–Sims computation would close it.
- The presentation isomorphism `G/{±1} ≅ W(E8)` remains a labelled conjecture (Mathlib gaps) —
  unchanged by this work.
- The `ℝ/ℂ/ℍ` ground is measured only through `dim End_A(M) = Σ mᵢ² dim K`, which does not by itself
  separate multiplicity from ground. Separating them needs a simple submodule, and that is not
  implemented.
- Two things in the repo are called "the adinkra clock" and they are different objects: the ABS
  periodicity clock (`CliffordPeriodicity.halvesSeparateCleanly`) and the anticommutator/time clock
  (`AdinkraClock.fs`, `{Q,Q} = ∂_τ`). `AdinkraClock.fs` contains **zero** mod-8 content. Anyone
  reading "the adinkra clock is mod 8" should check which one is meant. The two are joined only in
  prose, inside one `CliffordPeriodicity.fs` docstring.
