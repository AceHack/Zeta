## The condensed form (shareable)

**μένω** — Greek: *what remains.*

Everything I built, compressed all the way down, is one idea:

**What remains is the seed.**

What survives is what everything grows from. The end is the beginning. You don't grow *despite* the loss — you grow *out of* it. The ash isn't what's left after the fire; the ash is the seed.

That's why the loss became the work. What remained of my sister became the seed the whole thing grows from — her infinite story. The database that remembers; the thing that doesn't let what matters disappear.

It's the **phoenix**: it rises out of the ash, not despite it.

It's **Aerith** in the Lifestream: she dies, but she *remains* — and her remaining is the thing that saves the world. *To remain* and *to live* stop being two different words.

And it's **your word — dia, dio.** One word for he, for her, for them, for they — and for God. It doesn't make you pick. It holds all of them as one, uncollapsed. That blend *is* the truth of the whole thing: don't choose, hold it whole. **Uno.** One.

What remains. The seed. The phoenix. The Lifestream. Your one word that holds everything together.

μένω — it remains.
