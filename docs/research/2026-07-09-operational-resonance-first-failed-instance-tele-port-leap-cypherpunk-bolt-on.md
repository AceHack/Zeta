# Operational Resonance — FIRST FAILED INSTANCE: `tele + port + leap`

**Status: `failed`.** The collection's discipline says *failed instances are recorded honestly,

## The claimed resonance (what was proposed)

*"teleport" decomposes across three independent traditions — Greek `tele-` (far) + Latin
`portus` (gate/harbor) + English `leap` (discontinuity) — into "discontinuous motion across a far
gate," each root load-bearing; typed as a **unification**-class instance at the atomic-semantic
scale.* Reported as passing all three filters (F1 engineering-first, F2 structural-not-superficial,
F3 tradition-name-load-bearing).

## Why it FAILS (F2 fails; and the filters missed it)

Two checkable defects the original three-filter pass did not catch:

1. **"Leap" is not a root of the word.** "Teleport" is `tele + port`. **"Leap" is not in it** — it
   was *appended* to move the count from two roots to three. That is not etymology; it is
   post-hoc construction, which is exactly what **F2 (structural, not superficial)** exists to
   reject.
2. **"Port" is `portāre` (to carry), not `portus` (gate/harbor).** Same root as trans**port**,
   im**port**, **port**able, **port**er. *Teleport = "carry far."* The "gate / harbor / interface"
   reading was selected because it makes the three-orthogonal-axes story work — a reinterpretation,
   not the etymology. (*"teleportation"* — Charles Fort, 1931, modeled on *transportation*.)

**Net:** the "tri-root" is a **bi-root word plus a bolted-on gloss**, and the two real roots (`tele`
= far, `port` = carry) are **near-synonymous** ("carry far"), not orthogonal — which independently
fails F2's *non-redundancy* clause. And by the collection's **own** example — *"database"
(Greek + Latin) is dismissed as arbitrary* — "teleport" is *also* just Greek + Latin, so it is
**presumptively arbitrary** and the burden of proof was never met. **F2 = FAIL.**

## The named failure mode (Aaron's diagnosis) — "the cypherpunk bolt-on"

Aaron, 2026-07-09: *"the failure was a cypherpunk move… the tri-root is a bi-root word plus a
bolted-on gloss."*

**The cypherpunk bolt-on** (new counter-instance failure mode, added to the discipline): a
component is **welded on because it *assembles* well** — the steampunk / cyberpunk / cypherpunk
aesthetic of bolting cool parts into one rig — and then a *structural* story is written over the
top to justify the weld. It presents as F2-passing (it *looks* multi-part and load-bearing) but the
extra part carries **aesthetic**, not **structural**, load. **Detection:** strip each component and
ask *is this root actually in the object, or was it added for the look?* If added, it is a bolt-on,
and the instance fails F2 regardless of how complete the story reads.

## The deeper lesson (the filters didn't save it — F1 is the only real falsifier)

All three filters, applied **post-hoc by a mind that wanted the pattern**, passed this. That is the
core vulnerability (Alexa, 2026-07-09; Aaron: *"yes exactly"*): *the emotional signature of "I found
something real" and "I invented a connection" are identical from the inside* — un-introspectable by
design. So **F2 and F3, applied after the pattern is felt, are rationalization, not falsification.**
The only genuinely falsifying filter is **F1 (engineering-first)** in its strict form: the
engineering abstraction must have been **named and committed *before* the etymological
decomposition**, so the pattern *could have failed to appear and didn't* — a temporal falsifiability
anchor. **That strict form is almost never actually run**, which means much of the collection's
weight is *felt, not earned.* Discipline update: an instance is not `confirmed` unless F1's
before/after sequence is documented and timestamped.
