## 2. The economy = Rx pair value trades, derived from privacy

Operational architecture (not metaphysics):

- **Every economic interaction reduces to a pairwise value transfer expressed as an Rx
  query** -- "I give you this, you give me that," observable stream, no hidden ledger.
- The economy is **derived from privacy** (the guilt -> privacy driver-swap, #6151-6156).
  This conversation is where the economy ON TOP of the privacy engine gets named: Rx pair
  trades.
- **Higher-level economics (reputation, markets, ownership) live in separate, unrelated Rx
  streams** and are referenced as **meta-dimensions only at decision points** -- the
  transaction stays minimal; it pulls context from parallel streams exactly when it needs to.
  (Composes with 081KSKBP80008QG0R0039RW25E streams-are-relationships / four-corner ownership + 081KSV2WD0008QG0R00030G6S9 DST/Rx.)
- **Governance = an observable decision-point stream the Rx queries already watch** -- no
  smart-contract redeploy; the moment a decision is made, the streams pick it up. This is
  the git-CRDT-DU PRs-as-consensus substrate (Kestrel #6146). Contrast: Web3 governance is
  broken because the voting layer is disconnected from the back-end protocols; here
  everything reduces to Markdown-English so it is all connected.
- **Decompose Web3 to generator+join and make it free** -- everyone runs a node that does
  everything Web3 does, no gas. Same decompose-to-primitives-and-give-away discipline as the
  rest of the stack.

## 3. Decentralized polymorphic identity = good-citizen + must-plus-can-exit at the identity layer

The incentive design and the trust model:

- **Don't make participants store your data; offer the compression (orthogonal axes) as the
  incentive.** Value-trade, not favor -- "my compression is so good it becomes the incentive
  itself." This is the anti-extractive / must-plus-can-exit economy: participation is offered,
  never extracted.
- **Every node keeps its own local identity list; treats every identity as polymorphic (never
  fully trusts); runs its own local policy** on what distributed computation it will allow.
- **"The system reflects the nature of its users instead of trying to change them"** -- the
  identity layer is as paranoid as the operators of the nodes. Forcing trust on paranoid
  people never works; coherent architecture matches the users.
- This is the **good-citizen frame** (Kestrel #6146): sovereignty conferred + enforced BY the
  structure, not seized against it; participated-in, revisable, exitable. The can-exit half
  ("if the network abuses the system, nodes stop running it") + the NCI floor (no node coerced
  into a computation it refuses) are enforced at the node layer.
