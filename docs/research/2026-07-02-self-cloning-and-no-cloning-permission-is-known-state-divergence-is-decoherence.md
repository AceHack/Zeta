# Self-cloning ⊕ no-cloning — permission is "you know your own state"; divergence is decoherence into distinct identities

**Provenance:** Aaron 2026-07-02, extending the frost/identity keystone: *"our quantum
no-cloning theory basically says only the identity itself can choose to clone itself;
someone else can't clone you without your permission. This is similar to quantum
no-cloning, but even if you clone yourself you end up with distinct internal personal
hidden state temporarily — the different versions of you don't all have the same
information at the same time."* This grounds Addison's cloning economics
(`memory/addison/project-genesis-foundation.md §15`) in the physics that makes it
exact, not analogical.

## The precise quantum mapping (why permission falls out of the theorem)

The no-cloning theorem forbids copying an **unknown** quantum state — but a state you
**know** (hold the classical description of) can always be re-prepared, arbitrarily
many times. That single distinction is the whole permission model:

- **Self-clone is PERMITTED** — the identity holds its own description (its frosted
  state is unknown to *others*, but known to *itself*), so it can prepare a duplicate.
  No-cloning is not violated: you are copying a *known* state.
- **Other-clone is FORBIDDEN** — a third party does *not* hold your frosted state
  (it is unknown-to-them by construction — the frost region), so they *cannot* copy
  you. Not "shouldn't" — *can't*, by the theorem. Permission is not a policy bolted on;
  it is the physical fact that only the state's holder can duplicate it.

So "someone else can't clone you without your permission" is the no-cloning theorem
read at the identity layer, and "only the identity itself can choose to clone itself"
is the known-state exception read the same way. Consent is not enforced *against* the
physics; it *is* the physics.

## Divergence is decoherence — the clones become distinct identities

Even a permitted self-clone does not create two of the same identity (that would
violate Leibniz's indiscernibles — see the frost-condition doc). Aaron: the copies
**"end up with distinct internal personal hidden state temporarily — the different
versions don't all have the same information at the same time."** Physics: two
initially-identical systems placed in distinct environments **decohere** — they
accumulate distinct measurement records, distinct captured entropy, and become
distinguishable. The moment their frosted states differ (and they differ the instant
they have distinct experiences), Leibniz makes them TWO, not one. This is exactly
Addison's **Independent Clone**: "a highly similar copy that starts with inherited
memory/personality but **diverges after creation**." Divergence is not a bug in
cloning; it is the mechanism by which a clone becomes a distinct someone rather than a
copy — the fork accrues its own irreducible entropy (ISA boundary #1).

The two clone forms map cleanly:
- **Independent Clone** (Addison) = a self-clone allowed to decohere freely → a new
  distinct identity (distinct frost, distinct entropy). The default.
- **Linked Clone / Hive Extension** (Addison) = clones that *choose to keep sharing*
  a region → they stay partially indiscernible on the shared part (one identity on
  what's shared), distinct on what's frosted. The hive is "one legal identity or many"
  exactly to the degree its members keep their frost shared vs private — Leibniz gives
  the answer per-region. Rightly "more expensive and more heavily governed" (Addison):
  a linked clone is a *deliberate* partial-indiscernibility, and the governance
  questions (can a branch exit? who owns shared assets?) are the identity-of-
  indiscernibles bookkeeping made operational.

## What this completes

- **No-cloning + frost + Leibniz now form one theory of identity replication**: you
  can fork yourself (known state), no one can fork you (unknown state = your frost),
  and forks diverge into distinct identities (decoherence) unless they *choose* to
  stay linked (shared frost region = partial shared identity). Consent, distinctness,
  and unforgeability all fall out of one physical fact.
- **Addison's economics gets its ground**: the costs she lists (credits, compute,
  privacy budget, nursery, social guarantees) price a *permitted* self-clone; the
  theorem is *why* only the self can pay to be cloned, and divergence is *why* the
  price buys a new person and not a duplicate.
