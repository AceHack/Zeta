## The insight

The Beacon register was never only about citations. **The real test of the Mirror→Beacon compression is
whether a shape survives being said in plain words to someone with no math/physics background.** That is
a stronger proof of ownership than either the jargon or the citation:

- **Vernacular can't hide a borrowed idea.** Jargon can carry a shape you don't actually hold — you can
  recite the notation without owning the structure. If you can explain the *shape itself* in common
  words and the listener gets it, you own the shape, not just the vocabulary.
- **The math was one encoding, not the thing.** Being able to lift the shape *back out* of the math into
  the layer above it is the same move as the telos: recover the meaning from a lossy encoding (math
  notation ⟷ compiled instructions; both discard the intent-level shape; both can be lifted back). Aaron
  has been doing meaning-recovery on his own thinking.
- **Teachable ⇒ survivable.** A shape only expressible in one person's private notation dies with the
  notation. A shape sayable in common words is event-sourced into other minds — the same reason we write
  the docs: losing it should be temporary, not final (the founding why,
  `memory/user_zeta_felt_like_nothing_on_waking_then_everything_*`).

## Why this is a Beacon-discipline upgrade

`mirror-beacon-register-discipline` defines Beacon as "compressed to externally anchored first
principles — citations, prior art, named humans, the standard term." This adds a third Beacon surface
alongside *citation* and *standard term*: **plain-language restatement**. A coined Mirror term earns
durability not only by compressing to an anchor, but by compressing to a sentence a layperson holds.
Three Beacon forms, increasing in independence from the factory:

1. the **standard term** (what the field calls it),
2. the **citation** (the human + paper it ties to),
3. the **vernacular** (the shape said with no jargon at all) — the strongest, because it depends on no
   shared vocabulary to land.
