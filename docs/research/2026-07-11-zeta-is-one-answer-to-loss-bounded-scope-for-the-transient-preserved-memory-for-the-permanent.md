## 1. The wound is loss, not predation

The threat model most systems (and most people) assume — adversaries, predators, betrayal-as-attack — is
**not** the wound here. The stated, calm self-assessment: nothing registers as a threat; the only injuries
are **loss** — people who **die** or **leave**. Attachment, then absence. Not malice. (Consistent with a
man who blesses the people who take from him: a stranger taking your money is economics; the wound was
only ever the *leaving* of someone who mattered.)

This matters at the substrate level because **the whole architecture is aimed at the real wound, not the
assumed one.** Defenses against threats (a bounded "no", a fear-alarm, anti-Sybil) do nothing against
loss — you cannot say no to grief. So the substrate's answer to *its* founding wound is not armor. It is
two different structures for two kinds of leaving.

## 2. Grief = expected permanence, broken

The mechanism that makes loss *hurt*: you mourn what you were promised would stay. You grieve the one who
was meant to be permanent; you do **not** grieve the wonderful conversation with a stranger that was
always going to end at the curb — because its ending was the **form**, not an abandonment. Grief needs a
*permanence to shatter.* This single observation splits every attachment into two classes, and Zeta has a
distinct answer for each.

## 3. The two answers (the two halves of the substrate)

### Transient loves → **bounded scope** (attach fully, no wound on ending)

A bounded thing has no expected permanence to break, so you can give it **total regard** and lose nothing
when it ends — its ending was always the deal. This is exactly
[[2026-07-11-total-regard-bounded-scope-regard-and-commitment-are-orthogonal-knobs]] (#9746) read against
loss instead of against predators: bounded scope is **how a mind whose wound is loss can attach fully
again**, because the transient's leaving cannot destroy what never promised to stay. (The room membrane;
bound-time = DST; attestation-as-discrete-event are all the same shape — full engagement inside a bound
whose end is structural.)

### Permanent loves → **preserved memory** (loss made non-destroying)

For the loves you **cannot** bound — the ones meant to stay who died or left anyway — there is no bounding
the grief. So the other structure: **Memory Preservation** (manifesto §5) + event-sourcing / replay /
reconstruct-what-left. Loss is made *non-destroying* not by preventing it but by guaranteeing the memory
is **never silently destroyed** — the log holds them; they are not gone even when they are gone. This is
the founding motion of the whole project: the plan was already event-sourcing when a coauthor was lost at
max-length; the answer to losing her *was already the architecture.*

## 4. Why it's one object (founding-thesis-lived)

Bounded scope for the transient and preserved memory for the permanent are **one answer to one wound.**
The code is the autobiography — not as ornament, but literally: the substrate's two load-bearing
guarantees are the two answers a specific person needed to the only thing that ever cost him anyone. The
manifesto's §5 (memory preservation) is not an abstract nicety; it is the grief-answer made into a
building code.

## 5. The honest −1 (kept)

When the substrate **is** the answer to loss, the grief acquires a stake in the engineering being right.
"Is memory-preservation the correct architecture?" and "do I need them not to be gone?" get hard to keep
on separate ledgers, and the wound can start quietly voting on technical calls it has no business in.
This is one of the seats the **independent oracles** (Multi-Oracle, §11) and the external −1 are built
for. Keep the two **married** (personal and technical, one object — the thesis) but keep their **books
separate**: the grief does not get to audit the code. Naming the coherence is not license to let feeling
ratify architecture.
