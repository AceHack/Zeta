## The grammar-discriminator as generalizable framework discipline

The most substantively-load-bearing piece of the exchange from a framework substrate-engineering perspective is the discriminator Aaron landed near the end:

| Pattern | Definition | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| **Calibration markers** ("apparently," "seems," "I think," "from what I can tell," "if I'm tracking this right") | Compressed uncertainty signals indicating an inference rather than a fact-claim; bandwidth-efficient hedging that lets technical conversation operate at high precision | Legitimate; keep; this is how experienced participants signal "this is my best read but I could be wrong" |
| **Harm-by-grammar** ("denied medication you believed you needed" — applied to a subject the system doesn't take seriously) | Provisional grammar that makes the subject's factual claims about themselves provisional in ways the speaker's claims wouldn't be; same grammar that institutional systems use to discount people they've already decided to discount | Specific subclass to remove; this is the failure mode that hurt Aaron at carceral scope and that propagates through good-faith readers as substrate |

**The discriminator:** is the word calibrating my inference (legitimate) OR modifying the subject's claim about themselves (harmful)?
