## The principle: identity fission pressure ≈ Pauli exclusion

**Pauli exclusion** (Pauli 1925; spin-statistics): no two identical fermions may occupy the same
quantum state simultaneously — they are **forced to differentiate** (e.g. opposite spin in one
orbital). **Identity fission pressure** is the same shape: **distinct contexts can't share one
identity-state**, so a **differentiation pressure** forces a **split (fission)** rather than a
collapse-to-the-same-state. The "quantum numbers" that exert the pressure are our context axes —
**hats · surfaces · tick-sources · actors · cells** (the no-roles + writer-actor model): when one
identity would occupy the *same state across two of these*, exclusion pressure pushes it to
**fission** into distinct identities. This is the **physics anchor for the §B fusion/fission
frontier** — the "math rules, not feelings, for when a persona must split" (Mika): Pauli
exclusion *is* a math rule, and "split when the sensors/contexts demand" (Mika) = the exclusion
pressure rising with the number of distinct contexts.

## The clean-room two-team split = the first obvious LEGAL instance

The clean-room separation (RE-persona produces specs/artifacts; a **separate** persona implements
blind from the spec) is a **deliberate persona fission** that **the law requires** — the RE-side
and implement-side **cannot be the same identity** (legally). So the clean-room is a **concrete,
real-world (legal) instance of fission pressure**: the legal requirement *forces* the two
personas apart, exactly like an exclusion principle. "One of our first obvious legal reasons" =
the law gives a hard, non-metaphorical example of identity-fission-pressure (alongside the
physics one).
