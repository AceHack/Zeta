# Engagement profiles: compile public work freely — ask (don't infer) about inner life

Carved sentence:

> A profile on a third party splits by **whether they published it** — not by where it lives or
> how many sources it spans. **Public work they chose to expose** (papers, talks, public
> professional profiles, stated positions — documented across many venues *on purpose*) is
> theirs-to-be-engaged: **compile it freely in our own repo and cite it.** They chose public;
> aggregating public info is **scholarship, not surveillance**, and it *is* the
> anchor-to-human-prior-art discipline. **Never infer or model their inner life** — for internal
> states (motivations, beliefs, qualia) the method is **ask, and believe their account**
> (first-person authority; consent-first). **Do not compile the non-public sphere** — personal
> facts they did *not* offer publicly (that is their frost) — and keep **our own private
> relationships / approach-strategy** out of public docs. Reframe "dossier / persons-of-interest"
> (Mirror) → "engagement profile" (Beacon).

## The line in one test

**Did they publish / offer it for engagement?** → compile & cite; repo-good; **cross-source is fine**
(a literature review of a person's public output). **Is it their internal state?** → **ask them; don't
guess** — believe their account, and let "I don't know" be a real answer. **Private, non-public
sphere?** → don't compile. **Our relationship / approach-strategy with them?** → keep out of public docs.

## Internal states: ask, don't infer

Modeling/guessing someone's motivations is the objectifying move (a person solved as a thing);
**asking and believing is consent-first** (their inner life is theirs to disclose). You cannot see
insides from outside — the only honest access is the self-report, elicited with non-biased questions
(the non-biased-elicitation method — Beacon: survey methodology / psychometrics, concretely Tullis &
Albert, *Measuring the User Experience*, 2008; the private individual who pointed Aaron at that
published work is **not named — asked directly, name use declined for employer confidentiality**, see
the book CONSENT-LEDGER; the qualia-axiom respect). Honest refinement so "believe them" stays
true: believe them as **authority over their own account**, not as an infallible instrument on their
own causes — introspective access is genuinely limited (**Nisbett & Wilson 1977**, people confabulate
reasons), so **"I don't know" is often the most honest answer.** Asking-not-inferring surfaces the true
state (uncertainty) instead of manufacturing a false one (your inference *or* their confabulation) —
the four-register discipline in interpersonal form: let unknown be unknown.

## Why

People **choose what to expose**; compiling their chosen-public output is legitimate (and required by
anchor-to-human-prior-art). Privacy-as-hard-money protects the frost they **kept**, not the work they
**published** — so the guard is *public-chosen vs private-not-offered*, never *single- vs cross-source*.
And the inner life is never inferred, only asked — the consent-first / glass-halo ethos turned outward.
