# Engagement profiles are public-work-only — never surveillance dossiers

Carved sentence:

> A profile kept on a third party is a **public-work engagement profile**, never a
> surveillance **dossier**: build it *only* from what they **published to be engaged**
> (papers, talks, public positions) so outreach lands in their terms — **never** by
> aggregating personal/private information across sources or "tracking" a person.
> Personal cross-source aggregation is forbidden (it spends frost they never offered);
> and even public-work profiles on named living third parties stay **out of the
> shared/fleet-visible repo**. Reframe the Mirror term "dossier / persons-of-interest"
> to the Beacon "engagement profile."

## Why

It is the consent-first / privacy-as-hard-money / glass-halo ethos turned **outward** — you
cannot found a substrate on "frost is inviolable, privacy is earned" and simultaneously keep
non-consensual files on people. **Published work is consent-implicit for engagement** (they
released it to be read); **personal life is not.** Practical corollary: "you clearly know my
work" opens a door; "you've been watching me" slams it. The legitimate use (tailor a proof to a
researcher's published output) is excellent scholarship and the *produce* move; the surveillance
version is the extract move and a self-halo violation.
